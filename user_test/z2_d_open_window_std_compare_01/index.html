Advanced(基準・参照)
The Open Window
1 Framton sat on the edge of his chair, turning his letter of introduction between his fingers. His doctor had recommended peace and country air, but visiting strangers was hardly peaceful.
2 “My aunt will be down soon,” said Vera, her fifteen-year-old niece. “Do you know much about her?”
3 “Almost nothing,” he admitted.
4 Vera glanced towards the French window, which stood wide open to the lawn.
5 “Her great tragedy happened three years ago today.”
6 Framton stopped turning the letter.
7 “Her husband and her two young brothers went out shooting through that window. Their little brown spaniel went with them. They tried to cross a bog, but the ground gave way beneath them. They all disappeared. Their bodies were never found.”
8 “How terrible,” Framton murmured.
9 “My aunt still thinks they’ll come back. She keeps the window open until dark, waiting for them.” Vera’s voice grew quieter. “Her husband had his white coat over his arm. Her youngest brother was singing, ‘Bertie, why do you bound?’ He always sang it to tease her.”
10 She looked out across the empty lawn.
11 “Sometimes, on evenings like this, I almost expect to see them.”
12 Before Framton could reply, her aunt hurried into the room.
13 “I hope Vera has kept you company! You don’t mind the open window? My husband and brothers will be back soon. They’ve been shooting. Their boots will be covered in mud, of course!”
14 Framton felt a chill. So she really was waiting.
15 He began describing his illness, trying to keep his eyes away from the window. His hostess listened with only half her attention. Again and again, she looked past him towards the lawn.
16 Then her face brightened.
17 “Here they are at last!”
18 Vera stared outside, her eyes wide with horror.
19 Framton turned.
20 Three figures were crossing the lawn in the fading light, carrying guns. A white coat hung over one man’s arm. A brown spaniel followed close behind.
21 Across the quiet garden came a young voice:
22 “Bertie, why do you bound?”
23 Framton could scarcely breathe. The dead were coming towards the house.
24 He grabbed his hat and stick and rushed out. Within seconds, he was running down the drive.
25 The men stepped through the window, muddy and hungry.
26 “Who was that?” asked the husband.
27 “A most peculiar visitor,” his wife said. “He talked about his nerves, then suddenly ran away.”
28 “It was the spaniel,” Vera said calmly. “He told me he was terrified of dogs. In India, a pack chased him into an empty grave. He spent the whole night there, with them snarling above him.”
29 Inventing convincing stories at a moment’s notice was Vera’s speciality.

D: 今回の新方式
The Open Window
1 Framton waited in a stranger’s house. He felt nervous about meeting someone new. The woman’s young niece, Vera, sat with him.
2 “My aunt will come down soon,” Vera said. “Do you know her well?”
3 “No. I know very little about her,” Framton said.
4 Vera turned towards the big, open window.
5 “My aunt lost her husband and two brothers three years ago. They went out through that window with their dog. They fell into deep mud and could not get out. Nobody ever found their bodies.”
6 “I’m very sorry to hear that,” said Framton.
7 “She believes they will return. That’s why the window stays open until night.” Vera spoke more quietly. “Her husband carried a white coat over his arm that day.”
8 Vera watched the empty garden.
9 “On evenings like this, I sometimes feel they might come back.”
10 Her aunt came in before Framton could answer.
11 “I hope Vera looked after you,” she said brightly. “Is the open window all right? My husband and brothers will be home soon. Their boots will be so dirty!”
12 Framton felt cold. She really did expect the dead men to return.
13 He started talking about his illness. He tried not to look outside. But Vera’s aunt hardly listened. Her eyes kept moving towards the garden.
14 Suddenly, she smiled.
15 “Look! They’re back now!”
16 Vera looked out. Her face was full of fear.
17 Framton turned to the window. Three men were walking across the grass. One carried a white coat over his arm. A dog followed them.
18 Framton could hardly breathe. The dead men were coming closer.
19 He took his hat and stick and hurried out. A moment later, he was running down the road.
20 The men came in through the open window. Their boots were dirty, and they wanted dinner.
21 “Who was that man?” her husband asked.
22 “A very strange visitor,” his wife replied. “He told me about his illness. Then he just got up and ran out.”
23 “He was afraid of the dog,” Vera said calmly. “He told me about it. Once, in India, some dogs ran after him. He jumped into a deep hole to hide. He stayed there all night. The dogs stood above him and made angry noises.”
24 Vera was very good at making up stories in a moment. She knew how to make people believe them.

D案のMust-keep選定結果(Advanced本文・Story Seedから、全作品共通の選定基準で自動生成。人手の修正なし)

語りの維持(narration): Keep third-person narration, mainly close to Framton’s frightened understanding of events, with an outside view after he leaves. Use a cool, matter-of-fact voice that does not expose Vera’s deception before the ending.

① Essential(絶対保持)

  • Framton is a nervous visitor who knows almost nothing about his hostess. Vera, her young niece, keeps him company while he waits.
    理由: His nervousness and ignorance make him vulnerable to Vera’s story.
  • Vera says her aunt’s husband and brothers left through the open window and were lost, believed dead. She says her aunt keeps the window open because she expects them back.
    理由: This gives the window its frightening meaning and prepares Framton to mistake the returning men for ghosts.
  • Vera’s aunt arrives cheerfully, says the men will return soon, and keeps watching for them while paying little attention to Framton’s talk about his illness.
    理由: Her innocent behavior seems to confirm Vera’s account of a woman still waiting for the dead.
  • The men approach with details matching Vera’s story. Framton believes the dead have returned and runs away in terror.
    理由: The apparent confirmation and his flight are the central suspense payoff.
  • After Framton flees, the men enter through the window as ordinary living people.
    理由: Their normal return overturns the apparent ghost story.
  • Vera calmly explains Framton’s flight by claiming he fears the dog and supplying a convincing story about a past frightening encounter with dogs.
    理由: Her instant second invention provides the comic payoff and demonstrates how she deceives people.
  • The narrator closes by saying that inventing convincing stories at a moment’s notice is Vera’s special skill.
    理由: This final observation confirms the meaning of both stories and preserves the dry humor.

② Flexible(意味・役割を保持。具体物・表現は変更可)

  • Matching clues in Vera’s account and the men’s return
    保持する機能: Plant at least one distinctive detail in her account and repeat it recognizably when the men approach.
    変更可: The white coat and the brother’s song need not both remain. The song’s wording and the descriptions may be simplified, provided the chosen clue clearly matches.
    理由: Recognition is necessary; every original identifying detail is not.
  • The account of the men’s disappearance
    保持する機能: Make their supposed deaths credible enough for Framton to believe Vera and understand the aunt’s alleged waiting.
    変更可: The bog, missing bodies, shooting trip, exact three-year interval, and anniversary can be compressed or generalized.
    理由: The supposed tragedy matters more than its precise circumstances.
  • The dog and Vera’s final explanation
    保持する機能: Include a dog with the returning men so Vera can use it to explain Framton’s escape, supported by a concrete past fright.
    変更可: The dog’s breed, color, and size are not required. The India setting, empty grave, pack of dogs, and overnight ordeal may be shortened or generalized.
    理由: The second lie needs a plausible immediate trigger and persuasive support, not every detail of the anecdote.

③ Optional(省略・統合・一般化可)

  • The names Framton and Vera, and Vera’s exact age of fifteen
    理由: Clear, consistent names or role labels are sufficient; the relationships matter more than the exact labels.
  • The letter of introduction, the doctor’s advice, and the reason for Framton’s country visit
    理由: They explain the visit but are not needed once he is established as a nervous stranger.
  • The exact number and ages of the aunt’s brothers
    理由: The family group must remain consistent, but its precise size does not create the effect.
  • Framton handling the letter, Vera’s quiet voice and horrified expression, fading light, guns, muddy boots, hunger, and Framton’s hat and stick
    理由: These support atmosphere, performance, or physical action but can be omitted without breaking the central deception.
  • The aunt’s exact remarks about the visitor and the men’s brief question about him
    理由: The ending needs an occasion for Vera’s explanation, not these particular exchanges.

結末前に示唆しないこと: Until Framton has fled and the men enter normally, do not reveal that they are alive or hint that Vera invented the tragedy. Save her second invention and the narrator’s observation about her storytelling skill for the closing sequence. Do not add a moral or further explanation after the final observation.
