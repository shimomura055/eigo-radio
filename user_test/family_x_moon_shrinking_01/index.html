Full Story Part 1
88.3秒〜 (53.2秒)
The Moon has wrinkles, too. But it does not need beauty cream. These are wrinkles in its land. Scientists think they are “marks left as the Moon cooled and got smaller.” If that is true, studying them closely should show how much the Moon got smaller. A good detective might think so. But this study says, “Wait a moment.”

The wrinkles are cliffs, steep on one side and gentle on the other. Scientists think they formed when forces pushed rocks together. One block of rock then moved up over another. The research team used pictures from a spacecraft camera to map these cliffs. In a way, they carefully checked the Moon’s wrinkles.

But estimates from recently formed wrinkles showed very little shrinking of the Moon’s radius late in its history. This was far less than models of the Moon’s cooling history predicted. The result makes you want to ask, “Moon, did you leave out some of your shrinking?”

Full Story Part 2
155.9秒〜 (47.1秒)
But it is too soon to give the models a failing grade. The models show shrinking over time. The young wrinkles, however, tell us about shrinking late in the Moon’s history. We cannot compare account books from different time periods and shout, “The numbers don’t match!” This is not a case where people measured the same time in the same way and got two different answers.

Still, this gap is hard to ignore. The research team suggested one possible reason: a thick layer of broken rock. This layer has many gaps. It may have acted like the Moon’s “warm jacket.” If heat could not get out easily, the inside would not cool fast. If it did not cool fast, it would get smaller more slowly. This idea slows down the shrinking itself.

Full Story Part 3
226.5秒〜 (61.5秒)
Another possibility is the strength of the rock. In strong, springy rock, breaks may have been less likely to form near the surface. The pushing force may first have needed to reach a certain level. This is not about keeping the Moon warm. It is about when shrinking appears as wrinkles on the surface. It seems the Moon does not always turn all its shrinking into wrinkles and show it as it happens.

Both ideas could explain the same gap. But one is about how the Moon cools, and the other is about how its wrinkles form. They explain different parts of the process. Of course, both are only ideas. No one has found the cause yet. The Moon is still being questioned.

The Moon’s wrinkles may not be a “receipt” that shows the full amount of shrinking. So we should not ask only, “How much did it get smaller?” We also need to ask, “How does that shrinking leave wrinkles behind?” To read the Moon’s face, we must look not only at the wrinkles we can see. We must also look at what happens before they form.
