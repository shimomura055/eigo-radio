Advanced(基準・参照)
Before the Last Light
1 Melos’s voice rang through the hall. “You kill men because you fear them. You won’t even let them speak.”
2 Silence followed. Then the guards seized the young shepherd, and the ruler sentenced him to death.
3 “Give me three days,” Melos said. “My sister is getting married. Let me see her wedding. I will return.”
4 The ruler smiled. “Your friend will stay here. If you are late, he dies instead.”
5 Melos turned to his friend, but before he could speak, his friend held out his hands for the chains.
6 “Go. I know you’ll come back.”
7 At the wedding, his sister’s laughter rose above the other voices. Melos listened closely. He wanted to remember exactly how it sounded.
8 Before dawn on the third day, he left for the city.
9 At the river, he stopped. The bridge was gone. Rain had turned the water brown and violent. He stepped back, ran, and threw himself into the flood.
10 The current pulled him under. He fought toward the light, caught a branch, and dragged himself onto the far bank.
11 Farther along, three men blocked the road. A knife flashed. Melos struck the man holding it with his walking stick and ran uphill, their shouts following him.
12 By afternoon, every step hurt. Blood stained his feet. He fell beside a rock and tried to rise, but his legs would not obey.
13 He could wait here. Someone might find him. He could go home.
14 His friend would die.
15 For one terrible moment, that seemed easier than getting up.
16 Then he remembered the hands held out for the chains. His friend had trusted him without asking for another promise.
17 Water dripped from a crack in the rock. Melos drank, pushed himself onto his knees, and stood.
18 One step. Another.
19 At the city gates, the execution bell rang.
20 He ran toward the square. Above his friend, the sword was already raised.
21 “Stop! I am here!”
22 His friend turned. Melos fell against him.
23 “I nearly abandoned you,” he whispered.
24 “I nearly stopped believing,” his friend answered.
25 They held each other as the last sunlight touched the walls.
26 The ruler stared at them.
27 “Lower the sword,” he said at last. “Free them both.”
28 Then, more quietly, he asked, “How does a man earn such trust?”
29 For the first time, he waited for an answer instead of ordering one.

D: 今回の新方式
Before the Last Light
1 Melos spoke loudly to the ruler. “You are afraid of people, so you kill them. You do not listen to them first.”
2 The guards took hold of Melos. The ruler said he must die.
3 “Please let me leave for three days,” Melos said. “My sister is getting married. After her wedding, I will come back here to die.”
4 “Return before sunset on the third day,” the ruler said. “Your friend must stay here. Come back late, and I will kill him instead.”
5 Melos looked at his friend. Before Melos could say anything, his friend put out his hands. He was ready for the chains.
6 “Go to the wedding. I am sure you will return.”
7 At the wedding, Melos heard his sister laughing. He listened carefully. He wanted to keep that happy sound in his mind.
8 Early on the third morning, Melos started back. It was still dark.
9 He reached the river, but the bridge was not there. Heavy rain had made the river dangerous. He jumped into the water.
10 The water pulled him down. He fought to reach the top. At last, he caught a branch and pulled himself out.
11 Later, some men stopped him on the road. One had a knife. Melos hit that man with his stick and ran away.
12 By afternoon, Melos could hardly walk. He fell beside a large rock. He tried to stand, but his legs were too weak.
13 He could stay here and save himself. Perhaps he could go home.
14 But then his friend would die in his place.
15 For one terrible moment, Melos wanted to stay.
16 Then he remembered his friend’s hands, ready for the chains. His friend had been so sure he would return.
17 Melos drank some water from the rock. Slowly, he got to his feet and started moving again.
18 Near the city, he heard the bell. There was almost no time left.
19 Melos ran into the square. A man stood above his friend with a sword. He was ready to bring it down.
20 “Wait! I have come back!”
21 His friend turned toward him. Melos fell into his friend’s arms.
22 “I almost left you here to die,” Melos said quietly.
23 “I almost lost my trust in you,” his friend said.
24 The two men held each other close.
25 The ruler watched them.
26 “Put down the sword,” he said. “Let both men go free.”
27 Then his voice grew softer. “What must I do for someone to trust me like that?”
28 He gave no order for an answer. For the first time, he waited for one.

D案のMust-keep選定結果(Advanced本文・Story Seedから、全作品共通の選定基準で自動生成。人手の修正なし)

語りの維持(narration): Keep third-person narration closely following Melos, including his thoughts and temptation to give up. Keep the serious, sympathetic stance, showing the friend’s and ruler’s feelings through their words and actions.

① Essential(絶対保持)

  • Melos condemns the ruler for killing people out of fear without hearing them, and the ruler sentences him to death.
    理由: This establishes the ruler’s cruelty and the conflict between fear and trust.
  • Melos receives short leave for his sister’s wedding and promises to return for his execution. His friend will die instead if he is late.
    理由: The wedding explains his departure, while the exchange makes keeping his promise a matter of life and death.
  • His friend willingly becomes the hostage and expresses confidence that Melos will return.
    理由: The friend’s freely given trust motivates Melos’s later decision to continue.
  • On the return journey, Melos becomes too exhausted to continue and briefly considers saving himself, knowing his friend would die.
    理由: His real temptation gives the promise its emotional weight; this must be more than ordinary tiredness.
  • Remembering his friend’s trust, Melos gets up and continues toward the city.
    理由: Trust, rather than physical strength alone, drives the central turning point.
  • Melos reaches his friend just as the execution is about to happen.
    理由: The last-moment arrival resolves the race without losing its suspense.
  • At their reunion, Melos admits he nearly abandoned his friend, and his friend admits he nearly stopped believing. They embrace.
    理由: Their mutual honesty makes the loyalty human rather than perfect and supplies the emotional payoff.
  • The ruler frees both men, asks how to earn such trust, and waits for an answer instead of commanding one.
    理由: His movement from fearful control toward listening completes the story. Merely stopping the execution would lose this final change.

② Flexible(意味・役割を保持。具体物・表現は変更可)

  • The leave period and approaching execution deadline
    保持する機能: Establish a clear return deadline and increasing urgency as Melos approaches the city.
    変更可: The exact three-day count and departure time may be generalized. The bell and fading sunlight need not both remain.
    理由: A clear time limit matters more than the exact schedule or time signals.
  • Dangerous water and attackers on the return journey
    保持する機能: Show that serious external obstacles delay Melos and drain his strength before he nearly gives up.
    変更可: Shorten the episodes and simplify the missing bridge, current, branch, weapons, number of attackers, and escape movements.
    理由: The accumulating danger is necessary, but the detailed action is not.
  • The friend’s offered hands and Melos’s later memory of them
    保持する機能: Connect the friend’s early willing trust with the memory that helps Melos rise again.
    変更可: The hands and chains may be replaced by a simple recollection of the friend’s earlier words or willing acceptance.
    理由: The setup and later recall must connect, but the exact gesture is not essential.
  • The execution already being prepared when Melos arrives
    保持する機能: Make it immediately clear that his friend is only moments from death.
    変更可: The raised sword, square, bell, and Melos’s exact shouted words may be simplified.
    理由: The immediate danger creates the payoff; the execution’s precise staging does not.

③ Optional(省略・統合・一般化可)

  • Melos being a young shepherd
    理由: His occupation and age do not cause the conflict or determine his choices.
  • His sister’s laughter and his wish to remember it
    理由: This adds tenderness, but the wedding and the risk of his own death already establish what he may lose.
  • Bloody feet, the rock, and detailed attempts to stand
    理由: A simple description of collapse can establish the necessary exhaustion.
  • Water dripping from the rock and Melos drinking
    理由: This supplies physical relief, but his recovery can be shortened without losing trust as the reason he continues.
  • The ruler’s smile, the hall’s silence, and sunlight on the walls
    理由: These strengthen atmosphere but are not needed to follow the action or understand the ending.

結末前に示唆しないこと: Keep uncertain whether Melos will arrive in time until the execution scene. Save the friend’s admission of doubt for the reunion, and do not announce or foreshadow the ruler’s decision to free both men or his new willingness to listen before the final exchange.
