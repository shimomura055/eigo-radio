Full Story Part 1 (Aoede / Algieba / Schedar)
82.3秒〜 (60.8秒)
Bob waited in the doorway of a closed tool shop. He was out of the rain, and it was nearly ten. He moved his unlit cigar between his teeth and watched the street. Even after twenty years, he still remembered this place. A police officer stopped beside the shop's doorway.
"I'm only waiting for a friend,"
Bob told the officer.
"There was a restaurant here once."
"The restaurant was knocked down five years ago."
Bob struck a match and raised it to his cigar. Its flame made his face bright for a moment.
"Jimmy and I ate there twenty years ago tonight. We were best friends and grew up like brothers."
Rain hit the street as the officer listened quietly.
"I moved West, but Jimmy stayed in New York. For him, no other place was good enough. We promised to meet here tonight at ten, whatever happened."

Full Story Part 2 (Schedar / Algieba / Aoede / Fenrir)
154.8秒〜 (71.9秒)
"Do you hear from him these days?"
"We lost touch years ago,"
Bob said with a smile.
"But Jimmy always kept his promises, so he'll come if he's alive."
"How much longer will you stay?"
"I'll give him thirty minutes."
"Good night, and I hope your friend arrives soon."
The officer continued down the street. Bob leaned back in the doorway, listening for footsteps. He checked his watch, then put it away. After such a long journey, a few minutes hardly mattered. At ten thirty, a tall man in a long overcoat hurried towards Bob.
"Bob, is that really you?"
"Jimmy, you're here!"
The man held both Bob's hands, and Bob laughed happily. They seemed young again, with their lives still in front of them. They walked away with their arms linked together. Bob eagerly talked about his life, business and money out West. After twenty years, there was so much to tell. They passed a drugstore, and its windows lit their faces. Bob suddenly stopped and pulled his arm away.

Full Story Part 1 (Aoede / Algieba / Schedar)
78.6秒〜 (61.7秒)
Bob stood in the doorway of a closed hardware store, sheltered from the rain. It was almost ten. He shifted his unlit cigar between his teeth and looked down the street. Twenty years was a long time, but he had not forgotten the place. A patrolman stopped beside him.
"Just waiting for a friend,"
Bob explained.
"There used to be a restaurant here."
"They pulled it down five years ago."
Bob struck a match. The flame lit his face as he raised it to his cigar.
"We had our last dinner there twenty years ago tonight. My best friend, Jimmy. We grew up together, like brothers."
The patrolman listened calmly while rain tapped against the pavement.
"I went West,"
Bob continued.
"Jimmy stayed here. He thought New York was the only place worth living. Before we parted, we promised to meet here at ten tonight, whatever happened."

Light from its windows fell across their faces. Bob stopped and pulled his arm free.
