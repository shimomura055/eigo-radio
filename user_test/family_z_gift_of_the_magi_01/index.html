Full Story Part 1 (Aoede / Zephyr / Kore)
85.7秒〜 (73.0秒)
Della counted her coins again, pressing them against the table. She still had only $1.87, and Christmas was tomorrow. She had saved every coin she could for Jim, her husband. How could she buy him a good enough gift? She cried for a while on their old couch. Then she noticed her face in the mirror. They were proud of two things: Della's long brown hair and Jim's gold watch. His grandfather, then his father, had owned the watch before him. She slowly let down her hair, shining below her knees. She ran her fingers through it in the pale light. Quickly, she put on her coat before changing her mind. Outside a shop selling hair, she stopped to catch her breath.
"Would you like to buy my hair?"
The shop owner lifted her thick waves of hair.
"I can pay you twenty dollars."
"Please cut it off right now."
Della closed her eyes as the scissors first touched her hair. For two hours, she rushed between shops until she found it: a simple platinum watch chain. Like Jim, it needed nothing extra to show its value. Its price was twenty-one dollars.

Full Story Part 3 (Aoede / Zephyr / Algieba)
197.3秒〜 (74.1秒)
Footsteps sounded. The door opened. Jim stopped. His eyes stayed on her hair. Della rushed toward him.
"I sold it, Jim. I couldn't let Christmas come without giving you something. It'll grow again!"
He put his arms around her. Then he gave her a package. Inside were the tortoise-shell combs she had so often admired in a shop window. She held them against her chest, smiling through tears.
"My hair grows quickly."
Suddenly remembering, she brought out the chain.
"Look! Isn't it perfect? Give me your watch."
Jim sat down and smiled gently.
"I sold the watch to buy your combs."
Della sat beside him and took his hand. Her hair was gone; the combs could not hold it. His watch was gone; the chain had nothing to carry. Each had sold their greatest treasure to buy the other a gift. The magi were wise men who brought gifts on the first Christmas. Yet here, with gifts they could not use, sat two foolish young people whose love made them the wisest of all gift-givers. They were the magi.
