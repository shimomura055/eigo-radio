# Who Says “We Won’t Attack”? A Warning About Mixing Up British Support “Won’t attack.” When you hear those words, you may already be ending the meeting in your mind. You may think, “So Britain will only defend.” But wait! One important detail is missing from the meeting notes: who will not attack? This time, the news detective must find the subject. First, let’s look at the announcement. A spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition forces in Yemen said they had stopped and destroyed one ballistic missile. The Houthis had fired it toward Riyadh. In this case, the UK Ministry of Defence announced an air-defense mission for Royal Air Force Typhoon fighter jets. They will protect Saudi cities and important sites from air attacks. Their mission is only to defend. The jets will not attack the Houthis. Now you may want to say, “Right, they will only defend!” But the announcement also mentions another task: refueling in the air. Britain also said it would continue refueling support for Saudi air-defense work. This support also helps operations to weaken Houthi military targets in Yemen. Britain says these targets threaten people in Saudi Arabia and Yemen. “Wait! You just said they wouldn’t attack, right?” Here, the detective looks closely at the name tag. “Won’t attack” means the Typhoon mission. At the same time, refueling support also helps operations to weaken Houthi military targets. Both are in the same box called “British support.” But they are not all the same kind of activity. It is like someone saying, “I won’t drive,” but helping to put fuel in a car. Not driving is not the same as not helping the trip. But this example cannot tell us who is responsible for military support. We need to check what the words include. So, if you say, “Britain won’t attack,” you take what was said about the Typhoon mission too far. You make it sound like it covers all British support. But if you look at the refueling support and say, “Then the Typhoons will attack too,” you claim more than the facts show. The tools for reading news are not a magnifying glass. They are three questions: “Who?” “For which mission?” “Doing what?” Don’t stop after seeing the big sign that says “to protect.” Look at the name tag under it too. Putting one subject back in place can change how the news looks. ## In one line Britain said its Typhoon jets would defend Saudi cities and key sites, not attack the Houthis. Its refueling support also helps weaken Houthi military targets, so “won’t attack” means the Typhoon mission, not all British support.
