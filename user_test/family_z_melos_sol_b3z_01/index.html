(1) 従来方式 (381語)

The Three-Day Promise

In a small kingdom, the ruler was afraid of enemies. If anyone spoke against him, his guards could take that person to prison. Often, the ruler ordered executions without a fair trial.

One day, a shepherd named Melos stood before him.

“You punish people before you know the truth,” Melos said. “That is not justice.”

The ruler’s face became hard. “Then you will die.”

Melos’s sister was going to be married in three days. He fell to his knees.

“Please let me go to her wedding,” he said. “I will return before my death.”

The ruler laughed. “Why should I believe you?”

Melos’s friend stepped forward. “I will stay here in his place.”

So the ruler agreed. Melos had three days to go and return. If he did not come back, his friend would die.

Melos ran from the city. He reached the river, but heavy rain had made the water wild. The small boat broke against a rock. Melos fell into the cold river and nearly disappeared under the water. At last, he reached the other side, shaking and weak.

He hurried to the wedding. He smiled for his sister, but his eyes stayed on the road. When the wedding ended, he left at once.

On the way back, three attackers jumped from behind some trees. They took his horse and pushed him to the ground. Melos tried to stand, but pain filled his body. He walked through the night, then fell beside the road.

The sky was becoming bright. The time was almost finished.

Melos looked toward the city. “I cannot do this,” he whispered. “Perhaps my friend is already dead.”

For a moment, he wanted to hide in the forest.

Then he remembered his friend’s calm face. His friend had trusted him enough to face death.

“No,” Melos said. “I gave my word.”

He stood and ran.

At the city gate, the guards were leading his friend to the place of execution. Then Melos shouted, “Wait!”

Everyone turned. Melos fell before the ruler.

“I promised to return,” he said. “I am here.”

The ruler looked at the two friends. Slowly, he lowered his hand.

“Free him,” he said.

After that day, the ruler demanded fair trials. He had learned that trust and loyalty could be stronger than fear.

Before Sunset

Melos stood before the ruler. He was only a shepherd, but he was not afraid.

“You kill people without asking if they did anything wrong!” he shouted.

“My guards will kill you too,” said the ruler.

Melos thought of his sister. Her wedding was tomorrow.

“Give me three days,” he said. “I promise to return.”

“What if you run away?”

His friend stepped forward. “Keep me here. I trust him.”

The ruler smiled. “If you are not back by sunset on the third day, your friend will die.”

Melos looked at his friend.

“I will wait,” his friend said.

After the wedding, Melos started back. Rain fell hard all night. By morning, dark water covered the bridge.

He could not wait. His friend’s life depended on him.

Melos jumped into the river.

The water pulled him under. He fought to reach the other side. At last, he caught a tree branch and pulled himself out.

Cold and wet, he ran on.

Three men blocked the road.

“Give us your money!”

“I have none!”

One man hit him with a stick. Melos pushed past him and ran. The men shouted behind him.

Soon his legs shook. He could hardly move. He fell beside the road.

The sun was low.

He could rest here. He could stay alive.

But his friend would die.

“Why did I make that promise?” he whispered.

He closed his eyes. For a moment, he wanted to forget everything.

Then he remembered his friend’s face.

*“I will wait.”*

Melos opened his eyes. Water ran between some stones nearby. He drank and slowly stood up.

His friend was still waiting.

The river pulled him away. He fought the water. At last, his feet touched land.

He was safe, but he had lost hours.

Further along the road, some men attacked him. Melos pushed past them and ran.

His legs hurt. Each step was harder than the last.

The sun sank lower.

At last, Melos fell to his knees.

He could stay here. He could live.

But his friend would die.

Melos looked down at the road. For a moment, he did not want to stand. He wanted the pain to stop.

Then he remembered his friend’s voice.

“I trust him.”

His friend was still waiting. Still hoping.

“I am coming,” Melos said.

He stood slowly.

One step. Then another.

Pain filled his legs, but he kept moving. Soon, he was running again.

When he reached the city, the last light was leaving the sky.

His friend stood before the ruler.

A guard raised his sword.

“Stop!” Melos shouted. “I am here!”

The sword stopped.

Melos ran to his friend and held him tightly. His legs shook. He could hardly speak.

The ruler watched them. For a long moment, he said nothing.

“You came back to die,” he said quietly. “And your friend trusted you to come.”

Then he told the guard to free them both.

The sword was down. His friend was safe.

Melos held him close as the light disappeared.

This time, neither had to let go.
