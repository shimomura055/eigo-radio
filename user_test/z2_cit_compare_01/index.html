Before the Last Light
1 Melos spoke loudly to the ruler. “You are afraid of people, so you kill them. You do not listen to them first.”
2 The guards took hold of Melos. The ruler said he must die.
3 “Please let me leave for three days,” Melos said. “My sister is getting married. After her wedding, I will come back here to die.”
4 “Return before sunset on the third day,” the ruler said. “Your friend must stay here. Come back late, and I will kill him instead.”
5 Melos looked at his friend. Before Melos could say anything, his friend put out his hands. He was ready for the chains.
6 “Go to the wedding. I am sure you will return.”
7 At the wedding, Melos heard his sister laughing. He listened carefully. He wanted to keep that happy sound in his mind.
8 Early on the third morning, Melos started back. It was still dark.
9 He reached the river, but the bridge was not there. Heavy rain had made the river dangerous. He jumped into the water.
10 The water pulled him down. He fought to reach the top. At last, he caught a branch and pulled himself out.
11 Later, some men stopped him on the road. One had a knife. Melos hit that man with his stick and ran away.
12 By afternoon, Melos could hardly walk. He fell beside a large rock. He tried to stand, but his legs were too weak.
13 He could stay here and save himself. Perhaps he could go home.
14 But then his friend would die in his place.
15 For one terrible moment, Melos wanted to stay.
16 Then he remembered his friend’s hands, ready for the chains. His friend had been so sure he would return.
17 Melos drank some water from the rock. Slowly, he got to his feet and started moving again.
18 Near the city, he heard the bell. There was almost no time left.
19 Melos ran into the square. A man stood above his friend with a sword. He was ready to bring it down.
20 “Wait! I have come back!”
21 His friend turned toward him. Melos fell into his friend’s arms.
22 “I almost left you here to die,” Melos said quietly.
23 “I almost lost my trust in you,” his friend said.
24 The two men held each other close.
25 The ruler watched them.
26 “Put down the sword,” he said. “Let both men go free.”
27 Then his voice grew softer. “What must I do for someone to trust me like that?”
28 He gave no order for an answer. For the first time, he waited for one.

Before the Last Light
1 Melos stood before the city’s ruler. “You are afraid of people, so you kill them. You never even listen to them.”
2 The guards took hold of Melos. The ruler said he must die.
3 “Please give me three days,” Melos said. “My sister is getting married. Let me go to her wedding. I promise to come back here to die.”
4 “Return before sunset on the third day,” the ruler said. “Your friend must wait here. Come late, and I will kill him instead.”
5 Melos turned to his friend. Before Melos could say anything, his friend held out his hands. He was ready for the guards to put chains on him.
6 “Go to the wedding. I’m sure you’ll return.”
7 At the wedding, Melos heard his sister laughing. He listened carefully. He wanted to keep that happy sound in his memory.
8 Before sunrise on the third day, Melos started back toward the city.
9 When he reached the river, the bridge was gone. Heavy rain had filled the river. He jumped into the fast water.
10 The water pulled him down. He fought his way up and caught a branch. At last, he pulled himself onto the other side.
11 Later, three men blocked his way. One held a knife. Melos hit that man with his stick. Then he escaped up the hill.
12 By afternoon, his feet were bleeding. He fell beside a rock. He tried to stand, but his legs were too weak.
13 He could stay here. Perhaps someone would help him. Then he could go home.
14 His friend would die. Melos knew that. But for a moment, saving himself seemed easier than standing up.
15 Then he remembered his friend’s hands, ready for the chains. His friend trusted him to return.
16 Some water was running down the rock. Melos drank it. Slowly, he got to his knees. Then he stood and started walking again.
17 As he reached the city, a bell rang. He ran toward the sound. A man stood over his friend, ready to bring down a sword.
18 “Wait! I’ve come back!”
19 His friend turned toward his voice. Melos fell into his arms.
20 “I almost left you to die,” Melos said quietly.
21 “And I almost lost my trust in you,” his friend said.
22 They held each other close in the evening light.
23 The ruler watched them.
24 “Put the sword down,” he said. “Let both men go free.”
25 Then he asked softly, “What must I do for someone to trust me like that?”
26 He did not order anyone to answer. For the first time, he simply waited.

The Open Window
1 Framton waited in a stranger’s house. He felt nervous about meeting someone new. The woman’s young niece, Vera, sat with him.
2 “My aunt will come down soon,” Vera said. “Do you know her well?”
3 “No. I know very little about her,” Framton said.
4 Vera turned towards the big, open window.
5 “My aunt lost her husband and two brothers three years ago. They went out through that window with their dog. They fell into deep mud and could not get out. Nobody ever found their bodies.”
6 “I’m very sorry to hear that,” said Framton.
7 “She believes they will return. That’s why the window stays open until night.” Vera spoke more quietly. “Her husband carried a white coat over his arm that day.”
8 Vera watched the empty garden.
9 “On evenings like this, I sometimes feel they might come back.”
10 Her aunt came in before Framton could answer.
11 “I hope Vera looked after you,” she said brightly. “Is the open window all right? My husband and brothers will be home soon. Their boots will be so dirty!”
12 Framton felt cold. She really did expect the dead men to return.
13 He started talking about his illness. He tried not to look outside. But Vera’s aunt hardly listened. Her eyes kept moving towards the garden.
14 Suddenly, she smiled.
15 “Look! They’re back now!”
16 Vera looked out. Her face was full of fear.
17 Framton turned to the window. Three men were walking across the grass. One carried a white coat over his arm. A dog followed them.
18 Framton could hardly breathe. The dead men were coming closer.
19 He took his hat and stick and hurried out. A moment later, he was running down the road.
20 The men came in through the open window. Their boots were dirty, and they wanted dinner.
21 “Who was that man?” her husband asked.
22 “A very strange visitor,” his wife replied. “He told me about his illness. Then he just got up and ran out.”
23 “He was afraid of the dog,” Vera said calmly. “He told me about it. Once, in India, some dogs ran after him. He jumped into a deep hole to hide. He stayed there all night. The dogs stood above him and made angry noises.”
24 Vera was very good at making up stories in a moment. She knew how to make people believe them.

The Open Window
1 Framton was visiting a woman in the country. He felt nervous as he waited. Her young niece, Vera, sat with him.
2 “My aunt will join us soon,” Vera said. “Do you know her well?”
3 “Not really,” he answered.
4 Vera looked towards a large window. It was open to the garden.
5 “Something terrible happened to her three years ago. Her husband and two brothers left through that window. They went hunting, and their dog followed them. Later, they tried to cross some wet ground. They fell into deep mud. No one ever found their bodies.”
6 “That’s awful,” said Framton.
7 “My aunt believes they’re coming home. Every evening, she leaves this window open for them. Her husband was carrying a white coat over his arm that day.”
8 Vera kept looking at the garden.
9 “On evenings like this, I sometimes feel they might come back.”
10 Just then, her aunt came into the room with a smile.
11 “I hope you enjoyed talking to Vera. Is the open window all right? My husband and brothers are coming home soon. They’ll have mud all over their boots!”
12 Framton felt cold with fear. She really was waiting for them.
13 He started talking about his health. But the woman hardly listened. Her eyes kept moving towards the window.
14 “There they are! They’re home at last!”
15 Vera’s eyes opened wide. She looked terribly afraid.
16 Framton turned to see what was outside.
17 Three men were walking across the garden. They carried guns. One had a white coat over his arm. A dog walked behind them.
18 Framton could hardly breathe. Dead men were walking towards him.
19 He picked up his hat and rushed from the room. Soon he was running down the road.
20 The men came in through the window, with mud on their boots.
21 “Who was that man?” her husband asked.
22 “What a strange visitor,” his wife replied. “He talked about feeling nervous. Then he left without a word.”
23 “The dog frightened him,” Vera said in a calm voice. “He told me he was very afraid of dogs. Once, in India, some dogs chased him into a deep hole. He had to stay there all night. The dogs stood above him and barked.”
24 Vera had a special skill. She could make up stories in seconds and make people believe them.
