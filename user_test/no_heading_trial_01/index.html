# It Was Supposed to Be an AI Call—Then a Human Appeared Backstage

Imagine asking AI to book a haircut. Or asking it to call a store and check whether something is in stock. It could even ask a supplier for a price estimate. In this convenient future, the AI on your screen handles everything for you.

Meta’s AI agent, Muse, really does have phone features like these. So it sounds like a simple story: you make a request, and AI takes care of the call. But the picture changed once people looked behind the stage.

### The call that was not made by AI

Some calls through Muse were not made by AI at all. Trained human contract workers made them. Muse received the request—that part was AI—but then passed the job to a person. The worker made the call and finished the conversation. It was like an AI-led play with a hidden supporting actor. The system was called “human concierges.”

The hidden role made the simple picture less simple.

### Why the hidden role mattered

Meta employees worried that sensitive information needed for the calls might be shared by mistake with contract workers at a call center. The issue was not that humans handled calls. It was that testing began without clearly telling users about it. A service that seemed simple now looked like a mystery story.

No large information leak was confirmed. But users could not know who was really doing the work they had asked AI to do—and their personal information might reach that person. That uncertainty turned convenience into a mystery.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without a proper explanation was a mistake. Meta then temporarily put the human-call feature back on hold. The company did not stop Muse itself.

In the future, Meta will make the feature public only when it is ready and can clearly explain who will make the call.

Even in an age when AI does our work, a human may still need to help. But that person should not appear secretly. If they step onto the stage, they should give their name first. This case made that simple rule unusually clear.

# It Was Supposed to Be an AI Phone Call, but a Human Appeared Behind the Scenes

Ask AI to book a haircut. Have it call a store, ask about products in stock, or get an estimate from a company. The AI on your screen does all that work for you. It is a pretty convenient future.

Meta’s AI agent, Muse, actually has phone features like these.

But an unexpected turn took place behind the scenes. In some calls made through Muse, it was not AI making the calls, but trained human contract workers.

Muse receives the request. Up to that point, it is AI. But after that, it hands the work over to human staff. The staff makes the call and finishes the conversation. It was like a supporting actor, hard to spot in the script, had appeared in a play starring AI.

This system was called “human concierge.” The fact that humans handled the calls was not, by itself, an immediate problem. The bigger problem was that testing began without properly telling people about it.

Meta employees raised concerns that users’ sensitive information needed for the calls might be shared by mistake with contract staff at a call center.

This is important. It was not confirmed that a large-scale information leak had happened. But users did not know who was actually handling the work they had asked AI to do. Also, their personal information might be passed on to that person. When that happens, a convenient service suddenly starts to look like a mystery story.

A Meta executive admitted that it was a mistake to start the test without a proper explanation. The company then temporarily rolled back the feature in which humans handled the calls. It did not shut down Muse itself.

Going forward, the plan is to make it available to the public only when everything is ready and the company can clearly explain who will make the calls.

Even in an age when AI does the work, humans may step in to help. But when that happens, they should not appear secretly. If they are going to step onto the stage, they should say their names first. This incident showed that simple rule in a very striking way.
