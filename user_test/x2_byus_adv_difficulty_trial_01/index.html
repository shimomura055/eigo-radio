A Korean reviewer captured a familiar frustration: fifty-four episodes, and the character still hasn’t confessed his love? Fine. I’ll do it myself!

If you’ve ever stared at a manga page thinking, “Just tell her already,” you understand.

Usually, all you can do is turn the page—or wait for another chapter.

That’s the invitation behind byUs, an AI story-chat service launched on LINE Manga in Japan in October 2026. You become the main character and talk with characters from participating manga. Your conversation helps create a new story.

You can also invent a different situation or relationship and see where it goes.

At last, you get a turn to speak.

But there’s a catch: what if getting the answer you wanted makes the character less interesting?

2. A Good Memory—and a Disappearing Café

Here’s where the experience can become surprisingly convincing.

A Korean reviewer reported that the AI remembered earlier conversations and used them as the story continued.

Think about telling someone something, then hearing them bring it up later. “You remembered?” That small moment can make a conversation feel personal.

For a manga fan, the effect could be powerful: a familiar face, a familiar personality—and now, a shared history.

Then the scenery breaks.

The same review described a conversation taking place in a café, only for the background to become an office.

One moment, you’re practically sitting across the table from your favorite character. The next, you’re wondering where your coffee went.

Remembering your conversation helps. Remembering which room you’re in would help, too.

3. The Answer You Didn’t Want

Still, a disappearing café may be easier to forgive than a character who feels wrong.

One Japanese blogger described becoming irritated by the AI’s smooth talk.

That reaction gets to the heart of the experience. You might want a shy character to confess—but not suddenly sound like someone who flirts with everybody.

LINE Manga says the works used in byUs have permission through formal contracts with their original authors. But permission to use a character leaves another question:

“Would he actually say that?”

Perhaps the most satisfying answer isn’t always an immediate “I love you.”

Perhaps it’s an awkward pause. A clumsy change of subject. Something that makes you groan, “Oh, come on”—and then smile because, yes, that sounds like him.

After fifty-four episodes, you want the confession.

But you still want it to come from the character you fell for.
