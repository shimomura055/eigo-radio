英文本文（Part 1〜3）

Part 1

Imagine this. You are reading a love story on your phone. Your favorite character is about to say something important.

Then the episode ends.

“No! Just say you like her!”

Next week feels very far away.

But what if you could enter the story and speak first?

That is the idea behind byUs, an AI story chat service. It came to LINE Manga in Japan in October 2026.

You become the main character. You type a message, and an AI version of a manga character answers. Together, you make a new story.

So, back to our love story. This time, you type:

“I like you.”

Now it is his turn.

But what do you really want? A sweet answer—or an answer that feels like him?

Part 2

The exciting part is not just getting an answer. It is feeling that the character knows you.

A Korean writer who tried byUs said the AI remembered earlier conversations. It used those details later in the story. That made the chat feel more real.

Think about talking to someone who remembers your favorite drink. A small detail can make you smile.

But the same review also described a funny problem.

The characters were talking in a café. Then the story described the place as an office.

Wait. An office?

Where did the coffee go? Are we on a date, or do we have a meeting?

One strange detail can break the feeling.

And a strange place is not the only possible problem.

What if the character’s words feel wrong, too?

Part 3

Let’s return to our imagined love story.

Your favorite character is quiet. He finds it hard to talk about his feelings. That is part of why you like him.

Now imagine he suddenly sends you ten sweet messages.

“You are my whole world!”

At first, you smile.

Then you think: “Would he really say that?”

byUs uses works with the original authors’ permission, according to LINE Manga. But permission to use a character does not promise that every AI reply will feel right.

That is the tricky part.

You want to change the story. But you may not want to change the person you love inside it.

So your phone lights up. His answer is here.

Do you want him to say exactly what you hoped?

Or would you rather hear something less perfect—and think, “Yes. That sounds like him”?

英文本文（Part 1〜3）

Part 1

You have three small toy bears. You want to carry them in one bag.

Easy? Not quite.

One bag is too small. Another costs too much. You have twenty shopping pages open. The bears are still on your desk.

So you ask ChatGPT:

“Can you help me find a bag for these three toys?”

Imagine it asks about their size and your budget. Soon, you have three bags to compare instead of twenty.

You ask, “Should I buy the expensive one?”

It answers, “You may not need it. The cheaper one could be enough.”

Wonderful. A shopping friend who helps you spend *less*.

You look at the bears.

“Good news. We may have money left for snacks.”

Part 2

But now imagine a shopping ad appears near the answer.

Wait.

Did the AI choose that bag because it fits your bears? Or because someone wants to sell it?

The bag has not changed. But your feeling has.

An ad on a street poster is easy to understand. It wants your money. A chatbot feels different. It listens. It remembers what you said. It sounds helpful.

That can make a sales message feel like advice from a friend.

The company behind ChatGPT says its ads have a “Sponsored” label and stay separate from its answers. “Sponsored” means it is a paid ad.

Also, a brand name inside an answer does not prove it is an ad.

Still, you want one thing to be clear:

“Are you helping me choose, or asking me to buy?”

Part 3

And ads are not the only thing to check.

One writer reported losing $150 at a fake online store found through ChatGPT. That report was about a search result, not a paid ad.

So even an answer without an ad needs a second look.

Back to your bears.

Before buying, measure them. Check the bag’s size on the store’s own page. Look at reviews elsewhere. Make sure the shop is real.

One Japanese shopper described using AI to find choices, then comparing prices and reviews on shopping sites before buying.

That sounds like a useful job for AI: make the list shorter, not make the final decision.

You close the extra pages. You check the measurements.

The bears fit.

AI helped with the shopping. But you kept control of the money.

And yes—there is still money for snacks.

英文本文（Part 1〜3）

Part 1

Imagine this. You spend Sunday morning cleaning. You put the books away. You wash the cups. You can finally see the table.

You sit down with some tea.

Then your daughter comes home. Her bag lands on the floor. Her sweater lands on a chair.

You look at the chair.

Really? Already?

In photos online, every room looks perfect. No bags. No clothes. Do people even live there?

Your room looks different. And now you feel bad.

But wait. Before you move that sweater, here is a question:

Is it a problem? Or could it be a small sign that something is going right?

Yes, today we are talking about one sweater.

Part 2

Researchers talked to one person from each of 22 homes in New Zealand. People took photos of tidy and messy places at home. Then they explained the photos.

This was a small study. It cannot tell us what works for every family.

But in an article about the study, a researcher shared one father’s story.

The father left his daughter’s sweater on a chair.

Why?

Before, she had left her clothes on the floor. Now, she was putting them on the chair.

For him, that was progress.

An empty chair might look better in a photo. But this father saw something the photo could not explain.

His daughter was doing a little better.

He did not see only a messy chair. He saw a small win.

Suddenly, that sweater looks a little different, doesn’t it?

Part 3

So, should we all throw our clothes on chairs?

No. Nice try.

A chair full of clothes is a problem if someone needs to sit there. A table full of things is a problem if there is no space for dinner.

And old food still needs to go in the bin.

The point is not to love every mess. It is to ask what matters in your home.

Can we eat here? Can we walk safely? Is anyone upset?

Talk about that. Then choose what to tidy together. The work should not fall on just one person.

And next time you see a sweater on a chair, look again.

Maybe it needs moving.

Or maybe, today, the floor is clear.

That can be a small win, too.
