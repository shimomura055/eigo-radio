現行のResearchによる正式本文

## Part 1 You have three small toy bears. You want to carry them in one bag. Easy? Not quite. One bag is too small. Another costs too much. You have twenty shopping pages open. The bears are still on your desk. So you ask ChatGPT: “Can you help me find a bag for these three toys?” Imagine it asks about their size and your budget. Soon, you have three bags to compare instead of twenty. You ask, “Should I buy the expensive one?” It answers, “You may not need it. The cheaper one could be enough.” Wonderful. A shopping friend who helps you spend *less*. You look at the bears. “Good news. We may have money left for snacks.” ## Part 2 But now imagine a shopping ad appears near the answer. Wait. Did the AI choose that bag because it fits your bears? Or because someone wants to sell it? The bag has not changed. But your feeling has. An ad on a street poster is easy to understand. It wants your money. A chatbot feels different. It listens. It remembers what you said. It sounds helpful. That can make a sales message feel like advice from a friend. The company behind ChatGPT says its ads have a “Sponsored” label and stay separate from its answers. “Sponsored” means it is a paid ad. Also, a brand name inside an answer does not prove it is an ad. Still, you want one thing to be clear: “Are you helping me choose, or asking me to buy?” ## Part 3 And ads are not the only thing to check. One writer reported losing $150 at a fake online store found through ChatGPT. That report was about a search result, not a paid ad. So even an answer without an ad needs a second look. Back to your bears. Before buying, measure them. Check the bag’s size on the store’s own page. Look at reviews elsewhere. Make sure the shop is real. One Japanese shopper described using AI to find choices, then comparing prices and reviews on shopping sites before buying. That sounds like a useful job for AI: make the list shorter, not make the final decision. You close the extra pages. You check the measurements. The bears fit. AI helped with the shopping. But you kept control of the money. And yes—there is still money for snacks.

## Part 1 Imagine this. It is winter. Your room is dry. You wake up with a dry throat. You ask ChatGPT, “How do I choose a humidifier?” It gives you some helpful ideas. Check the room size. Think about cleaning. Decide how much to spend. Great! Shopping feels easier. Then you see an ad for a humidifier below the answer. Wait a minute. “Are you helping me? Or are you selling me something?” One user in Japan wrote about this feeling. After seeing a humidifier ad, they felt a little hurt. It was almost like a friend had let them down. An ad on a shopping website feels normal. But an ad next to friendly advice? That can feel different. ## Part 2 But here is an important difference. An ad beside an answer is not the same as an ad inside the advice. A Japanese article from 2026 quoted OpenAI’s help page. It said ChatGPT ads appeared separately from answers, with a “Sponsored” label. That means “paid ad.” It also said ads did not change the answers. Still, a label may not stop that uncomfortable feeling. Imagine asking a friend, “Which rice cooker should I buy?” Your friend gives you advice. Then someone puts a shop flyer on the table. Maybe your friend’s advice is still good. But now you want to check. The real question becomes: “Why is this a good choice for me?” ## Part 3 So, should we stop asking AI for shopping help? Not necessarily. Remember the person with the humidifier ad? They used ChatGPT to learn what to look for. Then they chose a humidifier at a nearby store. That is a useful way to use AI. Instead of asking only, “What should I buy?” try: “What should I check?” For a humidifier, ask: “Is it easy to clean? Is it right for my room? Does it need new filters?” Then check the price, size, and reviews yourself. In one US study about AI shopping, 78% of participants visited a retailer or online marketplace during their shopping journey. That does not describe everyone, but checking elsewhere was common. AI can help you write your shopping list. It does not need to hold your wallet. And the best answer may not be a product name. It may be a better question.
