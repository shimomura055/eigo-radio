On night eight, my thumb slipped on the lantern's metal catch.
"Who is in here?"
the old man called out. I stood still for an hour while he sat listening. A small cry of fear came from his bed. At last, I let a thin line of lantern light fall across his eye. This time, his eye was wide open. Then I heard a low, fast beat, like a watch inside cotton. His heart was beating faster and louder. What if a neighbour heard it? I shouted and jumped forward, and he screamed once. I dragged him onto the floor and pulled the heavy bed over him. His heart kept beating for a little longer. At last, there was no sound. I hid his body under three wooden floorboards. I put them back carefully, leaving no mark.

At four in the morning, three police officers knocked because a neighbour had heard a scream. I smiled and welcomed them inside. I explained that my scream came from a bad dream. The old man, I said, was away in the country. After showing them the house, I brought chairs into his bedroom. I sat right above the body, calm and sure of myself. The officers sat down and chatted. My ears began ringing, so I spoke more loudly. Now the sound was clear: a low, fast beat, like a watch inside cotton.
I got up, walked around, and dragged my chair across the floor. Yet the beating only grew louder. The officers smiled and talked as if they heard nothing. But they knew, and their friendly smiles were laughing at me! How could they sit there? I could not bear another second. "Don't pretend anymore!" I screamed. "I killed him! Pull up the floorboards here, under my feet! It is his horrible heart beating!"

At four in the morning came a knock. Three police officers stood outside; a neighbour had heard a shriek. He smiled and welcomed them. The cry had been his own, he explained—a bad dream. The old man was away in the country. He showed them the house, then brought chairs into the bedroom. Calm and confident, he sat directly above the hidden body. The officers settled down and chatted. A ringing troubled his ears. He spoke louder, but now the sound was clear: low, quick, like a watch wrapped in cotton.
He stood and paced. He scraped his chair across the boards. Still the beating grew louder. The officers kept smiling and talking. They seemed to hear nothing. But they knew. They must know. Their pleasant smiles were mocking him. How could they sit there? How could he bear another second? "Stop pretending!" he screamed. "I did it! Tear up the planks! Here, beneath my feet! It is the beating of his hideous heart!"
