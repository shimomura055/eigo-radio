Full Story Part 1 (Aoede / Zephyr / Algieba)
82.8秒〜 (79.3秒)
Framton sat on his chair's edge, turning his letter of introduction. His doctor suggested country air, but visiting strangers brought little peace.
"My aunt will join us soon,"
said fifteen-year-old Vera.
"How well do you know her?"
"I know very little,"
Framton answered. Vera looked at the wide-open window leading onto the lawn.
"Something terrible happened to her exactly three years ago today."
The letter lay still in Framton's hands.
"Her husband and two younger brothers left through that window. They went shooting, taking their small brown spaniel with them. They tried crossing wet ground, but it broke under them. All of them disappeared, and nobody ever found their bodies."
"How awful,"
said Framton quietly.
"She still believes they'll return, so she waits until dark. That's why she leaves the window open,"
Vera said more quietly.
"Her husband carried his white coat over his arm. Her youngest brother sang, 'Bertie, why do you bound?' He always sang that song to annoy her."

Full Story Part 2 (Aoede / Zephyr / Kore)
175.2秒〜 (53.8秒)
Her eyes moved across the lawn, but nobody was there.
"On evenings like this, I sometimes think they might appear."
Her aunt came in quickly before Framton could answer her.
"Has Vera been looking after you? I hope the open window doesn't bother you. My husband and brothers are coming back from shooting soon. Their boots will be muddy, as usual!"
Framton went cold because she really did expect them back. He talked about his health problems and avoided the window. But his hostess hardly listened, often looking past him towards the lawn. Then a happy smile suddenly appeared on her face.
"Look, they're finally home!"
Vera's eyes opened wide as she looked outside in fear. Framton looked behind him.

241.9秒〜 (64.8秒)
Three men crossed the lawn with guns as daylight faded. One carried a white coat, and a brown spaniel followed. A young man's voice reached them across the silent garden:
"Bertie, why do you bound?"
Framton struggled for breath as the dead approached the house. He took his hat and stick and hurried outside. Moments later, he was racing down the drive. The men came through the window, hungry and covered in mud.
"Who just left?"
her husband asked.
"Such a strange man,"
she replied.
"He described his nervous problems, then ran off without warning."
"The dog frightened him,"
Vera said, sounding perfectly calm.
"He told me dogs terrified him. A group chased him into an empty grave in India. He stayed there all night while they growled above him."
Vera needed only seconds to invent a story people believed.

Full Story Part 1 (Aoede / Zephyr / Algieba)
79.0秒〜 (73.5秒)
Framton sat on the edge of his chair, turning his letter of introduction between his fingers. His doctor had recommended peace and country air, but visiting strangers was hardly peaceful.
"My aunt will be down soon,"
said Vera, her fifteen-year-old niece.
"Do you know much about her?"
"Almost nothing,"
he admitted. Vera glanced towards the French window, which stood wide open to the lawn.
"Her great tragedy happened three years ago today."
Framton stopped turning the letter.
"Her husband and her two young brothers went out shooting through that window. Their little brown spaniel went with them. They tried to cross a bog, but the ground gave way beneath them. They all disappeared. Their bodies were never found."
"How terrible,"
Framton murmured.
"My aunt still thinks they'll come back. She keeps the window open until dark, waiting for them."
Vera's voice grew quieter.
"Her husband had his white coat over his arm. Her youngest brother was singing, 'Bertie, why do you bound?' He always sang it to tease her."

Full Story Part 2 (Aoede / Zephyr / Kore)
162.2秒〜 (51.5秒)
She looked out across the empty lawn.
"Sometimes, on evenings like this, I almost expect to see them."
Before Framton could reply, her aunt hurried into the room.
"I hope Vera has kept you company! You don't mind the open window? My husband and brothers will be back soon. They've been shooting. Their boots will be covered in mud, of course!"
Framton felt a chill. So she really was waiting. He began describing his illness, trying to keep his eyes away from the window. His hostess listened with only half her attention. Again and again, she looked past him towards the lawn. Then her face brightened.
"Here they are at last!"
Vera stared outside, her eyes wide with horror. Framton turned.

Full Story Part 3 (Aoede / Schedar / Kore / Zephyr)
223.7秒〜 (66.0秒)
Three figures were crossing the lawn in the fading light, carrying guns. A white coat hung over one man's arm. A brown spaniel followed close behind. Across the quiet garden came a young voice:
"Bertie, why do you bound?"
Framton could scarcely breathe. The dead were coming towards the house. He grabbed his hat and stick and rushed out. Within seconds, he was running down the drive. The men stepped through the window, muddy and hungry.
"Who was that?"
asked the husband.
"A most peculiar visitor,"
his wife said.
"He talked about his nerves, then suddenly ran away."
"It was the spaniel,"
Vera said calmly.
"He told me he was terrified of dogs. In India, a pack chased him into an empty grave. He spent the whole night there, with them snarling above him."
Inventing convincing stories at a moment's notice was Vera's speciality.
