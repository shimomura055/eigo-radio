# A 20% Strait Charge Disappears—but Oil Markets Stay on Alert

For about one day, ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz faced the possibility of a major new cost.

On July 13, 2026, US President Donald Trump said the United States would seek repayment equal to 20% of all cargo moving through the strait. He presented the plan as a way to recover the cost of providing security there. He also said work on the system would begin immediately.

However, it was still only a proposal. The announcement did not explain who would collect the money, who would pay it, or how the 20% would be calculated. It also gave no details about exemptions, payment currency, enforcement, or legal authority.

Then the plan changed.

About 25 hours after the first post, Trump said the charge would be replaced by trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States. He said the decision followed productive talks with Middle Eastern leaders.

Later that day, Trump told reporters that nobody should charge ships for passing through the strait. He also said he did not like the idea of such a fee. The 20% plan was therefore dropped before a collection system was created.

Oil markets reacted, but not in a simple straight line.

On July 13, Brent crude futures rose nearly 10% and settled at $83.30 a barrel. Reuters linked the wider rise to military tension, a planned US naval blockade against Iran, and fears about energy shipments through the strait—not to the fee proposal alone.

After the reversal on July 14, Brent briefly reduced its gains. It soon returned close to its earlier high level. Brent finally settled at $84.73, up 1.7% for the day. The fee had disappeared, but concerns about attacks, the blockade, and tanker safety remained.

### Point One: A possible bill worth tens of millions

If the 20% rate had applied to cargo value, a large tanker carrying about two million barrels at roughly $85 each would hold around $170 million in oil. The possible charge could then reach about $34 million. Another estimate was about $32 million. These were examples, not real bills.

### Point Two: International rules point in another direction

In a separate decision, the IMO Council said passage through the Strait of Hormuz should remain free from tolls and charges under international law and IMO conventions. It also called for equal and unblocked passage for all ships. There is no confirmed evidence that this decision caused Trump’s reversal.

## In one line…

The 20% charge lasted only about a day, but the market’s message was clear: removing one risk does not calm prices when several other dangers remain.
