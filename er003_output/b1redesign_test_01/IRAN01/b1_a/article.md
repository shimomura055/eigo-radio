# Hormuz: Trump Talks Territory as Iran and Oman Map a Route Back

Two very different stories are competing over the Strait of Hormuz.

One is loud and political.

On August 12, President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social that the United States had “total control” of the strait and might “keep it.” Two days later, at a rally in New York, he joked that he might soon declare the waterway US territory.

These statements did not make the strait American territory. Iran also rejected them. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the strait could not be seized “by tweet.” He called Trump’s claims “fanciful delusions.”

At the same time, a more practical development was taking place on the water.

On August 15, Iranian state media reported that Iran and Oman had agreed on a **shipping route map** for the strait. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei announced the agreement. It followed technical talks under a memorandum signed on June 18.

Under the reported plan, ships entering the strait would use a route closer to Iran. Ships leaving it would travel closer to Oman.

This does not mean that the strait has fully reopened. The announcement did not settle transit fees or security arrangements. Those issues are expected to be discussed separately. Iran’s foreign minister has also said that a full reopening would require sanctions relief and war compensation from the United States.

For now, there is still a huge gap between agreeing on a map and returning to normal traffic. Before the war, about 130 ships crossed the strait each day. By August, only a few were passing through daily.

### Point One: Different meanings of “control”

Trump uses “control” as a political and military claim. Iran answers with the language of sovereignty. Iran and Oman are dealing with a different issue: the routes that ships should follow. All three are talking about power in the strait, but they do not mean the same kind of power.

### Point Two: A small waterway with a global impact

Before the war, about one-fifth of the world’s crude oil supply passed through Hormuz. Brent crude has risen by about 20% since the conflict began, reaching the high $80s per barrel in early August. This is why even a limited technical agreement matters to global energy markets.

## In one line…

**Trump is talking about control, but Iran and Oman are working on routes—and the strait is still far from fully reopening.**
