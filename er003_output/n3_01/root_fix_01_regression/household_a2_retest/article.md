# Your Crisper Drawer Has a Secret Job

Open the refrigerator, and the crisper drawer may look like a simple box.

But that small drawer is doing more than holding vegetables.

Many refrigerators have two settings: **low humidity** and **high humidity**. Some models use separate drawers. Others have a slider that changes the airflow.

Iowa State University Extension explains that a low-humidity drawer lets more air move through. A high-humidity drawer is closer to being closed, so it keeps more moisture inside.

Why does this matter?

Fresh food faces two different problems.

The first problem is a gas called **ethylene**. Some fruits release a lot of this gas as they become ripe. Apples and pears are examples.

If ethylene stays near other fruits and vegetables, those foods may become ripe too quickly. Then they can spoil sooner. A low-humidity drawer lets the gas escape more easily.

The second problem is water loss.

Leafy greens, such as kale, can lose water quickly. Broccoli can also dry out. As a result, these foods may become soft or wilted.

A high-humidity drawer keeps more moisture around them. This helps them stay firm and fresh for longer.

So the real rule is not simply “fruit here, vegetables there.”

Instead, think about what the food does. If it releases a lot of ethylene, choose low humidity. If it loses water quickly, choose high humidity.

### Point One: The food label can fool you

“Fruit means low humidity” sounds easy, but it is not always correct. Strawberries and citrus fruits, including oranges, prefer high humidity. UC Davis gives 90 to 95 percent as the best humidity range for both strawberries and oranges. The food’s behavior matters more than its name.

### Point Two: Some foods should skip the drawer

Not every fruit or vegetable belongs in the refrigerator. Tomatoes and bananas are better on the counter at room temperature. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration. They prefer a cool, dry place instead.

## In one line…

Your crisper drawer is a small climate-control tool: let ethylene out, keep moisture in, and do not sort food by “fruit” and “vegetable” alone.
