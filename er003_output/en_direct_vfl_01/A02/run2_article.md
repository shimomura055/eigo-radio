# 🌙 “A Quieter Feed After Midnight”: Britain’s Planned Night Settings for Teens

As of August 12, 2026, Britain’s new nighttime social-media settings are still a plan—not a rule already in force. Parliamentary approval is required, and government materials say the changes are expected to begin around spring 2027.

The target is specific: users aged 16 and 17 on covered social-media services.

From midnight to 6 a.m., a default nighttime restriction would switch on, including muted alerts and push notifications.

But this is not an unbreakable curfew.

The protective settings would activate automatically, while teens could change them.

In other words, the phone does not suddenly become a brick at midnight.

The proposal is closer to putting a speed bump in front of the endless scroll: fewer digital taps on the shoulder during the night. The final details of which services are covered will come later.

## Two Points Behind the Settings

### Nighttime quiet is only one layer

Another part of the plan operates all day.

Autoplay and personalised feeds would be off by default for 16- and 17-year-olds—not merely between midnight and 6 a.m. Those settings could also be changed by the user.

That distinction matters.

One measure softens the night. The other reduces the app’s ability to keep choosing and playing the next thing, regardless of the hour.

### The pilot was a clue—not a verdict

A 2026 qualitative pilot involved 309 households in total across four groups. It was not 309 participants testing a night curfew.

The 81 households in the night-curfew group were assigned an intervention blocking selected social apps from 9 p.m. to 7 a.m.—a different schedule and approach from the planned policy. Reported adherence was 53 complete, 23 with minor changes and five non-compliant, based on participant reports rather than remote monitoring.

Among the three interventions, participants described the night curfew as the easiest to fit into everyday routines. They also reported the most consistent sleep-related improvements in that group, including earlier bedtimes, quicker sleep onset and feeling more alert the next day. Many households said they had kept some modified form of curfew afterward.

Still, the research was small, self-selected and qualitative. It did not prove cause and effect.

Many participants also reported little change in daytime social-media use: scrolling sometimes moved earlier, surged before the cutoff, resumed after 7 a.m., or gave way to television and games.

A quieter night, then, did not automatically mean less screen time overall.

## In One Sentence

**Britain is not planning to confiscate the night; it is planning to make social media quieter by default—while leaving older teens able to reach for the controls.**
