# “Hello, this is AI”—but a human was behind the voice

Letting AI handle a phone call sounds like a useful service from the future. A person only needs to explain what they want. Then the AI makes the call. At Meta, a service like this was being built.

The main character was Muse, Meta’s AI agent for personal use. Meta was trying to give Muse a feature that would make phone calls for users.

But a look backstage showed something unexpected.

During an internal test, human contract workers—not AI—handled some parts of the calls. Meta called these workers “human concierges.”

The sign out front said “AI phone service,” but people handled some calls behind it. The show seemed to have one performer, while human stand-ins waited behind the curtain.

That was the most surprising part. You might think the AI phone service was doing everything by itself. Instead, it was like a piano that seemed to play on its own, while another player was hidden inside.

### The people behind the “AI” voice

Having people help is not necessarily wrong. Humans can fill in the parts that AI still finds difficult, and that is a natural way to use the technology. But phone calls may contain private information. That makes it important to know who is really listening and speaking.

### Privacy worries put the feature on hold

Meta employees raised concerns that call content could leak outside. If users thought they had handed a conversation to AI, then learned that contract workers had actually heard it and responded, they might be surprised. According to internal posts confirmed by Reuters, a Meta executive said the feature had been put on hold.

## In one line

An AI phone service can be trusted only when it explains who is speaking onstage—and who is behind the curtain.
