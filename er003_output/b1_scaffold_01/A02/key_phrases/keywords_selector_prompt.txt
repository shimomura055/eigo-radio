# Britain Plans to Turn Down the Social Media Noise at Night

At midnight, the screen would not be seized, and social media apps would not necessarily disappear. Instead, the UK government wants the digital world to become quieter by default.

Under the planned rules, users aged 16 and 17 would receive additional protection settings on covered social media services. From midnight until 6 a.m., a default night restriction would apply, including the muting of alerts and push notifications.

The word **“default”** matters. This would not be an unchangeable curfew. The settings would switch on automatically, but young users could change them.

And the plan does not stop at bedtime.

Autoplay and personalised feeds would also be turned off by default for 16- and 17-year-olds. Unlike the night setting, these protections would apply throughout the day. Users would still be able to switch them back on.

In other words, there are two clocks at work: overnight protections designed to reduce late-night interruptions, and all-day settings aimed at features that keep content flowing.

The government intends to cover the same range of platforms as its planned social media restrictions for children under 16. However, the final definitions and exceptions for services have not yet been confirmed.

The plan has not crossed the finish line. As of 12 August 2026, it still requires parliamentary approval. If approved, the government expects the changes to begin around spring 2027.

### The pilot is a clue, not a crystal ball

A separate 2026 pilot provides context, not a forecast. The whole study involved 309 households with young people aged 13 to 17, while 81 households were in the night-curfew group. Participants described that curfew as the easiest intervention to manage and reported the most consistent sleep-related improvements. The findings were self-reported, not proof of cause and effect.

### A quieter night may not mean less screen time

Many participants in the night-curfew group reported little change in daytime social media use. Some concentrated their scrolling before the restriction, returned early in the morning, or moved to television and games. The main shift may therefore be **when** screens are used, rather than how much they are used.

## In one line…

Britain is not proposing a midnight padlock, but a default pause: fewer digital nudges at night, less automatic content throughout the day, and the final choice still left with the user.
