# When the Pings Stop: Britain Plans Night Settings for 16- and 17-Year-Olds

Late at night, one small sound can pull a person back to their phone.

“Ping.”

A new message. A new video. One more look.

The UK government wants to make the night quieter for social media users aged 16 and 17.

Under the plan, covered social media services would use a night limit from midnight to 6 a.m. This would be the default setting. In other words, it would turn on automatically.

During these six hours, alerts and push notifications would be muted.

However, this is not a complete ban on social media. It does not mean that every app will become impossible to use. Young users will also be able to change the setting.

The plan has another part that works all day, not only at night.

Autoplay would be off by default. This feature normally starts the next video by itself. Personalised feeds would also be off. These feeds choose posts and videos for each user.

Users would be able to turn these features back on.

The exact social media services covered by the new rules are not yet final. The government plans to give more details later.

The most important word is “plan.”

As of August 12, 2026, these changes are not yet in force. They still need approval from Parliament. If approved, the government expects the changes to begin around spring 2027.

### A separate pilot gives an early clue

In a separate 2026 study, 309 households joined four groups. The night-curfew group had 81 households and was assigned a stronger 9 p.m.-to-7 a.m. access block. Among the three limits tested, participants said this one was easiest to fit into family life. Families also reported earlier bedtimes and feeling more awake the next day.

### A quieter night may not mean less screen time

Many people in the pilot said their daytime social media use changed little. Some used it more before the curfew or after 7 a.m. Others moved to TV or games. The study was small and mainly used self-reports, so it gives a clue, not proof.

## In one line…

Britain is planning a quieter default, not a hard night-time ban, for 16- and 17-year-olds. If Parliament agrees, the change may begin in spring 2027.
