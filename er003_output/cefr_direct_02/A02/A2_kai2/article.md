# UK Plans Quieter Social Media Nights for 16- and 17-Year-Olds

The phone may still be in your hand. But after midnight, it may become much quieter.

The UK government plans new social media settings for people aged 16 and 17. From midnight to 6 a.m., night-time limits will be on automatically. Alerts and push messages will be silent during these six hours.

This is not a complete social media ban. Young users will be able to change the settings themselves.

There will also be protection during the day. Autoplay will be off, so videos will not start by themselves. Personalised feeds will also be off. This means apps will not automatically choose a special feed for each user. Again, users can change these settings.

The final list of social media services is not ready yet. The government will give more details later.

As of 12 August 2026, these rules are still only a plan. Parliament must approve them first. If it does, the government expects the changes to start around spring 2027.

### A separate pilot gave an early clue

A different UK pilot studied 309 households with young people aged 13 to 17. It had four groups. The night group included 81 households and was given a 9 p.m. to 7 a.m. app block. Participants reported that this was easier to manage than the other tested limits. They also reported better sleep.

### Less night use may not mean less screen time

The pilot did not prove that a night block improves sleep for everyone. The study was small, and it mainly used reports from families. Some young people used social media more before the block, returned early in the morning, or moved to television and games instead.

## In one line…

The UK wants social media to become quieter at night for older teenagers—but the plan is a changeable safety setting, not a locked door.
