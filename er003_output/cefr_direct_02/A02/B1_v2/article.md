# When the Night Goes Quiet: UK Plans New Social Media Settings for Teenagers

At midnight, a teenager’s phone would not suddenly become useless. But social media could become much quieter.

The UK government plans to introduce new default settings for 16- and 17-year-olds. On social media services covered by the planned rules, night restrictions would apply from midnight until 6 a.m.

During those six hours, alerts and push notifications would be muted by default. However, this is not a complete and unchangeable ban. The settings would turn on automatically, but young users could change them.

The plan also includes another type of protection. Autoplay and personalised feeds would be switched off by default for the same age group. These settings would apply throughout the day, not only during the night.

In simple terms, the government wants the first setting to be the quieter choice. A teenager could still change it, but the app would no longer begin by automatically pulling them toward the next video, post or late-night alert.

However, the rules are still only a plan. As of August 12, 2026, they have not taken effect. Parliamentary approval is needed, and the government expects the changes to begin around spring 2027. The final details about which services will be covered will come later.

### What a separate pilot suggests

A completed study involved 309 households across four groups. Of these, 81 were assigned to a stronger 9 p.m.-to-7 a.m. social media curfew. Participants reported that it was the easiest of the three interventions to manage and showed the most consistent sleep improvements. However, the study was small, self-selected and mainly based on personal reports.

### The clock may move the habit, not remove it

Many pilot participants said their daytime social media use changed little. Some used apps heavily before the curfew, returned early in the morning, or moved to television and games. This suggests that a night restriction may change when screen use happens without necessarily reducing total screen time.

## In one line…

The UK is not planning to lock social media away at night. It is planning to make quiet, less attention-grabbing settings the starting point for older teenagers.
