# After Midnight, the Phone May Go Quiet: The UK’s New Teen Social Media Plan

Midnight. A phone is still bright. Then its social media alerts go silent.

That is the kind of change the UK government is planning for 16- and 17-year-olds.

On social media services covered by the proposed rules, a night limit would turn on automatically from midnight to 6 a.m. Alerts and push notifications would be muted during these six hours.

But this is **not a hard, unchangeable ban**. Young users would be able to change the settings. The plan also does not say that every social media app must become impossible to open at night.

The government is planning two other settings for the same age group. Autoplay—the feature that starts the next video by itself—would be off by default. Personalised feeds, where an app chooses posts for each user, would also be off.

These two settings would apply all day, not only at night. Users could switch them back on.

The exact list of social media services and any exceptions has not yet been decided.

As of August 12, 2026, these changes are still only a plan. The UK Parliament must approve the new rules. If that happens, the government expects the changes to start around spring 2027.

### A useful clue: Night rules may fit family life

A separate one-month pilot looked at 309 UK households with young people aged 13 to 17. One group, with 81 households, was asked to try a social media access block from 9 p.m. to 7 a.m. Among three limits tested, participants said this was the easiest to fit into daily life. This group also reported the clearest sleep improvements.

### An important warning: Screen time can move

The pilot cannot prove that the curfew caused these changes. Families chose to join, groups were based on their wishes, and app use was not checked directly. Many in the curfew group said daytime use stayed similar. Some used social media more before 9 p.m. or after 7 a.m.; others moved to television or games.

## In one line…

**The UK is not planning to lock older teens out of social media after midnight—it is planning quieter default settings, which users can change, if Parliament approves the rules.**
