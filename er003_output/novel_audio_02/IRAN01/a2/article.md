# Trump Talks Territory. Iran and Oman Draw a Route.

One narrow sea route is now at the center of two very different stories.

On August 14, President Donald Trump spoke at a meeting in New York. In a joking way, he said he might soon declare the Strait of Hormuz to be US territory.

This followed an August 12 social media post. In that post, Trump said the United States had “total control” of the strait and might keep it.

But these were **claims by the president**. The United States has not made the strait its territory.

Iran strongly rejected Trump’s words. An Iranian deputy foreign minister said the strait could not be taken by a tweet.

Then, on August 15, a quieter but more practical development arrived.

Iranian state media said Iran and Oman had agreed on a shipping route map for the Strait of Hormuz. The map is designed to show commercial ships which way to travel.

Under the reported plan, ships entering the strait would use a route closer to Iran. Ships leaving it would use a route closer to Oman.

The agreement does not mean the strait is fully open again.

Important questions are still unanswered. Iran and Oman have not announced full details about security rules or possible fees. Those subjects are expected to be discussed in another phase of talks.

For now, shipping remains extremely low. Before the war, about 130 ships passed through the strait each day. By August, only a few ships a day were making the journey, although different reports give different numbers.

So, the picture is clear.

Trump’s words are loud. But on the water, Iran and Oman are doing the quieter work of drawing a route that ships may actually use.

### Why one shipping map matters

Before the war, the Strait of Hormuz carried about one-fifth of the world’s oil supply. Brent crude rose from around $72 a barrel before the conflict to the high $80s in early August. A safer route could therefore matter far beyond Iran and Oman.

### A map cannot create trust by itself

The International Maritime Organization reported at least 64 ship incidents and 17 deaths in and around the strait by August 10. Iran’s foreign minister has also said full reopening requires lighter US sanctions and payment for war damage. These are Iranian demands, not agreed conditions.

## In one line…

**While Trump speaks about owning the Strait of Hormuz, Iran and Oman are working on the route map that could help ships move through it again.** The map is real progress—but it is not yet a full reopening.
