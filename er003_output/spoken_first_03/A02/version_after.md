# Britain’s Midnight Reset: A Quieter Social Media Night for Older Teens

Midnight arrives. A phone lights up.

One alert leads to another. One video rolls into the next. Bedtime quietly slips away.

The UK government is planning a new response. It would not lock every door. Instead, it would change which doors are open by default.

As of 12 August 2026, the plan has not taken effect. Subject to parliamentary approval, the changes are expected to begin around spring 2027.

The plan covers social media users aged 16 and 17. From midnight to 6 a.m., nighttime protections would switch on automatically. Alerts and push notifications would be muted.

But this would not be an unchangeable curfew. Users could alter the settings. And the government has not said that social media must become completely inaccessible overnight.

There is also a separate, all-day change. Autoplay and personalised feeds would be off by default—not just overnight.

So the proposal is not simply saying:

“Put the phone away.”

It is asking whether the phone should work quite so hard to pull young people back in.

## Two points worth watching

### ⭐ The pilot offers a clue—but not proof

In the pilot, participants described the night curfew as the easiest of the three interventions to manage, and reported the most consistent sleep improvements. But the study was small, self-selected, and based mainly on personal reports. The findings are promising signals—not proof of cause and effect.

### ⭐ A quieter night may not mean less screen time

Participants often reported little change in daytime social media use. Some used it more before the restriction or after it ended; others switched to television or games. A nighttime setting may therefore shift screen use across hours or media, rather than make it disappear.

## In one line…

**Britain is not proposing a permanent lock on social media, but a midnight pause: quieter defaults, fewer automatic pulls, and a little more space before the next scroll.**
