# England Saw the Final—Then Messi Opened the Door

England could almost see the World Cup final.

At Atlanta Stadium on 15 July, the 2026 FIFA World Cup semifinal began at 15:00 local time, with England facing Argentina in Match 102.

The first half offered tension without release: **0-0 at the break**, every attack feeling like a question neither side could yet answer.

Then, in the 55th minute, England found one.

Harry Kane’s long ball started the move, Morgan Rogers sent a low cross in from the right, and Anthony Gordon met it at the far side.

**England 1-0 Argentina.**

For half an hour, that single goal stood between England and the final. The clock moved toward 90. Argentina kept searching.

In the 85th minute, the door finally opened.

From a short-corner sequence, Lionel Messi rolled the ball sideways to Enzo Fernández, who struck right-footed from outside the penalty area.

**1-1.**

Extra time appeared to be waiting.

But the match had one more turn.

In second-half stoppage time, Alexis Mac Allister hit the post. Messi recovered the ball and delivered a right-footed cross to the far side. Lautaro Martínez—introduced in the 81st minute—rose to meet it.

**90+2 minutes. Argentina 2-1 England.**

One header changed everything. Argentina advanced to the final; England were sent to the third-place match.

## Two Defining Angles

### Argentina’s pressure was written across the numbers

The score was close, but Argentina held the statistical edge: **15 shots to England’s five**, five shots on target to two, and an xG of 1.47 against 0.46.

FIFA’s possession classification gave Argentina 56.5 percent and England 35.2 percent, with 8.2 percent in contest. Argentina also completed 549 of 610 passes, compared with England’s 293 of 353.

England came within minutes of protecting their lead. Yet the wider picture shows how persistently Argentina occupied the attacking landscape.

### Messi decided the semifinal without scoring

Messi did not put his own name on the scoresheet. He put his signature on both Argentine goals.

His pass created Fernández’s equalizer; his cross supplied Lautaro’s winner. At **39 years and 21 days**, he became the oldest outfield player to appear in a World Cup semifinal and was named FIFA’s Superior Player of the Match.

Lautaro provided the finish. Messi provided the final answers.

## In One Line…

**England touched the door to the final—but Argentina, guided twice by Messi, turned the key at the very last moment.**
