At midnight, a phone lights up.

One message. Then another alert. Then one more video.

The UK government wants to make that night-time pull quieter for 16- and 17-year-olds.

Under its plan, social media services covered by the new rules would turn on night protections by default from midnight to 6 a.m. Alerts and push notifications would be muted during these six hours.

But this is not a complete, unchangeable ban.

The settings would turn on automatically, but young users could change them. The final details about which services will be covered have also not yet been decided.

A separate UK pilot tested a stronger kind of night rule. The full pilot included 309 households in four groups. In one group, 81 households were assigned a rule that blocked selected social media apps from 9 p.m. to 7 a.m.

Among the three types of limits tested, families said the night rule was the easiest to fit into daily life. People in that group also reported earlier bedtimes, faster sleep, and feeling more awake the next day.

However, the study was small and was based mainly on what families said. It did not prove that a night rule caused better sleep.

The pilot rule and the government plan are also different. The pilot used an access block from 9 p.m. to 7 a.m. The planned policy uses default protections from midnight to 6 a.m., and users can change the settings.

As of August 12, 2026, the policy is still only a plan. It needs approval from Parliament, and the government expects the changes to begin around spring 2027.
