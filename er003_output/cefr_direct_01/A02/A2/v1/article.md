At midnight, one more video can easily become ten. A quick check of a message can turn into another hour of scrolling.

The UK government wants social media to respond differently at that moment.

Under its planned rules, users aged 16 and 17 would receive default nighttime protections from midnight to 6 a.m. During those six hours, social media alerts and push notifications would be muted. A default limit on nighttime use would also apply.

In other words, the apps would stop calling so loudly in the middle of the night.

But this is not a locked digital curfew.

The settings would switch on automatically, but young users could change them. Social media would not necessarily become completely unavailable until morning. The plan is closer to putting a quiet setting in place first—and then allowing the user to decide whether to remove it.

That difference matters.

Instead of asking teenagers to search through menus and turn protections on, the safer setting would become the starting point.

The change is also not in force yet. As of August 12, 2026, it remains a government plan that needs parliamentary approval. Government documents say the new rules are expected to begin around spring 2027.

The final details of which social media services will be covered are still to come.

For now, the direction is clear: when the clock reaches midnight, the government wants the endless call of social media to become a little easier to ignore.
