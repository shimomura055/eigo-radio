At midnight, the phone may still be beside the bed.

But under the UK government’s plan, it could become a much quieter companion.

Britain is preparing new default social media protections for 16- and 17-year-olds. From midnight to 6 a.m., covered services would apply nighttime restrictions, including muted alerts and push notifications.

The idea is simple:

“Let the night feel like night again.”

This is not a complete, unchangeable ban on social media. The protections would turn on automatically, but users would still be able to change the settings themselves.

That difference matters.

The government is not proposing to take control of teenagers’ phones for six hours. Instead, it wants to change the default—from platforms constantly inviting young people back, to a quieter setting that requires a conscious choice to change.

And for now, this remains a plan, not an active rule.

As of August 12, 2026, the changes had not yet taken effect. Parliamentary approval is still required, and the government expects the changes to begin around spring 2027.

So the screen is not going dark tonight.

But Britain is preparing to ask a much bigger question: when midnight arrives, should social media still be designed to keep calling?
