At 12:01 a.m., a phone lights up beside the bed.

A new post. A fresh alert. One more reason to keep scrolling.

Under a plan from the UK government, that familiar midnight invitation could soon become much quieter for 16- and 17-year-olds.

The proposal would apply a default night-time restriction to social media from midnight until 6 a.m. During those six hours, alerts and push notifications would be muted.

At first glance, it sounds like a digital curfew.

But there is an important distinction.

This is not a compulsory, unbreakable ban. The protective settings would switch on automatically, but young users would be allowed to change them.

In other words, the government is not planning to lock the door. It wants closing the door to become the starting position.

The idea is simple: when the screen stops calling for attention, putting the phone down may become a little easier.

However, the change is not yet in force. As of August 12, 2026, it remains a government plan and still requires parliamentary approval. The government expects the new rules to begin taking effect around spring 2027.

The final details of which social media services will be covered are also due to be set out later.

So Britain’s proposed approach is less like a hard command to “log off now” and more like turning down the volume of social media during the quietest hours of the night.

The question is whether that small amount of friction will be enough to change what happens after midnight.
