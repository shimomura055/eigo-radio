At 11:59 p.m., the screen is still glowing.

“One more video.”

“One more message.”

“One more quick look.”

Then midnight arrives—and under a new British government plan, social media could become much quieter for 16- and 17-year-olds.

The proposal would introduce a default night-time restriction from midnight until 6 a.m. During those six hours, social media alerts and push notifications would also be muted.

In other words, the phone would stop calling for attention just when sleep is supposed to begin.

But this is not a locked door.

The protective settings would switch on automatically, but young users would be able to change them. So the plan is not a complete or unchangeable social media ban. It is more like a gentle barrier placed in the path of late-night scrolling.

The idea appears simple:

“Make rest the default—and make endless scrolling the active choice.”

However, the change has not yet taken effect. As of August 12, 2026, it remains a government plan and still requires parliamentary approval. Government materials suggest that the new settings could begin to appear around spring 2027.

That leaves an important gap between the policy on paper and life behind the screen.

A default setting may create a moment to pause. But users can change it, habits can move to another time, and a silent phone does not automatically mean an early night.

Britain is therefore preparing more than a technical adjustment. It is testing whether a small change in the digital environment can influence one of the strongest modern habits: the urge to keep scrolling when the day should already be over.
