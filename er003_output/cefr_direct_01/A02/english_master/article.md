At midnight, the phone may still be beside the bed.

But under the UK government’s plan, it could become a much quieter companion.

Britain is preparing new default social media protections for 16- and 17-year-olds. From midnight to 6 a.m., covered services would apply nighttime restrictions, including the muting of alerts and push notifications.

The idea is simple:

“Let the night feel like night again.”

This is not a blanket, unchangeable social media ban. The protections would switch on automatically, but users would be able to alter the settings themselves.

That distinction matters.

The government is not proposing to seize control of teenagers’ phones for six hours. Instead, it wants the starting position to change—from platforms constantly inviting young people back in, to a quieter default that requires a conscious choice to override.

And for now, this remains a plan, not an active rule.

As of August 12, 2026, the changes had not yet taken effect. Parliamentary approval is still required, with the government expecting implementation to begin around spring 2027.

So the screen is not going dark tonight.

But Britain is preparing to ask a much bigger question: when midnight arrives, should social media still be designed to keep calling?
