It is after midnight. The room is dark, but a phone screen is still bright. One alert arrives, then another. Going to sleep can wait for “just one more check.”

The UK government wants to change what happens by default in that moment.

Under its planned rules, social media services covered by the policy would apply a default nighttime restriction to users aged 16 and 17. It would run from midnight to 6 a.m. During those six hours, social media alerts and push notifications would be muted.

At first, this may sound like a strict digital curfew.

But it is not a complete ban.

The protective settings would turn on automatically, but 16- and 17-year-olds would be able to change them. The plan does not mean that social media would become completely unavailable at midnight. Instead, the safer nighttime setting would become the starting choice.

The age group also matters. The plan is for 16- and 17-year-olds, not all minors. It would apply to social media services covered by the new rules, but the final details about those services have not yet been decided.

There is another important pause in this story: the system is not in force yet.

As of August 12, 2026, it remains a government plan. The new rules still need approval from Parliament. If they receive that approval, the government expects the changes to begin around spring 2027.

So, for now, no switch has been turned. The next question is whether changing the default setting can change late-night habits—even when young users can still turn that setting off.
