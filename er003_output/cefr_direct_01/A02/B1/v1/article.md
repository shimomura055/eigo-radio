At midnight, a phone can suddenly feel very loud.

One alert appears. Then another. A quick check becomes ten more minutes of scrolling.

The UK government is planning a new response for 16- and 17-year-olds:

“After midnight, social media should become quieter by default.”

Under the plan, covered social media services would automatically apply nighttime restrictions from midnight to 6 a.m. Alerts and push notifications would also be muted during those six hours.

But this is not a locked digital curfew.

Young users would be able to change the settings themselves. The proposal is designed as a default protection—something that is already switched on unless the user chooses otherwise—not an unchangeable ban.

That difference matters.

The government is not simply trying to place a wall between teenagers and social media. It is putting a small pause into the night: fewer signals from the screen, and fewer invitations to return to an app.

Picture the moment.

The clock reaches midnight. The phone does not disappear. The internet does not shut down. But the usual stream of notifications becomes quiet.

The message is simple:

“Nothing is demanding your attention right now.”

As of August 12, 2026, however, this remains a plan rather than a rule already in force. Parliamentary approval is still required, and the government expects the changes to begin around spring 2027.

So the setting has not yet reached teenagers’ phones. The next question is whether a quieter default will change nighttime habits—or whether young users will simply switch it off.
