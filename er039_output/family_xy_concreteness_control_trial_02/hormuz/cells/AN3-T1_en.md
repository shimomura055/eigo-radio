# The Crude Oil Market’s Main Character: The 20 Percent Bill That Vanished

At first glance, this news seems to have Trump as its main character. But the most interesting move came from crude oil, which did not fall.

On July 13, Trump proposed a 20 percent “reimbursement fee” on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. In simple terms, the United States would pay to keep the strait safe, then ask all cargo passing through to share the cost. It was like handing a large bill to every shipment on the route.

Then, the next day, the scene changed suddenly. The direct request for money was about to become something else.

### The bill gives way to trade and investment

Trump withdrew the 20 percent plan. Instead, he said it would be replaced by trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. He explained that the change followed “very productive talks” with Middle East leaders. A direct fee had become a story about trade and investment. For a moment, the rough waves seemed a little calmer.

### The oil price keeps its warning

Brent crude futures cut their gains after the announcement. Soon, however, the price returned near its high level from before the announcement. At the time of reporting, it was above $85 a barrel. Oil does not speak, but its movement seemed to say: “I know the bill is gone, but that alone is not enough to feel safe.”

## In one line

The answer reflected dangers that had not changed: attacks between the United States and Iran continued, fears of a naval blockade remained, and worries about tanker safety did not disappear; on oil’s stage, oil prices still connect that distant sea to gasoline and transport costs, and changing a political plan’s name cannot erase the route’s danger.
