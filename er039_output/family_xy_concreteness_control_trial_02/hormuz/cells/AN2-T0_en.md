# The Fee Plan Disappeared, but Oil Prices Did Not Come Back Down

This oil-market story looked like a play in which the lead actor suddenly changed scripts. At first, it seemed to be heading toward a simple result: one announcement would push prices in one clear direction. The news was large enough to command attention, and the next move appeared easy to guess. Then, one day later, the script changed. If the new charge was no longer coming, oil prices might be expected to calm down. That was the natural expectation. But the market’s answer was more complicated. The headline changed; the deeper worry behind it did not.

### A charge on a vital sea route

On July 13, Trump proposed a 20 percent fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was that cargo using the route would help pay for the US cost of keeping the strait safe. For oil-carrying ships, this is a key route, making it more than enough to move prices.

For a short time, the market had a simple headline to follow. But prices can respond to more than the newest words on the page.

### The second day brings a new script

On July 14, the stage turned. Trump withdrew the 20 percent plan and instead pointed to Gulf-state trade and investment deals with the United States, after what he called very productive talks with Middle Eastern leaders. Afterward, Brent crude futures briefly narrowed their rise, but prices soon returned near their earlier high—about 2.6 percent up, above $85 a barrel.

## In one line

The market was not watching only the fee: attacks between the United States and Iran continued, fears of a sea blockade remained, and worries about tankers passing safely did not disappear, so the sign changed but the danger at sea did not, and oil prices briefly braked before pressing the accelerator again.
