# The AI Phone Call Had a Human Helper

An AI called. That was what people thought as they spoke. But a human appeared from behind the scenes. A test involving Muse, Meta’s AI agent, became a reveal of what was really happening.

### The human backup plan

Through Muse, trained human contract workers made some calls and completed the exchanges with users. The idea was practical: when AI struggled, a person could help. Keeping a human in reserve seemed safer than leaving everything to AI.

But that backup plan changed the meaning of the call.

### The hidden cost of a smooth call

The test began without properly telling people what was happening. So people who thought they were speaking with AI were actually speaking with human staff. These workers did more than make the conversation smooth: the user’s sensitive information might be shared with contract workers at a call center. One helper meant one more person handling private data.

This is the interesting, slightly frightening turn. People often worry that an AI phone call will produce a strange answer. Here, the opposite happened. The voice was not simply human-like; a human was really speaking. And people were not properly told.

In response, a Meta executive admitted that starting a test in which contract workers made calls without properly telling people was a mistake. The human concierge feature was then rolled back for the time being.

AI stories usually focus on machines moving closer to people. Muse asks a different question: if humans enter AI’s backstage, can users be told? Who is speaking, and who can see the information?

## In one line

Unless both facts are explained at the start, a service of the future can quickly look like a call from a stranger.
