# The AI Phone Twist: A Human Was on the Other End

A phone rings. On the screen, it looks as if Muse, an AI agent, is making the call. The person who answers would naturally expect to speak with AI.

But the story turns here.

In a test of Muse’s phone feature, human contract workers handled some calls instead of AI. They received training before making the calls and bringing their conversations with callers to an end.

In other words, an AI label was on the service, but human performers were behind the scenes.

At first, this may look like a small reveal.

### Why the twist may seem helpful

If you thought AI was speaking but found a human instead, it might look like a small reveal. In some situations, a human may understand you better than AI. When a conversation goes off track and you must repeat the same explanation, reaching a human from the start may seem like the better choice instead of having to repeat yourself.

But the real problem is not whether the conversation goes more smoothly.

### What an unseen human may hear

People share information on calls, including private details. If they believe they are speaking to AI, they decide what to say on that basis. A contract worker may hear information they did not expect to share with a person. That is not a minor detail; it can affect whether they speak. It started without properly telling people; privacy concerns arose.

## In one line

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without properly telling people was a mistake, and the feature that let humans handle calls was rolled back for the time being; the deeper twist is that while people expected AI to replace humans, humans had become part of AI’s backstage, so as AI calls become more familiar, callers should be told who is on the other end before they decide whether to speak.
