# The AI Caller Was Actually a Human Helper

An AI called. At least, that is what it seemed like. People spoke through Meta’s AI agent, Muse, expecting an AI on the other end. Then the story turned into a kind of backstage reveal.

The basic idea sounded practical. When AI has trouble, a person can help. Keeping a human in reserve may feel safer than leaving everything to AI. But the calls quickly became more complicated than they first appeared. The question was not only how well the call went, but who was actually on the other end.

### The people behind some Muse calls

In some calls through Muse, trained workers hired under contract—not AI—made the calls and finished the conversations. Their job was not only to make the talk smooth. When they handled a call, sensitive user information could also be shared with contract workers at a call center.

Add one human helper, and there may be one more person handling information. Convenience had opened the door to a privacy problem.

People often worry that AI may give a strange answer. This time, the surprise was the opposite. The voice was not too human; a human was really speaking. And people were not given enough notice.

### The mistake Meta later admitted

A Meta executive admitted that starting a test in which contract staff made calls without proper notice was a mistake. Meta then rolled back its human concierge feature for the time being.

AI stories often focus on machines becoming more like people. Muse raises a different question: if people enter the system behind AI, can users be told? Who is speaking? Who can see the information?

On a phone call, those two facts must be explained at the start. Otherwise, a future service can suddenly look like a call from a stranger.

## In one line

If an AI call has a human behind it, users need to know who is speaking and who can see their information.
