# Schools That Ban Phone Use During Breaks: Different Voices

I support the rule at my daughter's high school that students can't use their phones during breaks or lunch. Just banning them during class isn't enough. I get home late from the job site, and fighting about phones every night is exhausting. If I can say, “That's the rule at school too,” then I don't have to be the only bad guy. My daughter didn't keep our agreement to put her phone in the charging basket in the living room. The morning after I took it away, it was hard to talk to her at breakfast. She also said, “If I'm the only one who replies late, I'll be left out.” I couldn't answer. I want her to stick to what we've agreed. I don't want to yell like my father, either. But even if she can't use her phone at school during the day, nothing changes at home at night. Maybe I just want to ask the school for help so things are easier for me. I'm a site supervisor who can't even get things organized at home.

First thing every morning, I see the faces of the kids who come to the nurse's office. “I'm sleepy,” they say. “My head hurts,” they say. The other day, a phone screen was glowing beside the pillow of a student resting on the bed. When I hear they were up until dawn, I wonder what I should say. I'm also worried about the student who stopped coming in the day after their phone was taken away during the day. If they don't come, does that mean they aren't having a hard time? A teacher asked me, “If we ban phones, will fewer kids come to the nurse's office?” I couldn't answer. If the school makes phones off-limits even during breaks, won't it end up thinking it has dealt with the problem? That really scares me. I don't take phones away in the nurse's office. I want to keep a place where students can talk about what happened at night. Once they can no longer use their phones, who will listen to that student?

I'm against a rule that makes phones off-limits even during breaks. I work at a warehouse during the day and go to evening high school at night. So I can't promise to cut off contact the whole time I'm at school. During breaks between classes, I get messages about changes to my shifts. If I reply late, the work is given to someone else. Once, my workplace kept calling during class. I couldn't answer, and later they told me it was a problem that they couldn't reach me. I almost had my shifts cut, too. I want to graduate from here. I need money to give to my family. Now that I'm beginning to understand my classes, I don't want to choose between work and school. I promise to keep my phone at the bottom of my bag and not touch it during class. But before deciding that I can't use it even during breaks, I want you to hear about how I work, too.

At the breakfast table in the morning, beside the pillow in the nurse's office, and in work messages during breaks, they all want to still be able to talk to each other. Extending the time when phones can't be used may reduce the arguments over the basket at night. But a student whose phone is taken away may stop talking to us at the bedside. Even if he follows the rule during class, stopping the shift messages he gets during breaks could make his employer lose trust in him. The father who asks the school for help also doesn't want to go through the silence after taking the phone away again. They may disagree about how long phones should be allowed, but they agree that they want someone they can still talk to. What messages do you want to reply to right away? When do they arrive, and who are they from?

## In one line
A ban on phones during breaks may ease fights at home, but it could also stop students from sharing problems or answering work messages. Though they disagree about when phones should be allowed, the parent, nurse, and student all want someone they can still talk to.
