# Schools That Ban Phones During Breaks: Different Voices

I support the rule at my daughter's high school. Students can't use phones during breaks or lunch. Just banning them during class isn't enough. I get home late from the job site. Fighting about phones every night wears me out. If I can say, “That's the rule at school too,” I don't have to be the only bad guy. My daughter didn't keep our agreement. She was meant to put her phone in the charging basket in the living room. The morning after I took it away, it was hard to talk to her at breakfast. She also said, “If I'm the only one who replies late, I'll be left out.” I couldn't answer. I want her to keep to what we agreed. I don't want to yell like my father, either. But even if she can't use her phone at school during the day, nothing changes at home at night. Maybe I just want to ask the school for help, so things are easier for me. I'm a site supervisor who can't even get things organized at home.

First thing every morning, I see the faces of kids who come to the nurse's office. “I'm sleepy,” they say. “My head hurts,” they say. The other day, a student was resting on the bed. Their phone screen glowed beside the pillow. When I hear they were up until dawn, I wonder what I should say. I'm also worried about the student who stopped coming in the day after their phone was taken away during the day. If they don't come, does that mean they aren't having a hard time? A teacher asked me, “If we ban phones, will fewer kids come to the nurse's office?” I couldn't answer. If the school bans phones during breaks too, won't it end up thinking it has solved the problem? That really scares me. I don't take phones away in the nurse's office. I want to keep a place where students can talk about what happened at night. Once they can no longer use their phones, who will listen to that student?

I'm against a rule that bans phones during breaks, too. I work at a warehouse during the day. At night, I go to evening high school. So I can't promise to stop all contact while I'm at school. Between classes, I get messages about changes to my shifts. If I reply late, the work goes to someone else. Once, my workplace kept calling during class. I couldn't answer. Later, they told me it was a problem that they couldn't reach me. I almost had my shifts cut, too. I want to graduate from here. I need money to give to my family. Now that I'm beginning to understand my classes, I don't want to choose between work and school. I promise to keep my phone at the bottom of my bag. I won't touch it during class. But before deciding I can't use it even during breaks, I want you to hear about how I work, too.

At breakfast in the morning, beside the pillow in the nurse's office, and through work messages during breaks, they all want to keep talking to each other. Making phone-free time longer may mean fewer fights about the basket at night. But a student whose phone is taken away may stop talking to us at the bedside. He may follow the rule in class. But if he cannot answer messages about his shifts during breaks, his employer may lose trust in him. The father who asks the school for help also doesn't want to face the silence again after taking the phone away. They may disagree about how long phones should be allowed. But they all want someone they can still talk to. What messages do you want to reply to right away? When do they arrive, and who are they from?

## In one line
A ban on phones during breaks may ease fights at home, but it could stop students from sharing problems or answering work messages. The parent, nurse, and student disagree about when phones should be allowed, but all want someone they can still talk to.
