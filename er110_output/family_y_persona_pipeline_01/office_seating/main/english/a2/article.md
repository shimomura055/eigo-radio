# Assigned Desks or Free Seating? Different Voices

I’m not late. I drop my father off at his day service. When I arrive at the time in my contract, papers are already spread across my coworker’s desk. I look for an empty seat, with my bag still over my shoulder. The other day, a coworker was on a call at the desk I used the day before. So I went farther into the room to avoid bothering them. A coworker carrying a note to check an order stopped by that desk, then found me. I thought, I made them look for me when they’re busy. It’s handy to sit near someone I need to talk to. But with assigned seats, we might end up far apart. Even so, empty seats are there, but it’s a little tiring to look around each time I come in. I’ll go to someone’s desk if I need to. So I want the company to give each of us a seat.

My coworker in sales is someone I can rely on. It’s not that I dislike them. Before I open my proposal at work, I look at them and at the empty desks. I wonder if I should sit next to them and talk, or start farther away. The other day, revisions kept coming all morning. I avoided their row and opened my laptop at a desk away from the window. When we met in the hall at lunch, I greeted them as usual. I was glad I hadn’t forced myself to smile and talk that morning. I know it would be easier to ask for advice with assigned seats. But I’d feel awkward telling the company why I wanted to change seats. It’s not that I dislike that person. I just want a little quiet today. I don’t want to make that sound like a complaint about someone. I’d rather choose a seat without giving a reason.

Don’t be so quick to say assigned seats will make things easier. I hand the corrected estimate back to the sales rep. I carefully stack the papers I use for checking. They still have sticky notes on them. I put my calculator and ruler back in their case, then go home. The next morning, I bring the papers over from the shelf and set them out again. Then I can carry on checking them where I left off. Assigned seats came up in a meeting. I asked if I could leave papers I was still working on at my desk. They said that was still to be decided. So, will I still have to put things away when I leave? Where will I keep them? I admit I tend to save old papers. But putting my name on a desk won’t make this work go away. I want to find out what I can leave there and what I’ll have to carry. Then I’ll decide whether to change seats.

We all want to put down our bags and open our papers quietly. We want to spread out the work we’re doing and work in peace. But looking for an empty seat in the morning, talking with someone beside us, and putting away papers with sticky notes at day’s end can cause trouble. These things happen at different times and in different places. What do we want to keep? A desk we can use without thinking twice? The space we need that morning? Or papers set out so we can continue the next day? With assigned seats, we can sit down without looking for a place. But it may be harder to sit apart quietly. And we still don’t know if papers can stay on the desk. The question of leaving papers there is also part of wanting an easier start to work. Where could you sit when you go in the next morning? What could you spread out on your desk to help you start work in peace?

## In one line
Workers want different things from office seats: a regular desk, space to sit apart, or a place to leave work papers. Assigned seats mean no search for a place, but may make it harder to sit apart, and we still don’t know if papers can stay.
