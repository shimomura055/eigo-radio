# Assigned Desks or Free Seating? Different Voices

I’m not late. I drop my father off at his day service, and when I arrive at the time set in my contract, papers are already spread across my coworker’s desk. I look for an empty seat, my bag still over my shoulder. The other day, a coworker was on a call at the desk I’d used the day before, so I went farther into the room to avoid bothering them. A coworker carrying a note to check an order stopped by that desk, then found me. I thought, I made them look for me when they’re busy. It’s handy to choose a seat near someone I need to talk to, and if we have assigned seats, we might end up far apart. Even so, though there are empty seats, it’s a little tiring to look around each time I come in. I’ll go to someone’s desk if I need to, so I want the company to assign each of us a seat.

My coworker in sales is someone I can rely on. It’s not that I dislike them. Before I open my proposal at work, I look at them and at the empty desks, and think about whether to sit next to them and talk, or start farther away. The other day, on a morning when revisions kept coming, I avoided the row where they were and opened my laptop at a desk away from the window. When we met in the hall at lunch, I was able to greet them as usual. I was glad I hadn’t forced myself to smile and talk that morning. I know it would be easier to ask for advice if we had assigned seats. But if we had assigned seats, I’d feel awkward even telling the company why I wanted to change seats. It’s not that I dislike that person. I just want a little quiet today. I don’t want to turn that into a complaint about someone. I’d rather be able to choose a seat without giving a reason.

Don’t be so quick to say assigned seats will make things easier. I hand the corrected estimate back to the sales rep, carefully stack the papers I use for checking, still marked with sticky notes, put my calculator and ruler back in their case, and go home. The next morning, I bring them over from the shelf and set them out again so I can pick up where I left off checking them. When assigned seats came up in a meeting, I asked if I could leave papers I was still working on at my desk. They said that was still to be decided. So, will I still have to put things away when I leave? Where will I keep them? I admit I tend to save old papers. But just putting my name on a desk won’t reduce this work. I want to find out what I can leave there and what I’ll have to carry. Then I’ll decide whether to change seats.

We all want to put down our bags, open our papers quietly, spread out the work we’re in the middle of, and work in peace. But looking for an empty seat in the morning, talking with someone beside us, and putting away sticky-note-marked papers at the end of the day cause trouble at different times and in different places. What do we want to hold on to: a desk we can go to without hesitation, the distance we need that morning, or an arrangement of papers we can continue from the next day? Assigned seats mean we can sit down without looking for a place, but it may be harder to quietly sit apart, and we still don’t know if we can leave papers on the desk. The question of leaving papers behind also connects to the wish to make starting work take less effort. When you go in the next morning, where could you sit, and what could you spread out on your desk to help you start work in peace?

## In one line
Workers want different things from office seating: a reliable desk, freedom to sit apart, or a place to leave work in progress. Assigned seats can end the search for a seat, but may make it harder to keep distance, and it is still unclear whether papers can stay.
