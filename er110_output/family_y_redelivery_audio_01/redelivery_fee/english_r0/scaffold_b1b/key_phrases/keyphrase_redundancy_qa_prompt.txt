以下は、同じ記事から選ばれた3件のKey Phrase(英語学習ポッドキャストの
リスニング教材)です。3件から作れる全てのペア(3組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず3組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

# Package Redelivery: Different Voices

If you miss a package because you’re not home, you can have it delivered again. What if the person receiving it had to pay for that redelivery, on top of the usual delivery fee? Here are the voices of three people with different lives and points of view. These are not the stories of specific real people. They are voices created from several opinions shared online and elsewhere.

When I leave a delivery notice and head off, I do feel a little down. But then I go on to the next house. I wish there were fewer redeliveries, if possible, and it’s true that they’re hard on us. Even so, I can’t just say that customers should pay for them. I think it makes more sense to cover the cost through the overall fee for delivering packages than to charge each customer who missed a delivery for that redelivery. To do that, I’d also like companies to think about whether free shipping on online orders should stay as it is. If they review shipping fees, I want them to review how the fees are set, too, so that the money actually goes to the people delivering the packages.

It just doesn’t sit right with me to pay for redelivery. My husband and I are both out of the house for work, so for packages where I can choose, I pick a day and time when I know I’ll be home. But once, I chose a morning time slot and the package didn’t come. I went out to take care of an errand, and when I got back, there was a delivery notice. Many packages don’t let you choose a delivery time, either, so you don’t know ahead of time when they’ll arrive. Before charging us, I want them to first do something about showing up at the time we chose and telling us when the package will arrive. The delivery workers are probably having a hard time because they’re short-staffed, and maybe it can’t be helped. But I can’t accept having to pay for that. Am I wrong to feel that way?

I try to pick up packages at a delivery office or a locker so I don’t cause trouble with redelivery. It’s convenient to pick them up when it suits me, but some products can’t be sent to a locker. And even if I ask for a package to be sent to a delivery office, it can arrive late, so in the end I sometimes have to receive it in person. Still, I think it’s honestly wrong that I pay the same fee as people who are always out and ask for redelivery, when I’m the one thinking about how to receive my packages and taking the trouble. Redelivery uses the delivery worker’s time, so I think it’s fine to charge for it. I’d be okay with making the first redelivery free and having people who are repeatedly out pay for it. Though maybe that’s just my view, since the delivery office is nearby and I can choose how to receive my packages.

The person who leaves a delivery notice, the person who comes back and sees it, and the person who stops by a locker all want fewer redeliveries. What matters to them is different: money going to the people delivering packages, the promise of waiting for a morning delivery, and how the effort of going to a locker is rewarded. They disagree on whether people who are repeatedly out should pay, or whether the cost should be covered by the overall shipping fee. Packages that don’t arrive in the morning and packages that can’t be sent to lockers are problems the person receiving them can’t solve through their own efforts alone. On the day your next package is supposed to arrive, where will you be, and what will you be doing while you wait?

## In one line
Three people all want fewer package redeliveries, but disagree over who should pay for them. Customers cannot always prevent them on their own, because deliveries may miss promised times or offer no locker option.
