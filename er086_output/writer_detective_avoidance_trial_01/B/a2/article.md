# “We Won’t Attack”—Does That Also Include Refueling?

“We won’t attack.” If that is true, their job must be to act as a “shield.” But the UK’s announced help for Saudi Arabia means more than this phrase says. It is not only about fighter planes. It also includes giving fuel to planes while they are flying.

What happened in Saudi skies first? A spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said it had stopped and destroyed a ballistic missile. The Houthis had fired it toward Riyadh.

The UK Ministry of Defense responded to these threats. It said Royal Air Force Typhoons would fly air defense missions. They would protect Saudi cities and key sites. Their missions would be only for defense, it said. They would not attack the Houthis.

So far, the explanation is easy to understand: “The Typhoons are a shield.” It fits neatly in the explanation box. But the box will not close if we try to put all UK help inside it.

That is because there is another kind of help: refueling in the air.

The UK says it will continue refueling support for Saudi air defense missions. This means giving fuel to planes while they are flying. But we must not miss one point: what will the planes getting fuel do?

The announcement says this help also includes missions to weaken Houthi military targets in Yemen. The UK says these targets are military ones that threaten people in Saudi Arabia and Yemen.

“Wait. Didn’t they say they wouldn’t attack?”

Let’s return to the first sentence. The claim “there will be no attacks” is about the Typhoons’ mission. It does not mean every Saudi mission helped by refueling is only for defense. Even within the same UK support, the missions are different. If we change the subject from “the Typhoons” to “all UK support,” the meaning changes completely.

One mission protects cities and important sites. The other gives fuel to planes for their missions. If we sum it all up as “The UK won’t attack,” this difference disappears.

Of course, knowing the difference does not tell us if the support is right or wrong. Before seeing this as a choice between “defend” and “attack,” first ask: “Who is not doing what?” And what work does the fuel support at the other end of the hose? The shorter the explanation, the more important it is to notice what comes next.

## In one line
The UK says its Typhoons will only defend Saudi cities. Its refueling help also covers missions to weaken Houthi targets in Yemen. So “the UK won’t attack” describes the Typhoons’ mission, not all UK support.
