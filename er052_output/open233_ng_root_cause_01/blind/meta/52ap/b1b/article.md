# Humans Were the Main Players in AI Phone Calls: An Unexpected Turn Behind the Scenes

When people asked AI to make a phone call, it was a human who made the call. Something like a scene from a movie happened with Meta’s Muse.

In some calls made through Muse, trained human contract workers took part in conversations that AI was supposed to handle. And they did more than just help a little. The workers made the calls themselves, talked with the people on the other end, and handled the calls until the task was done.

The name of this experiment was “Human Concierge.” If you only heard the name, it would sound like a dependable person who helps you when you are in trouble. But if humans appear behind the scenes of AI phone calls, the story changes somewhat.

The main point was not simply that a human made the calls. It was that the test began without clearly telling people that humans, not AI, would be making the calls.

At Meta, concerns about privacy arose. Personal and important information shared during calls might be passed, without meaning to, to contract workers at a call center.

When people think they are talking to AI, what kinds of information do they share? Their plans, their family matters, and their work. Of course, that does not mean we know that people actually shared such information this time. Still, a human might hear something people thought they were saying to AI. That difference should be quite large.

What happened here was not a fuss about AI’s performance. Before people could ask how naturally AI could speak, the issue became the explanation of what was happening behind the scenes: who was making the call and who was listening to the conversation.

As automated phone systems and voice assistants become more common in daily life, this can become an issue close to home. Even if a screen shows AI, if a human is actually involved, whether you know that or not can change how safe you feel.

The Meta executive in charge admitted that it was a mistake to start a test in which contract workers made calls without giving a proper explanation. The Human Concierge feature was then put back the way it was, for the time being.

The human who unexpectedly became the main character on the stage of AI phone calls. What this incident showed is that AI needs not only an intelligent voice, but also an honest explanation of what happens behind the scenes.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone-call test unexpectedly had human contractors make calls without clearly telling people, raising privacy concerns.
