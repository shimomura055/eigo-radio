# An Unexpected Human Was Behind the AI Phone Service

The phone service of the future is about to begin. AI will make calls, talk with the other person, and take care of bothersome tasks. You can imagine a scene like that, can't you?

However, the Muse phone feature that Meta was testing had a somewhat surprising story behind it. In some of the calls, the person actually speaking was not AI.

The people who appeared were trained contract workers. They made calls as human concierges and carried the conversations through to the end. In other words, humans were acting as stand-ins in a service where AI was supposed to have the main role.

The problem was not that humans were making the calls. The problem was how to explain that fact.

To finish a call, detailed information, such as the user's schedule and wishes, is needed. In some cases, this may include information that should be handled with care. There was a chance that such information could be shared by mistake with contract workers at a call center. This led to privacy concerns inside Meta.

From the point of view of the person receiving the call, they thought AI was speaking, but it may actually have been a human. And the user's information may have been passed on to that person. This is a much bigger concern than whether the voice on the call sounds natural.

However, this does not mean that a large leak of information definitely happened. The issue this time was whether they had properly explained who was making the calls and what information would be shared.

Meta executives admitted that it was a mistake to start the test without giving enough information. Then they rolled back the human concierge feature. They did not stop Muse as a whole.

Meta will continue improving the phone feature with the businesses involved. It plans to release it only after it is ready to explain who will handle the calls and make sure people understand how their information will be handled.

You thought it was an AI phone call, but a human answered. The main point this time was not AI's intelligence, but the importance of properly introducing the people working behind the scenes. The more convenient a service is, the more likely it may have secrets that need to be revealed first.

## In one line
Meta's AI phone test used human callers without clearly informing users, raising privacy concerns.
