# That Call May Not Have Been Made by AI

If you ask AI to make a phone call, you imagine that AI will make the call and handle the conversation for you. But in a feature of Muse that Meta tested, there was an unexpected person taking part: a human.

For some calls made through Muse, the people actually making the calls were not AI, but trained contract workers. The workers talked with the other person and finished the conversation. In other words, a human helper had appeared in a call that users thought they had asked AI to make.

The name of this feature was “Human Concierge.” The name alone makes it sound very dependable. Some calls were handled by trained human contractors. That is what this polished system seemed to be like behind the scenes.

But here, the mood of the story changes. Inside Meta, people became concerned about privacy.

When human contract workers handle calls, sensitive information about users may be shared with the workers by accident during the call. It was not confirmed that a large-scale information leak had actually happened. The problem was that, even though this possibility existed, the test began without enough explanation for users.

You may think you are looking at an AI screen, while a human is doing the work on the other side. That is quite a big secret behind a convenient service.

In an internal post, Meta’s vice president in charge admitted that starting a test in which contract workers made calls, without proper disclosure, was a “mistake.” The Human Concierge feature was then returned to its original state for the time being.

The important point here is that Muse as a whole was not stopped. Only the Human Concierge feature was returned to its original state. Also, humans did not handle every call. It was only some of the calls in the test.

The age of AI does not always run on machines alone. If there is a human on the other side of the screen, that fact is part of the service too. The next time you ask AI to make a phone call, you may care about more than whether the answer is correct. Who is making the call? That too may become very important information.

## In one line
Meta’s AI calling test sometimes relied on undisclosed human contractors, raising privacy concerns for users.
