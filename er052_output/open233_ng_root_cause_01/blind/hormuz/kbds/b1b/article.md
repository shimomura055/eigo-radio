# A 20 Percent Fee Proposal, Sent Backstage the Next Day

The story over the Strait of Hormuz this time looks, just from the way it developed, like a short play. In Act One, a fee proposal appears, and the next day it is replaced by another proposal. But crude oil prices in the audience did not settle down until the end.

On July 13, Mr. Trump posted that he would seek a 20 percent reimbursement fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was for the cargo to repay the cost of keeping the strait safe for the United States.

However, this was not a fee that had actually been put into effect. It was only a proposal. No details were given about who would pay or how the money would be collected. A large sign had appeared on the stage, but no toll booth had been built.

Then, the next day, the scene suddenly changed. Mr. Trump posted that he was withdrawing the 20 percent reimbursement fee and replacing it with trade and investment deals with the United States by Gulf countries. He explained that this decision was based on “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders.

He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself. The fee proposal that appeared the day before had gone backstage the next day.

So, did crude oil prices also feel safe enough to fall? There was another twist. After the announcement, Brent futures briefly gave up some of their gains. However, they soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. When Yahoo Finance carried the report, they were up about 2.6 percent, above $85 a barrel.

The interesting point in this scene was that removing the fee sign did not remove the worries about the strait. At that time, concerns over attacks by the United States and Iran, a sea blockade, and tanker safety were still continuing.

In fact, Brent futures the day before had risen 9.59 percent and settled at $83.30 a barrel. Reuters linked this rise to a U.S. naval blockade of Iran, expected to begin the next day, and concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz.

Even after the sign for the fee proposal was replaced, the question of whether ships could pass safely remained on the stage.

## In one line
Trump withdrew a proposed 20 percent Hormuz shipping fee, but oil prices stayed high as safety concerns remained.
