# Behind the Scenes of AI Calls: An Unexpected Human Cast

At Meta’s Muse, an unexpected performer had joined some phone calls. It was not AI, but trained contract workers. An experiment was under way in which these workers made the calls and handled the conversations with the other person to the very end. They were called “human concierges.”

The problem here was not the fact that humans had helped. The problem was that the test began without properly telling users who was making the calls.

In a service that people use while thinking they are talking to AI, humans were actually taking part. What’s more, these workers were people on the call-center side. Information about health, money, and family could come up in a phone call. Users thought this information would go to AI, but it might also be shared with contract workers without their knowing it. People inside the company raised concerns about privacy.

What matters here is that this was not news confirming that information had leaked. The problem this time was the possibility that, without users knowing, the people receiving the information could expand from AI to humans.

It was like opening a door to talk to AI and finding another person standing behind it. That was the uncomfortable feeling.

With AI services, it is hard to see what is on the other side of the screen. Is a machine answering, or is a human making the call? The more convenient these services become, the more important that boundary is. Even with ordinary automated responses, there are times when people wonder who is reading the content. This story brought that question right into the center of the AI era.

The vice president in charge admitted that it was a mistake to start a test in which contract workers made calls without properly telling users. Meta put the human concierge feature back on hold for the time being and returned it to its earlier state.

There is nothing necessarily wrong with AI needing help from humans. However, if the performers are hidden, the audience cannot watch the show with peace of mind. What AI calls truly need is not only human-like responses, but also a clear notice that humans are there when they are.

## In one line
Meta paused an AI calling test after contract workers handled conversations without users being properly informed.
