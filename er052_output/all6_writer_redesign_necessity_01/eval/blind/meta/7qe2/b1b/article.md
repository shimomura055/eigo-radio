# Muse’s Phone Calls: A Human Steps Out from the Wings

Booking a haircut, checking a store’s stock, getting an estimate from a business. It was reported that Muse has a feature that can call companies and stores in the United States to take care of tasks like these. But in some calls, trained human contract workers, not AI, made the calls and finished the conversations. This was a test of “human concierges.”

Imagine a human stand-in appearing in a scene where AI is supposed to appear. The main point this time is not how well the stand-in did the job. It is whether people were properly told who would handle the call.

Meta employees worried that sensitive information about users might be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center during a call. This does not mean that an actual information leak was confirmed. The problem was that, even though information might be shared with a person, the test began with contract workers making calls without proper disclosure.

When people ask AI to take care of a task, they may want to know more than whether the task gets done. Who is speaking on the other end of the call, and who hears the information? Even if Muse is what people see onstage, users should be able to use it knowing that a person may be on the phone.

A vice president of the team in charge of this at Meta admitted that it was a mistake to start the test with contract workers making calls without proper disclosure. Meta then rolled back the human concierge feature for now. If a person is going to appear from behind the scenes of an AI show, the audience should be told who is in the cast before the curtain goes up. This incident was also about the absence of a simple, obvious statement like that.

## In one line
Meta paused Muse’s test after human contractors, not AI, made calls without proper disclosure.
