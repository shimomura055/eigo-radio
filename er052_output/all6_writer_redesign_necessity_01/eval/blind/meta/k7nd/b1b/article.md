# The AI Phone Operator Was Actually Human

I thought I had left the call to AI, but a human voice came from the other end. This surprising setup was being tested in Meta’s Muse.

In Muse, they were testing a feature in which AI made phone calls for users. But for some of those calls, it was not AI making the call. It was trained contract staff.

The staff placed calls that users had asked for, in their place. Then they finished the conversation with the other person. Rather than humans working behind the scenes to make it look like an AI service, more exactly, this was an experiment in which human concierges handled the calls behind the scenes.

At this point, the story starts to feel a little like a mystery, rather than just a convenient call service. Who exactly is looking at the user’s information? And who should the person receiving the call think the call came from?

To complete a call, it may sometimes be necessary to use sensitive information about the user. That information could be shared, without intending to, with the contract staff handling the call. Inside Meta, people voiced concerns about privacy.

No one has said that a large-scale information leak actually happened. The problem was that testing began before they had fully worked out how to manage a system where people handled the information.

And there is another main person in this story: the person receiving the call. For that person, it may make a difference whether the call is from an AI service or a human speaking on its behalf. If the conversation moves forward without properly explaining that difference, the other person does not have the information needed to decide.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without a proper explanation was a mistake. They temporarily returned the feature with human call operators to its previous form.

However, Muse as a whole was not stopped. For the phone feature, they are continuing to improve it with partner businesses. Their policy is to release it to the public only when preparations are complete and they can give a proper explanation.

What was questioned in this incident was not whether AI can speak like a human. When we think a call is from AI, who is working behind it, and who is looking at the information? Behind a convenient call service, it seems that revealing who is really behind it is required before the voice itself.

## In one line
Meta’s supposed AI phone calls were sometimes handled by human contractors, raising privacy and transparency concerns.
