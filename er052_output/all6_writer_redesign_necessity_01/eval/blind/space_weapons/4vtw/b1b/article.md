# Space Weapons Quiz: First, Find the Address

Here is a question. What the United States officially admitted for the first time was that it had deployed weapons in space. So, when reading this news, what should you check first?

The answer is not the name of the weapon. It is its address. Is it on the ground or in orbit? Is it for attack or defense? If we do not look at this, stories about space are quickly pushed into one box.

On September 14, the U.S. Secretary of the Air Force said that the United States was deploying space-control weapons in orbit to protect joint forces from the actions of hostile parties. An official U.S. government article recorded this as the first statement acknowledging that the Space Force had deployed weapons in space.

However, we should not imagine the details of secret weapons here. The names of specific devices, their ability to attack, and their targets have not been revealed. What we know is that there has been an announcement about deploying weapons in orbit.

Now for the next question. Which country fired a missile from the ground and destroyed an artificial satellite? Russia. There are now more than 1,500 pieces of trackable debris. This was a major event, but it was a test to destroy a satellite directly from the ground. It is different from keeping weapons in orbit.

Even when we are talking about weapons in space, ground-launched weapons and weapons deployed in orbit have, so to speak, different addresses. They may look like the same kind of thing when we look only at news headlines, but they are separate matters in terms of classification.

Also, the term “counterspace activities” does not refer only to outer space. In the U.S. Space Force framework, there are three arenas: orbit, communications with satellites, and ground facilities. It includes disrupting communications with radio waves, cyberattacks, and attacks on ground facilities.

What is more, counterspace activities also include defense. They include systems that warn of threats, ways to make equipment harder to damage, spreading satellites out, and keeping backups. If these are put in the same box as offensive weapons in orbit, the classification chart gets lost.

Finally, there is the issue of treaties. Article IV of the Outer Space Treaty clearly bans placing nuclear weapons and other weapons of mass destruction in orbit around Earth, as well as setting up military bases or weapons on celestial bodies such as the Moon. It is not a rule that bans all space weapons. The announcement alone also cannot tell us whether any particular weapon is legal or illegal.

In other words, the answer to this news is not “whether there are weapons in space.” It is where they are, what they do, whether they are a system for protection, and what part of the treaty they fall under.

As more of our daily lives depend on satellite communications and location data, this is not a quiz about some faraway space. If we get the answer wrong, it also affects our communications and the way we get around.

## In one line
The key to understanding space weapons news is knowing where the weapons are and what they do.
