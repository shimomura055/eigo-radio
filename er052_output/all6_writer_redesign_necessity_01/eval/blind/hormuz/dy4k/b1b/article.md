# The 20 Percent Figure Is Out, but There Are No Details. Oil Prices Climb Back Toward Their Highs

If you follow only the headlines, it seems as if the story suddenly changed. Trump first posted that he wanted to charge a rate of 20 percent on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, as repayment for the cost of the United States keeping the strait safe. But the next day, he announced that he was replacing the plan with trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States. The focus quickly shifted from cargo passing through the strait to investment in the United States.

But just because the figure of 20 percent was announced, that does not mean the system for collecting the fee was decided. Who would pay? What would the fee be based on? How would it be collected? The key details were not given. The figure made a splash, but there was still no guide to how the system would work. There was no report that a 20 percent toll had actually been collected.

So how did oil prices move? After the replacement plan was announced, Brent futures briefly gave back some of their gains. But soon they were back near the high level they had reached before the announcement. At the time of the report, they were up 2.6 percent, above $85 a barrel. The gains got smaller, then quickly returned. The line on the chart was not moving in a simple way, either.

Meanwhile, concerns continued over attacks by the United States and Iran, the naval blockade, and the safety of tankers. Even though the fee plan changed, tensions over the strait did not disappear. What stands out this time is that the policy talk changed in a single day. And even after the statements changed, oil prices did not just fall once and stay down. They returned to a high level. The headlines may move quickly, but worries about events at sea cannot be settled at the same speed.

## In one line
Trump replaced his proposed Hormuz shipping fee with investment deals, but oil prices soon returned near their highs.
