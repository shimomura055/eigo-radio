# The Script Changed in One Day. Oil Prices Didn't Calm Down Right Away

The news story took a sharp turn in just one day. A plan to charge cargo passing through the strait gave way the next day to talk of trade and investment by Gulf states in the United States. It looked like a show of policy plans changing costumes, but oil prices did not treat it as “case closed.”

On July 13, Trump posted that the United States would seek 20 percent in reimbursement from all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to cover the cost of keeping the strait safe. But this was only a proposal. The charges had not begun, and no details were given about who would pay or how.

That same day, Brent crude futures rose by $7.29, or 9.59 percent, and closed at $83.30 a barrel. It was too soon to conclude that prices had risen because of the 20 percent plan. Reuters linked the rise to concerns about a naval blockade of Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the strait.

Then, on the 14th, Trump posted that he was withdrawing the reimbursement plan and replacing it with trade and investment deals involving Gulf states and the United States. He said the decision was based on “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders. Until yesterday, the main story was charges on cargo. Today, it was investment in the United States. The story moved very fast.

So, if the plan was withdrawn, would oil prices fall right away? After the announcement, the rise in Brent crude futures narrowed for a while. But before long, prices returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel.

Attacks by the United States and Iran, the naval blockade, and concerns about tanker safety continued. Even if the contents of the post changed greatly in one day, worries about the strait would not disappear along with them. The news took a sudden turn. But the market did not allow such an easy ending.

## In one line
Trump replaced a proposed fee on Hormuz shipping with U.S.-Gulf investment plans, but oil prices stayed high.
