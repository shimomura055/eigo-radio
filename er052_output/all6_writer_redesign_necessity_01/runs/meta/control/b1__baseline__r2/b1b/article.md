# Some Calls Made Through AI Were Handled by Humans Midway: Behind the Scenes of Muse

The time has come when you can ask AI to make phone calls for you. Just when it seemed that way, the main actor unexpectedly changed.

Meta's personal AI agent, Muse, can make phone calls to businesses and stores in the United States. It can book haircuts, check whether products are in stock, and get estimates from service providers. It is a handy phone assistant that makes troublesome calls for you.

However, in some calls made through Muse, the call was actually made by a human, not AI. A trained contract worker took over from Muse and finished the conversation with the other person. Meta called this test “human concierge.”

If you thought you were on a call with AI, a human helper could appear from behind the scenes. It was almost like a stand-in taking over during a phone call.

There were also reports in internal posts of cases where the person on the other end realized that Muse was AI and hung up. News reports also cited an employee's report that an insurance company repeatedly hung up after realizing that a call from Muse was made by AI.

Humans were involved in some calls, and another problem comes into view. When user information is needed to continue the call, that sensitive information may be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center.

In addition, employees who asked Muse to negotiate their internet or cable bills reported that the call records included inappropriate remarks about race by human contract workers. This was a report from one employee, covered in news reports, and it was not about contract workers as a whole.

Looking at it this way, AI phone calls seem to be difficult for reasons beyond the way the AI talks. If there are humans behind the AI, that fact needs to be clearly disclosed. Without knowing who is making the call, privacy worries arise before convenience does.

The vice president of Meta's Superintelligence Labs division admitted that it was a “mistake” to start a test in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure. In an internal post, the vice president explained that the human concierge feature had been rolled back for now—in other words, put back for the time being.

Meta said it would continue improving the feature with businesses and would roll it out widely only when it was ready and could provide proper disclosure.

If you leave phone calls to AI, the next thing you may want to check is who the voice really belongs to.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone assistant sometimes handed calls to human workers, raising privacy and disclosure concerns.
