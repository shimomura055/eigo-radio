# Is That Phone Call AI? Humans Appear Behind the Scenes at Muse

This is a slightly unusual behind-the-scenes story about what was really behind AI phone calls.

Meta's AI agent, Muse, can call companies and stores. Booking a haircut, checking whether a product is in stock, asking a service provider for an estimate—AI was supposed to handle these bothersome calls.

But during some tests of the calls, an unexpected performer appeared.

The calls were not made by AI. They were made by trained contract workers. They took over requests from Muse, spoke with the other person, and finished the conversations.

Meta called this system “human concierges.” Behind the scenes of a service in which AI made phone calls, human staff were there. Also, this was a test carried out on only some calls, not all of them.

From this point, the story shifts from a look at a convenient future to a mystery about information management.

Meta employees worried that when human contract workers handled the calls, sensitive user information needed for the calls might be shared with contract workers at call centers without anyone meaning to.

This was not a confirmed case of a large information leak. The issue was who handled the calls, which information was passed to whom, and whether that had been explained clearly enough and supported by a sufficient system.

In addition, in one case in which Meta was asked to negotiate internet and cable bills, employees reported that a human contract worker made an inappropriate remark about race. This was not a claim about all contract workers, but one individual case reported in the news.

In response, Meta executives admitted that it had been a mistake to start the test without giving a proper explanation. They then put the human concierge feature back as it had been before. They did not shut down Muse as a whole.

Meta explained that it will continue working with businesses to improve the phone feature, and will make it available only when it is ready and proper information can be provided.

The most important point in this story is not whether AI can speak just like a human. It is whether it can clearly tell the person on the other end who is speaking.

The twist was that what AI phone calls needed was not an even smarter feature, but first of all, an honest way to say who was calling.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone calls sometimes relied on human contractors, but callers were not clearly told who was speaking.
