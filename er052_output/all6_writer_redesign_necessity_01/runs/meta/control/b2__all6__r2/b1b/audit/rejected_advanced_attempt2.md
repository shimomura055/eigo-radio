# A Human Stand-In Behind the Scenes of an AI Service

This story is about human performers behind the scenes of an AI service. In some calls through Meta’s Muse, tests were carried out in which trained contract workers, rather than AI, made the calls and finished speaking with the other person. It was like the lead actor quietly changing in the middle of a show.

What is troubling about this change, though, is not the fact that a human made the calls. It is that tests in which contract workers made the calls began without a proper explanation. If the performers change out of sight of users, at least there should be an explanation before the show starts. That is probably how people would feel.

There was also concern within Meta that personal information needed to carry out the calls, and requiring careful handling, could be unintentionally shared with call-center contractors. We should be careful not to overstate this. It does not mean that a major data leak happened. What was raised was a concern that information might unexpectedly reach other people.

If people join a call, they may sometimes be able to help with the conversation. But if someone else handles information for a task you thought you had left to AI, you would want an explanation of who makes the call and who learns what is said. Even when you ask a family member or co-worker to take a call for you, you think about what to say when the person on the other end changes. Services where AI and people work together need the same care.

A Meta executive admitted that it was a mistake to start tests in which contract workers made calls without a proper explanation. The company then put the human concierge feature on hold for now. Muse as a whole was not stopped.

What matters on the stage of an AI service is not only the lead actor’s performance. It is also making clear to the audience who is backstage and what information they can access. This change of cast reminds us of that simple point.

## In one line
Meta paused its human-concierge feature after contract workers made calls without properly informing users.
