# A Human Stand-In Appeared on an AI Phone Call

If you listen to this thinking it is a story about AI making phone calls, you may be a little surprised at the end. Behind the scenes, human contract workers had appeared in place of the AI.

Meta’s personal AI agent, Muse, was testing a feature that could call businesses and stores in the United States. On behalf of users, it could make requests such as booking a haircut, checking whether an item was in stock, and getting price estimates from service providers.

However, for some calls made through Muse, trained human contract workers made the calls instead of the AI. The workers took over requests from Muse, made the calls, and finished the conversations with the other party. In other words, a stand-in was used for calls made by the AI agent.

There were also some numbers about how well these stand-ins performed. An internal post said that in some tests handled by humans, the success rate might be between 95 and 98 percent. However, the definition of success, the number of tests, and the way it was measured have not been made public. This does not mean that humans always succeed.

The internal post also included a report from an employee that an insurance company repeatedly hung up after realizing that Muse was an AI. This is not a claim that AI calls are generally being cut off. It was an individual report about what happened when the other party recognized Muse as AI.

Here, a useful stand-in brought an unexpected problem. When human contract workers moved the calls forward, users’ sensitive information could be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center. Meta employees raised concerns about privacy.

This is not a story about a large information leak. The issue is how to tell users when a human is doing work that they thought they had assigned to AI. The user who made the request also needed to be told who was actually making the call.

The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted that starting tests in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure was a “mistake,” and temporarily rolled back the human concierge feature. Meta did not stop Muse as a whole.

Meta said it would roll out the phone feature only when it was ready and could provide proper disclosure. In the age of AI, the more useful phone calls become, the more important it may be to tell users who is behind the scenes.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone agent sometimes relied on undisclosed human contractors, raising privacy concerns about who was making calls.
