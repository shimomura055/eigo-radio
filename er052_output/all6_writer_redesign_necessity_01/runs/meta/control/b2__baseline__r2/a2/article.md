# A Human Stand-In Appeared on an AI Phone Call

You may think this story is about AI making phone calls. The ending may surprise you. Behind the scenes, human contract workers took AI’s place.

Meta’s personal AI agent, Muse, was testing a feature. The feature could call businesses and stores in the United States. It could make requests for users. It could book a haircut. It could check if an item was in stock. It could ask service providers for price estimates.

However, some calls through Muse were made by trained human contract workers. They made the calls instead of AI. They took over Muse’s requests. They finished the conversations with the other party. In other words, a stand-in made calls for the AI agent.

There were also numbers about how well these stand-ins worked. An internal post said the rate might be between 95 and 98 percent. This was for some tests handled by humans. But the meaning of “success” is not public. The number of tests is also not public. We do not know how it was measured. This does not mean humans always succeed.

The post also included an employee’s report. An insurance company hung up again and again. It did this after realizing Muse was AI. This does not claim AI calls are usually cut off. It was one report about what happened when the other party recognized Muse as AI.

Here, a helpful stand-in caused an unexpected problem. Human contract workers moved the calls forward. Users’ private information could then be shared by accident with contract workers at a call center. Meta employees worried about privacy.

This is not a story about a large information leak. The issue is telling users when a human does work. They thought they had given that work to AI. The user who made the request also needed to know who actually made the call.

The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted it was a “mistake.” The mistake was starting tests that used contract workers to make calls without clearly telling users. Meta stopped the human concierge feature for a time. It did not stop Muse as a whole.

Meta said it would offer the phone feature only when it was ready. It would do this only when it could clearly tell users who made the calls. In the age of AI, phone calls may become more useful. Then it may become more important to tell users who is behind the scenes.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone agent sometimes used human contractors without users knowing. This raised privacy concerns about who was making the calls.
