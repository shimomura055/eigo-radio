# Behind AI Phone Calls, Humans Played Supporting Roles

Human supporting actors were also part of the show in services where AI makes phone calls. What stands out in this story about Meta’s Muse is not how well the calls were made, but how the people involved were introduced and how information was handled backstage.

For some calls made through Meta’s AI service, Muse, trained contract workers actually placed the calls and completed the exchange with the person on the other end as part of a test. This did not happen on every call. But if you thought the AI was performing alone, human workers also appeared in some scenes. That was the setup.

The point is not that it is bad for people to make phone calls. In fact, having a person handle a call may help get things done. But if users are not clearly told who is making the call, the audience watches the show without knowing who is in the cast. What matters here is not the change from AI to a person itself, but whether users were given enough information about it.

Another concern came up backstage. Sensitive user information needed to complete a call might be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center. To be clear, this does not mean that a large-scale data leak took place. It is a concern that was raised about how information was shared.

A Meta vice president admitted that it was a mistake to start testing calls made by contract workers without properly explaining this. Meta has also put its human concierge feature on hold for now. This does not mean that Muse as a whole has ended.

With a service that lets AI call a store for you, people care not only about whether the task gets done, but also about who helped and what information they received. The more convenient the show, the more we want to see who is working backstage and how things are arranged. The main point this time seems to have been less about AI’s phone skills and more about how much of the service’s backstage users should be shown.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone service used human contractors for some test calls without clearly telling users.
