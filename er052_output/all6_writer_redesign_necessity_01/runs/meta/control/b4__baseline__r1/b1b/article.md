# I Asked AI to Make a Phone Call, but a Human Took the Main Role Backstage

The request was made to AI. But the person actually making the calls was human. What happened with Meta’s Muse was a test in which the main role changed hands.

Muse is an AI agent for personal use. It can call businesses and stores in the United States to make a haircut appointment, check whether something is in stock, or get a price estimate from a service provider. However, for some calls made through Muse, trained contract workers—not AI—made the calls and completed the conversations.

The system worked by passing requests from Muse to human workers. On screen, it looked like a job given to AI, but a human had stepped onto the phone stage. This raises the question of whether the person taking the role had been introduced.

Employees worried that if humans handled calls without proper disclosure, sensitive user information needed for the calls could be shared with contract workers without the users meaning for that to happen.

This is not a story about a major data leak. The key point is that if humans join a job requested from AI, users need to be told about it. You see the AI sign and walk in, but there are human workers backstage. And it is not clear how much information reaches them. Convenience suddenly turns into suspense.

In one case where a user asked Muse to negotiate internet or cable fees, employees reported that the call record contained inappropriate remarks about race by a human contract worker. This was an individual report, not a claim about all contract workers. Still, the test brought up problems not only about privacy, but also about how humans themselves handled the calls.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without proper disclosure was “a mistake.” The human concierge feature was rolled back for now. This does not mean that Muse as a whole was stopped.

Meta explained that it will release the phone feature more widely when it is ready and can provide proper disclosure. This time, the main focus was not just how smart AI is. It was also how to tell users who is on the other end of the phone. Muse’s test showed the importance of introducing the people taking part.

## In one line
Some of Meta’s AI phone assistant calls were actually made by human contractors without users being told.
