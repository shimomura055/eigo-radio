# I Asked AI to Make a Phone Call, but a Human Took the Main Role Backstage

I made the request to AI. But a human actually made the calls. Meta’s Muse was being tested. During the test, the main role changed hands.

Muse is an AI agent for personal use. It can call businesses and stores in the United States. It can book a haircut, check if something is in stock, or ask for a price estimate. However, trained contract workers made some calls through Muse. They were not made by AI. The workers also finished the conversations.

The system sent requests from Muse to human workers. On screen, it looked like a job for AI. But a human had stepped onto the phone stage. This raised a question: had the person taking the role been introduced?

Employees worried about this. If humans handled calls without clear information, contract workers could see private user information. They needed this information for the calls. Users might not have meant for workers to see it.

This is not a story about a big data leak. The important point is that humans joined a job requested from AI. When this happens, users need to know. You see the AI sign and walk in, but human workers are backstage. It is not clear how much information reaches them. Convenience suddenly becomes suspense.

In one case, a user asked Muse to discuss internet or cable fees. Employees said the call record had improper comments about race. A human contract worker had made those comments. This was one person’s report, not a claim about every contract worker. Still, the test raised privacy problems. It also raised questions about how humans handled the calls.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without clear information was “a mistake.” The human concierge feature was pulled back for now. This does not mean that Muse as a whole was stopped.

Meta explained that it will release the phone feature more widely when it is ready. It must also give users clear information. This time, the main focus was not only how smart AI is. It was also telling users who was on the other end. Muse’s test showed why the people taking part must be introduced.

## In one line
Some of Meta’s AI phone assistant calls were actually made by human contractors, without users being told.
