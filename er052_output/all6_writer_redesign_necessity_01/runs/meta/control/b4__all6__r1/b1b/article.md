# Humans Show Up in AI Calls. Behind the Scenes, Phone Calls Are Surprisingly Busy

Leaving phone calls to AI sounds smart and a little futuristic. But with Meta’s Muse, tests included having trained contract workers, not AI, make some calls and finish the conversations with the other person. In other words, human helpers appeared in the calls of an AI service. Of course, people did not handle every call.

Phone calls do not stay on the screen. The other person responds. Reports based on posts inside the company included an employee’s account that an insurance company, after realizing Muse was AI, repeatedly hung up. This is separate from the test with human staff, but on a phone call, the conversation includes not only what needs to be discussed, but also who the other person thinks they are talking to. It is a pretty challenging stage.

But the human helpers brought another concern. Employees worried that sensitive user information needed for phone calls might be shared with contract staff without anyone meaning to. The concern raised inside the company was about the way things were being done, since that could allow information to be shared.

Meta executives admitted that starting the test without proper disclosure was a “mistake” and rolled back the human concierge feature for now. What was stopped was the human concierge feature, not Muse as a whole.

Should we leave it to AI, or to people? There is more to it than that: how should users be told who is on the other end of the call? When we looked at the future of AI, the spotlight finally fell on the familiar old question: “Who is speaking?”

## In one line
Meta paused a feature of its AI phone service after human contractors handled some calls without proper disclosure.
