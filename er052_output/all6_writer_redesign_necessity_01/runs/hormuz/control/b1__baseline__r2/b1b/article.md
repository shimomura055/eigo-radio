# Even After the 20 Percent Plan Left the Stage, Oil Prices Did Not End the Show

It was as if a flashy number, 20 percent, suddenly left the center of the stage, but the audience did not go home. Instead, they kept watching the next dangerous scene.

It began on July 13. Mr. Trump posted that the United States would put a 20 percent charge on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to cover the cost of keeping the strait safe.

As a headline, it was quite shocking. But the stage set for this number was not complete. Who would collect the money? Who would pay it? How would it be calculated? What was the legal basis? No specific system like this was shown. Even though a big number had appeared, the system for charging the fee was not complete.

On the same day, Brent crude oil futures rose sharply. The market was watching more than just the fee plan. Reuters linked the rise to concerns about a US sea blockade of Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Then came the next day, July 14. Mr. Trump posted that the 20 percent fee would be replaced with trade and investment deals between the Gulf countries and the United States. He told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the strait, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

At this point, one might expect oil prices to fall sharply. But that did not happen. After the announcement, Brent briefly reduced its gains. Soon, however, it returned to a high level close to where it had been before the announcement. At the time of the report, it was up about 2.6 percent and above 85 dollars a barrel.

This was because, behind the market's stage, attacks involving the United States and Iran, the sea blockade, and concerns about tanker safety were continuing. In other words, the star of this story was not the flashy 20 percent number. Even after the fee plan was replaced, worries remained about whether ships could safely pass through the dangerous strait.

Even news about a distant strait reaches the market as a worry about the world's supply. The oil market seemed to react to political statements while quietly watching whether ships could safely move forward beyond those statements.

## In one line
Oil prices stayed high after Trump dropped the 20 percent fee plan because shipping dangers in Hormuz remained.
