# It Took the Stage, Then Left the Next Day: A Two-Act Play of the Fee Proposal and Brent Futures

The fee proposal that appeared on the stage of a policy announcement gave up the lead role the next day. But the curtain did not come down on Brent futures price movements. What stood out this time was that the statements changed, while concerns about shipping continued.

The first act was on July 13. Trump posted a proposal to charge a 20 percent fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. He said the money would pay the United States back for the cost of keeping the strait safe. But this was only a proposal. They had not actually started collecting the fee.

Then the second act began the next day. Trump posted that he was withdrawing the fee proposal and replacing it with plans for trade and investment involving Gulf countries and the United States. He said the decision came after “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders. From fees on cargo to trade and investment: the sign on the story had changed.

This is where Brent futures came in. After the announcement of the change, futures briefly gave up some of their gains. But soon they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the news report was published, they were up about 2.6 percent, above $85 a barrel. Even after the fee proposal was dropped, prices did not move in just one direction.

During that time, concerns about attacks by the United States and Iran, a blockade at sea, and tanker safety continued. The change in statements and the concerns about the strait were both on the screen at the same time.

In other words, following only the arrival and departure of the fee proposal does not tell the full story of that day’s price movements. The policy script was rewritten, but it is also important to remember that concerns about shipping continued on the same day.

## In one line
Trump withdrew the proposed Strait of Hormuz fee, but Brent futures stayed high as shipping concerns persisted.
