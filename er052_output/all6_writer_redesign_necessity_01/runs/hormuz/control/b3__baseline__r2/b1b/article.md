# The Fee Plan’s Disappearing Act: Oil Prices Did Not Disappear

If we look at this news as a short drama over two days, the story moves very quickly.

On the first day, July 13, Mr. Trump introduced a 20 percent reimbursement fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was to have the costs of keeping the strait safe and guarding it paid back to the United States.

But what matters here is that this was a proposal, not a fee that had been put into effect. No system was given for who would pay, what the fee would be based on, or how it would be collected.

Then, on the next day, July 14, the scene suddenly changed. Mr. Trump posted that the 20 percent reimbursement fee would be replaced with trade and investment deals by Gulf countries for the United States. He explained that this decision was based on “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders.

He also told reporters that no one should charge fees to ships passing through the strait, and that he did not like the idea of fees itself. The fee plan announced the day before was simply replaced by a different plan the next day.

Now, how did the oil market react? Since the fee plan had disappeared, we might expect prices to safely fall.

But that did not happen.

Just after the announcement, Brent crude oil futures briefly reduced their gains. But before long, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of the report, they were up about 2.6 percent, at more than 85 dollars a barrel. At the end of trading, they were still at 84.73 dollars a barrel, up 1.7 percent from the previous day.

The rise on the previous day was not only about the fee plan. Brent futures settled at 83.30 dollars a barrel on July 13, up 9.59 percent from the day before. Reuters connected this move to concerns about a US naval blockade against Iran and energy shipments passing through the strait.

In other words, what disappeared was the fee plan. Concerns about attacks by the United States and Iran, a naval blockade, and the safety of tankers did not disappear.

Even when the news is about a distant strait, following oil prices shows that the fee plan and concerns about supply are moving separately. It is a drama in two acts.

## In one line
The fee plan disappeared, but fears about oil supply and tanker safety kept prices high.
