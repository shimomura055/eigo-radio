# A 20 Percent Fee Plan Becomes an Investment Deal the Next Day: The Sign on the Strait Story Changes Fast

Isn't it a little interesting? It is like watching the moment a sign is changed in the middle of a news story.

On July 13, Mr. Trump posted that he would seek a 20 percent fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz to pay for keeping the strait safe. A 20 percent fee plan had suddenly appeared for the strait.

However, it was not as if a fee meter had actually started running. Who would collect it, who would pay it, and how would it be calculated? No details were given about how the fee would be collected or how the system would work. Rather than a finished fee table, it was first a broad policy proposal.

That day, Brent crude oil futures settled at $83.30 a barrel. They were up $7.29, or 9.59 percent, from the previous day. Reuters linked the rise to the US blockade of Iran at sea, which was expected to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. It cannot be said that the 20 percent plan alone caused the rise.

Then, on the 14th, the sign on the news changed again.

Mr. Trump posted that he would replace the 20 percent fee with trade and investment deals with the United States by Gulf countries. He explained that the decision was based on “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders. He also said that no one should charge ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the very idea of a fee.

The day before, it was 20 percent on cargo. The next day, it was trade and investment. The news menu was changing very quickly.

So, what happened to oil prices? After the announcement of the withdrawal and replacement, Brent futures briefly gave up some of their gains. But soon they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of the report, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel.

What we can confirm here is that prices did not fall across the board after the plan was withdrawn. Even then, concerns about attacks by the US and Iran, a sea blockade, and the safety of tankers continued. We cannot say that the fee plan had no effect, nor can we say that it alone explains the price movement.

The sign changed. But many factors remained on the stage. That is the most interesting part of this news.

## In one line
A 20 percent fee on Hormuz shipping was replaced the next day with trade and investment deals.
