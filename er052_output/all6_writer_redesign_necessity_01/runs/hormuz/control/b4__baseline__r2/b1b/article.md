# The 20 Percent Plan Turned into an Investment Proposal the Next Day

This news was like a short play set in the crude oil market. The main player changed twice, but one thing stayed onstage until the end: concern about the Strait of Hormuz.

The first act was on July 13. Trump posted that the United States would ask for a 20 percent reimbursement from all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, as payment for the cost of keeping it safe. The United States would protect the strait and get that cost back. That was the proposal.

But the important point here is that the United States had not actually started collecting the money. It was not clear who would pay, what the amount would be based on, or how it would be collected. A flashy number had appeared, but how the plan would work was still behind the curtain.

On the same day, Brent crude oil futures rose 9.59 percent from the previous day and settled at $83.30 a barrel. Reports linked this rise to concerns about a US naval blockade against Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. We cannot say that the 20 percent plan alone caused the rise.

Then came the second act. On the next day, July 14, the main player suddenly changed. Trump posted that the 20 percent reimbursement fee would be replaced by deals involving Gulf countries' trade with and investment in the United States. He explained that this decision was based on “very productive discussions” with Middle Eastern leaders.

A proposal that had asked cargo to bear a cost until the day before became a story about investment and trade the next day. The script for the policy was rewritten quite boldly.

Did the crude oil market then lower the curtain in relief? No. After the announcement, the rise in Brent futures narrowed for a while. But before long, they returned to nearly the high level seen before the announcement. At the time of publication, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel.

The market's answer in the third act was quite silent. Even after the fee plan was replaced, concerns about attacks by the United States and Iran, a naval blockade, and the safety of tankers remained.

In other words, what the market was watching was not where the bill would go, but whether transport through the strait was truly safe. Even if the main policy player changes, crude oil prices will not easily settle down as long as anxiety at sea remains. News from a faraway strait gave its answer through market prices.

## In one line
Trump replaced a 20 percent fee proposal with investment deals, but oil prices stayed high amid shipping fears.
