# Even After the 20 Percent Fee Plan Disappeared, Anxiety in the Oil Market Did Not

If we look at the oil market as one stage, the latest developments were quite busy.

The first thing to appear on the stage was a 20 percent reimbursement fee. Trump said he would ask for a fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, saying it would repay the costs the United States spends on security.

However, this fee has not actually been collected yet. No clear system had been shown for who would pay, who would collect it, or how the amount would be calculated. A large sign had been put up, but there was still no store or cash register. That was the situation.

Then, the very next day, the scene suddenly changed. Trump posted that he was withdrawing the 20 percent fee plan and replacing it with trade and investment deals between the Gulf countries and the United States.

At that point, people might have expected the market to feel relieved and quickly calm down. But that did not happen.

After the announcement that the plan was withdrawn and replaced, Brent crude futures at one point cut their gains. But soon after, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of publication, they were up about 2.6 percent and had passed 85 dollars a barrel.

It was as if the audience kept murmuring even after the fee plan had left the stage.

That was because the market was watching more than just the fee plan. Concerns remained about attacks between the United States and Iran, a sea blockade, and the safety of tankers.

Even if the fee plan disappeared, would ships be able to move safely? Would concerns about the supply of energy shipments become weaker? Unless those points became clear, oil prices would not easily settle down.

The main focus this time was not the 20 percent figure itself, but the uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz. When policy statements change, prices react once. But if worries about a sea blockade and tanker safety remain, prices return to a high level.

Although the news was from a distant sea, the oil market’s reaction seemed to be moving in fast-forward. The fee plan appeared and left, but anxiety alone remained on the stage. This time’s price movement showed such an unsettled play.

## In one line
Oil prices remained high after the fee plan was withdrawn because uncertainty over Hormuz security and shipments persisted.
