# 20 Percent Takes the Stage, Then Investment Deals Take the Lead the Next Day

If you look at this news as a play, the story moves very quickly.

The curtain rose on July 13. In a post, Mr. Trump said he would impose a 20 percent charge on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. He explained that this would recover the money the United States spends to keep the strait safe.

A large number suddenly appeared at center stage. But the key details were not clear. Who would collect the money? Who would pay it? On what basis would the 20 percent be calculated? Would there be exceptions? What was the legal basis? No details about how this system would work were given.

In other words, this was not a charge that had actually started. Only the rate of 20 percent had appeared first. It was a proposal that had not yet taken concrete form. The number was striking, but no instructions had been handed out yet. That was the situation.

Here, let us look at another stage: oil prices. On July 13, Brent crude futures rose $7.29 from the previous day and settled at $83.30 a barrel.

However, Reuters linked this rise to concerns about a US naval blockade of Iran that was expected to begin the next day, as well as concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. It was not confirmed that the 20 percent plan alone had caused the rise.

Then, on July 14, the script was suddenly rewritten. Mr. Trump announced that he would replace the 20 percent reimbursement fee with trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the strait, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

The day before, he had asked for a fee. The next day, he replaced it with investment deals. He even distanced himself from the idea of a fee. The lead had suddenly changed from 20 percent to investment deals.

Even so, oil prices did not fall sharply. After the announcement, they briefly gave up some of their gains, but soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, the price was about 2.6 percent higher and above $85 a barrel.

Attacks by the United States and Iran, the naval blockade, and concerns about tanker safety continued. Even though the fee proposal’s role as a number was over, the tense situation around the Strait of Hormuz had not yet come to an end.

## In one line
Trump replaced his proposed 20 percent Strait of Hormuz fee with trade and investment deals the next day.
