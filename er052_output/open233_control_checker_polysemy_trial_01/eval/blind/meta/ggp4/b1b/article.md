# An Unexpected Stand-In for AI Phone Calls: Human Concierges Appear

Imagine this. You want to book a haircut at a store in the US. You want to ask about something in stock. You want to get a price estimate from a business. So you ask Muse to make the call. Up to this point, it sounds like a useful feature from the future.

But in some of the test calls, trained human contract workers—not AI—were making the calls. They took over the work from Muse and finished the conversations with the people on the other end.

They were called “human concierges.” In other words, an unexpected human cast appeared in the AI phone feature. And this did not happen on every call. It was a system tested on only some calls.

At this point, the story changes from an introduction to a useful AI feature into a mystery behind the scenes. The problem was not only that humans made the calls. When sensitive user information was needed to move a call forward, that information might be shared by accident with contract workers at a call center.

Meta employees raised privacy concerns inside the company. In addition, an employee who asked Muse to negotiate internet and cable fees reported that the call record contained inappropriate remarks about race by a human contract worker. This was an individual report described in news reports, not a claim about all contract workers.

The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted that starting tests in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure was a “mistake.” Meta then put the human concierge feature back the way it was, for the time being.

Meta says it will continue improving the phone feature and will make it available more widely only when it is ready and can provide proper disclosure.

What is interesting about this story is not that AI worked just like a human. It is that a human appeared halfway through a performance that people thought was being done by AI. As more reservations and inquiries are left to apps, what matters is not only how smart AI is. We also need to know who is doing the work and what information they can see. Only when we know that much can we feel safe using convenient services.

## In one line
Some of Meta’s AI phone calls were actually handled by human contractors, raising privacy concerns.
