# Some of the AI Phone Operators Were Human

Meta’s AI agent “Muse” is a service that lets you tell it to call businesses and stores in the United States. You can ask it to make a haircut appointment, check whether something is in stock, or ask a company for an estimate. AI even handles the phone call itself. It feels very much like the near future.

But behind the scenes, there was an unexpected role. In some of the calls made through Muse, the calls were actually being made not by AI, but by trained contract workers. They took over requests from Muse and finished the conversations with the other people. Meta called this system “human concierges” and was testing it.

At this point, the story shifts from an introduction to useful AI into a slightly unusual suspense story. The issue was not simply that humans were helping. The test had begun without people being properly told about it.

In some cases, sensitive information about the user is needed to continue the call. That information could be shared without the user knowing with contract workers at a call center. Meta employees raised privacy concerns inside the company. It was not confirmed that a major information leak had happened. Even so, while a user had asked AI to handle a task, another human might be handling that user’s information. For users, that is a major thing happening behind the scenes.

There was another drama on the other side of the call. According to one employee’s report, an insurance company repeatedly hung up when it realized that Muse’s calls were being made by AI. This is one individual report, but it means there were cases where a conversation ended the moment people learned it was AI.

A vice president in Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted that starting a test in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure was a “mistake.” Meta then put the human concierge feature back the way it was before, for the time being. It did not stop the phone feature as a whole.

Meta says it will make the phone feature available only when it is ready and can clearly tell people about it. What this incident showed was not simply that humans were behind the AI, but whether those humans could be properly introduced. Future phone operators need more than smooth replies. First, they must make clear who is speaking. If that is skipped, an AI phone call becomes a mystery in an instant.

## In one line
Some calls handled by Meta’s AI were actually made by human contractors without users being told.
