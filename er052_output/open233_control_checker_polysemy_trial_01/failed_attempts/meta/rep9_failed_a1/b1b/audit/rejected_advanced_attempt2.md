# Behind AI Phone Calls, There Were Human Concierges: Why Meta Pulled the Feature Back

This is a story about an unexpected helper appearing in a useful feature that feels like the near future.

The setting is Muse, Meta’s AI agent. Muse can call businesses and stores in the United States to book a haircut, check whether something is in stock, or ask service providers for price estimates.

You ask the AI on your screen, and the AI makes the call for you. When you hear that, it sounds very futuristic, doesn’t it? But in some of those calls, something unexpected happened.

The calls were actually made not by AI, but by trained human contract workers. The workers handled the conversation through to the end. Meta called this test “human concierges” and “phone calls by humans.”

In other words, when you asked the AI to make a call, part of the task was handed over to a human helper. It does sound useful. A human might be better able to handle the detailed conversations that AI has trouble with.

But this is where the privacy problem appeared.

Meta employees raised concerns inside the company that, when contract workers handled the calls, users’ sensitive information could be unintentionally shared with call center workers.

It was not reported that a large-scale information leak had actually happened. Even so, users may wonder, “Who is making my call?” and “Who knows my information?” If a human appears without your knowing in a task you thought you had left to AI, it is natural to want to be told first.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without proper disclosure was a mistake. Meta then rolled back the human concierge feature—that is, it pulled back that feature for the time being. What was pulled back was this feature; this does not mean that Muse as a whole was stopped.

Meta says it will continue making improvements with businesses and stores, and will release the feature only when it is ready and can be properly explained.

The true main actors in AI phone calls were not only AI. This incident showed us what was happening behind the scenes. The more useful a feature is, the more important it may be to explain who is doing the work.

## In one line
Meta pulled back its AI calling feature after human contractors handled some calls without users being told.
