# An Unexpected Stand-In for AI Phone Calls: Human Concierges Appear

Imagine this. You want to book a haircut at a store in the US. You want to ask if something is in stock. You want a price estimate from a business. So you ask Muse to make the call. Until now, this sounds like a useful feature from the future.

However, trained human contract workers made some of the test calls. AI did not make those calls. They took over Muse’s work. Then they finished talking with the people who answered.

These workers were called “human concierges.” In other words, an unexpected human cast appeared in the AI phone feature. This did not happen on every call. The system was tested on only some calls.

The story now moves from a useful AI feature to a mystery. It becomes a mystery about what happened behind the scenes. The problem was not only that humans made the calls. Sometimes, a call needed sensitive user information to continue. That information might be shared by accident with contract workers at a call center.

Meta employees raised privacy concerns inside the company. Also, one employee asked Muse to negotiate internet and cable fees. The employee reported that the call record had inappropriate comments about race. A human contract worker had made the comments. This was one person’s report, as described in news reports. It was not a claim about all contract workers.

The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted that starting these tests was a “mistake.” The tests used contract workers, but people were not properly told. Meta then put the human concierge feature back the way it was. It did this for the time being.

Meta says it will keep improving the phone feature. It will make it available to more people only when it is ready. It will do this only with proper disclosure.

The interesting part is not that AI worked like a human. It is that a human appeared halfway through a performance. People thought AI was doing the whole performance. As more reservations and questions go to apps, AI’s intelligence is not all that matters. We also need to know who does the work. We need to know what information they can see. Only then can we feel safe using convenient services.

## In one line
Human contractors handled some of Meta’s AI phone calls, raising privacy concerns.
