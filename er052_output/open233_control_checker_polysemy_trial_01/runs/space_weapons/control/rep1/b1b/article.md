# To Solve the Mystery of Space Weapons, Start with Their “Address,” Not Their “Capabilities”

When we hear that “weapons have been deployed in space,” it feels as if a huge new weapon has appeared. But the main point of this news is not how flashy the weapon is. The story has four different faces that look much like a story about military activity in space. The key is to tell them apart by their “addresses.”

On September 14, U.S. Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink said that space-control weapons had been deployed in orbit to protect joint forces from the actions of hostile opponents. An official U.S. government article recorded this as the first statement acknowledging that the Space Force had deployed weapons into space.

However, we should not start imagining a blueprint right away. No specific system name or attack ability has been confirmed. What we know is that the United States has officially acknowledged deploying weapons in orbit. The details of what these devices are have not been made public.

First, a weapon that can attack satellites and a weapon placed in space are not the same thing. In 2021, Russia destroyed a satellite with an anti-satellite missile that rose directly from the ground. This test created more than 1,500 pieces of space debris that could be tracked. It was a shocking event, but the weapon’s address was on the ground. That is a different matter from keeping a weapon in orbit.

Next, we come across the term “counterspace.” This term does not refer only to attack weapons. According to the U.S. Space Force, it is a general term for attacks and defenses carried out in orbit, through communication links, and at ground facilities. It includes protecting satellite communications, watching what is happening in space, and tracking missiles. The fact that surveillance or protection is taking place does not, by itself, confirm that an attack weapon has been placed in orbit.

We should also check the rules of the Outer Space Treaty here. What the treaty clearly bans includes placing nuclear weapons and other weapons of mass destruction in orbit around Earth or in outer space. It does not ban all space weapons. Based on this statement alone, we cannot decide that the treaty has been violated.

If we rush after flashy words in space news, we can easily get lost. Is it a test launched from the ground, an activity that includes attacks and defenses, a surveillance task, or a deployment into orbit? First, check the address, and the true nature of the story becomes clear. Above the satellites that support location information and communications, the address of weapons is becoming a new point of dispute.

## In one line
To understand reports of space weapons, first ask where they are located, because their capabilities remain unclear.
