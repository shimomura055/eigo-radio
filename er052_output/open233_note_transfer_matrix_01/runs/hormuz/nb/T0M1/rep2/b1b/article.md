# The 20% That Disappeared, but Not the Fear Over the Strait

This is a short suspense story about the crude oil market. The setting is the Strait of Hormuz. A proposal for a 20% reimbursement fee appeared. Then, the next day, the proposal changed. But crude oil prices did not settle down easily.

It began on July 13. Trump posted that the United States would seek a 20% reimbursement on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to pay for the cost of U.S. efforts to ensure safety.

However, this 20% was not already part of a fee system. No details were given about who would pay, what the amount would be based on, or how it would be collected. What appeared first was only a proposal.

That day, Brent futures jumped sharply. They settled at $83.30 a barrel, up $7.29, or 9.59%, from the previous day. Reuters linked this rise to the U.S. blockade at sea against Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. It would be wrong to say that the 20% proposal alone caused the rise.

Then, on July 14, the story took a sharp turn. Trump posted that he would replace the 20% reimbursement fee with trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the strait, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

So, the fee proposal was gone. Would this finally calm the oil market at once? But that was not what happened. Brent futures briefly reduced their gains. Soon, however, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, they were up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel. The final settlement price was also $84.73, up 1.7% from the previous day.

It was as if only the 20% proposal had disappeared backstage, while fear over the strait remained the main character. The blockade at sea, attacks by the United States and Iran, and concerns about tanker safety continued.

The key point this time is not only the appearance and withdrawal of the fee proposal. One policy proposal disappeared. But concerns about the supply of energy through the Strait of Hormuz did not disappear. That is why the market story did not end with the withdrawal of the fee proposal.

## In one line
The proposed 20% fee disappeared, but fears about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz kept oil prices high.
