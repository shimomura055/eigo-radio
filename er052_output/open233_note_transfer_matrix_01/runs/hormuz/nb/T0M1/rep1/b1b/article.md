# The 20 Percent Plan Disappeared, but Crude Oil Prices Did Not

There was a short, two-day drama in the crude oil market.

The first act was on July 13. Mr. Trump posted that he was seeking a 20 percent payment on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to cover the costs the United States would spend on security.

A large figure had suddenly appeared. However, this did not mean that the money had actually started being collected. Who would pay? What would the amount be based on? How would it be collected? The plan for the system had not yet been shown.

Even so, Brent crude oil futures moved sharply that day. The settlement price was $83.30 a barrel. It was $7.29 higher than the day before, a rise of 9.59 percent.

This price movement was linked to the US sea blockade of Iran, which was said to start the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. The strait is an important route for ships carrying crude oil. The stage for the news was, quite literally, at sea.

Then came the second act. On July 14, Mr. Trump changed his policy.

He posted that the 20 percent fee would be replaced with trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. The plan that had appeared the day before was moved into a different form the next day. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships a fee for passing through the strait, and that he did not like the idea of a fee.

At this point, we might have thought that the price would leave the stage too. But it did not.

After the announcement, Brent crude oil futures narrowed their gains for a while. But soon after, they recovered to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel. The final settlement price was also higher than the day before, at $84.73.

What was withdrawn was only the 20 percent fee plan. At the same time, the sea blockade, attacks between the United States and Iran, and concerns about tanker safety continued.

In other words, the interesting point this time was that pressing the button to withdraw the statement did not also press the reset button for crude oil prices. The news disappeared, but the tension at sea remained on the screen.

## In one line
The 20 percent fee plan disappeared, but ongoing tensions at sea kept crude oil prices elevated.
