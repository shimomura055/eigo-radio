# The 20 Percent That Was Canceled, and the Strait Anxiety That Wasn't

In the oil market, there was a small drama in which the main actor changed for two days in a row.

The first act was on July 13. What Mr. Trump suddenly put forward was a plan to make all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz pay a 20 percent reimbursement fee. The idea was to have the cargo side cover the costs that the United States spends to keep the strait safe.

But there was something a little strange about this plan. A large number was shown, but it had not been decided who would pay, what the fee would be based on, or how it would be collected. The price tag was there, but there was no cash register and no way to pay yet. That was the feeling.

On the same day, Brent crude oil futures rose by $7.29, or 9.59 percent, from the previous day, ending at $83.30 a barrel. Reuters linked this rise to the U.S. sea blockade against Iran, scheduled to begin the next day, and concerns about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Here, we should not look only at the eye-catching number of 20 percent. We cannot say that the rise in oil prices on July 13 was only a reaction to that plan. At the same time, the market was also moved by worries that the strait might become unstable and that energy supplies might be disrupted.

The second act was the next day, July 14. Mr. Trump posted that he was withdrawing the 20 percent reimbursement fee plan and replacing it with trade and investment deals involving the Gulf countries and the United States. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the strait, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

It might have seemed that the matter was settled, but the oil market did not end the show so easily. After the announcement, Brent briefly gave back some of its gains. However, it soon returned to a high level close to where it had been before the announcement. When the article came out, it was up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel. The settlement price for the day was also $84.73, up 1.7 percent from the previous day.

The sea blockade, attacks by the United States and Iran, and concerns about tanker safety continued. The fee plan had a cancel button, but the anxiety over the strait did not. It seemed that the market was looking not at one 20 percent price tag, but at a much bigger question: Could energy pass safely through the strait?

## In one line
Although Trump canceled the proposed 20 percent fee, worries about safe Strait of Hormuz shipments kept oil prices high.
