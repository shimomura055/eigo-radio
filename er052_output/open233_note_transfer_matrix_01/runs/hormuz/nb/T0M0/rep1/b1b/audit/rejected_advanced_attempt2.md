# The 20 Percent Fee Proposal Disappeared the Next Day. Even So, Oil Prices Did Not Fall

News about the Strait of Hormuz is moving too quickly. On July 13, a major fee proposal suddenly appeared, and it was withdrawn the next day, July 14. Even so, oil prices did not respond by saying, “Then everything is safe.”

First, on July 13, Mr. Trump said that the United States would seek a 20 percent reimbursement charge on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, as payment for the cost of ensuring security.

However, we need to be careful here. This was not a fee that had actually started. No details were given about who would collect it, how it would be calculated, or what its legal basis would be. In other words, it was a powerful preview.

That day, Brent crude futures settled at $83.30 a barrel. This was an increase of $7.29, or 9.59 percent, from the previous day. Reuters linked this rise to concerns about the US maritime blockade against Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Then came the next day, the second act. Mr. Trump posted that he was withdrawing the 20 percent reimbursement fee proposal and replacing it with trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

Normally, oil prices would also seem likely to fall sharply at this point. In fact, Brent futures briefly reduced their gains after the withdrawal was announced. But before long, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. When the article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel. The settlement price at the end of trading was also $84.73, up 1.7 percent from the previous day.

This does not mean that the withdrawal pushed up oil prices. On the same day, the maritime blockade, attacks between the United States and Iran, and concerns about tanker safety continued. Uncertainty about supplies through the Strait of Hormuz also remained.

The 20 percent proposal disappeared from the stage the next day. Even so, the main character—the tensions surrounding the strait—had not yet left the stage.

## In one line
Oil prices stayed high after the proposed 20 percent Strait of Hormuz fee was withdrawn because tensions there continued.
