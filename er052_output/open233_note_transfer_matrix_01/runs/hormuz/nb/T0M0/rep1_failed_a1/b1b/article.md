# Appeared, Disappeared, Returned: One Day of the Strait of Hormuz and Oil Prices

If you follow only the news as it develops, it looks like a short drama. On the first day, a major fee plan appears, and the next day it is replaced by another plan. At the same time, the graph for Brent crude oil futures did not move in a straight line with each change in the news.

First, on July 13, Mr. Trump posted that the United States would seek 20 percent reimbursement from all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, as payment for the cost of ensuring security.

The number 20 percent stood out. But at that point, no details had been given about who would pay or how the money would be collected. It was as if a proposal with a large number had first appeared on the news screens.

On the same day, Brent futures settled at 83 dollars and 30 cents per barrel. That was 7 dollars and 29 cents higher than the day before, a rise of 9.59 percent. Reuters linked the rise to concern about a US sea blockade against Iran, said to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Then came July 14, the next day. The story quickly moved to its next scene. Mr. Trump posted that the 20 percent reimbursement fee would be replaced with deals involving trade with the United States and investment in the United States by Gulf countries. The fee plan was withdrawn.

He also told reporters that no one should charge fees to ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the idea of fees itself. The plan that appeared the day before had taken a different form by the next day.

So how did the oil price graph move? After the withdrawal was announced, Brent futures temporarily gave up some of their gains. But before long, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. When the article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent and above 85 dollars per barrel.

During the same period, attacks between the United States and Iran, the sea blockade, and concerns about tanker safety continued. The fee plan left the stage, but that did not mean that all the events around the strait disappeared at the same time.

This is the key point. The announcement was replaced the next day. Even so, the price first fell back and then returned to a high level. A line in the news may be rewritten quickly, but the events unfolding at the same time cannot be rewritten so quickly.

## In one line
Although the proposed Strait of Hormuz fee was withdrawn, oil prices stayed high as tensions continued.
