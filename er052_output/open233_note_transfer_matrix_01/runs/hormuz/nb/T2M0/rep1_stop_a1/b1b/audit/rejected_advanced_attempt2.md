# The 20 Percent Plan Left the Stage in One Day, but the Curtain Did Not Fall on the Crude Oil Market

There was a story in the crude oil market that unfolded like a short play lasting only two days. On the first day, a plan appeared to seek 20 percent reimbursement for cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The next day, the plan left the stage. But crude oil prices did not disappear from the stage with it.

The first act was July 13. Trump posted that the United States would seek 20 percent reimbursement for all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to cover the costs the United States would use to provide security.

However, this did not mean that collection of the fee had begun. Who would pay? What would be the basis for the calculation? How would it be collected? No system for these details had been shown. Although the plan made a dramatic entrance, the detailed script was not yet there.

On the same day, Brent crude oil futures moved sharply. They settled at $83.30 per barrel, up $7.29 from the previous day. The gain was 9.59 percent.

Here, it would be a mistake to see the fee plan as the only main character. Reuters linked the rise to a U.S. sea blockade of Iran that was expected to begin the next day, as well as concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. From the start, there was more than one factor moving prices.

The second act came the next day, July 14. Trump posted that he would withdraw the 20 percent reimbursement fee and replace it with deals involving trade with and investment in the United States by Gulf states. The fee plan was replaced by a different story about a day after it appeared.

If prices had returned to where they were, the plot would have been easy to understand. In fact, Brent briefly gave up some of its gains after the announcement. But before long, it returned to a high level close to where it had been before the announcement. When the article was published, it was up about 2.6 percent, at more than $85 per barrel.

The sea blockade, attacks by the United States and Iran, and concerns about tanker safety continued. Even when events happen in a faraway strait, concerns about energy shipments show up in international crude oil prices.

The key point this time was not that the fee plan disappeared in one day. It was that tension around the strait remained behind the plan even after it disappeared. The fee plan left the stage, but the stage itself had not ended.

## In one line
Trump’s proposed Hormuz fee vanished within a day, but tensions there continued to support high crude oil prices.
