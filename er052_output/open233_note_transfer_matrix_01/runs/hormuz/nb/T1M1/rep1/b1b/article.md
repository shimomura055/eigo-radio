# The 20 Percent That Vanished in One Day, and the Oil Worry That Remained

A small twist took place in the crude oil market.

On July 13, Mr. Trump posted that the United States would seek a 20 percent payment for all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to cover the cost of keeping it safe.

It was suddenly a large fee plan. But he did not say who would pay, what the fee would be based on, or how it would be collected. The sign for the fee was large, but the place for the cash register had not yet been decided. That was the situation.

Brent crude futures rose $7.29 from the previous day and settled at $83.30 a barrel.

The important point here is not to explain the rise only through the 20 percent plan. Reuters linked the rise to the US sea blockade of Iran, which was said to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

In other words, the market had two pieces of news that day. One was the eye-catching fee plan. The other was a much bigger worry about the safety of ships and energy shipments.

Then, on July 14, the second act began. Mr. Trump posted that he was withdrawing the 20 percent fee plan and replacing it with trade and investment deals between the Gulf countries and the United States. The fee plan that appeared the day before had been replaced by a different story in just one day.

Normally, if the fee plan disappeared, crude oil prices would seem likely to fall sharply. In fact, after the withdrawal was announced, Brent briefly reduced its gains.

But the market did not simply end there. Before long, it returned to a high level close to where it had been before the announcement. At the time of the report, it was up about 2.6 percent and had risen above $85 a barrel. The settlement price that day also ended higher, at $84.73 a barrel.

The market seemed to be watching more than whether the fee plan would survive. The US sea blockade, attacks between the United States and Iran, and concerns about tanker safety were still continuing.

The fee plan disappeared, but concerns about supplies through the Strait did not. What made this episode worth watching was that one policy statement was replaced in a single day. And the crude oil market continued to focus on the bigger question of whether ships could pass safely, rather than on that loud statement.

## In one line
The fee plan vanished in a day, but worries about safe oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz remained.
