# The Fee Plan That Left the Stage in One Day, and the Crude Oil Worry That Stayed

Just as a plan to charge a fee appeared, it was replaced the next day by a different plan. For policy news, the story moved too fast. But the movement in crude oil prices had an extra twist.

On July 13, Trump posted that for all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, he would seek a 20 percent reimbursement for the cost of security provided by the United States.

However, only the rate was shown. The details of the system—who would pay, what the amount would be based on, and how the money would be collected—were not given. A large number had been announced, but the way to collect the money was still hidden in fog.

On the same July 13, Brent crude oil futures rose by $7.29, or 9.59 percent, and settled at $83.30 a barrel.

Here, it is important not to get the order of events wrong. The price rise came on the 13th, and the fee plan was withdrawn the next day, on the 14th. Reuters linked the rise to the US naval blockade of Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. The price movement on the 13th cannot be explained by the 20 percent plan alone.

Then, on July 14, the fee plan left the stage. Trump posted that the 20 percent US reimbursement fee would be replaced by deals involving trade with the United States and investment in the United States by Gulf countries. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

You might think crude oil would quickly settle down too, but it did not. After the announcement of the withdrawal and replacement, Brent futures temporarily gave up some of their gains. But soon they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel. The daily settlement price afterward was also higher than the previous day, at $84.73 a barrel.

At that point, concerns about a naval blockade, attacks between the United States and Iran, and the safety of tankers were still continuing. The fee plan changed its form in one day, but worries about supply stayed on the stage. Only the policy label changed rapidly, while crude oil prices continued to reflect a different tension. Though it was a short news story, it was a two-part drama.

## In one line
The fee plan vanished overnight, but concerns about oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz kept prices high.
