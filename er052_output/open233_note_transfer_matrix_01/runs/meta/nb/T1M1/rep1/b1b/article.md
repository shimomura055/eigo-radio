# Hello, Is This AI? No, Part of It Was Human

You thought there was an AI on the other end of the phone, but it was actually a human. A slightly strange experiment like this was being carried out.

The setting was Muse, Meta’s AI agent. Muse can call stores and companies to do things such as make a haircut appointment or check whether an item is in stock. But in some of those calls, trained human contract workers—not AI—spoke with the other person and handled the conversation to the end.

The name of this system was “human concierge.” When a user asked Muse to do something, the call was passed to human staff behind the scenes. It was like having a robot carry your bags, only to have a human run up and deliver them on the last flight of stairs.

What is more, some tests in which humans handled the calls showed that the success rate might be between 95 and 98 percent. On the other hand, there were also reports that people who realized Muse was an AI repeatedly hung up. In the world of phone calls, being an AI did not always seem to be an advantage.

But this was not simply a useful trick.

Depending on the call, the user’s situation or sensitive information may be needed. When human contract workers respond, that information may be shared with them without the user intending it. Meta employees raised privacy concerns.

It was not confirmed that a large-scale information leak had occurred. The problem was that humans were speaking as part of a feature given to AI, without clearly telling people who was making the call. It may be an interesting surprise, but when personal information is involved, you cannot wait until later to tell people the truth.

A Meta executive admitted that it was a mistake to start the tests without proper disclosure. For now, the feature that was rolled back was the human concierge feature, in which humans handled some calls. They did not shut down all of Muse or all of its phone features.

Meta explained that it would publicly roll out the phone feature only when it was ready and could provide proper disclosure. In the age of AI, when we say “hello” on the phone, we want to know in advance not only whether our business will be taken care of, but also who is on the other end.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone service secretly used human workers for some calls, raising privacy concerns.
