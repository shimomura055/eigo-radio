# I Asked AI to Make a Phone Call, but There Was a Human on the Other End

We are entering an age when we can leave bothersome phone calls to AI: making reservations, asking about stock, and getting estimates from businesses. Meta’s AI agent, Muse, can call companies and stores in the United States to make haircut appointments, check stock, and get estimates from service providers.

However, this future had a somewhat unexpected cast member. Meta had been testing a system in which trained human contract workers, rather than AI, made some calls through Muse and completed the conversations.

A human had appeared on the stage of AI phone calls. This feature was called “human concierge.”

At this point, the story changes from one about a convenient near future to one about a privacy mystery. To continue a call, personal information that users do not want other people to know may sometimes be needed. If a human handles the call, that information could be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center. Meta employees raised this as a problem.

There was another unusual scene in internal posts. According to an employee’s report, an insurance company repeatedly hung up after realizing that the call from Muse was from AI. As soon as it realized the call was from AI, the conversation was forcibly ended. This is one case in which the other side’s response changed after recognizing that the caller was AI. It does not mean that every call from AI will be cut off.

This is not a claim that a large-scale information leak occurred. What was confirmed was a concern that information might be shared unintentionally with human contract workers.

Even so, a call you thought you had left to AI was actually handled by a human. How much information was shared with that person, and how was this set up? For users, this is a behind-the-scenes matter of real concern.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test in which contract workers made the calls without proper disclosure was a “mistake.” Meta then rolled back the human concierge feature for the time being. What was stopped was this test feature, not Muse’s phone feature as a whole.

Meta says it will make the feature available to the public only when it is ready and can properly explain who is making the calls. The real highlight of AI phone calls may not be the ability to speak smoothly. In the end, what is needed is to clearly reveal “who is handling your call.”

## In one line
Meta paused its AI calling test after human contractors handled some calls without proper disclosure.
