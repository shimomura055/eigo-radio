# When AI Was Put in Charge of Phone Calls, Humans Made Some of Them

This is a story about a “change of the main player” in the AI era, but the change looked a little different.

Meta’s AI agent for individual users, Muse, can call companies and stores in the United States to make haircut appointments, check whether items are in stock, and ask service providers for price estimates.

However, in some of the phone calls tested by Meta, it was not AI that made the call and completed the conversation. Trained human contract workers did. Inside the company, they were called “human concierges.”

It is important not to get the story of these calls wrong. What has been confirmed is that human staff handled some calls made through Muse. It has not been confirmed that AI first made the call and then handed it over to a human partway through. The point is that tests were carried out in which human staff made the calls and completed the conversations.

The drama came not from the fact that humans made the calls. It came from starting the tests without properly telling people about it.

Sensitive information from the user may be needed to complete a call. If human contract workers handle the call, that information might be shared with them by accident. From the user’s point of view, the feature was supposed to be handled by AI, but it was hard to see who was making the call and who was handling the information. The problem was not that humans appeared. The problem was that the people involved were not explained clearly enough.

If there are humans behind the scenes, their presence is also part of the service. Who will make the call? What information will be needed? Users can make a decision only when these things are explained first. If a human handles the call without that explanation, even a convenient feature can make people feel uneasy first.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the tests without proper disclosure was a mistake. For now, the human concierge feature will be returned to its original state. In the future, Meta plans to make it available only when it is ready and can provide proper disclosure.

Humans appearing in AI phone calls were not the main point this time. The main point was the plain but important guidance to tell people first who was speaking.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone service used human callers in some tests without clearly telling users first.
