# Behind the Scenes of AI Phone Calls, Human Helpers Step In

AI will make a phone call for you. If you hear that, you would think AI was on the other end. But this time, another actor appeared from the side of the stage. They were trained human contract workers.

Meta was testing whether human contract workers could complete calls with the other person on some calls made through Muse. A call that people thought would be handled by AI alone now had human helpers join in. It was a team effort by AI and humans.

So far, this may look like a useful system. AI moves the call forward, while a human helps when needed. But the performance began without showing what was happening behind the scenes. That became a major problem.

The test had started without clearly telling people that human contract workers would handle calls. From a user's point of view, when they left a call to AI, they could not know when a human would take over the conversation.

What is more, completing a call may require sensitive information about the user. That information could have been shared with contract workers even though the user did not intend for that to happen.

In short, the human helpers brought in to add convenience also brought another problem: privacy. This is not a story about AI driving humans out. The problem was that humans were still very much there, but their presence had not been made clear enough.

When employees raised concerns, a Meta executive admitted that starting the test without properly telling people had been a mistake. The test was not simply continued, and the feature was rolled back for now. Simply put, it was returned to its previous state.

From now on, Meta plans to release the phone feature only when it is ready to clearly explain that contract workers will be involved.

In AI services, if you look only at the AI in front of you, you may understand only half the story. Who makes the calls behind the scenes and what information they can access are also important roles in the service. It is not enough for AI to give smart replies. Users must first be told who is on the other end of the call. This case shows that without that explanation, a helper can cause a problem big enough to take center stage.

## In one line
Meta paused its AI phone-call test after undisclosed human contractors joined calls and potentially accessed users’ sensitive information.
