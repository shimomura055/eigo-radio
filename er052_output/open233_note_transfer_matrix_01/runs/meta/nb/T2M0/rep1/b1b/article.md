# The Twist in AI Phone Calls: There Was a Human on the Other End

AI makes the phone calls. When you hear that, it sounds like a service from the future.

Meta’s consumer AI agent, Muse, calls stores and companies in the United States. It can make haircut appointments, check whether items are in stock, and get price estimates from service providers. Users do not have to make the calls themselves. They can leave the difficult parts of phone calls to AI.

But this phone service took an unexpected turn.

For part of a call, the person who handled the conversation with the other side to the end was not AI. It was a trained contract worker. Meta was testing this setup as a “human concierge” service.

It was supposed to be a service in which AI made the calls, but a human appeared in the middle of the show. It was as if a human actor suddenly came out from the side of the stage while you were watching a play with robots.

There was another clue in internal posts. One employee reported that someone had hung up after realizing that Muse was AI. Sometimes, the conversation ended as soon as the other person found out it was AI. At least, such cases had been reported.

But adding humans does not solve everything. If user information is needed to continue a call, sensitive information could be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center. Employees pointed to this as a privacy concern.

This is not a story about a large information leak. What has been reported is concern about the possibility of information being shared unintentionally. But if a human joins a call that users intend to leave to AI, who is speaking becomes a major issue.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without proper disclosure was a “mistake.” And when Meta rolled the service back for a time, it was not the whole service. It was the human concierge feature.

Meta explained that it will launch the phone feature publicly only when it is ready and can provide proper disclosure. What AI phone calls really need is not just smooth conversation. People must be clearly told who is on the other end. In this case, the main actor was not AI, but that basic rule.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone service sometimes handed calls to human workers without clearly telling users.
