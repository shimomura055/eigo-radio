# I Asked AI to Make a Phone Call—But a Human Made It Instead

Letting AI handle phone bookings sounds very much like the future, doesn't it? But an unexpected person was involved in that future-like call: a human.

Meta's consumer AI, Muse, has a feature that can call companies and stores in the United States. It can book a haircut, ask whether a product is in stock, and get price estimates from businesses. The phone feature was also being made available to users in stages.

At the same time, there was a test in which trained human contract workers made some of the calls from Muse and finished the conversations with the other party. Humans did not handle every call. This system was tested only with some calls.

The name was “human concierge.” It means a human guide or helper. The name sounds elegant, but what it does is quite bold. A human handles a call that the user thought they had asked AI to make. It is like choosing automatic ordering at a restaurant, only to have someone come out of the kitchen and ask for your order again.

Of course, the real problem was not only that humans handled the calls. It was that the test began without properly telling users. If sensitive information about a user is needed to continue a call, that information may be unintentionally shared with contract workers. Meta employees raised this inside the company as a privacy concern.

When we ask AI to do a task, we tend to think that only AI will see the information. But in fact, humans may be involved in making the call happen. Then the question of convenience quickly becomes a question of who handles the information.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without proper notice was a “mistake.” The company then temporarily rolled back the human concierge feature. This did not mean rolling back Muse's entire phone feature. What was rolled back was the experiment in which human contract workers handled the calls.

From now on, the plan is to release it only when it is ready and users can be properly informed. AI phone calls need more than smart replies. They also need an honest name tag that tells us who is on the other end of the call.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone-booking test unexpectedly involved human contractors, prompting privacy concerns and a temporary pause.
