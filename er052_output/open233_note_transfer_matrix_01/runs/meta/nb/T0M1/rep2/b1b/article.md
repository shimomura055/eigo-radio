# A Human Showed Up in an AI Phone Test

When someone asked AI to make a phone call, a human answered on the other end.

This slightly strange turn of events happened with the phone feature of Meta’s AI agent, Muse. Muse can call companies and stores on behalf of users. Users can leave it tasks such as booking a haircut, checking stock, and asking a business for a price estimate.

AI was supposed to be doing the main work. But in some tests of the phone feature, requests from Muse were handed over to trained human contract workers.

Those workers actually made the calls and completed the conversations with the other person. The name used inside the company was “human concierge.” In other words, humans had quietly stepped in as stand-ins on the stage of AI phone calls.

However, this does not mean that a human handled every call made by Muse. It was only a test involving some calls. This point is important. Humans were not involved in the AI phone service as a whole.

So, what was the problem? The problem was less that humans helped and more that the test began without proper notice.

Depending on the call, sensitive information about the user may be needed. That information might be shared by accident with contract workers at a call center. Meta employees raised privacy concerns inside the company.

There is no need to make this story more frightening than it is. It has not been confirmed that a large data leak actually happened. The problem was that humans handled the conversations without fully explaining who was behind the calls.

Having humans help in situations where AI has trouble is quite realistic as a system. But from a user’s point of view, if a human did the job they had asked AI to do, that is important information about the service. A secret helper may be useful, but first it needs to be explained.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without proper notice was a “mistake.” Meta then rolled back the feature in which humans handled the calls for the time being. This does not mean that the entire phone feature was stopped.

Meta says it will continue working with companies and stores to improve the service, and will release it only when it is ready and proper notice can be given.

Even when the time comes to let AI make phone calls for us, we will want to check more than just how good its answers are. Is the one on the other end AI or human? Telling users that in advance is also part of the service.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone service secretly used humans for some calls, raising privacy concerns and prompting a temporary rollback.
