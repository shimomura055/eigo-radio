# An Unexpected Stand-In for AI Calls: Humans Were Behind the Scenes

AI makes phone calls, books store reservations, checks product stock, and asks businesses for price estimates. Meta’s AI for individuals, Muse, was given these useful jobs.

But something unexpected happened during the test. On some calls made through Muse, the callers were not AI but trained human contract workers. The workers completed the conversations with the people on the other end.

In a sense, human stand-ins had appeared in AI calls. If you hear only this, it may seem useful as long as the calls go well. But the interesting point is not just how well the conversations went. It is how to tell the other person who is on the other end of the call.

In an internal post, an employee reported that an insurance company repeatedly hung up after realizing that the calls were made by Muse’s AI. There were cases in which people hung up after learning it was AI. At the same time, in another part of the test, human contract workers handled the calls.

However, the human stand-ins created another problem. When user information was needed to move a call forward, sensitive information could be shared with call center contract workers without meaning to. A Meta employee pointed this out as a privacy concern.

What is important here is that this does not mean a large amount of information was actually leaked. What has been confirmed is concern about the possibility that information could be shared. The moment humans appeared in AI calls, not only a useful stand-in but also the responsibility to explain came onto the stage.

A Meta executive admitted that it was a mistake to start testing calls by contract workers without proper disclosure, and temporarily rolled back the human concierge feature. What Meta stopped was this experiment, not Muse as a whole.

Meta explained that it will publicly release the phone feature in the future only when it is ready and proper disclosure can be made. In an age when AI handles phone calls, who will be the main player: AI or humans? What this test forced us to face was the question that comes before that answer: Can we tell people who is on the other end of the call?
