# Humans Enter AI Phone Calls: What Happened in Meta’s Test

Suppose you ask AI to make a phone call and leave the rest to it. AI handles the call. Just as you think that, what if it turns out that a human staff member actually finished the conversation? This is the story behind such a phone call.

Meta was testing a feature in Muse, its AI service for individuals, that let AI make calls and talk to the other person. However, in some of those calls, not only AI but also trained human contract workers handled them and completed the conversation.

Meta called them “human concierges.” Hearing only the name, they may sound reassuring, like the staff at a hotel front desk. But if users did not know that they were there, the story would be different.

On a call you think you left to AI, a human staff member may actually finish the conversation. What is more, sensitive information about the user that is needed to complete the call may also be shared with that staff member. The issue here was not the fact that a human helped. It was that users were not properly told that humans were involved.

This is not a story of AI completely replacing humans. Rather, it is the opposite. Humans were behind phone calls presented as AI. It is a small near-future surprise, but it is not easy to simply laugh off.

However, this was not said to be a large-scale information leak. There were privacy concerns that information might be unintentionally shared from employees with contract workers.

A Meta executive admitted that it was a mistake to start the test without giving enough explanation. Meta then put the feature involving human contract workers back on hold for now. It did not stop Muse as a whole or all of its phone features.

Going forward, the plan is to make the phone feature available only when preparations are complete and users can be properly informed.

With AI phone calls, what we want to confirm in the end is not how naturally they can speak. It is who is on the other side of that voice. Making that clear first may be the most important thing.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone calls sometimes ended with undisclosed human contractors handling the conversation, raising privacy concerns.
