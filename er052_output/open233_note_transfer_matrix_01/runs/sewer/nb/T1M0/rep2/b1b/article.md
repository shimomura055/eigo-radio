# A New Route for Toilet Water: Microbe Teams Take the Lead in Sewage Treatment

Where does the water go after you flush the toilet?

In a town where many homes are close together, the water travels through underground sewer pipes to a large treatment plant far away. It is, in a way, a main road for water that connects the whole town.

But in areas where homes are scattered far apart, this main road can become an expensive system. When the population falls, fewer people use the sewers. Then income from usage fees falls. At the same time, the underground pipes and treatment plants grow old, and the cost of repairs and rebuilding keeps rising.

For local governments, continuing to maintain this main road over long distances, even though fewer people use it, is a heavy task.

So some local governments are considering a plan to spread out sewage treatment. Instead of collecting water at one large treatment plant, they would treat it near each home. The main players in this plan are combined-treatment septic tanks.

These tanks do not handle only toilet water. They also treat household wastewater from kitchens, baths, and washing. Microorganisms work inside them. They break down dirt in the water and make the wastewater cleaner.

In other words, the system changes from one long trip to a large treatment plant far away to a small treatment team near each home.

Kitakata, Fukushima Prefecture, reviewed its plan for treating wastewater because the cost of maintaining old equipment was rising and income from usage fees was falling as the population declined. Areas outside those served by shared treatment will use combined-treatment septic tanks, and the city will provide extra subsidies for their installation costs.

However, this does not mean that all existing sewers will be removed at once. It means reviewing which system to use in each area from now on. Shared treatment can be an advantage in areas where homes are close together. In areas where homes are spread out, individual septic tanks may be a better fit.

And here is an unexpected problem. Microbe teams are highly capable, but they cannot run completely on their own. Small household septic tanks basically need maintenance checks at least once every four months and cleaning at least once a year. The number of times varies depending on the type and size.

If a small treatment plant is placed near each home, its care must also continue near the home. The future of sewage treatment means choosing between connecting things on a large scale and dividing them into smaller parts. We are entering an age in which the route of water’s journey is chosen to match how people are spread out.

## In one line
As rural populations decline, some towns are considering household septic tanks, but each still requires regular maintenance.
