# A Big Player and a Small Helper in Wastewater Treatment

Two systems are being considered over where wastewater should go.

One is a centralized system that collects wastewater from homes in large sewer pipes and carries it to treatment plants. The other is an individual system that places a combined-treatment septic tank at each home, or at a similar small unit, and treats the wastewater there.

The microorganisms inside these tanks play an important role. Their work treats not only toilet wastewater but also household wastewater from kitchens, baths, and washing. It is like having a small wastewater treatment team near your home.

However, this does not mean that one system is always better than the other. According to the Ministry of the Environment, septic tanks may be more economical in areas where homes are spread out, while centralized treatment, such as public sewer systems, may be more economical in areas where homes are close together. Which system is suitable changes depending on whether homes are close together or far apart.

This issue is drawing attention partly because sewer systems face some difficulties. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism explains that sewer systems are becoming harder to run because income from sewer fees is falling as the population declines, while the cost of repairing and replacing old facilities is rising.

Even if fewer people use them, sewer pipes and treatment plants do not quietly become smaller on their own. So in some areas, people are beginning to consider choosing a small system instead of expanding a large one.

In light of rising maintenance costs caused by aging facilities and falling fee income caused by population decline, Kitakata City in Fukushima Prefecture revised its wastewater treatment plan. It says that areas outside the centralized-treatment areas will become individual-treatment areas using combined-treatment septic tanks. The city will also provide extra subsidies for installation costs.

However, a septic tank does not retire the moment it is installed. For small tanks used in homes, maintenance inspections are basically required at least once every four months, and cleaning is required at least once a year. The number of times varies depending on the treatment method and the number of people, and inspections required by law are also needed separately.

A large sewer system, or a small treatment plant for each home. Wastewater treatment is a job that must be decided by looking not only at the number of homes, but also at how those homes are arranged.

## In one line
Whether wastewater is treated centrally or at each home depends on how closely homes are grouped.
