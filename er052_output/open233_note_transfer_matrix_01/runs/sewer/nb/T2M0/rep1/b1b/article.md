# A New Sewer Strategy: A Team of Microbes Working Near Your Home

Sewer systems have a somewhat surprising weakness. When fewer people use them, they do not become easier to run. Instead, they become harder to manage.

Normally, if a store has fewer customers, it can become smaller. But sewer pipes and facilities do not become smaller on their own when fewer people use them. Also, as they grow old, they need repairs and replacement. The money coming in from usage fees falls, while the cost of repairs rises. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism explains that falling income caused by population decline, along with rising costs for rebuilding and replacing old facilities, makes sewer businesses more difficult to run.

This is where combined-treatment septic tanks come in. They are like small water-treatment teams working near each home. They treat not only toilet wastewater, but also water from the kitchen, bath, and washing machine. The main workers are microbes. Their work is used to clean household wastewater.

What is interesting here is that we do not have to think of sewer systems as one large system. In areas where homes are close together, it may be more economical to treat wastewater together, as in a public sewer system. On the other hand, in areas where homes are far apart, it may be better to treat wastewater separately by placing a septic tank at each home, rather than laying a long network of pipes.

In other words, the best answer for wastewater treatment changes with the shape of the town. It is not as simple as saying that septic tanks are always cheaper. How the homes are arranged is an important point when choosing a system.

One local government moving ahead with this strategy is Kitakata City in Fukushima Prefecture. In light of falling usage-fee income caused by population decline and rising maintenance costs caused by aging facilities, the city reviewed its plan for wastewater treatment. It decided to make areas outside the centralized-treatment zones into individual-treatment zones using combined-treatment septic tanks, and also to provide extra subsidies for installation costs.

However, this does not mean removing all the public sewer systems that have already been built. It means reviewing which areas will use centralized treatment and which will use individual treatment in the future. It is a review of the areas and the plan.

The microbe team also has a surprising weakness. It does not automatically keep working after it is installed. For small household septic tanks, maintenance checks are generally needed at least once every four months, and cleaning is needed at least once a year. The frequency changes depending on the treatment method and the number of people served.

When a large sewer system is divided into smaller units, the facilities become closer to people. In return, the work of looking after them also becomes closer to people. Wastewater treatment in an age of population decline is not a strategy of simply leaving everything to microbes. It is a strategy in which people support the microbes so they can do their work.

## In one line
As populations shrink, communities may turn to household septic tanks, but people must still maintain them.
