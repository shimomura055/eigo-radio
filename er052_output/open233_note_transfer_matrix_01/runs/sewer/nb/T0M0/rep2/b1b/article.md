# The New Main Player in Wastewater Treatment: A Team of Microorganisms

A somewhat unusual main player has appeared in the town’s wastewater treatment. It is not a large sewer system. It is a combined-treatment septic tank that works at each home.

Behind the scenes is a major change: a falling population. When the population falls, income from sewer fees falls. At the same time, rebuilding and replacing old facilities costs money. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism says these two things make it harder to run sewer services.

The answer is a plan that fits the shape of the town. According to the Ministry of the Environment, in areas where homes are close together, shared treatment systems such as public sewers may be more economical. In areas where homes are spread out, individual treatment, such as septic tanks, may be more economical.

In other words, large facilities do not always win. Are the homes close together, or are they spread out? The treatment method is chosen by looking at that. It is like a game in which the plan changes for each town.

Kitakata City reviewed its wastewater treatment plan based on this idea. It took into account the rise in maintenance costs caused by aging facilities and the fall in fee income caused by the declining population. The city decided to make areas outside the shared-treatment zones into individual-treatment zones using combined-treatment septic tanks. It also decided to provide extra subsidies for the installation costs of septic tanks.

So, what does a combined-treatment septic tank do? It does more than treat toilet waste. It also treats household wastewater from the kitchen, bath, and laundry. Microorganisms do the work here. The wastewater is treated by using their ability to purify it. In other words, a small, unnoticed team works near each home.

However, this team does not run completely by itself. For small household septic tanks, maintenance checks are basically needed at least once every four months, and cleaning is needed at least once a year. The number of times differs depending on the treatment method and the number of people covered by the treatment.

Moreover, maintenance checks and cleaning are not all that is needed. A separate inspection required by law is also needed. In the Article 11 inspection, the function of the septic tank and the condition of its maintenance are checked once a year.

Because of the difficulty of maintaining large sewer systems, wastewater treatment is being spread out. But this change involves more than microorganisms. There are also people who inspect, clean, and test. The new main player is a team of microorganisms. But people’s regular work is what keeps the stage running.

## In one line
Kitakata is turning to household septic tanks as its population declines, relying on microorganisms and regular human maintenance.
