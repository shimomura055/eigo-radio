# Some of Muse’s Phone Calls Were Made by Humans

AI can call companies and stores for you. When we looked into this convenient feature from the future, we found humans behind the scenes.

Meta’s consumer AI agent, Muse, has a feature that can call companies and stores in the United States. It can ask them to book a haircut, check whether something is in stock, or provide a price estimate.

For some of these calls, it was not AI that made the call and finished the conversation. It was trained human contract workers. A test was being carried out in which humans who received requests from Muse handled the calls as “human concierges.”

The numbers involved are quite striking. An internal post suggested that, in some tests where humans handled the calls, the success rate could reach 95 to 98 percent. The success rate was said to be lower when AI alone made the calls.

However, it has not been made public how many calls were reviewed or how the success rate was calculated. This does not mean that humans always succeed.

In a separate case, an internal post reported that Muse was recognized as AI and the other side hung up. One employee reported that an insurance company repeatedly hung up after realizing that the call was from Muse’s AI. It was one case in which an AI call was removed from the conversation as soon as its true identity became known.

On the other hand, having humans handle the calls created another problem. Employees raised privacy concerns because sensitive user information needed for the calls might be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center. In one case involving price negotiations, a report said that the call record contained inappropriate remarks about race made by a human contract worker.

A Meta spokesperson explained that employees’ reactions were “overwhelmingly positive.” The explanation was that this was a test to put safety and privacy protections in place and improve the feature before its release.

However, by September 22, the vice president of the Superintelligence Labs division admitted that starting tests in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure was a “mistake.” The human concierge feature will be rolled back for the time being.

It was not Muse as a whole that was stopped. If humans are placed behind the scenes of AI, people need to be told in advance who is playing each role. This news showed that such a rule is needed.

## In one line
Some of Meta’s Muse AI calls were actually handled by human contractors, raising privacy concerns.
