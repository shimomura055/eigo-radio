# When I Asked AI to Make a Call, Humans Took the Lead in Some Cases

The AI makes the call. The AI also tells the other side what is needed. But an unexpected replacement appeared in this kind of future service.

Meta’s AI agent, Muse, has a feature that calls companies and stores in the United States. It can make requests for users, such as booking a haircut, checking whether a product is in stock, and asking a business for a price estimate.

However, in tests in mid-September, trained contract workers, not AI, made some of the calls through Muse. After Muse handed over the request, a human worker spoke with the other person and finished the call.

In other words, when I asked AI to make a call, a human phone operator appeared behind the scenes.

This approach even had a report card. An internal post suggested that some tests handled by humans might have success rates of 95 to 98 percent. The rate for AI alone was said to be lower, but no exact number was given.

If you hear only this, the human helpers look like a huge success. But the real problem was not whether the calls went well.

The problem was whether the person on the other end of the call had been clearly told who was speaking.

When human contract workers handled the calls, the user’s sensitive information needed to move the request forward could have been shared with call-center contract workers without meaning to. Meta employees raised privacy concerns about this inside the company.

There was also an employee report about one case in which Muse was asked to negotiate internet and cable fees. The report said that the call record included inappropriate remarks about race made by a human contract worker. This was one report, and it does not represent all contract workers or the tests as a whole.

Even so, a human had entered a call that people thought was being made by AI, and information might reach that person. If this is not explained, the convenient service suddenly becomes a mystery.

A Meta vice president admitted that starting the tests without properly telling people was a “mistake.” By September 22, Meta had temporarily rolled back the human concierge feature.

If the only goal was to compete over call success rates, the human stand-ins may have been excellent. But a phone service needs more than speaking well. It seems that saying who you are at the start was just as important.

## In one line
Meta’s AI calling service sometimes relied on undisclosed human operators, raising privacy concerns and prompting a rollback.
