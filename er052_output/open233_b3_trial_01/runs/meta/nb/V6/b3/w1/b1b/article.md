# I Asked AI to Make a Call, but a Human Was Speaking

AI can call companies and stores for people. It can book a haircut, ask whether something is in stock, and get price estimates from businesses. People look at their smartphones and wait for the results. Meta’s AI agent, Muse, was testing this seemingly futuristic feature.

But there was an unexpected person working behind those calls.

In some calls made through Muse, the person speaking was not an AI but a trained contract worker. Muse has a feature that lets it call companies and stores in the United States. When a user asked it to make a call, tests showed that some requests were handed over to human staff, who carried on the exchange with the other person until it was finished.

AI is supposed to take over human work. But this time, humans were doing AI’s work instead. It was as if the curtain on AI’s stage had lifted a little, revealing the human staff working behind it.

Another part of the background was that the people on the other end of a call might not welcome AI. Inside the company, there were reports that people hung up when they realized Muse was AI. One employee also reported that an insurance company hung up again and again whenever it realized the call was from AI.

Meanwhile, some tests in which humans handled the calls suggested that the success rate could be between 95 and 98 percent. When only AI was used, the rate was said to be lower, but no exact figure was given. Tests in which humans filled in for AI produced high numbers. However, we do not know how many calls there were, how success was measured, or how certain the difference really was.

And at this point, the story suddenly returned to reality.

What worried the employees was not only whether the calls would end well. Sensitive information about users that was needed during the calls might be shared with contract workers without anyone meaning for that to happen. A human was stepping into calls that users thought they had entrusted to AI. If this was not properly disclosed, a convenient feature could turn into a privacy problem.

A Meta executive admitted that starting tests in which contract workers handled calls without proper disclosure had been a “mistake.” Meta then returned the human concierge feature to its previous form for now.

In the age of AI, the question is not only how much like a human AI can sound. It is also who is on the other end of the phone. Telling users from the start who was on the other end was also part of the feature.

## In one line
Meta’s AI caller sometimes relied on undisclosed human workers, turning a futuristic convenience into a privacy concern.
