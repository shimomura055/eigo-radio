# Behind the Scenes of AI Phone Calls, Some Callers Were Human

You can ask AI to make a phone call. When people hear that, they tend to imagine AI talking with a store worker on the other side of the screen. Meta’s Muse is an AI agent that can call businesses and stores in the United States to book a haircut, check whether an item is in stock, or get a price estimate from a contractor.

However, for some of these calls, the roles behind the scenes changed. The calls were not made by AI, but by trained human contract workers. They took over the work from Muse, called stores and businesses, and completed the conversations with the people they reached.

This point is important. This is not a story about humans answering calls. Humans were the ones making the calls for Muse users. The service appeared to be AI, but humans appeared when the calls were made. In other words, there was a surprising human cast behind the AI service.

An internal post suggested that, in some tests handled by humans, the success rate could reach 95 to 98 percent. The rate was said to be lower when only AI was used, but the exact numbers and the way they were compared have not been made public. There were also reports from employees that people hung up when they realized the call was from Muse’s AI. One report said that an insurance company hung up repeatedly. However, these were individual reports.

There was also a privacy concern: when contract workers handled the calls, the user’s sensitive information could be shared unintentionally with the call center. A Meta executive admitted that it was a “mistake” to start using humans in this way without proper disclosure.

As a result, the human concierge feature was rolled back for now. The rollback affected the system in which humans made the calls. People thought they were asking AI to do the work, but humans were the main actors in the actual work. This news stands out not because of how smart the AI was, but because of who was performing behind the scenes.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone service used human contractors to make calls, raising privacy concerns and leading to a rollback.
