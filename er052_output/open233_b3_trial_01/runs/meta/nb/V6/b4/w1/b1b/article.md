# There Was a Human on the Other End of Some AI Phone Calls

The main point of this story is not how well AI could make phone calls. It is who was on the other end.

Meta’s AI agent, Muse, can call companies and stores in the United States. It can make haircut appointments, check whether items are in stock, and ask businesses for price estimates. It is a service that lets people ask AI to handle tasks that are troublesome but used to require making the call themselves.

But then came an unexpected turn. In some calls made through Muse, the calls were made not by AI, but by trained human contract workers. And they did more than just make the calls. They completed the conversations with the other parties.

This system is called “human concierge.” Just hearing the name makes it sound like a person who can arrange anything for you at a hotel. In fact, it was a test in which human contract workers handled some calls made through the AI agent.

One thing to note is that it has not been confirmed that people took over only when the AI failed. What we do know is that human contract workers handled some calls made through Muse. People thought they were making an AI phone call, but a human was actually taking part in the conversation. Seen this way, a story about a convenient future suddenly becomes a story about what was happening behind the scenes.

And a problem arose over those behind-the-scenes operations. The test had begun without properly telling people that humans would handle the calls.

Depending on the content of a call, the user’s sensitive information may be needed. That creates a possibility that the information could be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center. Meta employees raised privacy concerns about this.

What has been reported here is concern about the possibility that information could be shared. This is not a story that allows us to say that a large-scale information leak definitely happened. The issue is whether users were properly able to know whether they were speaking to a human or an AI, and who might receive their information.

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without proper disclosure was a “mistake.” Meta then temporarily rolled back the human concierge feature. Meta says it will make the phone feature available only when it is ready and can provide proper disclosure.

In an age when people leave phone calls to AI, natural replies are not enough. People must also be clearly told who is on the other end.

## In one line
Some phone calls people thought were handled by Meta’s AI were actually completed by human contract workers.
