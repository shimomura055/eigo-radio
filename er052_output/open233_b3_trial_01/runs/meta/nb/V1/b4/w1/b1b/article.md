# Humans Crash AI Phone Calls: An Unexpected Behind-the-Scenes Story at Meta

The main character in this little mystery is not AI. It is the human on the other end of the phone.

What Meta was testing through Muse was a system in which AI made phone calls and carried on conversations with the people it called. However, in some of the calls that were made, trained human contract workers, not AI, were making the calls.

This system was given names such as “human concierge” and “calls by human agents.”

If humans handled the calls, conversations might sound more natural. They might also be able to respond more carefully to the other person’s words. Humans could help in situations that AI finds difficult. Put that way, it sounds like a pretty clever plan.

But here, the mood of the story changes.

When human staff make the calls, sensitive information about the user may be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center during the conversation. Inside Meta, this raised privacy concerns.

What matters is that there was no confirmation that a large-scale information leak had actually occurred. The issue is not that anyone concluded that information had leaked. When humans enter a situation that people think is an interaction with AI, the way information is handled may change. That was the concern.

Moreover, the test in which contract workers made the calls began without proper notice. The humans who had appeared as convenient helpers had become the center of a privacy problem before anyone knew it.

The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted in an internal post that starting it this way was a “mistake.” The company then temporarily pulled back the human concierge feature.

Here, one more point needs attention. What was pulled back was the human concierge feature, not the service as a whole.

As we move toward an age when AI makes phone calls and handles inquiries for us, it becomes important to ask not only whether it works conveniently but also who is speaking right now.

The fact that humans appear on AI’s stage may not itself be a problem. But if people are not told about their appearance, the audience cannot watch with peace of mind. This incident showed us an unexpected story behind the scenes.

## In one line
Meta’s AI phone-call test used undisclosed human contractors, raising privacy concerns and prompting Meta to suspend the feature.
