# Humans Were Behind Some of Those AI Phone Calls

Here is a quiz. In a service where AI makes phone calls, who is on the other end?

The answer is: a human.

But not every call. Some of the calls made using Meta’s Muse were handled by trained human contract workers. The workers made the calls and handled them until they finished speaking with the other person.

Human performers had appeared on a stage where AI was supposed to be the main star.

The fact that humans made the calls was not, by itself, an immediate problem. The issue became bigger because the test began without clearly telling users that those performers were human.

On the phone, you cannot see the other person’s face. If you only hear a voice, it may not be easy to tell right away whether it is AI or a human. Also, if the conversation involves personal information or something someone does not want others to know, the other person’s identity matters a lot.

People thought they were talking to AI, but in fact, contract workers at a call center were listening. In such a situation, sensitive information—that is, personal information someone does not want others to know—could be shared without meaning to. Within Meta, this raised privacy concerns.

What was interesting about this case was not that AI spoke like a human. It was that humans were handling some of the AI phone calls, but people were not clearly told this.

Even though this happened on the stage of the latest technology, the mystery left at the end was very simple.

“Who is on the other end of the phone?”

The vice president in charge of this area at Meta admitted that starting the test without a proper explanation was a “mistake.” The feature was then put back the way it was for the time being. In other words, it was stopped for now and returned to its earlier state.

In the age of AI, smart replies are not the only thing we need. People must first be told who is on the other end of the phone. In this case, the main characters were not AI, but the humans behind the scenes and the explanation about them.

## In one line
Some calls people thought were handled by AI were actually handled by human contractors without clear disclosure.
