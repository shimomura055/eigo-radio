# Even After the 20 Percent Plan Is Gone, Crude Oil Is “Still High”

The crude oil market showed a slightly surprising reaction. Although a major fee plan was replaced by another plan, Brent crude futures did not keep falling. They only gave up some of their gains for a while, then soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement.

It began with a post by U.S. President Trump. On July 13, he showed a policy of asking for a 20 percent fee from all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, as a cost of ensuring safety in the strait.

The important point here is that this did not mean fees had actually started to be collected. No specific system was shown for who would pay, how the fee would be calculated, or how it would be collected. What appeared first was the number 20 percent. It was as if only the number had stepped onto the stage first.

But the next day, the stage moved again. President Trump posted that the fee would be replaced by trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. In other words, the plan to ask for a fee changed into a discussion of trade and investment. He also told reporters that no one should charge fees to ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

The market reacted to this sudden change only once. After the replacement was announced, the gains in Brent futures temporarily became smaller. But the reaction ended there. The price soon returned to a high level close to where it had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, it was up about 2.6 percent, above 85 dollars a barrel.

If we turn this price movement into one story, it goes like this: Even if the sign for the fee plan was taken down, that did not mean concerns about supply had also gone away. Concerns about attacks between the United States and Iran, a sea blockade, and tanker safety were continuing.

So this time, it is not a story in which crude oil fell across the board after the withdrawal was announced. It is a story in which the market reduced its gains once and then quickly returned. Even as the fee plan changed back and forth, concerns about supply around the Strait of Hormuz were still there beneath the price movement. The market seemed to be watching the concerns around the strait more than the fee sign.

## In one line
Oil stayed high after the fee plan was replaced because supply concerns in the Strait of Hormuz continued.
