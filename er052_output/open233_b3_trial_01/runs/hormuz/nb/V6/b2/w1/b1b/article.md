# The Day After Trump Played the 20 Percent Card, He Put It Away. Even So, Brent Did Not Fall All the Way

This oil market was like a short drama series. The title of the first episode was “Twenty Percent for the Strait of Hormuz.”

On July 13, Trump said he would demand a 20 percent fee from all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was for those using the route to pay the costs the United States spends to keep the strait safe.

However, details such as who would pay and how the money would be collected had not yet been given. A big number appeared first, while an explanation of the system was left for later.

That day, Brent crude futures rose 9.59 percent from the previous day and settled at $83.30 a barrel. Here, it would be too soon to blame the fee proposal alone.

Reuters linked this rise to the US sea blockade of Iran that was due to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz. On the market screens, not only the fee proposal but also the blockade and transport worries appeared at the same time.

And then came episode two. On the next day, July 14, the story changed suddenly. Trump announced that he would replace the 20 percent fee plan with Gulf countries’ trade and investment deals with the United States. The fee proposal that appeared the day before was exchanged for another card the next day.

So did Brent quickly return to where it had been? Here, the market went against the drama’s expected story. After the announcement, Brent futures briefly narrowed their gains. But before long, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. When the article was published, they were about 2.6 percent higher and above $85 a barrel.

The sea blockade, attacks by the United States and Iran, and concerns about tanker safety continued. Even when the fee proposal disappeared, the concerns that remained did not disappear.

The key point this time was not only how quickly the fee proposal appeared and disappeared. Just when one might expect the market to retreat after the proposal was withdrawn, Brent dipped once and then returned to a higher level. Even when events take place in a faraway strait, news about oil prices is like a drama in which several storylines move forward at the same time.

## In one line
Although Trump withdrew his 20 percent Strait of Hormuz fee plan, Brent prices did not fully fall.
