# The Fee Plan Left the Stage the Next Day, but Crude Oil Stayed

This crude oil news story is like a short play.

On the first day, the main character was a 20 percent repayment fee. The next day, that main character quickly left the stage. But the price of crude oil did not leave with it. Instead, it stayed at a high level.

The curtain rose on July 13. Trump posted that he would seek a 20 percent repayment fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. He explained that the fee would repay the costs the United States uses to keep the strait safe.

However, the plan had no detailed rules. It did not say who would pay, how the amount would be calculated, or how the money would be collected. It was as if only the number 20 percent had been placed in the center of the stage.

That day, Brent crude futures rose sharply. They settled at $83.30 a barrel, up $7.29 from the previous day. The increase was 9.59 percent.

However, the rise could not be explained by the fee plan alone. Reuters linked it to worries about a US sea blockade against Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz. On the day the fee plan appeared, worries about the strait also came onto the same stage.

Then, the next day, the story changed. On July 14, Trump announced that he was withdrawing the 20 percent repayment fee and replacing it with deals involving trade with and investment in the United States by Gulf states.

He also told reporters that no one should charge fees to ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself. The fee plan that had appeared the day before was replaced by another plan the next day.

At this point, one might have expected crude oil to quietly fall along with the announcement. But that did not happen. After the withdrawal, the gain in Brent futures briefly became smaller. However, as worries continued about attacks, a sea blockade, and the safety of tankers, prices soon returned to a high level close to where they were before the announcement.

The settlement price that day was $84.73 a barrel. It was up $1.43 from the previous day, and it was the highest settlement price since June 12, making it the second straight day to reach a new high.

The fee plan left the stage, but worries about the strait stayed on it. Statements may have an undo button, but the crude oil market seemed not to have a button that would return it to where it was with one push.

## In one line
Trump withdrew the fee plan, but crude oil stayed high as worries about the Strait of Hormuz continued.
