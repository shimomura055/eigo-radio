# The Fee Plan Appeared, Then an Investment Proposal the Next Day. The Oil Market Did Not Applaud

This news was like a short stage play. The stage was the Strait of Hormuz. The first thing to appear was the big number 20 percent.

On July 13, Trump posted that he would seek 20 percent reimbursement from all cargo passing through the strait, in order to recover the costs the United States spends on keeping the strait safe.

However, this was not a toll that had actually been put into effect. It was only a proposal. Who would pay it, what it would be based on, and how it would be collected were not explained. A big number had appeared on the stage, but the detailed script was not ready yet.

Then, on the next day, July 14, the scene suddenly changed. Trump posted that the 20 percent reimbursement would be replaced by trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. He explained that this decision was based on talks with Middle Eastern leaders.

He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself. The day before, he had talked about a cost for cargo. The next day, he distanced himself from the fee itself. The policy script seemed to have been rewritten in great haste.

So, did the oil market celebrate the fee plan’s exit? Here, the story takes a small turn.

After the announcement of the replacement, Brent crude futures briefly gave up some of their gains. But soon, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of the report, they were up about 2.6 percent, above 85 dollars a barrel. The disappearance of the fee plan did not mean that oil prices simply kept falling.

At that time, attacks by the United States and Iran, a sea blockade, and worries about tanker safety were continuing. Even though the talk about fees had changed, the worries about whether ships could move safely and whether energy could be transported did not disappear.

In other words, the fee plan was not the only main character this time. Statements may change in one day, but the risks around the strait do not change so quickly. The fee plan disappeared backstage, but worries about supply were still at the center of the stage.

## In one line
Trump’s proposed Strait of Hormuz fee vanished, but ongoing shipping risks kept oil prices high.
