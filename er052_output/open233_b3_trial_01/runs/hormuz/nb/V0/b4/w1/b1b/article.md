# The Toll That Disappeared and the Danger at Sea That Did Not

The real main story was not just the number 20 percent. What was interesting was that the number disappeared from the public stage in one day, but the worry in the crude oil market did not.

On July 13, a very bold proposal appeared in a post by Trump. All cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz would have to pay a 20 percent fee. He explained that this would recover the money the United States spent to keep the strait safe.

If you pass through the strait, please pay 20 percent. It sounded like a toll for passing through. And it applied not to certain ships, but to all cargo.

On that day, Brent crude oil futures rose a little more than 9 percent from the day before. However, we cannot say that the fee plan alone caused the rise. At the strait, the question was whether ships carrying energy could pass safely. A sea blockade against Iran was reportedly scheduled to begin the next day, and worries also grew about attacks involving the United States and Iran, and about tankers being put in danger.

Then, the next day, when the curtain rose on the next scene, the fee was redirected. Trump posted that he would replace the 20 percent fee with deals involving trade between Gulf countries and the United States, and investment by Gulf countries. He explained that this decision followed very productive talks with leaders in the Middle East.

The talk of collecting a fee from cargo passing through the strait had turned into talk of trade and investment between Gulf countries and the United States. It was as if the invoice for the toll had been put into a different envelope overnight.

Normally, when a burden disappears, oil prices would be expected to settle down. In fact, just after the announcement, the rise briefly became smaller. However, the market soon returned to a high level close to where it had been before the announcement. At the time of the article, it was up about 2.6 percent, at more than $85 a barrel.

The oil market was watching more than just the fee label. Even after the fee proposal disappeared, the possibility of a blockade at sea remained. Worries about attacks remained, and so did fears for the safety of tankers. Even if the price tag is changed, the danger at sea itself does not change. The market's movement this time was almost like checking an answer: it showed that this was the reality.

## In one line
The proposed 20 percent toll disappeared, but fears of danger at sea kept oil prices high.
