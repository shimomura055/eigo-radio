# The 20% Fee Plan Left the Stage. Crude Oil Prices Did Not.

If you follow only the headlines, the main player changes again and again over two days. It is as if the script in the oil market had suddenly been rewritten.

Act One came on July 13. U.S. President Donald Trump posted that all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz should pay a 20 percent fee. It was a plan to get back the money the United States would spend to keep the strait safe.

Although the 20 percent figure was given, no details were provided, such as who would pay or how the money would be collected.

Even so, Brent crude futures moved sharply on the same day. They rose $7.29 from the previous day, a 9.59 percent increase. They settled at $83.30 a barrel.

Here, a major factor separate from the fee plan comes onto the stage. Reuters linked the rise to the planned U.S. sea blockade of Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Then came Act Two on the following day, July 14. President Trump posted that the 20 percent fee would be replaced by Gulf countries’ trade with and investment in the United States. The plan to charge 20 percent on cargo was replaced onstage by another plan.

A member of the audience might think: If the fee plan has disappeared, shouldn’t oil prices also fall?

In fact, according to Yahoo Finance, after the announcement, Brent futures briefly gave back some of their gains. But soon they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. When the article was posted, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel.

However, we cannot say that the 20 percent plan alone caused the rise on the 13th. On the 14th as well, concerns over attacks by the United States and Iran, the sea blockade, and the safety of tankers continued.

In other words, even though the scene involving the fee plan was rewritten, other factors continued to move on the same stage. This price movement alone does not fully show how the market judged the 20 percent plan. All we can confirm is that the rise briefly became smaller after the announcement and then returned to a high level.

Even news about a distant strait is reflected at the same time in the international benchmark for crude oil prices, along with policy statements, military events, and shipping information. The market movements over these two days teach us not to give a news story just one main player.

## In one line
Crude oil prices remained high after Trump replaced his 20 percent Hormuz fee plan, as other concerns continued.
