# The 20 Percent That Disappeared, and the Crude Oil Price That Held Firm

At the crude oil market, numbers changed clothes quickly. The number on display on July 13 was the flashy figure of 20 percent. But the next day, that number changed into a story about trade and investment. Even so, Brent crude futures did not come down from their high level.

First, let us look at the scene on the first day. On July 13, President Trump posted that he would seek payment at a rate of 20 percent for all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was to get back the costs that the United States spends to keep the strait safe.

However, this did not mean that the actual collection of fees had begun at that point. Who would collect them? Who would pay them? What would be used to calculate the amount? The details of the system had not been shown. The number was large, but the system was still hidden backstage.

On the same day, Brent crude futures jumped sharply. The increase was seven dollars and twenty-nine cents, or 9.59 percent. They settled at 83 dollars and 30 cents a barrel.

Here, we should not make the 20 percent figure the only cause of the price rise. Reuters linked the rise to a US sea blockade against Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The appearance of the number and the major worries about the strait were present in the same scene.

Then, the next day, came an unexpected turn. Trump posted that he would replace the 20 percent payment plan with trade and investment deals between the Gulf countries and the United States. The plan that had appeared the day before changed into a different form in about one day.

So, would crude oil prices fall sharply? That seemed likely, but Brent futures only gave up a little of their gains just after the announcement. Soon, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. When the article was published, they were about 2.6 percent higher than the day before and above 85 dollars a barrel. The July 14 settlement price was also 84 dollars and 73 cents, 1.7 percent higher than the previous day.

The main story over these two days was not only the 20 percent figure. Attacks between the United States and Iran, a sea blockade, and concerns about tanker safety remained onstage. Even though the number in the plan disappeared, the problem of energy shipments through the strait did not disappear. This time, the market was paying attention to the transport risk behind the rapidly changing statements.

## In one line
The 20 percent payment plan disappeared, but oil prices stayed high as risks to Strait of Hormuz shipments remained.
