# The 20 Percent That Disappeared, and the Crude Oil Price That Stayed High

In the crude oil market, numbers quickly changed clothes. The number on July 13 was an eye-catching 20 percent. But the next day, it became a story about trade and investment. Even so, Brent crude futures stayed high.

First, let us look at the scene on the first day. On July 13, President Trump posted about a 20 percent payment. He would seek it for all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was for the United States to get back money it spends keeping the strait safe.

However, this did not mean actual fees had started at that point. Who would collect them? Who would pay them? What would set the amount? The details of the system had not been shown. The number was large, but the system was still hidden backstage.

On the same day, Brent crude futures jumped sharply. They rose by seven dollars and twenty-nine cents, or 9.59 percent. They settled at 83 dollars and 30 cents a barrel.

The 20 percent figure was not the only reason for the price rise. Reuters linked the rise to a US sea blockade against Iran. The blockade was expected to begin the next day. Reuters also linked the rise to worries about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. The number appeared with major worries about the strait.

Then, the next day, came an unexpected turn. Trump posted that he would replace the 20 percent payment plan. He would use trade and investment deals between the Gulf countries and the United States instead. The plan from the day before changed form in about one day.

Would crude oil prices fall sharply? That seemed likely. But just after the announcement, Brent futures lost only a little of their gains. Soon, they returned to a high level close to where they were before. When the article was published, they were about 2.6 percent higher than the day before. They were also above 85 dollars a barrel. The July 14 settlement price was also 84 dollars and 73 cents. It was 1.7 percent higher than the previous day.

The main story over these two days was not just 20 percent. Attacks between the United States and Iran remained onstage. So did the sea blockade and worries about tanker safety. The plan's number disappeared, but the problem with energy shipments through the strait did not. This time, the market paid attention to the transport risk behind the quickly changing statements.

## In one line
The 20 percent payment plan disappeared, but oil prices stayed high as risks to shipments through the Strait of Hormuz remained.
