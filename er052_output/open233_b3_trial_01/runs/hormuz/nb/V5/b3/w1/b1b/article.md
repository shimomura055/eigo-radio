# A 20% Bill Became an Investment Proposal the Next Day

News about the Strait of Hormuz is like a short drama with a sudden turn. The first act was on July 13. US President Donald Trump announced that he would require all cargo passing through the strait to pay a 20 percent reimbursement fee.

It was a plan to recover from cargo the costs of providing security and protection for the strait. However, although the figure of 20 percent was given, details such as who would collect it, who would pay it, and what it would be based on were not explained. Only the big number appeared first.

Then, the next day, the second act began. President Trump posted that he would replace this reimbursement fee with trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States. He gave as the reason “very productive discussions” with leaders in the Middle East.

He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself. The day before, it was 20 percent; the next day, he rejected the fee itself. The main character in the news seemed to change from a bill into an investment proposal.

After the announcement, Brent crude futures temporarily gave up some of their gains. But before long, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. When the article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent, at more than 85 dollars a barrel.

The important point here is that the price movement cannot be explained only by the announcement being withdrawn. Yahoo Finance reported that concerns about attacks between the United States and Iran, a sea blockade, and tanker safety were also continuing.

The day before, Brent futures had risen by 7.29 dollars, or 9.59 percent, and settled at 83 dollars and 30 cents. Reuters linked this rise to concerns about a US sea blockade of Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the strait.

The 20 percent plan changed shape in one day. Even so, other information remained in the market. Removing the sign that said “fee” did not mean that the attacks, blockade, and safety problems on the news stage had all disappeared at the same time.

## In one line
Trump’s proposed 20 percent Hormuz fee became trade and investment deals the next day, while security concerns continued.
