# The 20 Percent Plan Leaves the Stage the Next Day. Still, the Curtain Does Not Fall on the Crude Oil Market

This is less like news and more like a fast-moving stage play.

Act One is July 13. President Trump of the United States said that all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz would have to pay a 20 percent fee to repay the money the United States spends to keep the strait safe.

This is where the audience starts to have questions. Who would collect the fee? Who would pay it? What would the 20 percent be applied to? What currency would be used for payment? If someone did not pay, how would it be enforced? What would its legal basis be?

None of these points had yet been explained. A big number had appeared on the stage, but the system for handling the fee had not. Only the main actor had appeared first, as if the script had fallen behind.

Act Two is the next day.

President Trump posted that he would replace the 20 percent fee plan with plans for Gulf countries to trade with and invest in the United States. He explained that the decision was based on “very productive discussions” with Middle Eastern leaders.

He also said that ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz should not be charged, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself. The fee plan that had appeared the day before was replaced by a different role the next day.

And then Act Three. The main actor is crude oil prices.

After the plan’s withdrawal and replacement were announced, Brent crude futures briefly gave up some of their gains. But soon they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcements. At the time of the report, they were up about 2.6 percent, above $85 a barrel.

During this time, attacks between the United States and Iran, a blockade at sea, and concerns about tanker safety continued. Even though the script for the fee plan was rewritten, the situation around the strait was not rewritten at the same time.

What makes this news interesting is that the statements changed so quickly and the price movement did not end after one move. The fee plan changed shape the next day. Meanwhile, the market had, at the same time, both the statement that had been withdrawn and the security concerns that continued. Although the main actor changed, the tension remained.

## In one line
Trump’s 20 percent Strait of Hormuz fee plan vanished the next day, but oil prices stayed high as tensions continued.
