# Yesterday, 20 Percent; Today, Investment: A Sudden Turn in News About the Strait

This is like a short drama in two acts.

The first act takes place on July 13 in the Strait of Hormuz. Trump suddenly posted that he would seek a 20 percent reimbursement from all cargo passing through the strait. The plan was for the cargo side to repay the costs the United States spends on keeping the area safe.

Looking only at the number, 20 percent, it was a very strong message. However, no details were given about who would pay, what the amount would be based on, or how it would be collected. A large number had been placed at center stage, but the key rules were still backstage.

On the same day, the crude oil market also moved sharply. Brent crude futures rose 9.59 percent from the previous day and settled at $83.30 a barrel. Reuters linked this rise to the planned sea blockade against Iran, which was due to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Then came the second act. On July 14, the scene suddenly changed.

Trump announced that he would replace the 20 percent reimbursement plan announced the day before with deals for Gulf countries to trade with and invest in the United States. The reason he gave was very productive talks with Middle Eastern leaders.

He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz a fee, and that he did not like the idea of charging a fee at all. The day before, there was a plan for a fee; the next day, there was talk of investment. The policy sign was changed far too quickly.

So, did oil prices change scenes at the same time? After the announcement of the withdrawal and replacement, Brent futures briefly narrowed their gains. However, they soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of reporting, they were up about 2.6 percent, at more than $85 a barrel. The settlement price that day was also $84.73, up 1.7 percent from the previous day.

The sign for the fee plan was taken down. Still, concerns about attacks, a sea blockade, and tanker safety continued. The policy discussion changed greatly in one day, but the situation around the strait did not change at the same speed. The news moved so fast that only the crude oil market seemed still to be in the previous act. That is the interesting part of this story.

## In one line
Trump quickly replaced a proposed Hormuz shipping fee with investment deals, but oil prices stayed high.
