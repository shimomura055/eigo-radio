# It Was Withdrawn, but Prices Did Not Fall: The Two-Day Mystery of Crude Oil Prices

This was like a political drama whose story suddenly turned upside down over two days. And the crude oil market was what kept us from seeing the ending easily, right up to the end.

The first act was July 13.

President Trump posted a plan to require all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz to pay 20 percent toward the cost of keeping the strait safe. A very large number appeared all at once, making the news especially eye-catching.

However, no one had actually started collecting the fee. The plan did not say who would pay, what the fee would be based on, how it would be collected, or what legal basis it had. A large sign had been put up, but the system behind it was not yet in place.

That day, Brent crude futures jumped sharply. They rose $7.29, or 9.59 percent, from the previous day, and settled at $83.30 a barrel.

This made it look as if the 20 percent plan had pushed oil prices higher. But the story was a little more complicated. Reuters linked the rise to concerns about a US blockade of Iran at sea, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The entire rise cannot be blamed only on the fee plan.

The second act was the next day, July 14.

President Trump posted that the 20 percent fee would be replaced with trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships passing through the strait, and that he did not like the idea of a fee itself.

If crude oil prices had fallen sharply here, the story would have been simple. The plan disappeared, so prices fell. But the market was not that straightforward.

After the announcement, Brent futures temporarily gave up some of their gains. But they soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, they were above $85 a barrel, up about 2.6 percent. The settlement price at the end of trading was also $84.73 a barrel, up 1.7 percent from the previous day.

The answer to this mystery is that the fee plan was not the real main character. As long as concerns continue about attacks by the United States and Iran, the blockade at sea, and tanker safety, withdrawing the plan will not remove the fear. Even after the sign was taken down, the Strait of Hormuz itself had not become safe. The crude oil market seemed to be looking at that.

## In one line
Oil prices stayed high after the fee plan was withdrawn because fears about the Strait of Hormuz remained.
