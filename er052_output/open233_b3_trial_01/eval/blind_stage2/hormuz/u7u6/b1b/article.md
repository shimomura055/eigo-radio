# From Cargo to Investment: The Sudden Change in the 20 Percent Plan for the Strait of Hormuz

Yesterday, cargo was the main subject. Today, it is investment. News about the Strait of Hormuz rewrote its script in just one day.

On July 13, U.S. President Donald Trump proposed asking for repayment at a rate of 20 percent on all cargo passing through the strait. The idea was to recover from passing cargo the money the United States spends to keep the strait safe.

However, this was not a case of a new toll booth suddenly appearing. Who would collect the money? Who would pay it? What would be used to calculate it? The details of the system were not shown. The figure of 20 percent had appeared onstage, but the system for collecting the money was not yet complete.

Then, the next day, the story suddenly changed direction. Trump posted that he would replace the 20 percent U.S. repayment fee with trade and investment deals between the Gulf states and the United States.

The day before, cargo passing through the strait was at the center of the payment discussion. The next day, the focus had changed to trade and investment between the Gulf states and the United States. Talk of a toll for passing through the strait quickly became something like business talks between countries. You opened the rate sheet, and the next page was an investment plan. That was the kind of turn this story took.

Brent crude oil futures, one of the benchmarks for oil prices, also moved during this scene. After the replacement was announced, they briefly gave back some of their gains. But soon, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time this article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent and above 85 dollars a barrel.

Even during this time, however, the situation in the background was not quiet. Attacks between the United States and Iran were continuing, and the blockade at sea was continuing as well. Concerns about the safety of tankers also remained.

In other words, even though the main subject of the post changed from cargo to investment, the situation at sea did not change with it. The policy proposal changed clothes quickly, while oil prices shook once and then returned to a high level. In a short time, fees, investment, attacks, and ship safety appeared one after another, making this a very fast-moving news story.

## In one line
Trump suddenly replaced his proposed 20 percent cargo fee with U.S.-Gulf trade and investment deals.
