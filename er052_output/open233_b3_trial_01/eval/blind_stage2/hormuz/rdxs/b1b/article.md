# A 20 Percent Charge Proposal Became an Investment Deal the Next Day

This is a one-day, two-act policy drama set in the crude oil market.

The first act was on July 13. Trump posted that the United States would seek 20 percent reimbursement for all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to cover the costs the United States uses to ensure security. If your cargo passes through, you pay the cost. That was the idea.

But the next day, July 14, the story suddenly changed. Trump posted that this 20 percent reimbursement fee would be replaced by trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States. The idea of making cargo bear the cost had turned into a request to promote investment.

He also told reporters that no one should charge a fee to ships passing through the strait, and that he did not like the very idea of fees. The day after proposing a fee, he said he did not like fees. As a quick change in a policy plan, it was quite striking.

However, the 20 percent was not actually collected. Who would pay, what would be used as the basis for the calculation, and how would it be collected? No specific system was presented. A strong number appeared, but a finished fee system did not begin operating. Instead, the form of an idea that had not been made concrete changed.

The third act was the crude oil market. After the switch to investment deals was announced, Brent futures first gave up some of their gains. But soon, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time this article was published, they were up about 2.6 percent from the previous day and above 85 dollars a barrel.

During that time, concerns continued over attacks between the United States and Iran, a blockade at sea, and the safety of tankers. From this price movement alone, we cannot conclude that the market saw security issues as more important than the change in policy.

Even so, the fact that the fee plan changed into investment talk does not mean that worries about oil disappeared from the stage. The sign may have been changed in one day, but the actor called anxiety did not leave the stage. That is what makes this episode interesting.

## In one line
A proposed 20 percent fee on Hormuz cargoes became Gulf-U.S. investment deals the next day.
