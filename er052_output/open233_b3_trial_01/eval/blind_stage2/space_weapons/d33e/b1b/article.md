# Solving the Mystery of Space Weapons: First, Ask for Their “Address”

Military news about space has a tricky trap. When you hear that a weapon can attack satellites, it feels like the weapon is in space. But the way to tell space weapons apart is their address, not what they can do. The key is where they were launched from and where they are placed.

This time, the U.S. secretary of the Air Force revealed that the United States has deployed “space control weapons” in orbit to protect the U.S. military from actions by hostile forces. An official article from a U.S. government agency records this as the first statement acknowledging that the Space Force had deployed weapons in space. We cannot tell the type or number of weapons from this announcement. Still, the main point is that the United States officially acknowledged that weapons are in space.

Now, let’s start an investigation into the address of space weapons.

First, let’s look at Russia’s anti-satellite missile. Russia launched a missile straight up from the ground and destroyed one of its own satellites. A large amount of debris was also created. The event itself was quite dramatic.

However, the weapon’s address is on the ground. The missile is launched, destroys the satellite, and does not remain in orbit as a weapon. This is a ground-launched anti-satellite weapon. That is different from putting a weapon in orbit.

Then what about “counterspace”? From the sound of the word, it seems like a name for a space weapon, but in fact it is a broader term. According to the U.S. Space Force, it refers to all offensive and defensive activities carried out in orbit, through links used for communication, and at facilities on the ground. Even if it is called space, the activities are not limited to space.

GPS, missile tracking, monitoring space, and efforts to make satellites harder to destroy are also missions of the Space Force. These are activities that use and protect satellites. Therefore, simply because these jobs exist, we cannot say that weapons designed to attack have been deployed in orbit.

The answer to the mystery is surprisingly plain. Look not only at what a weapon can do, but also at whether its address is in orbit or on the ground. When you hear space military news, first check the weapon’s address. Just doing this makes stories about space much easier to understand.

## In one line
Space weapons are identified by their location, and the United States has acknowledged deploying some in orbit.
