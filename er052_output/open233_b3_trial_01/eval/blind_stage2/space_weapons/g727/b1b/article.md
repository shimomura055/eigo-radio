# Check the Name Tags in Space Weapons News: Who Is Really in Orbit?

“The United States has deployed weapons in space.” This story, which appeared on September 14, sounds like a trailer for a space movie. But the truly interesting part is not only the dramatic headline. Three very similar roles have appeared on the large stage of space defense.

If we line them up without name tags, the story quickly becomes confusing. A missile fired from the ground, the work of protecting satellites, and a weapon placed in orbit can all look the same.

First, there is counterspace. This is not the name of a weapon, but a general term for actions that interfere with an opponent’s space capabilities. It does not mean only attacks in orbit. It also includes interfering with communications with space through electromagnetic attacks, cyberattacks, and attacks on facilities on the ground.

In other words, seeing the word “counterspace” does not necessarily mean that a weapon has been placed in space. Even if something is aimed at space, the tool being used may be on the ground.

The next role is a ground-launched anti-satellite missile. In November 2021, Russia launched a missile directly from the ground toward a satellite’s orbit and destroyed its own satellite, Cosmos 1408. As a result, more than 1,500 pieces of trackable space debris were created.

This was a shocking event, but it was a destructive test fired from the ground. The fact that a missile reached space and the act of keeping a weapon in orbit are not the same thing.

The third role is rather plain, but it is actually closer to our daily lives. It is the work of watching over and protecting satellites, and making sure they can keep working even if there is an attack or a failure. The US Space Force lists GPS, missile tracking, keeping track of what is happening in space, and strengthening satellite systems among its missions and priorities.

However, these are activities for operating and protecting satellites. From a description of these missions alone, we cannot say that attack weapons have been deployed in orbit.

Now the key player appears. US Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink said that the United States has deployed space-control weapons in orbit to protect the joint force from actions by hostile opponents. An official article from a US government agency records this as the first statement acknowledging that the Space Force had deployed weapons in space.

The key point this time is not the broad story that “military activity is taking place in space.” Is it counterspace, a ground-launched test, satellite monitoring and protection, or truly a weapon in orbit? When you first check the name tags of the characters, the storyline of space news suddenly becomes clear.

## In one line
Although several space-defense activities sound alike, the United States has acknowledged deploying weapons in orbit.
