# Opening the Address Book of Space Weapons: Are They Fired from Earth or Placed in Space?

This becomes much more interesting if you read it as a space mystery. The key is not the weapon’s name or performance. It is its address. Is it on the ground, or is it in orbit? First, we need to check that.

On September 14, 2026, U.S. Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink said that the United States was deploying space-control weapons in orbit to protect its joint forces from the actions of hostile opponents. An official U.S. government article recorded his statement as the first acknowledgment that the Space Force had deployed weapons in space.

However, we should not jump to conclusions here. We do not know the specific system name, its attack abilities, or its targets. What we do know is that the United States publicly admitted that weapons had been deployed in orbit. Simply putting something in space makes this a different kind of issue from the anti-satellite weapons discussed up to now.

Now let us look at the second box. In 2021, Russia launched a direct-ascent anti-satellite missile from the ground and destroyed a satellite. As a result, more than 1,500 pieces of trackable space debris were created. This is the type that is fired from the ground. It was a powerful event, but it was not an example of a weapon being permanently deployed in orbit.

Even when weapons are related to space, their classification changes if their addresses are different. Do they fly from a launcher on the ground, or are they placed in orbit? In the world of space weapons, this difference is quite large.

Another large box that appears here is called counterspace. In the U.S. Space Force’s framework, the setting is not limited to orbit. It also includes communication links and the ground. It may include electromagnetic attacks, cyberattacks, and attacks on the ground. The term does not refer only to weapons that physically destroy satellites.

Systems used for defense must also be considered separately from attack weapons. Satellites for missile warning and tracking are announced as sensor demonstrations, not as the deployment of attack weapons. Passive defense includes warnings about threats, strengthening equipment, spreading things out, and having backups. Active defense may include weapons, but we cannot call all defense systems attack weapons in orbit.

Article IV of the Outer Space Treaty prohibits putting nuclear weapons and other weapons of mass destruction in orbit or in outer space, as well as placing weapons on celestial bodies. We cannot simply describe it as a rule that bans the deployment of all conventional weapons in orbit.

For those of us who depend on satellite communications and positioning, this may look like a distant story about space. In fact, it is a story about how to read the address book of space.

## In one line
The key to understanding space weapons is where they are: on Earth or already deployed in orbit.
