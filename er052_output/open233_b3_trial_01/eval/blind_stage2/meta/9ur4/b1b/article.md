# A Sudden Stand-In for an AI Phone Call: Behind the Scenes with Human Concierges

Behind the scenes of Meta’s AI service Muse, an unexpected stand-in appeared. Muse can call businesses and stores in the United States to make haircut appointments, check whether items are in stock, and ask service providers for price estimates. In some of these calls, tests were carried out in which trained contract workers, rather than AI, made the calls and completed the conversations.

They were called “human concierges.” The system worked by having contract workers take over requests from Muse and appear on the phone-call stage. An internal post also suggested that some tests handled by humans might reach a success rate of 95 to 98 percent. The rate was said to be lower when only AI was used, but no exact number was given. The definition of success and the number of tests were also not made public.

Up to this point, it looked like a reliable stand-in had appeared. But there was another problem behind the scenes. When user information was needed to move a call forward, there was a possibility that sensitive information could be shared with contract workers without meaning to. Employees raised privacy concerns inside the company.

What can be confirmed here is concern about the possibility of information being shared. There is no basis for saying that a major information leak occurred. The problem was that testing began without properly telling people that contract workers would handle the calls.

A Meta executive admitted that this was a “mistake” and put the human concierge feature back the way it was for the time being. Meta did not stop Muse as a whole.

Meta explained that the purpose of the tests was to put safety and privacy protections in place and gather feedback to improve the feature before its public release. It said the phone feature would be released only when it was ready and proper disclosure could be made.

The question raised by AI phone calls was not only who could speak well. If there are humans behind the scenes, can people also be shown their presence and the possibility that information may be shared? This time, the main character was not AI, but the spotlight called accountability.

## In one line
Meta used contract workers for some AI phone calls without proper disclosure, raising privacy concerns despite no confirmed major leak.
