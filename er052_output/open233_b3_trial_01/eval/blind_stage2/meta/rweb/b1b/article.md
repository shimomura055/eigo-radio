# The Other Side Was Not Only AI: Meta Pauses Experiment for Now

A phone call came in. If it was a call through an AI feature, you might think the other side was also AI. But in some of these calls, humans were playing the main role behind the scenes, where there was no screen.

The setting was Muse, an AI feature from Meta. In some calls made through Muse, Meta ran an experiment in which trained contract workers, rather than AI, made the calls. Their job was to talk with the person on the other end and carry the exchange through to the end.

This is not a story about AI being able to talk like a human. It is about humans taking on the role of supporting an AI feature. Humans handled conversations that AI alone would find difficult. Looking only at how the system worked, it could seem like a useful helper.

But there was one important part of the calls that could not be overlooked. Even if users thought they were talking to AI, there was a chance that contract workers were actually listening to the conversation.

During a phone call, people may sometimes talk about information they do not want others to know, without realizing it. Meta employees worried that such sensitive information could unintentionally be shared with contract workers at a call center.

What became a problem here was not just that humans handled the calls. It was whether users knew who was listening to them. What people feel okay talking about changes depending on whether they think they are speaking to AI or know that a trained human is responding.

In other words, what this experiment put into question was not how AI talked, but whether it had clearly explained what the call really was. If humans were wearing an AI mask, it was important to decide when to reveal that mask.

A vice president of a Meta division admitted that starting the test without properly telling users was a mistake. Meta then put the feature back the way it was, for now.

AI services can seem to work like magic. But if humans are working behind the scenes, that too is part of the service. Is it AI or a human on the other end of the phone? If that answer stays hidden, trust will hang up before convenience does.

## In one line
Meta paused an AI calling experiment after admitting that hidden human workers handled some users’ conversations.
