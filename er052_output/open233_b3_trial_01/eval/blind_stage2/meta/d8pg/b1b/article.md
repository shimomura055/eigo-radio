# Was the Voice AI or Human? A Human Was in Meta’s Phone Test

Imagine a small game of guessing who is on the other end. You get a call from a service that uses AI to make phone calls, and the voice sounds natural. So, is the caller AI? Or is it a human?

That was exactly the kind of service Meta was testing. According to Reuters, some of the calls made through “Muse” were handled not by AI, but by trained human contract workers. The workers made the calls themselves and completed the conversations with the other person.

In other words, human phone workers had appeared behind the scenes of an AI phone service. Inside the company, this role was called “human concierges” or “phone calls by human representatives.”

Here, the focus of the story changes. The problem was not simply that humans made the calls. In some situations, having a human respond may make it easier to give detailed explanations or continue a difficult conversation.

The problem was that the test began without clearly telling the other person that a human might appear. Someone who thought they were talking to AI might feel that the caller’s identity had changed halfway through. Of course, it is not clear how anyone actually felt during these calls.

A vice president in Meta’s Superintelligence Research division admitted in an internal post that starting a test in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure was a “mistake.” Meta then rolled back the human concierge feature for now.

This is the interesting part of the news. More than the fact that AI could speak like a human, the big problem came when humans entered the AI phone service: how to tell people about it.

It is also not known how many calls were handed to humans or what kind of explanation was needed. What we do know is that Meta admitted it had not given enough information and rolled back the feature for now.

AI makes the calls, and humans help in difficult situations. Such a system may be useful. However, knowing who is on the other end of the phone is basic information that users want to have.

In the age of AI, what has begun is not only a division of roles between machines and humans. It seems we also need a new kind of good manners: clearly telling people at the start who—or what—is speaking on the phone.

## In one line
Meta admitted its AI phone test used undisclosed human callers and temporarily rolled back the feature.
