# The Fee Plan Changes Overnight, but Oil Prices Still Haven’t Gone Home

The show begins. The main thing to watch this time is that the fee plan was replaced by a different one the next day, but worries about supply stayed on stage. A quick change in policy alone was not enough for the market to feel safe and bring the curtain down. It did not turn out to be such a simple story.

The U.S. president posted a proposal to charge a 20% fee on every shipment passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to repay the United States for the cost of keeping the route safe. The label to put up here is not “Already in effect,” but “Proposal.” The fee had not actually been put in place. What had appeared was a proposal.

Brent futures rose that day. Reuters linked the rise to a planned naval blockade against Iran, due to begin the next day, and to worries about energy shipments through the strait. In other words, it is a little too quick to blame the 20% plan alone for the rise. Worries about shipping were also a factor moving the market.

The next day, Trump posted that he would replace the 20% plan with trade and investment deals between the Gulf countries and the United States. He gave “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders as the reason. From a plan for shipments passing through the strait to deals with Gulf countries. On the policy stage, the lead actor changed costumes in a flash.

So, how did the market respond? After the announcement, Brent futures’ gains shrank for a while, but they soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before it. At the time the article was published, they were up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel. Worries also continued about attacks between the United States and Iran, the naval blockade, and the safety of tankers.

The fee plan has left the stage. But worries about supply will not stay hidden in the wings. For the market, this did not seem to be something that could be settled by a quick change in a post alone. The curtain seemed about to fall, but it did not. That is what makes this news interesting.

## In one line
Trump quickly replaced his proposed Hormuz shipping fee, but oil prices stayed high amid continuing supply worries.
