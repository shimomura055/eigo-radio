# A Quiz About Who Handles the Call, and What to Ask After the Answer

Here’s the first question. In some calls made through Muse, who called the other person and finished the conversation? The answer is trained human contract workers. Meta was testing workers making calls and completing the conversations.

Now for the second question. Once you know who handles the call, is that enough to put your mind at ease? Another concern is who might receive users’ information.

Meta employees raised privacy concerns that when human contract workers handle calls, sensitive user information needed to carry out the calls might be shared by mistake with contract workers at call centers.

It is important to get the answer right here. The concern was not that a large-scale leak of information had happened, but that information might be shared by mistake.

If someone made a call for you, would you want to know whether the person handling it was human? Or would you want an explanation of who might receive your information? Some people might want to know both, right?

And now for the final question. The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs said that starting tests in which contract workers made calls without properly informing users was a “mistake,” and rolled back the human concierge feature for now. The rollback applied to the human concierge feature. Muse as a whole was not being stopped.

What to think about in this story is not only who makes the call, but also how users are told about it. So, what would you want to check before letting someone handle a call for you?

## In one line
Meta paused its human call feature after admitting users weren’t properly told contractors might handle their calls.
