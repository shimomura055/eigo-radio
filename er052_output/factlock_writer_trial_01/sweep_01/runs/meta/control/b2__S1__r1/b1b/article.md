# Behind the Scenes of AI Phone Calls: Will We Be Told When the Lead Changes?

When the curtain goes up, the person on the other end of the phone is supposed to be AI. But then a human helper steps out from the wings. This kind of change of lead was being tested on some calls through Muse. Trained contract staff made the calls and handled the conversation all the way to the end.

What matters here is not the helper’s appearance itself, but whether the audience was told about the change. This test began without a proper explanation. A vice president in a Meta division admitted that this was a mistake.

AI or human? A quiz where you guess from the voice on the phone might be fun. But it is a different matter when users’ information is involved. Meta employees raised concerns that personal information needed to handle the call could accidentally be shared with contract staff at a call center.

This is not a place to make the story bigger just to heighten the suspense. A concern was raised; this does not mean that a large-scale data leak took place. But whether or not any leak actually happened, users want to know who handles their information.

Meta has put its human concierge feature on hold for now. But it has not stopped Muse as a whole. The stage itself has not closed; the scene in which a human appears has simply been pulled back for now.

People may help with AI’s work. But there is no need to keep that change secret. Rather than hold a contest to guess who is speaking, it would be much kinder to tell users before the call, “A person will handle this.”

## In one line
Meta paused its human concierge feature after testing AI calls without telling users when contractors took over.
