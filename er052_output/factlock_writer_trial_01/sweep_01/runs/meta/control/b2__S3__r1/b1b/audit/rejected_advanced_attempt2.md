# Who Was on the Other End of the Phone? The Unexpected Roles in an AI Service

A human showed up in a situation where AI was supposed to make the phone call. This story begins with that unusual casting. In some calls through Muse, a test was conducted in which trained human contractors made the calls and completed the conversations with the other person.

But this is not just a quiz about whether the caller is AI or a human. In some cases, sensitive information from the user may be needed to complete the conversation. Meta employees worried that when human contractors handled the calls, such information might be shared unintentionally with call center contractors.

The important thing here is not to overstate the story. This is not a case of a major data leak. What was raised was a privacy concern that information might be shared. In other words, the concern is less about the fact that humans helped, and more about whether users were clearly told who might take part in the conversation and might have access to their information. Even if a service looks smart on the surface, whether people can safely trust it is another question if they cannot see who is involved behind the scenes.

A Meta vice president admitted that it was a mistake to start testing calls handled by contractors without a proper explanation, and put the human concierge feature on hold for now. But only the human concierge feature was stopped. Muse as a whole was not shut down. What stands out this time is not only how smart AI is, but also whether people are clearly told when humans are involved behind the scenes. The “cast list” for a useful service may need to be explained in a way users can see.

## In one line
Meta paused Muse’s human concierge feature after concerns that users weren’t told contractors might access sensitive information.
