# The Twist in an AI Phone Call: Who Was on the Other End?

If we look at this story as a mystery, there is one question: Who was actually speaking on a phone call that someone had asked AI to make? Once you know the answer, it becomes hard to say, “As long as the task gets done, that’s all that matters.”

Meta’s Muse ran a test in which trained human contract workers, rather than AI, made some of the calls and finished the conversations with the other person. In other words, a human was speaking on the phone. In a stage play, a human helper stepped into the AI’s role. What matters to us as the audience is not only how well they acted. It is also: “Did you know who was playing the role?”

And the mystery does not end with who was on the other end of the call. To get things done, the human may sometimes need the user’s information. Meta employees raised privacy concerns that sensitive information might be shared unintentionally with call center contract workers. This means concerns were raised; it does not mean that a large-scale leak happened. “Concerns were raised” and “information was actually leaked widely” are different things. Readers playing detective should be careful not to confuse them.

The main issue this time seems to be not that there was a human helper, but that the role changed without telling the user. When you ask AI to handle a task, how much does AI do, and from what point do people get involved? Whether you are shown the list of who plays each role in advance also affects how comfortable you feel watching.

Now for the solution. Meta’s vice president in charge admitted that starting a test in which contract workers made calls without properly informing users was a “mistake,” and rolled back the human concierge feature for now. Muse as a whole was not stopped. The feature that was stopped was the one in which a human took over the calls. In other words, the curtain did not fall on all of AI, but on this feature where a human takes over the phone calls.

## In one line
Some calls made on behalf of Meta’s AI were actually handled by human contractors, without users being properly informed.
