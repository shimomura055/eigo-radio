# Toll Proposal Withdrawn, but Crude Oil Prices Did Not Fall Right Away

A 20% fee on cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. That was the reimbursement fee proposal put forward by Trump: all cargo passing through the strait would help pay for the cost of keeping it safe. It was a wide-reaching proposal, asking all cargo to pay, not just a limited group.

The market moved, too. On the day the proposal was announced, Brent crude futures rose sharply. Behind the rise were a planned naval blockade against Iran and worries about energy shipments passing through the strait. One thing to keep in mind is that the price rise cannot be blamed only on the fee proposal. Worries about the blockade and shipments also played a part.

Then, the next day, the story took a sharp turn. Trump posted that he would withdraw the 20% fee proposal, citing “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders. What he presented instead were deals involving trade with and investment in the United States by Gulf countries. The proposal to charge cargo passing through the strait was replaced by deals involving trade and investment between Gulf countries and the United States.

So, if the toll proposal is gone, will crude oil prices soon settle down, too? The answer was not so simple. After the announcement, Brent crude futures gave up some of their gains for a while. But before long, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of publication, prices were up about 2.6%, at more than $85 a barrel.

Other worries remained, which is why prices did not settle down just because the proposal was withdrawn. These included attacks between the United States and Iran, the naval blockade, and concerns about the safety of tankers. Worries about oil supplies had not gone away, either. Even with one proposal withdrawn, concerns about supplies through the strait remained.

## In one line
Trump withdrew the Strait of Hormuz toll proposal, but oil prices stayed high as supply and security concerns continued.
