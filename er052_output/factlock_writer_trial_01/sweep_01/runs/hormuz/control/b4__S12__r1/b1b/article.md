# From a Fee Proposal to an Investment Deal: Policy Turns the Wheel, and the Market Briefly Hits the Brakes

First, it was a fee proposal. But by the next day, it had become an investment deal. As we follow Mr. Trump’s posts, the story changes direction.

Mr. Trump proposed a 20% fee on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, saying it would pay back the cost of keeping the strait safe. The next day, he posted that he would replace the proposal with trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States. He said the decision followed “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders.

But the oil market cannot be steered as one wishes with a single announcement. On the day the proposal was made, Brent crude futures rose. Reuters linked the rise to concerns about a naval blockade of Iran, which was expected to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the strait. We should not assume that the 20% proposal alone caused prices to rise.

The next day, when the proposal’s withdrawal and replacement were announced, the rise in Brent futures briefly slowed. It looked as if the brakes had been applied, but only for a moment. Soon, prices returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, they were up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel. Meanwhile, concerns continued about attacks between the United States and Iran, the naval blockade, and the safety of tankers.

The fee proposal changed, and the market hit the brakes once, then rose again. If there is uncertainty about the very route goods travel, can we feel relieved just because the fee proposal has changed?

## In one line
After Trump replaced his proposed Hormuz fee with investment deals, oil’s rise briefly slowed before prices climbed again.
