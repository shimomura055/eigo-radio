# Sorting Space Weapons: Don’t Give Them the Wrong Labels

When you hear “space weapons,” you may picture weapons in space. But putting everything in one group can make this news confusing. Use different labels for “where it is” and “what the word means.”

First, use the label “deployed in orbit.” The U.S. Secretary of the Air Force said the United States is putting space-control weapons in orbit. They are meant to protect U.S. forces working together from actions by hostile groups.【Fact 1】An official U.S. government article says this was the first public statement that the Space Force had put weapons in space.【Fact 1】But no one has confirmed what devices they are. We do not know what they can attack or what their targets are.【Fact 1】

Next, use “launched from the ground.” Earlier, Russia destroyed a satellite with a missile fired from the ground. The missile was made to attack satellites. It left large amounts of space debris that people could track.【Fact 3】This was a test of a weapon launched from the ground. It is different from putting the weapon itself in orbit.【Fact 3】Both may look like “weapons involving satellites,” but they need different labels for where they are based.

Then there is a longer label: “counterspace.” This word does not mean only weapons in orbit.【Fact 11】In the U.S. Space Force’s system, it covers attacks and defense in orbit. It also covers them through messages between satellites and the ground, and at places on the ground.【Fact 11】In other words, this word’s box is quite large.

Let’s keep protection separate from attack weapons. In the Space Force’s system, defense includes active steps and passive preparations. These include warnings about threats.【Fact 13】They also include making systems stronger, separating functions, and spreading them across several systems.【Fact 13】Calling these protective steps the same as putting attack weapons in orbit would be a sorting mistake.【Fact 13】

The last label is the space treaty. The treaty bans putting nuclear weapons and other weapons of mass destruction in orbit around Earth. It also bans putting these weapons in outer space in other ways. It bans placing them on bodies in space, such as the Moon. On these bodies, it also bans things like military bases and weapons tests.【Fact 16】But it is not right to say, “All space weapons are completely banned.”【Fact 16】The news becomes clearer when we separate three labels: where something is, what a word covers, and what the treaty bans.

## In one line
News about space weapons is clearer when we separate where weapons are from what words and treaties cover.
