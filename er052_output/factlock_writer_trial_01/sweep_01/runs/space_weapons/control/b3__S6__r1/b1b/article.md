# Sorting Out Space News

Space news can feel like a maze when similar words appear side by side. This time, let’s put on sorting labels, one by one.

The first box is for space-control weapons in orbit. On September 14, 2026, the U.S. Air Force Secretary said that the United States was deploying them to protect joint forces from the actions of its adversaries. An official government article recorded this as the first statement to confirm the Space Force’s deployment of weapons. However, the weapon’s name, its ability to attack, and its targets are unknown. Even though deployment has been announced, we cannot fill in the details by guessing.

Next is a ground-launched test. In November 2021, Russia destroyed a satellite with a ground-launched anti-satellite missile, creating more than 1,500 pieces of debris that could be tracked. This was a test launched from the ground, separate from deploying weapons in orbit. Both are about space, but ground-launched tests and deployment in orbit need separate labels.

A wider box is counterspace. This refers to attacks and defenses in orbit, against communication links, and on the ground. It does not mean only weapons in orbit. Let’s not be led by how the word sounds; let’s check what it refers to.

Let’s not forget the box for defense, either. Defensive measures include warnings about threats, making equipment stronger, spreading things out, being able to move, and having backup systems. These are separate from deploying weapons that attack from orbit. Even when the goal is to “protect,” it is important not to put different methods in the same box.

The last box is for the space treaty. The treaty bans placing nuclear weapons and other weapons of mass destruction in orbit, as well as placing them on bodies in space such as the Moon. But it does not ban all space weapons. The key to reading this news is not to make the ban cover too much, and to keep weapons deployment, ground tests, counterspace, and defensive measures separate.

## In one line
The space treaty bans nuclear and other mass-destruction weapons in space, but not all space weapons.
