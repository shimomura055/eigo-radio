# The AI Phone Call Guessing Game: Is the Other Person a Human Staff Member?

Suppose you asked, “AI, please make a phone call,” but when the curtain opens, there’s a human staff member. If you imagine that kind of story, the call becomes a little guessing game: who’s really on the other end? Of course, this doesn’t mean users actually thought that was happening. What matters is how people are told when the person handling the call changes from AI to a human.

In some calls through Meta’s Muse, an experiment was run in which trained human contract workers, rather than AI, made the calls and completed the exchange with the person on the other end. In other words, the lead role on the call switches from AI to a human. Who handles the call is not just a question of casting. Depending on who the other person is, what you plan to say may change too. Would you want to know who is really on the other end?

Meta employees raised privacy concerns that when contract workers handled calls, users’ sensitive information could be unintentionally shared with call center staff. The key point here is “unintentionally.” Information could be passed on in a way the person did not intend. Seen that way, this isn’t a guessing game about whether a voice sounds like AI. It becomes a “Who am I talking to?” game.

The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted that it was a mistake to start tests in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure, and rolled back the human concierge feature for the time being. Rather than the curtain coming down on the story, the next scene is about how it is explained. If you thought you were asking an AI, you might want it to show its name tag before it comes onstage. This story may leave you feeling that way.

## In one line
Meta paused a calling feature after human contractors made calls without properly informing users.
