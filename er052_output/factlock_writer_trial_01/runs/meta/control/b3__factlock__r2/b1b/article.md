# Behind the Scenes of AI Phone Calls, the Star Was Human

When you hear that “AI makes phone calls,” you might assume the AI is also the one handling the call. But this time, there were people behind the scenes. It was as if, while you waited for the lead actor to appear, a different actor came onstage. Of course, this is only a comparison.

In some calls through Meta’s Muse, tests were carried out in which trained contract workers made the calls and completed the conversations with the people on the other end. You might think this was a story about AI pushing people offstage, but here, people were the ones who carried the calls through to the end. That casting was quite surprising.

But once people are in the wings, other props draw attention too. Meta employees raised concerns that sensitive information about users, needed to handle the calls, might be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center. We should not turn this into a claim that “a major data leak has happened.” The available information does not support that conclusion. The issue was the possibility that the information could be passed to the workers.

Meta executives also admitted that it was a “mistake” to start tests in which contract workers made calls without a proper explanation. Who is speaking to the person on the other end? How should that be explained? If people are working behind the scenes of a service, their presence should also be written into the script.

Meta rolled back its human concierge feature, but it did not shut down Muse as a whole. It plans to keep working with businesses to improve the phone feature, and to release it only when it is ready and can be properly explained.

The main point this time is not just that people appeared in AI phone calls. If people are helping, how and to whom that is explained also becomes part of the feature. The audience should be able to tell who the lead is in the call. That seems to be what matters if the show is to go on.

## In one line
Some calls through Meta’s AI service were completed by human contractors, whose role should have been clearly explained.
