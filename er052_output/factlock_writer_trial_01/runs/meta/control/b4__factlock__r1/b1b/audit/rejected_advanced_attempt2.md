# A Human Appears on an AI Call. What Happened Behind the Scenes?

If you heard, “AI will make a phone call for you,” you might imagine that AI would be the one holding the phone, too. But in this test, a human concierge came on from the side of the stage. Looking at the cast list, you might say, “Wait, that’s the one who talks?” In Meta’s Muse, a test was carried out in which trained contract workers—not AI—made some calls and completed the conversations with the other person.

The interesting thing about this story is not a contest over whether AI or people are better. Who is the main character in the call? And what information is handled behind the scenes? The spotlight falls on things you might miss in a description of the feature.

Employees worried that when contract workers handled calls, the sensitive information users needed for the calls could be shared unintentionally. However, this is not a story about an actual large-scale information leak. If we called it an “information leak incident,” the trailer would be more dramatic than the main story.

Another thing brought into the spotlight was the question of “explaining it.” Meta executives admitted that it was a mistake to start testing calls handled by contract workers without proper disclosure. The human concierge feature was rolled back for now. Muse as a whole was not stopped.

This is not to say that having people help is bad in itself. But if a person is involved in a task you asked AI to do, you would want to know who is handling it and what information they might be able to see. Maybe the main character this time is neither AI nor a person, but the message: “Please make sure to tell us who is playing which part.”

## In one line
Meta’s AI calling test used human contractors without proper disclosure, so the company rolled back that feature.
