# Human Cast Members Appear in an AI Phone-Answering Service

Imagine casting a phone-answering service. Who makes the calls? In some calls through Muse, tests were carried out in which trained contract staff called as human concierges and completed conversations with the people they called. In other words, there were human performers on the stage of phone answering, too.

Here, the spotlight suddenly falls on how information is handled. Meta employees raised concerns that sensitive user information needed to carry out calls might be unintentionally shared with contract staff.

But we should not make up an ending here. This is not a story where we can say that a large-scale data leak took place. It is only a concern about the possibility that information might be shared. Rather than embellishing the story by saying, “There was a leak,” the issue was who would be shown what. That is the important part of this stage.

Next is explaining things to the people receiving the calls. Did they clearly say who was calling? Meta executives admitted it was a mistake to begin testing calls by contract staff without proper disclosure, and rolled back the human concierge feature. It may help to think of it as opening the show without showing the cast list.

However, the curtain did not come down on all of Muse. What was rolled back was the human concierge feature. As for the phone feature, the plan is to keep improving it with businesses and release it only when it is ready and proper disclosure can be made.

In the end, there are two things to check before going onstage: who handles users’ information, and whether the person receiving the call is told who is speaking. We are interested not only in the actors who get the job done, but also in whose information is written in the script. In this little mystery play, what would you pay attention to?

## In one line
Meta rolled back its human concierge feature after admitting it had not clearly told call recipients that human staff were calling.
