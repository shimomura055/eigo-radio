# Behind the Scenes of AI Phone Calls, There Was a Human

When you hear “AI makes phone calls,” what scene comes to mind? If you picture an AI holding a phone receiver and busily making calls, here’s a small twist. In some tests of calls through Muse, trained contract workers—not AI—made the calls and finished the conversations with the people on the other end.

But when the spotlight turns to the human cast backstage, another concern comes into view. Meta employees raised privacy concerns that sensitive user information needed to carry out the call could be shared unintentionally with contract workers at a call center.

Let’s not build too much suspense here. This concern does not mean that a large-scale data leak took place. The twist is not only that “there was a human,” but also what information might be given to that person.

A vice president in Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division admitted that it was a “mistake” to begin tests in which contract workers made calls without a proper explanation, and rolled back the human concierge feature for now. The curtain fell on the human concierge feature, not on the whole Muse show.

This story is much more interesting when viewed not as a contest between AI and humans, but as a question: “If there are people backstage, what should the audience be told?” If you were to use this phone service, would you want to know not only how your request turned out, but also whether a human or AI handled the call?

## In one line
Some calls through Meta’s Muse were actually made by human contractors, not AI.
