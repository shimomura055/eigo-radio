# Behind the Scenes of AI Phone Calls: Who Says Who They Are?

In some calls made through Muse, an experiment was run in which trained human contractors, rather than AI, made the calls and completed the conversations with the people they called.

It feels like the lead role in phone calls is changing hands. But what I wonder about most is not the voice performance, but who is on the other end of the line. Wouldn’t you want to know that first?

Meta employees raised privacy concerns that when contractors handle calls, users’ sensitive information could be shared unintentionally with call center staff. If you think you’re talking to AI but are actually speaking to a person, you might want to know that before deciding what to say.

Then the story turns toward the question, “Could this be a problem?” A vice president in one of Meta’s divisions admitted that it was a mistake to start a test in which contractors made calls without proper disclosure, and rolled back the human concierge feature for the time being.

A human appears in an AI phone call. As a setup, it’s intriguing, but this time, what matters doesn’t seem to be which one speaks better. Who is on the other end of the phone, and will they clearly say who they are? Before the curtain goes up, perhaps that’s the first line we want to hear.

## In one line
Meta paused a phone-call feature after human contractors called people without clearly disclosing they were human.
