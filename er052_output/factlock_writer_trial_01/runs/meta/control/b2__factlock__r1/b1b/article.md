# The Mystery of the Phone Call: Who Was Handling It?

If this were a detective show, the first question would be: “Was it really AI that made the call?” In tests of some calls made through Muse, the callers were not AI but trained human contract workers. Those workers also completed the conversations with the people on the other end.

A human was involved. So where is the heart of the mystery? Who handles the calls, and how is that made clear? And who handles the information needed for the calls? From here, the story moves into the privacy chapter.

Meta employees raised concerns that if human contract workers handled the calls, sensitive user information needed for the conversations might be shared with call-center contract workers by mistake.

This is where we need to keep the detective’s reasoning from running wild. This is not a story about a large data leak. The concern was that information might be shared by mistake. Let’s keep “what actually happened” and “what people worried might happen” on separate pages.

A Meta vice president admitted that it was a mistake to start tests in which contract workers made calls without proper disclosure, and rolled back the human concierge feature for now. The rollback affected the human concierge feature; it did not shut down all of Muse.

The main point of interest this time may not be only whether the main figure in the calls was AI or a human. If there are people behind the scenes, how should that be made clear? What information is handled, and by whom? The next time you read this mystery, which clue would you check first?

## In one line
Meta paused Muse’s human concierge feature after contract workers made test calls without proper disclosure.
