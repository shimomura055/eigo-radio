# A Fee Proposal Quickly Turns into an Investment Story: What the Market Detective Followed

This news is interesting if you see it as a quick-change show in policy. In the first act, a proposal for fees on cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz appeared. The next day, the leading role changed to investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States.

First, Mr. Trump posted that the United States would ask all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz to pay 20% toward the cost of ensuring its safety. The next day, he posted that this payment would be replaced by trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States.

Well, that settles it. That is what one might want to think, but after the announcement, Brent futures briefly gave up some of their gains, then soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of reporting, they were up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel.

But the clues the market detective was following seem to have involved more than just the fee proposal. Concerns about attacks by the United States and Iran, a naval blockade, and the safety of tankers continued.

In other words, even though the proposal changed its outfit, concerns about safety at sea were a separate storyline. Keeping that in view makes it easier to understand how the gains in Brent futures, after briefly shrinking, returned to a high level.

If you changed only the movie poster but left the story inside as it was, that might be a fitting comparison for this market move. Why might we care about oil prices even though the story is about a distant strait? Perhaps it is because we think about how they are connected to our daily lives.

## In one line
Oil prices stayed high despite the shift from a proposed Hormuz fee to investment deals, as shipping-safety fears persisted.
