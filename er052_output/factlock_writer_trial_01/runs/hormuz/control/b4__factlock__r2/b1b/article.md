# The Fee Plan Gets a Quick Costume Change; The Oil Market Has Not Lowered the Curtain Yet

The main thing to watch this time is the policy proposal’s quick costume change, while the oil market does not change along with it right away.

First, Trump proposed charging all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz a 20% fee to cover the cost of keeping the strait safe. But the next day, citing “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders, he posted that he would replace the plan with trade and investment deals that Gulf states would make with the United States. From a fee on cargo to deals with the United States. The costume change was complete. So, did the market feel reassured too?

Brent futures ended higher on the day the fee plan was posted. Reuters linked the rise to concerns about a planned naval blockade of Iran, due to begin the next day, and about energy shipments through the strait. However, we cannot say that the 20% fee plan alone caused the rise.

After the announcement that the plan would be replaced, gains in Brent futures narrowed for a while. But soon, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time the article was published, Brent futures were up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel. Concerns about attacks between the United States and Iran, the naval blockade, and tanker safety also continued.

The fee plan left the stage. But the market did not seem ready to take a bow just yet. Changing the wording of the announcement did not make worries about ships and energy shipments disappear. It reads like a rather uneasy ending.

## In one line
Trump quickly replaced his proposed fee on Hormuz shipping with trade deals, but oil-market worries persisted.
