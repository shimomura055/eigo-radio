# The Fee Plan Changes Quickly. The Oil Market’s Curtain Has Not Fallen Yet

The main event this time is not a price tag placed on the Strait of Hormuz, but the quick change of the price tag someone tried to put there. First, Trump posted that he wanted to charge a 20% fee on all cargo passing through the strait, to cover the cost of the United States keeping it safe. It was only a proposal; tolls had not actually begun.

Then, the next day, the plan changed outfits. Trump posted that he would replace the fee to repay those costs with trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States. He said the decision was based on talks with Middle Eastern leaders. The price tag on cargo had become a matter of deals between countries. The stage props had been quickly swapped around.

So, when the fee plan changed outfits, did the oil market also settle down with a “All right, no need to worry”? Things did not play out so simply. After the announcement, Brent futures first gave up some of their gains, but soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of reporting, they were up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel.

Meanwhile, attacks between the United States and Iran, a blockade at sea, and concerns about tanker safety continued. Even if the fee plan moved offstage, worries at sea did not leave with it. A quick change of price tags alone will not bring down the curtain. That is the key point in this news.

## In one line
Trump replaced his proposed Hormuz shipping fee with trade deals, but oil prices stayed high amid ongoing tensions.
