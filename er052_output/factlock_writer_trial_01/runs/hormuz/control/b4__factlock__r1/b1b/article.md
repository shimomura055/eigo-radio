# The Fee Plan Is Replaced, but the Oil Market’s Story Isn’t Over

In this news story, the lead role on the policy stage changed hands the next day. But in the oil market, the curtain did not fall right away.

Trump proposed charging all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz a 20% fee to recover the cost of keeping the strait safe. It was like putting up a sign along the route that said, “Please pay for keeping this route safe.”

But the next day, the fee plan was replaced by trade and investment deals between Gulf countries and the United States. Trump said the decision was based on “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders. The prop of a fee for using the strait was taken backstage, and trade and investment came on in its place.

So how did the market react? Brent crude futures briefly gave up some of their gains after the announcement, but soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before it. At the time the article was published, they were up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel.

On the day the fee plan was posted, Brent crude futures ended higher. But it is too soon to blame the price rise on the fee plan alone. Reuters linked the rise to concerns about a blockade at sea against Iran, which was set to begin the next day, and about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

Even after the fee plan was replaced, concerns about attacks by the United States and Iran, the blockade at sea, and tanker safety continued. Even with the fee-plan card pulled back, worries were still on stage. The market’s response seemed to say, “There’s more to come,” rather than giving the story a neat ending.

The point to watch is not only that the proposal changed. Even when the announcement changes, what concerns remain? If you pay attention to that, market movements too begin to look like the next part of the story.

## In one line
Trump replaced his proposed Hormuz fee with trade and investment deals, but oil prices stayed high amid security concerns.
