# AI Is the Star, but the Trailer Is for the Whole Company! The “Cast” of Numbers in Broadcom’s Earnings

“AI semiconductor revenue: $16.7 billion!” If that number fills the screen, you hear a fanfare in your head. The star, AI, seems to have pulled off another big job. Then the words “Next: about $34.8 billion” appear. Wow, is the sequel suddenly going to be much bigger? Wait a minute. Has this edit joined two different numbers?

Broadcom’s earnings report is much easier to follow if we check the “cast” of the numbers. Revenue for the company as a whole was $29.591 billion. That is the result for the whole company. By contrast, $16.7 billion was revenue from AI semiconductors. Even though both appear in the same announcement, they show different things—like a group photo of the entire cast and a close-up of the star.

So what is about $34.8 billion? It is the company’s forecast for its total revenue in the next quarter. In other words, what is being previewed is the whole company next time, not the future of AI semiconductors alone. If a caption said, “AI revenue rises from $16.7 billion to $34.8 billion!”, that would not be the company’s announcement. It would be an overly bold re-edit by the audience.

Now let’s sort out the cast list. $16.7 billion is “AI semiconductor results.” $29.591 billion is “whole-company results.” About $34.8 billion is “whole-company forecast.” “AI or the whole company?” and “Actual results or a forecast?” If we remove these two labels, the earnings report quickly turns into a comedy of mix-ups.

Of course, AI does get its big moment. The CEO said that demand for custom AI accelerators and networking remains very strong. It is a strong line that gives a sense of the star’s momentum. We should listen to it as it is.

But even if the company’s forecast appears beside that line, we cannot say, “AI demand is strong, so revenue will be about $34.8 billion.” This announcement alone does not explain how the two are connected. We should not see two scenes next to each other and make up the script in between.

The key questions when looking at earnings are: “What is this number for?” and “Is it an actual result or a forecast?” Just these two questions are enough to clearly separate the star’s performance from the outlook for the whole production. AI deserves applause. But the sign for the next preview says “the whole company.” Let’s pause for a moment with the popcorn in hand and make sure we don’t miss that.

## In one line
Broadcom’s $34.8 billion forecast is for the whole company, not just AI semiconductors.
