# The “20% Plan” Exits at Once, but High Oil Prices Still Won’t Go Away

A major policy plan appeared, and oil prices shot up. The next day, the plan was withdrawn. You might expect the market to say, “Sorry for the fuss,” and return to where it was—but that did not happen. The lead actor left the stage, but the tension stayed behind. This time, the oil market has not followed a simple script.

It all began with Mr. Trump’s “20% plan.” It called for a 20% payment on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, as reimbursement for the cost of ensuring U.S. security. The size of the figure and the broad scope of the plan were enough to grab the headlines.

But the next day, it was announced that the plan would be replaced with trade and investment deals involving Gulf countries and the United States. The “20%” had only just appeared, and already it was heading backstage.

Meanwhile, oil prices made a dramatic move. On the same day the plan was announced, Brent futures rose by $7.29, or 9.59%. They closed at $83.30. That was a striking jump of about 10%.

But it is too soon to tell the whole story as, “The 20% plan caused the price surge!” Reports linked the rise to a U.S. naval blockade of Iran, said to be set to begin the next day, and to worries about energy shipments passing through the strait.

In other words, behind the eye-catching figure was a much bigger question: “Can the cargo even be transported safely?” If you focus only on the cost, you can lose sight of the key route.

Then, the next day, the replacement plan was announced, and Brent briefly gave up some of its gains. Just when it seemed things might finally calm down, prices soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. The feeling of relief did not last long.

At the time the article was published, worries about a naval blockade, attacks, and tanker safety continued. Changing the policy sign does not automatically remove the fears at sea.

That is the key point this time. The simple calculation, “Prices rose because of the 20% plan, so they will fall when the plan disappears,” does not explain everything. To begin with, the whole rise cannot be blamed on this plan alone.

The main things to watch in this policy drama are not just the people who make a flashy entrance. The planned naval blockade, worries about transport, and tanker safety are also important factors to keep in mind when reading the market. The “20%” may leave the stage, but the worries do not. The curtain did not come down on the oil market so easily.

## In one line
Oil prices soon rebounded after Trump withdrew his proposed 20% charge, while fears over safe shipping persisted.
