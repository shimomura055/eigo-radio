# A Car’s Red Lights Say “Braking!” Even When No One Is Pressing the Pedal—A Surprising Solo Play-by-Play

“I’m pressing the brake!” “No, I’m not!”

At the back of the car, the red lights are doing their best to get attention. But the driver has no idea why. If cars could talk to each other, the car behind might be confused too and say, “Could you get your story straight?” This BYD recall is about just this kind of mismatch between signals.

A recall notice in China named 183,211 cars from BYD’s Tang and Qin lines. The cause was a problem with the material in the “brake pedal stopper pad.” It is a part on the pedal, one that is hard to picture from its name alone. Often hidden behind showier features, this part started the trouble this time.

According to the notice, the part may crack or break over time, and in an extreme case, it may come off. As a result, there is a risk that the brake lights will stay on even when the brake pedal is not being pressed.

In other words, no foot is pressing the brake, but the lights alone keep announcing, “The brake is being pressed!” The people on the ground and the press office are not communicating. The driver might want to say, “Could you run that announcement by me first?”

But on the road, this is not something you can laugh off and correct. Cars behind can see the red light, but not the driver’s feet in the car ahead. If a signal that does not match the driver’s actions keeps appearing, drivers behind may get the wrong idea about the state of the car in front.

Brake lights are short messages between cars. But unlike in a chat, you cannot take back a message with “Never mind!” or add, “Actually, I’m not pressing the pedal.” Since they communicate with only a red light, it is important that the signal be accurate.

The key point this time is that the source of the problem and the place where its effects are felt are far apart. The problem starts at the pedal inside the car, but its effects reach cars behind through the lights at the back. It turns out that a part most people hardly notice had a surprisingly prominent “voice.”

BYD will replace the affected parts with improved ones free of charge through its authorized dealers. It is a parts replacement, but in a way, it is a measure to stop the car’s “solo play-by-play” and prevent a mismatch between actions and signals.

Car safety is not just about “moving” and “stopping.” “Sending the right message to those around you” is an important job, too. We want the red lights to be accurate, not just loud. On the road, we want cars to be good at getting the message across, not just good at talking.

## In one line
BYD is recalling 183,211 cars in China because faulty pedal pads may keep the brake lights on when drivers aren’t braking.
