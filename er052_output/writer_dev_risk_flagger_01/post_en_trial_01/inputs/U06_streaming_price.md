# Disney+ Prices Are Going Up: Don’t Miss the “Coming Up Next” on Your Bill!

The news that “Disney+ is raising its prices” may have made tense background music start playing in your mental household budget. Before you clutch your wallet and think, “Not my subscription fees again...,” pause here. The new prices in the U.S. do not mean everyone’s bill will go up on the same day. The star is the price tag, with the calendar as a surprising co-star.

First, let’s look at the numbers that directly affect your wallet. The monthly ad-supported plan by itself will go from $11.99 to $12.49, a difference of $0.50. The monthly Premium plan by itself will go from $18.99 to $21.49, a difference of $2.50. The yearly price for the Premium plan by itself is changing too, so people who pay by the year should take note.

The story now splits into two paths. New subscribers get the new prices first. For people who already have a subscription, the new prices start later, with a future billing cycle. The changeover time for each current subscriber depends on their billing cycle. Even if you watch the same show on the same night, the new price’s “release date” may not be the same.

So the headline about the price increase is a preview sent to everyone. But the day the main story begins on your bill is different for each person. It’s too soon to rush to the ending and ask, “The news is out. Does that mean my price changes today?” First, check whether you are a new subscriber or already had a subscription. Then check your billing cycle.

The price list tells you “how much.” When you look at it together with when you signed up and your billing cycle, you can see when the change starts for you. We should ask the calendar to join the household budget meeting, not just the calculator.

Now, the question many people have is, “So why are prices going up?” But there is no clear answer here. The Disney+ U.S. pricing page and the Reuters report that we checked did not clearly state Disney’s reason for this change. According to Reuters, Disney did not answer a request for comment right away.

The numbers are there. But the sources we checked do not tell us the reason. We must not fill this gap by writing a script based on guesses. If you are reviewing your subscription costs, check these three things together: how much, when, and how much has been explained about the reason. Next, check not only when your favorite shows will be available, but also when you are due to be charged.

## In one line
Disney+’s U.S. price increases apply first to new subscribers, while existing customers switch on different billing cycles.
