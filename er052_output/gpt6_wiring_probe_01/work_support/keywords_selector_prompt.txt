# The Fee Plan Gets a New Look the Next Day. Oil Prices Stay Near Their Highs

When it comes to policy proposals, this story is moving at fast-forward speed. Trump proposed seeking 20 percent reimbursement from cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, then replaced that proposal with another the next day. But Brent crude futures did not neatly follow the pattern of “proposal withdrawn, and the market turns around too.”

The first post said the United States would seek 20 percent reimbursement from all cargo passing through the strait, citing the cost of keeping it safe. The next day, Trump posted that he was replacing that plan with trade and investment deals involving the Gulf states and the United States. From a plan for cargo passing through the strait to deals involving the Gulf states and the United States: the policy proposal had already changed its look.

After the announcement, Brent crude futures temporarily pared their gains. Here, “pared their gains” does not mean prices kept falling. Before long, they returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. At the time of reporting, they were up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel.

While the fee plan changed shape, concerns continued over attacks by the United States and Iran, the sea blockade, and the safety of tankers. The change to the announcement and concerns about the strait were happening at the same time.

So if we sum it up in one line as “the plan was withdrawn, so oil prices fell,” we miss how prices pared their gains and then returned to a high range. Of course, this price movement alone cannot tell us what moved prices, or by how much. What stands out this time is that the policy proposal changed quickly, the market briefly slowed, and concerns continued—all at once. The figure above $85 is the price at the time the article was published, not that day’s closing price. It seems the market’s story cannot be read from a single headline.

## In one line
Trump quickly replaced his proposed fee plan for Hormuz shipping, but oil prices stayed near their highs.
