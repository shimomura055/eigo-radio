# Interest Rates: Watch Only the Central Bank, and You’ll Miss the Action

Think of interest-rate news as a sports broadcast. When the central bank makes a move, everyone pays attention. But bond markets and worries about inflation also take part in the mortgage-rate game. A rise in the policy rate does not mean mortgage rates automatically rise by the same amount.

On September 16, the meeting that sets U.S. monetary policy raised the policy rate by 0.25 percentage points, to a range of 3.75% to 4.00%. Economic activity and spending at home are strong, but inflation remains high. The aim was to help bring rising prices back to the 2% target sooner. But this is what officials hope to achieve. It does not mean the rate hike has already had an effect.

Let’s look at the mortgage-rate figures. The national average rate for a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage rose from 5.98% in late February to 7.40% on October 8. An Associated Press estimate shows that, for a $400,000 loan, this difference in rates would add about $376 to the monthly payment. Of course, this is an example based on an estimate. It does not mean everyone who borrows will see their payment rise by the same amount.

Let’s also check the rules of the game. With a fixed-rate mortgage, the rate in the contract stays the same throughout the repayment period. Even if the policy rate rises, payments do not automatically go up for people who have already borrowed. With an adjustable-rate mortgage, the rate may change depending on the terms of the contract.

So, who are the players that move the mortgage-rate game? They are inflation, central bank policy, and bond market investors’ views of the economy. The recent rise was also linked to swings in the bond market and stronger worries about inflation because of higher oil prices. In other words, it is too soon to blame the September rate hike alone.

When you look at mortgage rates, pay attention not only to what the central bank announces but also to what the market is worried about. When planning your budget for a big purchase, remember that the interest-rate game is not decided by just one player.

## In one line
Mortgage rates are shaped not just by central bank decisions, but also by inflation and bond-market expectations.
