# More Than “How Much Will You Pay?”: They Want AI Models Destroyed

People often think about money when they hear about AI copyright cases. But this complaint asks for more than money. The plaintiffs want AI models and training sets destroyed. They say these contain their content. So this case is not only asking, “How much will you pay?” It also asks, “What should happen to systems said to contain the articles?” That is why this case is worth watching.

Companies and businesses connected to USA TODAY say OpenAI got news content without permission. They say OpenAI copied it and used it to train models. They also say the models’ answers copied articles or put their contents in a new order. They say copyright information was removed. These are claims made by the plaintiffs in the complaint. A court has not confirmed them as facts.

The people bringing the case want more than $250 million in damages. They also want profits returned and court orders to stop certain actions. They ask for GPT and other large language models to be destroyed. They say these models contain their content. They also want training sets destroyed. They say these sets contain that content. But let’s hit the brakes here. No one has ordered their destruction. The plaintiffs are only asking for it.

AI answers may appear on a screen. But the material used to make them is harder to see. Here, the plaintiffs ask what should happen to that material. They also ask what should happen to the models they say contain it. This case makes you want to look behind the screen when you read AI-written text.

The case was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York. No court has found copyright infringement. When Reuters reported this, OpenAI had not yet replied to requests for comment.

## In one line
People connected to USA TODAY ask a court to destroy OpenAI models they say contain their content.
