# Beyond “How Much Will You Pay?”: A Demand to Destroy AI Models

When people hear about copyright lawsuits over AI, they tend to focus on the amount of damages being sought. But this complaint asks for more than money. The plaintiffs want AI models and training sets that they say contain their content to be destroyed. In other words, the case is not only about “how much will you pay?” It is also about “what should happen to the systems said to contain the articles?” That is what makes this case worth watching.

Companies and business entities connected to USA TODAY claim that OpenAI obtained and copied news content without permission, then used it to train models. They also claim that the models’ answers copied articles or rearranged their contents, and that copyright information was removed. These are claims made by the plaintiffs in the complaint, not facts confirmed by a court.

The people bringing the case are seeking more than $250 million in damages, as well as the return of profits and orders to stop certain actions. They are also asking for GPT and other large language models said to contain their content to be destroyed, along with training sets said to contain that content. But let’s hit the brakes here. No one has ordered their destruction. The plaintiffs are only asking for it.

AI answers may appear on a screen, but the material used to make them is harder to see. In this lawsuit, the plaintiffs are asking not only what should happen to that material, but also to the models themselves that they say contain it. This case makes you want to look at what is behind the screen when you read AI-written text.

The lawsuit has been filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, and copyright infringement has not been found. At the time of Reuters’ report, OpenAI had not immediately responded to requests for comment.
