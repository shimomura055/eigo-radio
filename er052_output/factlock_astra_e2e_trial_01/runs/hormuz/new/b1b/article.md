# The 20% Plan Is Out, but Brent Futures Are Still on Alert

When a flashy fee proposal disappears, the market should breathe a sigh of relief. You might think, “Okay, that’s the end of this whole thing!” But this time, Brent futures did not leave the scene. Even after the main figure in the headlines left, the tense drama around the Strait of Hormuz still had more to come.

The story opened with a post by Trump. He proposed asking for a 20% reimbursement on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, to cover the cost of US security. “Reimbursement” means being paid back for expenses. In other words, it was a proposed charge to cover security costs. The figure of 20% suddenly took center stage.

That day, Brent futures rose by $7.29, or 9.59%, and ended at $83.30. Those numbers alone made quite an impact.

But we should not end the search for a cause by saying, “The 20% plan pushed prices up!” Reuters linked the rise to the US naval blockade against Iran, which was set to begin the next day, and to worries about energy shipments through the Strait. There was too much going on behind the scenes for one striking comment to explain it all.

Then, the next day, the script changed. Trump posted that he would replace the reimbursement fee proposal with trade and investment deals involving Gulf countries and the United States. The focus shifted from charging cargo passing through the Strait to trade and investment with the United States. The 20% plan left the stage, and a different deal came in.

So Brent futures did not suddenly plunge. That is the key point in this story.

After the announcement, Brent futures briefly gave up some of their gains. Looking only at this moment, it might seem that tensions had eased. But before long, prices returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. It was too early to say, “That’s the end of it,” and roll the credits.

Concerns about the naval blockade, attacks, and tanker safety continued. Even though the fee proposal was replaced by another deal, worries about energy shipments did not disappear. The question of charges and the question of whether ships can pass safely are separate issues.

What we are looking at here is not crude oil prices overall, but Brent futures. And prices did not keep falling after the announcement. They first gave up some of their gains, then recovered. Flashy numbers grab the headlines. But when reading the market, we cannot set aside the issues of a blockade and safe transport. The end of the fee proposal and the end of worries about the Strait are two different things. There was still not enough to bring the curtain down.

## In one line
Brent futures soon recovered after Trump replaced his proposed 20% charge on cargo crossing the Strait of Hormuz.
