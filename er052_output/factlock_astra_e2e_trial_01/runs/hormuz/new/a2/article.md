# The 20% Plan Is Gone, but Brent Futures Are Still on Alert

When a flashy plan for a fee disappears, the market should feel relieved. You might think, “Okay, this is all over!” But Brent futures did not leave the scene. Even after the 20% plan left the headlines, the tense story around the Strait of Hormuz continued.

The story began with a post by Trump. He proposed charging 20% on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The money would pay for US security. “Reimbursement” means money paid back for costs. In other words, it was a suggested fee for security costs. The 20% figure suddenly took center stage.

That day, Brent futures rose by $7.29, or 9.59%. They ended at $83.30. Those numbers alone made quite an impact on the market.

But we should not say, “The 20% plan pushed prices up!” Reuters linked the rise to the US naval blockade against Iran. The blockade was due to begin the next day. Reuters also linked it to worries about energy shipments through the Strait. One striking comment could not explain all that was happening behind the scenes.

Then the next day, the story changed. Trump posted that he would replace the fee plan with trade and investment deals. These deals would involve Gulf countries and the United States. The focus moved away from fees on cargo passing through the Strait. It turned to trade and investment with the United States. The 20% plan left the stage, and a different deal took its place.

So Brent futures did not suddenly fall sharply. That is the key point in this story.

After the announcement, Brent futures briefly lost some of their gains. Looking only at that moment, it might seem tensions had eased. But soon, prices returned to a high level. They were close to where they had been before the announcement. It was too soon to say, “That’s the end,” and roll the credits.

Concerns about the naval blockade, attacks, and tanker safety continued. The fee plan was replaced by another deal. But worries about energy shipments did not go away. The fee question and the question of whether ships can pass safely are separate.

What we are looking at here is not crude oil prices overall. It is Brent futures. Prices did not keep falling after the announcement. They first lost some gains, then rose again. Flashy numbers grab the headlines. But when reading the market, we cannot ignore the blockade and safe transport. The end of the fee plan and the end of worries about the Strait are two different things. There was still not enough to bring the curtain down.

## In one line
Brent futures soon recovered after Trump replaced his proposed 20% fee on cargo crossing the Strait of Hormuz.
