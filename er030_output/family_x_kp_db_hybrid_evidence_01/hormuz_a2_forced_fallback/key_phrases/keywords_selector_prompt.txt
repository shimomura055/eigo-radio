# 20% Plan Taken Back—but the Oil Chart Was Not Done Yet

This news feels like a short play in three acts. Act One was “20%.” Act Two brought a surprise. Act Three was an unexpected move in oil prices.

The curtain rose on July 13. At 10:16 a.m., Trump posted about a US plan. The United States would seek a 20% charge on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The aim was to recover the cost of US efforts to keep the strait safe.

But “20%” was not a finished system. The post did not say who would pay. It did not say how the money would be collected. It did not say what law would support it. For the moment, only a large number stood at center stage.

The number stayed at center stage for about a day. Then the story took a sharp turn.

### The 20% plan changes overnight

About 24 hours and 48 minutes later, at 11:04 a.m. on July 14, Trump made another post. He said the 20% plan would be replaced by trade and investment projects between Gulf states and the US. He cited “very productive discussions” with Middle Eastern leaders. He also said no one should charge ships in the Strait. He said he disliked fees.

The change was sudden. But the dates must stay separate to understand the oil move. On July 13, Brent rose 9.59% and settled at $83.30. Reuters linked that rise to concern about a US sea blockade of Iran. The blockade was planned for the next day. Reuters also linked it to energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz.

### The chart refuses to stay down

After the withdrawal and replacement announcement, Brent crude futures briefly gave back some gains. Soon, they returned close to the high level before the announcement. At the time of reporting, Brent was up about 2.6%, above $85 a barrel. Its final settlement price was $84.73, up 1.7% from the day before.

## In one line

Concerns about US-Iran attacks, the sea blockade, and tanker safety continued on July 14. So the flashy 20% plan left the stage. But the chart pulled back only briefly before recovering. The policy turn and the oil chart’s “not over yet” move happened that same day.
