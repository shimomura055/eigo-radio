【B1 Article】
In a small kingdom, the ruler, Dionysius, was afraid of enemies. If anyone spoke against him, his guards could take that person to prison. Often, the ruler ordered executions without a fair trial.

One day, a shepherd named Melos stood before him.

“You punish people before you know the truth,” Melos said. “That is not justice.”

The ruler’s face became hard. “Then you will die.”

Melos’s sister was going to be married in three days. He fell to his knees.

“Please let me go to her wedding,” he said. “I will return before my death.”

The ruler laughed. “Why should I believe you?”

Melos’s friend, Selinuntius, stepped forward. “I will stay here in his place.”

So the ruler agreed. Melos had three days to go and return. If he did not come back, his friend would die.

Melos ran from the city. He reached the river, but heavy rain had made the water wild. The small boat broke against a rock. Melos fell into the cold river and nearly disappeared under the water. At last, he reached the other side, shaking and weak.

He hurried to the wedding. He smiled for his sister, but his eyes stayed on the road. When the wedding ended, he left at once.

On the way back, three attackers jumped from behind some trees. They took his horse and pushed him to the ground. Melos tried to stand, but pain filled his body. He walked through the night, then fell beside the road.

The sky was becoming bright. The time was almost finished.

Melos looked toward the city. “I cannot do this,” he whispered. “Perhaps my friend is already dead.”

For a moment, he wanted to hide in the forest.

Then he remembered his friend’s calm face. His friend had trusted him enough to face death.

“No,” Melos said. “I gave my word.”

He stood and ran.

At the city gate, the guards were leading his friend to the place of execution. Then Melos shouted, “Wait!”

Everyone turned. Melos fell before the ruler.

“I promised to return,” he said. “I am here.”

The ruler looked at the two friends. Slowly, he lowered his hand.

“Free him,” he said.

After that day, the ruler demanded fair trials. He had learned that trust and loyalty could be stronger than fear.
