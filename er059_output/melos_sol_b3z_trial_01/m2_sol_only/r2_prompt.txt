# Before Sunset

Melos stood before the ruler. He was only a shepherd, but he was not afraid. He looked straight at the ruler.

“You kill people without asking if they did anything wrong!” he shouted.

“The guards will kill you, too,” the ruler said.

Melos thought of his sister. “Her wedding is tomorrow. Give me three days. I promise to return.”

“And if you run away?”

His friend stepped forward. “Keep me here. I trust him.”

The ruler smiled. “Then you will die if he is not back by sunset on the third day.”

Melos looked at his friend.

“I will wait,” his friend said.

After the wedding, Melos started back. Heavy rain fell all night. By morning, the river had covered the bridge. There was only fast, dark water.

He could not wait. His friend would die if he was late.

He jumped in.

The water pulled him under. He fought to reach the other side. At last, he caught a tree branch and pulled himself out.

Wet and cold, he ran on.

Then three men blocked the road.

“Give us your money!”

“I have none!”

One man hit him with a stick. Melos pushed past him and ran. The men shouted behind him.

Soon, his legs began to shake. Each step was harder. He fell beside the road.

The sun was low. There was so little time.

He could stay here. He could rest. He could live.

But in the city, his friend would die.

“Why did I make that promise?” he whispered.

He closed his eyes. For a moment, he wanted to forget everything.

Then he remembered his friend’s face.

*I will wait.*

Melos opened his eyes. Nearby, water ran between some stones. He drank, then slowly stood.

His friend was still waiting.

He began to run again.

When Melos reached the city square, a guard was lifting his sword. His friend stood with his hands tied.

“Stop!” Melos cried. “I’m here!”

He fell at his friend’s feet.

“I almost left you,” he said.

His friend bent down. “But you came back.”

The ruler stared at them. For a moment, he said nothing.

“Let them both go,” he said at last. “I thought no one could trust another person so much.”

The guard cut the ropes.

Melos reached for his friend’s hand. This time, they left together.
