“You kill people because you think they are your enemies,” Melos said to the ruler of the kingdom. “You do not give them a fair trial. That is wrong.”

The ruler stood up. “Then you will die too.”

“Please give me three days,” said Melos. “My sister is getting married. I must go to her wedding. Then I will come back.”

His friend stepped forward. “I will stay here.”

“If Melos is not back before sunset on the third day, you will die in his place,” the ruler said.

“I trust him,” his friend answered.

After the wedding, Melos hurried towards the city. On the third morning, he reached a river. The water was high and fast. He tried to cross, but the water pulled him away. He fought his way to the other side.

He had lost hours.

Then some men attacked him on the road. He pushed past them and ran, but now his legs hurt. The sun was already low.

At last, he fell to his knees.

“I cannot do this,” he thought. “I could stay here. I could live.”

His friend would die. Melos knew it, but for a moment he did not want to stand up.

Then he remembered his friend’s voice: “I trust him.”

His friend was waiting. He believed Melos would return.

“I am coming,” Melos said.

He stood slowly. He took one step, then another. Soon he was running again.

At the city, the last light was leaving the sky. His friend stood before the ruler. A guard raised his sword.

“Stop!” Melos shouted. “I am here!”

The sword stopped. Melos reached his friend and held him tightly.

The ruler looked at the two men. For a long moment, he said nothing.

“You came back to die,” he said quietly. “And your friend trusted you to come.”

He told the guard to free them both.

Melos’s legs were still shaking. But his friend was safe, and the sword was lowered. They held each other, and this time neither had to let go.
