# The Last Light

Melos stood before the ruler of the kingdom. His heart beat fast, but his voice was clear.

“You kill people because you think they are your enemies,” he said. “You do not give them a fair trial. That is wrong.”

The ruler rose from his chair.

“Then you will die too.”

“Please give me three days,” Melos said. “My sister is getting married. Let me go to her wedding. Then I will return.”

His friend stepped forward.

“I will stay here.”

The ruler looked at him. “If Melos is not back before sunset on the third day, you will die in his place.”

“I trust him,” his friend said.

After the wedding, Melos hurried towards the city.

On the third morning, he reached a river. The water was high and fast. There was little time, so he stepped in.

The river pulled him away. He fought the water. At last, his feet touched land.

He was safe, but he had lost hours.

Further along the road, some men attacked him. Melos pushed past them and ran.

His legs hurt. Each step was harder than the last.

The sun sank lower.

At last, Melos fell to his knees.

He could stay here. He could live.

But his friend would die.

Melos looked down at the road. For a moment, he did not want to stand. He wanted the pain to stop.

Then he remembered his friend’s voice.

“I trust him.”

His friend was still waiting. Still hoping.

“I am coming,” Melos said.

He stood slowly.

One step. Then another.

Pain filled his legs, but he kept moving. Soon, he was running again.

When he reached the city, the last light was leaving the sky.

His friend stood before the ruler.

A guard raised his sword.

“Stop!” Melos shouted. “I am here!”

The sword stopped.

Melos ran to his friend and held him tightly. His legs shook. He could hardly speak.

The ruler watched them. For a long moment, he said nothing.

“You came back to die,” he said quietly. “And your friend trusted you to come.”

Then he told the guard to free them both.

The sword was down. His friend was safe.

Melos held him close as the light disappeared.

This time, neither had to let go.
