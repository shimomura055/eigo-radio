# The Last Light

Melos stood before the ruler of the kingdom.

“You kill people because you think they are your enemies,” he said. “You do not give them a fair trial. That is wrong.”

The ruler stood up. “Then you will die too.”

“Please give me three days,” Melos said. “My sister is getting married. I must go to her wedding. Then I will come back.”

His friend stepped forward. “I will stay here.”

“If Melos is not back before sunset on the third day, you will die in his place,” the ruler said.

“I trust him,” his friend answered.

After the wedding, Melos hurried towards the city. On the third morning, he reached a river. The water was high and fast.

He went into the water. It pulled him away. He fought to reach the other side. At last, he reached land.

But he had lost hours. Hours he could not get back.

Then some men attacked him on the road. Melos pushed past them and ran. His legs hurt, but he kept going.

The sun was low now. Melos fell to his knees.

“I cannot do this,” he thought. “I could stay here. I could live.”

His friend would die. Melos knew that. Still, for a moment, he did not want to get up.

Then he remembered his friend’s voice.

“I trust him.”

His friend was waiting. He still believed Melos would come back.

“I am coming,” Melos said.

He stood slowly. One step. Then another. His legs hurt with every step. But soon he was running again.

At the city, the last light was leaving the sky. His friend stood before the ruler.

A guard raised his sword.

“Stop!” Melos shouted. “I am here!”

The sword stopped. Melos reached his friend and held him tightly.

The ruler looked at them. For a long moment, he said nothing.

“You came back to die,” he said quietly. “And your friend trusted you to come.”

He told the guard to free them both.

Melos’s legs were still shaking. But his friend was safe. The sword was down.

They held each other. This time, neither had to let go.
