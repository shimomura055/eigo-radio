# The “20%” Vanished. Oil Prices Still Returned to Their Highs

The market hit the brakes. Moments later, it pressed the accelerator again. That was the pattern in oil this time.

It began on July 13. Trump posted that the US would charge 20% on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz to recover the costs the US bears to keep the strait safe.

“20%” was a powerful number for a headline. But the plan had no details: Who would pay? How would the money be collected? What law would support it? It was like putting up a price list before deciding where people would pay or how they would pay.

About 25 hours later, on July 14, Trump said the “20% reimbursement fee” plan would be replaced by trade and investment deals involving Gulf states and the US. He said this followed “very productive talks” with Middle Eastern leaders. He also said no one should charge ships using the strait, and that he did not like the idea of a fee.

The day before: “20%.” The next day: “I don’t like fees.” It was a major turn. The market now had to react.

### The market’s first pause

Right after the announcement, Brent crude futures briefly gave back some of their gains. With the extra cost and policy risk fading, oil might fall. The market seemed to pause and ask whether the main news had disappeared. But that pause did not last.

Brent soon returned to nearly its pre-announcement high. At the time of the report, it was up about 2.6% from the day before, above $85 a barrel. Its settlement price was $84.73, up 1.7%. The 20% plan was gone, but oil had returned to a high level, as if the market had braked once, checked behind it, and moved on.

But the larger story was still waiting.

### The risks behind the number

Even after the 20% plan was withdrawn, worries about attacks between the US and Iran, a blockade, and tanker safety had not disappeared. Those worries had already pushed prices up the day before. On July 13, Brent rose 9.59% and settled at $83.30. Reuters linked that rise to concerns about a blockade and energy transport.

Removing a flashy policy plan did not remove the security worries behind it or fear that energy shipments might be delayed. The withdrawal was not meaningless: prices did slow immediately. But the market was watching more than the biggest number. Even after “20%” disappeared, worries around the Strait remained, and oil quietly returned to its high.

## In one line

The 20% plan vanished, but the risks around the Strait of Hormuz remained, so oil prices climbed back.
