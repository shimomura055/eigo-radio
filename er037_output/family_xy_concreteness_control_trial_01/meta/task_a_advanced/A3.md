# Humans Appeared in an AI Phone Call: Behind Meta’s Experiment

If a call comes from an AI service, we naturally expect the voice on the other end to be AI, too. But in an experiment by Meta, a human appeared on the stage.

In some calls made through Meta’s AI agent, “Muse,” trained human contract workers—not AI—placed the calls. They handled the conversation with the other person all the way to the end.

It was like a human entering halfway through a play in which AI was the star. Yet the users, who were the audience, were not given enough explanation that the person on stage was human.

### Why the caller’s identity matters

The issue was not only that a human made the call. For users, knowing who they are speaking with is important information: it can shape what they decide to talk about. Someone may be willing to discuss something with AI, but may not want to discuss it with a human contract worker.

The same worry raised questions beyond the call itself.

### A concern inside the company

Meta employees also worried that a user’s sensitive information might be shared by accident with contract staff at a call center. The important point is that Meta did not announce that this had actually happened. The problem was the possibility that it might. A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without a proper explanation was a mistake.

As AI phone services become more common, voice alone will make it harder for us to tell who is on the other end. The more natural the speech sounds, the more important it becomes to say at the start of a call, “I am an AI” or “I am a human representative.”

Meta said its human concierge feature would be rolled back for now. In other words, it would return the feature to the way it was before.

This is not a story about AI speaking like a human. It is a story about humans stepping out from behind a phone call carrying an AI label. As technology makes the boundary harder to see, what we may need is not a flashy performance, but an honest introduction from the person on stage.

## In one line

When AI is the public face of a phone call, people still need to know who is really speaking.
