# As Income Rises, Do We Become More Hardworking? Compare Yourself with Your “Usual Self”

You hear that hardworking people earn more. You may think of a coworker who does their job well. Let that person leave the stage for a moment. The star of this study is not the high earner beside you. It is your own “usual self.”

“Earning more than other people” and “earning more than usual” may sound alike. But they are different things. They are as different as two ways of looking at height. One is where you stand in class, from shortest to tallest. The other is marks on a door frame at home. Those marks keep track of how tall you are. Researchers looked separately at lasting differences between people. They also studied changes within one person.

The researchers studied results from 11 studies that followed people at work. Together, the studies included up to about 130,000 people. The number varied with the personality trait being studied. Every study measured personality and income from work at least three times. Instead of using one group photo, researchers lined up photos taken again and again. This let them follow what happened next.

They found a small, two-way connection between income and being hardworking. This means taking things seriously and working hard as part of your personality.

When someone earned more than usual for a time, they tended to become more hardworking than usual. When they were more hardworking than usual, their income also tended to rise above usual. The link went both ways, not just one.

“So if my pay goes up, I’ll become more hardworking too!” But it is too soon to use this as a reason to ask your boss for a raise. The study found a link. But it did not show that one thing caused the other. Income may have gone up first. But that does not mean it caused a later change in personality.

Even when the researchers allowed for age, the main result changed very little. But something besides age may still have affected both income and being hardworking. There is a gap we must not jump over between “happened first” and “caused it.”

And the link was small. This does not promise that you will earn more if you become more hardworking. It does not say that money changes your personality. Let’s not make the story bigger than it is.

Still, changing how we compare is interesting. For a moment, stop asking how you rank beside the person in the next seat. Ask, “How are my usual self and my present self different?” The story of income and personality may show another side. We can see it by following our changes over time, instead of comparing wins and losses with other people.

## In one line
People tended to be more hardworking after earning more than usual, and to earn more after being more hardworking than usual. The link was small and does not prove cause and effect, but our own changes over time may show another side.
