# Broadcom’s Earnings: Leave During the End Credits, and You’ll Miss the Best Part

For anyone whose eyes start to close just from hearing “earnings announcement,” Broadcom’s latest results are interesting if you watch them like a movie. The main feature that has already played is impressive enough. But what gets the audience talking is the “next preview” afterward. Don’t put your popcorn away just yet.

First, let’s look at the results from the main feature. Revenue for the reported quarter was $29.591 billion for the company as a whole. AI chips made up $16.7 billion of that.

Those are big numbers right away, but let’s read the subtitles carefully. Neither figure is a wish about how much the company hopes to sell. These are actual results announced this time. In a movie, they would be scenes that have already been filmed and edited.

Now the lights go down, and the trailer begins.

According to the CEO, demand for the company’s special-purpose AI chips and related networking products remains very strong. The CEO sounded very confident about demand for the company.

Then, big words appear on the screen.

“Revenue for the next quarter is expected to be about $34.8 billion.”

It makes you want to lean forward and think, “Wow, is the sequel going to be even bigger?” But there’s an important note here. The roughly $34.8 billion is the revenue forecast for the whole company, not just AI chips. The figure doesn’t all belong to AI just because it comes right after a discussion of AI demand.

“Demand for AI is strong.” And “the company expects revenue of about $34.8 billion next quarter.” Seeing these two statements together makes us curious about what comes next. But even if they appear on the same screen, they are different pieces of information.

The trailer also has one important feature: even if it looks exciting, a big hit is not guaranteed.

The roughly $34.8 billion is not revenue the company has earned yet. The company also warns that actual results may differ from its forecast. The CEO’s confidence is worth watching, but it isn’t a receipt from the future.

In work terms, it’s the difference between “I met my goal this month” and “I think I can get this far next month.” It’s fine to applaud the second statement. But it’s still too early to put your name on a cake celebrating the achievement.

The key point this time is to look separately at the $29.591 billion in actual revenue and the forecast of about $34.8 billion for next quarter. What builds expectations between the two is the strong demand the CEO talks about.

How much of this preview will become the main feature in the next earnings report? We can put away the calculator, but that’s one question I’d like to take home.

## In one line
Broadcom reported $29.6 billion in quarterly revenue and forecast about $34.8 billion for the whole company next quarter. The CEO says demand for AI chips and networking remains strong, but that forecast is not AI-only revenue or a guaranteed result.
