# Broadcom’s Earnings: Leave During the End Credits, and You’ll Miss the Best Part

If “earnings announcement” makes your eyes start to close, watch Broadcom’s latest results like a movie. The main feature has already played, and it is impressive enough. But the “next preview” afterward is what gets people talking. Don’t put your popcorn away just yet.

First, let’s look at the results from the main feature. Broadcom’s total revenue for the quarter was $29.591 billion. AI chips made up $16.7 billion of that.

Those are big numbers, but let’s read the subtitles carefully. Neither number is a guess about how much the company hopes to sell. These are results the company has actually reported. In a movie, they would be scenes already filmed and edited.

Now the lights go down, and the trailer begins.

The CEO says demand is still very strong. This is for the company’s AI chips made for special jobs and related networking products. The CEO sounded very sure that demand was strong.

Then, big words appear on the screen.

“Revenue next quarter is expected to be about $34.8 billion.”

It makes you lean forward and think, “Wow, will the next film be even bigger?” But there is an important point here. The roughly $34.8 billion is the expected revenue for the whole company, not just AI chips. It does not all come from AI just because it comes after a talk about AI demand.

“Demand for AI is strong.” “The company expects about $34.8 billion in revenue next quarter.” Seeing these two statements together makes us wonder what comes next. But they are different pieces of information, even when they appear on the same screen.

The trailer also has one important point. Even if it looks exciting, a big hit is not certain.

The roughly $34.8 billion is not revenue the company has earned yet. The company also says actual results may be different from its forecast. The CEO’s confidence is worth watching, but it is not a receipt from the future.

At work, this is the difference between “I reached my goal this month” and “I think I can get this far next month.” It is fine to clap for the second statement. But it is still too early to put your name on a cake to celebrate the achievement.

The key point this time is to look at two numbers separately. One is $29.591 billion in actual revenue. The other is the forecast of about $34.8 billion for next quarter. The CEO’s words about strong demand build expectations between them.

How much of this preview will become the main feature in the next earnings report? We can put away the calculator, but that is one question I would like to take home.

## In one line
Broadcom reported $29.6 billion in quarterly revenue and expects about $34.8 billion for the whole company next quarter. The CEO says demand for AI chips and networking products is still strong, but the forecast is not only for AI and is not certain.
