# Broadcom Results: AI Is in the Lead, but Don’t Miss What Comes After the Main Feature

The movie ends, and the lights come on. You hold your box of popcorn. Then someone says, “Here is a special clip from the next film!” It would be a shame to look only at Broadcom’s results and leave. There is an interesting preview after them.

First, here are the main facts from the movie. Broadcom reported total revenue of $29.591 billion for the quarter. That is a long number, but remember one thing. It is an “actual result.” It shows sales that have already been recorded.

But the screen is still bright. Broadcom’s outlook for next quarter is about $34.8 billion in revenue. The number rises from $29.591 billion to about $34.8 billion. Now the story changes. We move from “How did this quarter go?” to “What happens next?”

Now AI chips are in the spotlight. Revenue from them was $16.7 billion. That is a big number. But do not add it to the company’s total revenue. The $29.591 billion is the total. The $16.7 billion from AI chips is part of it. Do not count it twice. That would be like adding the lead actor’s pay to the movie’s total budget again.

The CEO tells the story in the preview. Demand is still very strong for AI chips made for each customer, and for network products. This is when the numbers on the page start to feel exciting. You can almost hear someone say, “AI still has plenty of time on screen.”

But pause before you turn up the hopeful music all the way. “Very strong” is the CEO’s view of demand. This is not the same as revenue already recorded. Even strong words do not become actual results.

Remember to label the roughly $34.8 billion figure as the “company outlook,” too. It is a forecast for next quarter, not confirmed revenue. A trailer can be exciting, but you cannot judge the main film before it comes out.

Shopping in everyday life makes this easier to understand. “This product sold well.” “Customers are responding well.” “It looks like it will sell well next time.” Each gives you a clue about how the store is doing. But the money in the cash register, the manager’s view of how things are going, and the forecast for next time are separate things.

This is also the key to following these results: actual results, the view of demand, and the outlook for next time. Follow them as one story, but do not mix them up. AI’s flashy side may catch your eye. Still, keep the subtitles in view until the end. The trailer is out. The next report card will give us the answer.
