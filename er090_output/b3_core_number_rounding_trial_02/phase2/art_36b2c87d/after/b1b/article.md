# Broadcom Earnings: Even With AI in the Lead, Don’t Miss What Comes After the Main Feature

The movie ends, and the lights come up. You stand with your box of popcorn in your arms—and then: “Coming up, a special clip from the next film!” It would be a shame to look only at Broadcom’s results and leave. There is an interesting preview waiting after them.

First, the highlights from the main feature. Broadcom reported total revenue of $29.591 billion for the quarter. The long number may be hard to take in, but there is one thing to remember: this is an “actual result,” showing sales that have already been recorded.

But the screen has not gone dark yet. The company’s revenue outlook for the next quarter is about $34.8 billion. From $29.591 billion to about $34.8 billion. Here, the story shifts from “How did this quarter go?” to “What happens next?”

Now the spotlight is on AI chips. Revenue was $16.7 billion. It is a big number, but don’t let its size lead you to add it on top of the company’s total revenue. The $29.591 billion is the total; the $16.7 billion from AI chips is part of that total. Be careful not to count it twice, like adding the lead actor’s pay to the movie’s total budget a second time.

The CEO plays the narrator in the preview. Demand remains very strong for AI chips made for each customer and for network products. This is where the figures on the page start to feel exciting. You can almost hear: “AI still has plenty of time on screen.”

But before turning the hopeful music up all the way, hit pause. “Very strong” is the CEO’s view of demand. It is a different kind of information from revenue that has already been recorded. Even strong words do not turn into actual results.

Remember to put the label “company outlook” on the roughly $34.8 billion figure, too. It is a forecast for the next quarter, not confirmed revenue. No matter how exciting a trailer is, you cannot judge the main feature before it comes out.

Everyday shopping makes this easier to understand. “This product sold well.” “Customers are responding well.” “It looks like it will sell well next time.” Each gives you a clue about how the store is doing. But the money in the register, the manager’s sense of how things are going, and the forecast for what comes next are separate things.

That is also the key to following these results: actual results, the view of demand, and the outlook for next time. Follow them as one story, without mixing them together. Even if AI’s flashy side catches your eye, keep the subtitles in view to the end. The trailer is out. The next report card will give us the answer.
