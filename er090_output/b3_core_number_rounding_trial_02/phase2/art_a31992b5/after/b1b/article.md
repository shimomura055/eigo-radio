# Does a Higher Income Turn On the “Hardworking Switch”? The Key Is Changing Who You Compare With

“If people get paid more, do they become more serious about work?” “No, they get paid more because they work seriously, right?”

It’s a chicken-and-egg debate about income and personality. Both sides make you wonder, but before we rush to judge, there’s something we should check. In this discussion, who is being compared with whom?

A research team brought together data from 11 studies that tracked workers’ personalities and income from work. The studies included up to about 135,000 people, though the number varied depending on the personality trait being studied. Each study measured the same people’s income and personality at least three times. These were not one-time “personality and income checks.”

The key feature of this study is that it changes who is being compared.

Simply comparing Person A, who has a high income, with Person B, who has a low income, also mixes in differences they had from the start. So the researchers separated out stable differences between people, such as whether someone tends to have a higher income. They focused on “changes within each person.” The match-up was not Person A versus Person B, but each person versus their usual self.

This showed a small but interesting link. When a person’s income was higher than usual, they tended to be more hardworking than usual later on. Being hardworking is a personality trait related to taking one’s work seriously.

And the link did not go just one way. Being more hardworking than usual was also linked to having a higher income than usual later on. There was a small link not only from “income to personality,” but also from “personality to income.”

So, can we say, “A pay raise turns on the hardworking switch”?

That would be jumping the gun. Even if income was higher first and a person was more hardworking later, that does not prove the income caused it. The same is true of the link in the other direction. We know the order in time, but that does not tell us what caused what.

Taking age into account did not greatly change the main results or conclusions. Still, something other than age may have played a part. “It happened afterward” and “it happened because of that” may sound similar, but they are different things.

If you find yourself about to guess what someone’s personality is like from their income, try changing who you compare them with. Move from “What kind of person are they?” to “What changed within them?” The story of income and personality takes a different turn not when we add more people, but when we follow the same person closely.

## In one line
Following people over time and comparing them with their usual selves revealed small links in both directions between income and being hardworking. But the study cannot show that either one caused the other.
