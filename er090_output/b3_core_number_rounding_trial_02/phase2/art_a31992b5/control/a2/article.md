# The “Money-Making Personality” Contest Is on Hold for Now! The Star Is “Your Past Self”

“What kind of personality do people who earn money have?” A serious, hard-working person, or a friendly person with many contacts? Here begins the “money-making personality” contest in your head! But wait before getting excited. This time, the spotlight is not on the successful person beside you. It is on “your past self.”

The research team brought together information from 11 studies that followed the same people. The people in the studies had jobs. They answered questions about their personalities and income from work. The analysis included up to 134,515 people. The number changed depending on what the researchers studied.

The camera in this study points in a slightly different direction. The researchers looked separately at two kinds of difference. One kind was steady differences between people, like “That person earns a lot.” The other was change in one person, like “I earn more than I did before.” So they did not only compare where people stood in one group photo. They also looked at a series of photos of each person.

Now we come to “conscientiousness”: taking your work seriously and working hard. Within the same person, a rise in income had a small link to a later rise in conscientiousness. The reverse was also true: more conscientiousness had a small link to later income growth.

Oops, the arrows point both ways! Does becoming more serious make your wallet grow? It is too early to call this a rule for success. The links were small, and they do not prove that one thing caused the other.

We cannot know for sure that higher income made someone more serious. We also cannot know that becoming more serious made their income rise. Something else may be linked to both. We must not jump from “it happened later” to “it happened because of that.”

Still, this way of looking at things is interesting. Instead of ending the talk with “That’s just the kind of person I am,” we can look further. We can see income and personality as things that change in the same person. This gives us a different view from looking for other people’s “money-making type.”

The main thing to take from this is not a sure way to raise your income. It is a switch in the camera angle you use to look at yourself. For a moment, stop asking, “How much does that person earn?” Ask, “How am I different from the person I was back then?” What changes can you see in your series of photos?

## In one line
Across 11 studies, income and conscientiousness had small links in both directions within each person. These results do not show that one caused the other. They offer a new way to think about income and personality over time.
