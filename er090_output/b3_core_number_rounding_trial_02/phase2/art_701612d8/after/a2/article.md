# If You Stare at a Stone for Five Minutes, Will You Become a “Villain”?

Today’s star is a stone. It does not move. Nothing happens. You watch it for five minutes. If you think, “That’s enough—make something happen!” there is one more rule. While watching, you can take money away from another participant. Now you may feel bad.

An online study with people in the United States created this situation. By chance, each person watched one of two videos. One showed a stone, and nothing happened. The other showed a magician doing magic tricks. Sorry, stone, but you did not look like much fun beside magic.

But taking money away was not the only choice. Participants could also “add money” or “do nothing.” The amount could go up or down by 10 cents each time. It could change by up to 50 cents. So you could be mean to pass the time, but you could also be kind.

So, did the people who watched the stone press the “be mean” button?

The answer is, “It depends on the person.” In the boring group, people who liked seeing others suffer more were more likely to take money away. For people who felt this way less, boredom did not lead to more of this behavior.

So, personality matters. But it is too soon to end with that as today’s lesson. Here comes another experiment.

In this experiment, people could not choose to “add money” to the other person’s total. There was also a boring group. People in this group watched a stone video for five minutes. More people took money away in this group. This time, the increase happened whether people liked seeing others suffer a lot or only a little.

Oh. When we tried to explain things only through personality, the choices spoke up from the side. It is not only “who chooses” that matters. We also need to look at “what they can choose.” You may think this is about looking inside people’s minds. But it also makes you want to look at the menu on the screen.

But do not rush to call this a magic fix: “Add a kindness button, and meanness will disappear!” These results came from separate experiments. They did not show that letting people add money would stop everyone from being mean. And we cannot use results from short videos to understand our everyday relationships directly.

Even so, when you choose something on your phone, it is interesting to look at the choices and ask, “What can I choose here?” before asking, “What kind of person am I?” When I went looking for the switch for being mean, I began to wonder how the choices had been set up. The stone did nothing for five minutes, but it can really get a conversation rolling.

## In one line
With an option to add money, boredom led to more money-taking only among people who liked seeing others suffer more. Without it, boredom led to more taking for both kinds of personality, so choices matter too; but these separate studies do not show that a kind choice would stop everyone from being mean.
