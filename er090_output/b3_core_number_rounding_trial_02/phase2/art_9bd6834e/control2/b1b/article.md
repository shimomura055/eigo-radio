# The Bill Changed. But Tankers Are Still on Edge

“If the extra charge goes away, it should get a little cheaper, right?”

That is what your wallet would hope. But the oil market does not always read the room. The billing plan changed, but prices stayed high. This time, money was not the only main concern. Another was the question facing tankers at sea: “Can they get through safely?”

It started with a post by President Trump. He said he wanted a 20% payment on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was to get paid back for the cost of keeping the strait safe. To the companies moving the cargo, it was like a huge bill suddenly appearing halfway along the route.

That same day, the price of Brent oil futures rose 9.59%. That was quite a sharp jump. You might say, “So the extra charge came as a shock,” but we should not blame just one thing.

Reuters linked the rise to the US plan to blockade Iran at sea and worries about energy shipments passing through the Strait of Hormuz. In other words, the question was not just, “How much is the bill?” There was also a more basic worry: “Can the ships even get through in the first place?”

The next day, things changed quickly. Trump announced that he would replace the 20% payment with plans for Gulf countries to trade with the United States and invest there. The bill for cargo had turned into talk of a different deal.

Would the market breathe a sigh of relief now?

In fact, Brent futures briefly gave back some of their gains after the announcement. But be careful here. “The rise got smaller” does not mean “prices fell across the board.” Before long, prices were back near the high level they had been at before the announcement. The price of oil did not come down as people had hoped.

The key to understanding why lies at sea. Even after the announcement, worries continued about attacks by the United States and Iran, the blockade at sea, and tanker safety. Changing the way the bill worked did not make worries about the route go away too.

Think of home delivery. If you hear, “The extra delivery fee is being handled separately now,” you feel relieved for the moment. But what if you are then told, “We are still worried about whether your package will arrive safely”? Even a face that was just starting to brighten would freeze halfway. Delivery fees and delivery safety are separate issues.

The same was true of oil this time. Even as the question of “how much to pay” changed, worries about “whether it can be moved safely” remained. A bill can be rewritten. But that alone does not make the sea calm. The worry over the tankers was still there, beyond the price tag.

## In one line
Trump replaced his proposed 20% charge on cargo crossing the Strait of Hormuz with plans for Gulf countries to trade with and invest in the US. Brent futures briefly gave back some gains, but soon returned near their earlier highs as worries about the route and tanker safety continued.
