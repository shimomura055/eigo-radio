# The Disappearing “20%”: Why the Oil Market Was Still Worried

If someone takes back a bill, prices return to normal. If only the news worked that way, reading it would be easy. But the oil market was not so simple. The proposed 20% payment was gone. Yet worries still floated out at sea.

It began with a post by President Trump. He proposed a 20% charge on all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The U.S. would get back the money it spent keeping the strait safe. It was like someone suddenly holding out a bill on one of the world’s most important sea routes. “Here’s the bill,” they said.

That day, Brent futures rose about 10%. These are a guide to oil prices. It is easy to say, “I see! The market was shocked by the 20% bill!” But wait a moment.

Reuters said the rise was linked to a U.S. naval blockade against Iran. The blockade was set to begin the next day. There were also worries about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. The bill was not the only thing behind the story.

Then, the next day, the story changed. Trump said he would replace the 20% payment plan. Instead, Gulf countries would make deals to trade with and invest in the U.S.

But the oil market could not say, “Then we can relax and watch prices fall.” Yahoo Finance said prices fell for a while. But they soon returned to a high level, close to where they were before the announcement. They began to fall, then rose again. They did not move in a simple way.

“The 20% bill is gone, so why?”

To find the answer, we need to look away from the page and toward the sea. Worries about attacks, a naval blockade, and tanker safety continued. Paying 20% was one question. Whether ships could pass safely was another.

A number can be taken back with one announcement. But that does not settle worries about the route. “Taking back the payment plan” is not the same as “showing that ships can travel safely.” We can better understand these price changes by noticing the difference.

Even a faraway strait affects our wallets through gasoline prices. Next time you see a headline about rising oil prices, check what is gone and what is still there. Even after the bill leaves the scene, tankers are still making their journeys.
