# The Bill Was Withdrawn, but Worries at Sea Remained

A proposal to demand a 20% payment was withdrawn. So it is tempting to hope, “Could crude oil get cheaper too?” But the market was not so quick to agree. Prices first gave up some of their gains, then returned to a high level. Another problem was still hidden behind the price tag.

It all began with a post by President Trump. He said that all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz should pay 20%. The idea was to get back the cost to the US of keeping the strait safe. In other words, it was a proposal to ask others to “cover the cost of security.”

That same day, Brent futures, a global benchmark for crude oil, rose 9.59%. It might be tempting to write a headline saying, “The market is shocked by the 20% fee!” But wait a moment. Just because a number stands out does not mean it tells the whole story.

Reuters linked the rise to a US naval blockade of Iran planned for the next day, and to worries about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. The difficult question was not only “How much will it cost?” but also “Can the goods be shipped safely at all?”

The next day, Trump changed course. He announced that he would replace the 20% fee with deals involving trade between Gulf states and the US, and investment in the US. The proposal to charge cargo was withdrawn. The “20%” had disappeared from the bill.

But the market did not break into applause and send oil prices straight down. After the announcement, Brent futures first gave up some of their gains, but soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. Withdrawing the fee alone did not keep prices falling.

Worries about attacks between the US and Iran, a naval blockade, and the safety of tankers remained even after the announcement. The talk of a fee left the scene, but worries at sea did not leave with it.

If this were online shopping, it would go like this: “The shipping fee issue has been settled. But we’re worried whether your package will arrive safely.” In that case, you would want to check the delivery status again and again before you could relax. The payment issue and the shipping issue are separate.

This time, “20%” looked like the flashy star of the story. But what may have stayed on the market’s mind was the fate of ships moving ahead on their journeys. A bill can be rewritten. But that alone cannot put up a sign at sea saying, “It’s safe.”

## In one line
Trump withdrew his proposal for a 20% fee on cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz, but oil prices soon returned to a high level. Worries about attacks, the naval blockade, and tanker safety remained.
