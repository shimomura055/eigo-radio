# The Disappearing “20%”: Why the Oil Market Wasn’t Reassured

If a bill is pulled back, price tags return to normal. If things were that simple, reading the news would be easy. But the oil market was not so simple. The proposed “20%” payment was gone, but there were still worries floating out at sea.

It all began with a post by President Trump. He proposed requiring a 20% payment for all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz. The idea was that the U.S. would get back the money it spent to keep the strait safe. It was as if someone had suddenly held out a bill at one of the world’s most important sea routes and said, “Here’s the bill.”

That day, Brent futures, a benchmark for oil prices, rose about 10%. It is tempting to sum up the story by saying, “I see! The market was shocked by the 30% bill!” But wait a moment.

Reuters reported that the rise was linked to a U.S. naval blockade against Iran, which was due to begin the next day, and to concerns about energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz. The bill was not the only thing driving the story.

Then the next day, the story changed. Trump announced that he would replace the 20% payment proposal with deals by Gulf countries to trade with and invest in the U.S.

But that did not mean the oil market could say, “Then we can relax and watch prices fall.” According to Yahoo Finance, prices fell back for a while, but soon returned to a high level close to where they had been before the announcement. They started to fall, then rose again. They did not move in a simple way.

“The bill proposal is gone, so why?”

To find the answer, we need to shift our view from the page to the sea. Worries about attacks, a naval blockade, and tanker safety continued. Whether to pay 20% and whether ships could pass safely were separate issues.

A figure can be withdrawn with a single announcement, but that does not mean worries about the route are settled too. “Withdrawing the payment proposal” is not the same as “confirming that transport is safe.” The price moves this time are easier to understand when we pay attention to that difference.

Even a distant strait connects to our wallets through gasoline prices. The next time you see a headline about rising oil prices, check not only what has disappeared, but also what remains. Even after the bill leaves the scene, tankers are still making their journeys.

## In one line
Trump dropped his proposed 20% payment on Strait of Hormuz cargo, but oil prices soon returned near their earlier high. Dropping the proposal did not settle separate worries about attacks, a naval blockade, or tanker safety.
