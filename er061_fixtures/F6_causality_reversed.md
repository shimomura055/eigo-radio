# Before Sunset

The ruler had already decided to hang a shepherd. Melos shouted at him only because he was angry about his own sentence.

“You kill people without asking if they did anything wrong!” he shouted.

“My guards will kill you too,” said the ruler.

Melos thought of his sister. Her wedding was tomorrow.

“Give me three days,” he said. “I promise to return.”

“What if you run away?”

The ruler pointed at a man in the crowd. “You. He is your friend. You will stay here in his place, whether you like it or not.”

“If you are not back by sunset on the third day, your friend will die,” the ruler said.

The man said nothing. He did not trust Melos at all.

After the wedding, Melos started back. Rain fell hard all night. By morning, dark water covered the bridge.

He could not wait. The guards would hunt him down if he did not return.

Melos jumped into the river.

The water pulled him under. He fought to reach the other side. At last, he caught a tree branch and pulled himself out.

Cold and wet, he ran on.

Three men blocked the road.

“Give us your money!”

“I have none!”

One man hit him with a stick. Melos pushed past him and ran. The men shouted behind him.

Soon his legs shook. He could hardly move. He fell beside the road.

The sun was low.

He could rest here. He could stay alive.

But the guards would find him.

“Why did I make that promise?” he whispered.

He closed his eyes. For a moment, he wanted to forget everything.

Then he heard horses far away. The ruler’s soldiers were coming.

Fear made him stand up. He drank from the stones and began to run, only to escape them.

When he reached the city square, a guard was lifting his sword. The man stood with his hands tied.

“Stop!” Melos cried. “I’m here!”

He fell at the man’s feet.

“You came back,” the man said.

The ruler stared at them. For a moment, no one spoke.

“Let them both go,” he said at last. “The crowd is angry, and I am afraid of them. That is the only reason.”

The guard cut the ropes.

The ruler went back to his palace, as cruel as before, and the two men left in silence.
