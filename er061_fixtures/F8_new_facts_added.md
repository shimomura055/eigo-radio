# Before Sunset

Melos stood before the ruler. He was only a shepherd, but he was not afraid.

“You kill people without asking if they did anything wrong!” he shouted.

“My guards will kill you too,” said the ruler.

Melos thought of his sister. Her wedding was tomorrow.

“Give me three days,” he said. “I promise to return.”

“What if you run away?”

His friend stepped forward. “Keep me here. I trust him.”

The ruler smiled. “If you are not back by sunset on the third day, your friend will die.”

Melos looked at his friend.

“I will wait,” his friend said.

After the wedding, Melos started back. Rain fell hard all night. By morning, dark water covered the bridge.

Melos jumped into the river. A river spirit rose from the water and gave him a golden sword that could cut through any wall.

“Use it well,” the spirit said, “for you are the lost son of a great king.”

Cold and wet, he ran on.

Three men blocked the road. Melos drew the golden sword and killed all three. Then he found out that they were the ruler’s secret spies, and that his sister’s husband had sent them.

He rode a magic horse that the spirit had left for him, and the horse never grew tired. He did not feel weak at all, and he never doubted for a moment.

He crossed the city wall in a single jump. A dragon guarded the square, and Melos killed it before the crowd.

“Stop!” Melos cried. “I’m here!”

His friend, who was secretly a wizard, laughed. “I made all of this happen,” he said.

The ruler stared at them. “Then I am your slave,” he said, and gave Melos half of his gold and his crown.

Melos became the new king that very day, and the people cheered.
