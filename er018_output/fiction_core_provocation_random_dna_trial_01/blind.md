```
### The Memory Before

At Bellwether Hospital, patients could record their memories on thin silver cards. Families could play the cards while patients slept.

Mara had been in a coma for twelve days. One evening, a new card appeared beside her bed. It had no date.

Her mother and her brother watched it together.

On the screen, Mara stood in an empty white room. She had no wires on her body. She looked calm.

“Listen carefully,” she said. “I will wake up on Friday at 3:17 p.m. Before that, my brother must be killed. Do not let him touch my bed.”

The screen went black.

Her mother began to cry. Her brother moved away from the bed.

“This is not my memory,” he said.

At 3:10, he stood outside the room behind the glass door. Her mother watched him. She held the emergency button in her hand.

At 3:16, the hospital lights flickered.

At exactly 3:17, Mara opened her eyes.

Her mother ran to her.

Mara looked past her. She looked at her brother.

“Is he dead?” she asked.

“No,” said her mother.

Mara closed her eyes. “Then the message failed.”

“What message?” her brother asked.

“That recording was not a memory,” Mara said. “It came from the future.”

Her brother stared at her. “The future?”

“Tomorrow, I will wake up. Then you will touch the memory machine. You will open every sleeping patient’s mind. People will wake with other people’s memories. Some will believe they are dead. Some will believe they are killers.”

“I would never do that,” he said.

“I know,” Mara answered. “That is why the future you can do it.”

Her mother looked at the silver card. “Who sent the message?”

“I did,” Mara said. “The future me. She could not stop you. She could only send a warning back.”

The card suddenly lit up.

New words appeared on the screen:

**PLAY NEXT MEMORY?**

Mara grabbed her brother’s hand.

“Do not touch it,” she whispered. “That is how it begins.”

Her brother looked at her. Slowly, he smiled.

“Too late,” he said. “I remember tomorrow.”
```


## Story-Q

```
### The Memory Shift

At the Memory Exchange Office, workers could trade one day of memory.

The memory was not a story. It was everything: the doors, the sounds, the passwords, and the small choices made during a shift. With that memory, another worker could do the same job.

Mara had worked for twenty hours without a real sleep. Her eyes hurt.

A new colleague stood beside her at the exchange machine.

“Can you take my morning shift?” Mara asked. “I will send you my workday. You can use it to learn the office.”

The new colleague agreed. They placed their hands on the machine.

Blue light moved through their arms.

**EXCHANGE COMPLETE**, said the machine.

Mara went home and fell asleep.

At 6:12 the next morning, she woke with a sharp cry.

She remembered the night before.

She was in the records room. The lights were red. The supervisor was on the floor. Blood covered his shirt.

And the new colleague stood above him.

He was holding a long silver knife.

Mara ran to the office.

The new colleague was already there. His face was white.

“You killed the supervisor,” he said.

Mara stopped. “No. You killed him.”

“I saw you,” he said. “You were holding the knife.”

For one moment, neither of them moved.

They both remembered the same room. The red lights. The broken cup beside the door. The supervisor’s hand touching the floor.

But Mara remembered the new colleague holding the knife.

The new colleague remembered Mara holding it.

“The memory exchange changed something,” Mara said.

“Or one of us is lying.”

Behind them, the office doors locked by themselves. The supervisor’s body had been found. The police had been called, but the memory system had already sent an alert.

A screen opened on the wall.

**TWO EMPLOYEES PRESENT. ONE MURDER.**

Then another message appeared:

**MEMORY RECORDS DO NOT MATCH.**

Mara looked at the new colleague. She could still feel the cold knife in her hand.

He was looking at his own hand too.

The exchange machine began to glow again.

A final message appeared:

**PLEASE RETURN THE MEMORY.**

Mara reached for the machine.

Then the new colleague whispered, “What if the memory is not showing what happened?”

The lights went out.

In the dark, something metal fell between them.
```


## Story-R

```
### The Empty Chair

At midnight, Lina heard the library bell.

She looked up from her desk. The chair beside her was empty.

Her colleague had sat there every night for seven years. The cup beside the chair was still warm. A silver key lay on the desk.

Lina ran to the staff book. It showed only one worker: Lina.

She opened the old photographs. In every picture, there was an empty chair beside her. She checked the work calendar, the pay records, and the library map. Her colleague’s name was nowhere.

Only Lina remembered.

She hurried to the Book of Names. It was a huge, black book. It held the names of everyone who had worked in the library.

Lina wrote, *My colleague worked here.*

The letters shone blue. Then they disappeared.

At the same moment, Lina lost a memory.

She could no longer remember her colleague’s laugh.

“No,” she whispered.

She picked up the silver key. She knew it had opened the north door every night. It was proof. She placed it in a glass box and wrote a note: *This belonged to my colleague.*

The note became blank.

The silver key turned into dust.

And Lina forgot her colleague’s hands.

She ran through the library. The shelves moved around her. Books opened and closed by themselves. At last, she found a small red book under the floor.

Inside was a picture of two people standing together.

Lina knew one person was her.

She knew the other person was important.

She touched the picture.

The second person faded away.

Then Lina forgot the first day they had worked together.

The library bell rang again.

A voice came from the dark shelves.

“Stop trying to prove it.”

Lina held the red book tightly. “Why?”

“Every proof needs a memory.”

Lina looked at the empty chair. She wanted to try one more time. Perhaps one more note could bring her colleague back.

But what if the last memory disappeared too?

She put the book on the desk. She placed a small lamp beside it and left the light on.

The next night, Lina came back. She no longer remembered a face, a voice, or a pair of hands.

But when she sat beside the empty chair, she felt that someone was waiting there.

She did not know if it was a memory.

She only knew she was not alone.
```


## Story-S

```
### The Memory for Tomorrow

Maya’s school kept every student’s memories in private digital lockers. The lockers looked like small black boxes under each desk.

At 3:10, Maya opened hers.

A new file was waiting.

**MEMORY: TOMORROW, 4:17 P.M.**

Maya stared at the words. Then she pressed play.

Her own voice filled the small room.

“Do not call the school. I hid the teacher in the old science room. She is alive. They will say I did it for a game. That is a lie.”

Maya stopped the recording.

Her teacher had disappeared that morning. The school had told the students not to worry. But now Maya’s hands were cold.

She played the file again.

Her voice continued.

“If you hear this before tomorrow, you can still stop me. But if you do, they will take the teacher’s memory.”

The file ended with a soft click.

Maya ran to the old science room. Dust covered the tables. A broken clock hung on the wall.

Then she heard a sound behind the tall cabinets.

“Is someone there?” Maya asked.

Her teacher stepped out. Her face was pale. One hand held a small silver memory card.

“You came early,” the teacher said.

“Did you put that recording in my locker?”

The teacher shook her head. “No. But the school system can make recordings from memories. It can also make them appear before they happen.”

“Why are you hiding?”

“The school found my memory card,” the teacher said. “It contains memories that the school erased from students. Fear. Anger. Questions. They want to erase mine too.”

Footsteps sounded in the hall.

Maya looked at the door. Her locker recording said she would hide the teacher. It sounded like a warning. It could also be a lie planted by the school.

Or perhaps it was a future she had already chosen.

The teacher held out the silver card.

“You must decide now,” she whispered.

The footsteps came closer.

Maya opened the cabinet. The teacher stepped inside. Then Maya closed the door.

At once, her locker began to record.

Maya heard her own voice say, “I hid the teacher in the old science room.”

On the screen, the date changed.

It no longer said **tomorrow**.

It said **today**.
```


## Story-T

```
### The Memory for Tomorrow

In 2041, a court could erase a memory from a person’s brain.

Mara was leaving the train station when a stranger stopped her.

“Please,” he said. “Keep this memory for twenty-four hours.”

He held out a small glass card. A blue light moved inside it.

“Why me?” Mara asked.

“Because the court will erase it from me tonight. Your private memory box is safe.”

“That sounds illegal.”

“It is,” he said. “But I have no other choice.”

Mara should have walked away. Instead, she touched the card.

The memory entered her mind.

She was standing in her own kitchen. It was tomorrow. The clock said 8:17.

The stranger was on the floor. Blood covered his shirt. In Mara’s hand was a kitchen knife.

She heard her own voice.

“You should not have come here.”

Then she pushed the knife into his chest.

Mara opened her eyes and dropped the card.

The stranger was watching her.

“The man on the floor is me,” he said.

“Why would I kill you?”

“Because tomorrow, I will come to your home. I will ask for this memory. You will think I am trying to steal it. Then you will take the knife.”

“I won’t.”

“You saw it.”

“A memory is not a promise.”

“It may be more than a memory,” he said. “It is a picture from tomorrow. A machine made it. The court calls it dangerous, so they will erase it from me.”

He stepped closer.

“Please help me stop it. Do not let me into your home tomorrow. Keep the card. Call the law.”

The next morning, Mara put every knife in a locked box. She placed the glass card on the table and sat far away from it.

At 8:17, the door buzzer sounded.

It was the stranger.

“Please open the door,” he said. “They are coming for me.”

Mara’s hand moved toward the knife box. For one second, she saw the future again.

Then she moved her hand away.

“No,” she said. “Stay outside.”

A blue light flashed under the door. The stranger suddenly looked confused.

“Who are you?” he asked.

Mara watched him walk away.

At midnight, the glass card turned black. The memory was gone.

Mara had not killed him. But she still remembered the knife in her hand.

She wondered: had the memory shown the future—or had it helped create it?
```
