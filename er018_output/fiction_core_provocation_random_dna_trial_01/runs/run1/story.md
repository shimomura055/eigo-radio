### The Memory for Tomorrow

In 2041, a court could erase a memory from a person’s brain.

Mara was leaving the train station when a stranger stopped her.

“Please,” he said. “Keep this memory for twenty-four hours.”

He held out a small glass card. A blue light moved inside it.

“Why me?” Mara asked.

“Because the court will erase it from me tonight. Your private memory box is safe.”

“That sounds illegal.”

“It is,” he said. “But I have no other choice.”

Mara should have walked away. Instead, she touched the card.

The memory entered her mind.

She was standing in her own kitchen. It was tomorrow. The clock said 8:17.

The stranger was on the floor. Blood covered his shirt. In Mara’s hand was a kitchen knife.

She heard her own voice.

“You should not have come here.”

Then she pushed the knife into his chest.

Mara opened her eyes and dropped the card.

The stranger was watching her.

“The man on the floor is me,” he said.

“Why would I kill you?”

“Because tomorrow, I will come to your home. I will ask for this memory. You will think I am trying to steal it. Then you will take the knife.”

“I won’t.”

“You saw it.”

“A memory is not a promise.”

“It may be more than a memory,” he said. “It is a picture from tomorrow. A machine made it. The court calls it dangerous, so they will erase it from me.”

He stepped closer.

“Please help me stop it. Do not let me into your home tomorrow. Keep the card. Call the law.”

The next morning, Mara put every knife in a locked box. She placed the glass card on the table and sat far away from it.

At 8:17, the door buzzer sounded.

It was the stranger.

“Please open the door,” he said. “They are coming for me.”

Mara’s hand moved toward the knife box. For one second, she saw the future again.

Then she moved her hand away.

“No,” she said. “Stay outside.”

A blue light flashed under the door. The stranger suddenly looked confused.

“Who are you?” he asked.

Mara watched him walk away.

At midnight, the glass card turned black. The memory was gone.

Mara had not killed him. But she still remembered the knife in her hand.

She wondered: had the memory shown the future—or had it helped create it?
