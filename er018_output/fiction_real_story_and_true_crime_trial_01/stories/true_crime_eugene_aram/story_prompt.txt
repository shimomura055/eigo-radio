[Story Seed -- a real, documented historical crime case, faithfully retold; this is NOT a summary to reproduce verbatim, but facts/names/dates must not be changed]
- Eugene Aram and Richard Houseman murder Daniel Clarke after persuading him to go out at night to discuss the silver and jewels.
- Clarke disappears, while Aram continues a respectable life as a teacher for fourteen years.
- A labourer accidentally finds human bones at St. Robert’s Cave near Knaresborough.
- Houseman panics when questioned about the bones, then confesses and gives information that leads to the second burial site.
- Aram is arrested and gives an eloquent defence, but the physical evidence convinces the jury quickly.
- Aram privately admits his guilt before being executed at York on 16 August 1759.

[How to convert it]
Keep the historical English setting, the real names, dates, and order of events, and write the piece in third person. Shape it as a matter-of-fact Historical True Crime story of about 300–350 words for A2 learners, using simple sentences and explaining only the essential period details. Do not invent dialogue, motives, or feelings; paraphrase the documented evidence and Houseman’s confession while contrasting Aram’s learned defence with the bones and the final outcome.

[Common principles for this story family]
A good premise here makes the listener want to know what happens next. Prefer concrete events over abstract or literary implication. Keep to a small cast, one time frame, and few places. Meaning may come at the end, but the story itself must be clear first.

[Your single job]
Write a short true-crime story built from the Story Seed above. Make a reader genuinely feel some tension and curiosity about how the case unfolds. Do not follow a fixed structure -- choose whatever narrative shape makes this real case land most strongly. This must be a faithful, simplified retelling of the real historical event: do not invent facts, dialogue, motives, or details that are not part of the Story Seed. You may compress, omit minor detail, or paraphrase in your own words for length and clarity, and you may state plainly where something is uncertain, but do not add invented content presented as fact. Do not needlessly sensationalize violence or make victim descriptions more graphic than necessary, but also do not sand the story down until it loses what made this case genuinely striking.

[Listening-friendliness -- an editorial goal, not a hard rule]

Try to keep it easy for a listener to follow: avoid introducing more named people than necessary, avoid jumping between many different times or places, avoid long strings of abstract explanation, and try to keep it reasonably clear who is being discussed at each point.
