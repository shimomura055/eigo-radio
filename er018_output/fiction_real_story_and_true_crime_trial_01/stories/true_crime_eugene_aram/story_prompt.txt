[Story Seed -- a real, documented historical crime case, faithfully retold; this is NOT a summary to reproduce verbatim, but facts/names/dates must not be changed]
- Eugene Aram and Richard Houseman murder Daniel Clarke after persuading him to walk out at night to discuss how to dispose of his silver and jewels.
- Aram remains apparently respectable and works as a teacher for fourteen years while Clarke is believed to have disappeared.
- A labourer accidentally finds a human skeleton while digging for stone near St. Robert's Cave, leading to a coroner's inquest and suspicion of Aram and Houseman.
- When asked to handle a bone, Houseman exclaims, "This is no more Daniel Clarke's bone than it is mine!" This remark makes people suspect him because they wonder how he could know that without seeing the real bones.
- Houseman's confession identifies the true burial place, with Clarke's head lying a little farther to the right than the first skeleton; digging there reveals a second skeleton exactly where Houseman said it was.
- Aram is arrested, gives an eloquent written defence, is quickly convicted despite it, privately confesses guilt to attending clergymen, and is executed at York on 16 August 1759.

[How to convert it]
Write a 320-380-word, third-person, matter-of-fact historical true-crime story for A2 learners, keeping the 18th-century English setting and the real names, dates, and sequence of events. A good premise here makes the listener want to know what happens next. Prefer concrete events over abstract or literary implication. Keep to a small cast, one time frame, and few places. Meaning may come at the end, but the story itself must be clear first.
