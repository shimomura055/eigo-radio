### The Second Skeleton

In Knaresborough, on the night of 8 February 1745, Daniel Clarke went out with Eugene Aram and Richard Houseman. They had persuaded him to go with them. They wanted to discuss how to dispose of the silver and jewels Clarke had gathered on credit. That night, Aram and Houseman murdered Clarke.

Afterwards, people assumed that Clarke had left with the goods. This belief helped hide the crime. For fourteen years, Eugene Aram remained outwardly respectable. He worked as a schoolmaster and lived in plain sight. No one brought him to trial. Clarke’s body was still hidden, and the place of burial was not known.

Then a possible burial place was uncovered. People dug there and found a bone. Richard Houseman was questioned about it. He said exactly, “This is no more Daniel Clarke’s bone than it is mine!”

This answer made people suspicious. How could Houseman know that the bone was not Clarke’s? People wondered if he had seen the real bones.

Houseman then confessed. He said that the true burial place was different. Clarke’s head lay a little farther to the right than the first skeleton found. People dug in the place Houseman identified. There they found a second skeleton, exactly where he had said it would be.

The new discovery connected Houseman to the murder. It also pointed to Eugene Aram, who had hidden in plain sight for fourteen years as a respectable schoolmaster. Aram was arrested and brought to trial.

At the trial, Aram offered an eloquent and learned defence. He spoke with great knowledge, but the physical evidence led to his conviction. The bones and Houseman’s confession were too strong.

Eugene Aram was executed at York on 16 August 1759.
