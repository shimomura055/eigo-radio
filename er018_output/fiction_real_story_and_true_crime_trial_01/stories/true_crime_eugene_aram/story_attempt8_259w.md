### The Second Skeleton

In Knaresborough, England, Daniel Clarke had silver and jewels that he had gathered on credit. On the night of 8 February 1745, Eugene Aram and Richard Houseman persuaded him to go out with them. They said they would discuss how to dispose of the goods.

Clarke did not return. After that night, people assumed that he had left with the silver and jewels. No one knew where he was.

Eugene Aram continued to live an outwardly respectable life. He worked as a schoolmaster. For fourteen years, he stayed in plain sight, while the case seemed to disappear.

Then a possible burial place was uncovered. People dug there and found a skeleton. A bone from it was shown to Richard Houseman. He was questioned about it.

Houseman replied, “This is no more Daniel Clarke’s bone than it is mine!”

His words made people suspicious. How could he know that the bone was not Clarke’s if he had not seen Clarke’s real bones?

Houseman then confessed that the first skeleton was not Clarke. He said that Clarke’s head lay a little farther to the right. People dug in the place Houseman identified. There, they found a second skeleton, exactly where he had said it would be.

The discovery brought the old disappearance back into the open. The evidence connected Eugene Aram and Richard Houseman with Daniel Clarke’s murder.

At his trial, Aram gave an eloquent and learned defence. But the physical evidence was strong. He was convicted.

Eugene Aram was executed at York on 16 August 1759.
