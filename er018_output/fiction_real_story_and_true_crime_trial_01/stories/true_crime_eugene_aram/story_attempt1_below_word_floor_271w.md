## The Bones at St. Robert’s Cave

Daniel Clarke went out at night with Eugene Aram and Richard Houseman. They had persuaded him to go with them to discuss silver and jewels. Instead, Aram and Houseman murdered him.

After that night, Clarke disappeared. There was no body to explain what had happened. Eugene Aram, however, continued to live a respectable life for fourteen years. He worked as a teacher. During this time, the crime was not solved.

Then a labourer accidentally found human bones at St. Robert’s Cave near Knaresborough. The discovery changed the case. It also raised a serious question: who had been buried there?

Richard Houseman was questioned about the bones. He panicked and confessed. He also gave information about another burial site. This information led to a second place where a body had been hidden.

Eugene Aram was arrested. At his trial, he gave an eloquent defence. He used learned and careful words. His defence showed that he was an educated man. But the physical evidence was difficult to escape. The bones and the information from Houseman gave the case a clear direction. The jury was convinced quickly.

The case ended with a sharp contrast. In public, Aram defended himself with learned words. In private, before he was executed, he admitted his guilt.

Eugene Aram was executed at York on 16 August 1759.

For fourteen years, he had seemed to have a respectable life. Then a labourer’s accident brought the hidden crime back. Houseman’s panic and confession led to another burial site. The bones changed everything. In the end, Aram’s eloquent defence could not defeat the physical evidence.
