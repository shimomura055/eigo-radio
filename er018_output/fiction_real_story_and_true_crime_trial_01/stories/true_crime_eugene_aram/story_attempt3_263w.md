### The Bone at St. Robert’s Cave

On the night of 8 February 1745, Daniel Clarke had silver and jewels with him. He had gathered them on credit. Eugene Aram and Richard Houseman took him out into the night and murdered him.

Then Clarke disappeared.

For fourteen years, people accepted that he was gone. During this time, Eugene Aram lived and worked as a teacher in different English towns. He appeared to be a respectable man. Nothing connected him openly with Clarke’s disappearance.

In 1758, a labourer was working near St. Robert’s Cave, close to Knaresborough. By accident, he uncovered a human skeleton.

The discovery brought back old questions. Aram’s wife was estranged from him, and she said that she suspected Aram and Houseman. The authorities questioned Houseman about the bones.

They asked him to handle one of them. Houseman looked at it and cried, “This is no more Daniel Clarke’s bone than it is mine!”

His words were strange. How could he be so certain? People began to think that Houseman had seen the real bones of Daniel Clarke.

Houseman confessed. He said that the true burial place was a little farther to the right. He also said that the head was beyond the first skeleton.

People dug in the place he described. There, they found a second skeleton. It was exactly where Houseman had said it would be.

Eugene Aram was arrested. He wrote an eloquent defence, but he was quickly convicted. Later, in private, he confessed that he was guilty.

On 16 August 1759, Eugene Aram was executed at York.
