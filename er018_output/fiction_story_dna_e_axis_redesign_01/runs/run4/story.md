### The Memory Trade

In 2068, Mina’s company did not pay with money.

Workers paid with memories.

A good memory could buy a meal. A painful memory could buy a new phone. A large memory could pay a month’s rent.

Mina was a junior worker in the robot room. Her job was to check the machines before they left the building.

One night, her colleague opened a safety gate. He wanted to fix a robot quickly. He knew he should not do it, but the company had given them too much work.

The robot suddenly moved.

Mina pushed her colleague away. The robot hit a wall. Glass fell like rain. The alarm screamed.

The robot room became quiet.

Her colleague looked at the broken machine. His face was white.

“If they see what I did,” he said, “I will lose my job.”

Mina looked at the memory machine near the door. It could take one memory from her mind and place it in the company’s memory bank.

“I will trade the memory of this accident,” she said.

“You will forget everything?”

“Yes.”

The machine asked, *Do you accept?*

Mina pressed the green button.

For one second, she saw the robot, the glass, and her colleague’s frightened face.

Then the memory was gone.

The next morning, everyone in the company looked at Mina.

They remembered the accident.

They remembered Mina opening the safety gate.

They remembered Mina causing the robot to crash.

Even the company’s cameras showed her face beside the gate.

Mina touched her empty mind. She knew she had paid with a memory, but she could not remember what happened.

Her colleague found her in the robot room.

“Tell them I did not do it,” he said.

“But everyone remembers that I did.”

“It has not happened yet.”

Mina looked at the wall. It was not broken. The glass was whole. The robot stood quietly in its place.

“What do you mean?”

He held out a small memory card. A blue light moved inside it.

“This is the memory you traded,” he said. “But its date is tonight.”

Mina read the date again.

Tonight.

Her colleague stepped closer.

“At ten o’clock, the robot will move,” he said. “You will open the gate. I remember it.”

“Then we can stop it.”

He shook his head. “If we stop it, what will everyone remember tomorrow?”

At that moment, the robot’s eyes turned on.

A clock on the wall showed 9:59.

Mina reached for the red emergency button.

Her colleague caught her hand.

“Wait,” he whispered. “Maybe this is the first time.”
