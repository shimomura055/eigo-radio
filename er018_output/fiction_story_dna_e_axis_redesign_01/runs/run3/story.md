### The Memory She Left Behind

At 5:47 p.m., Noah’s colleague closed her desk drawer for the last time.

Tomorrow, she would not work at the company. She had said the manager pushed her out after she asked questions about missing safety reports.

She put a small box on the floor. Then she looked at Noah.

“Will you accept one memory?” she asked.

Noah stopped packing his bag. “You know what happens.”

“I know.”

When a person gives a memory, the other person receives it completely. But the giver loses it forever.

“Which memory?” Noah asked.

“The night we became friends.”

He remembered that night. A storm had closed the trains. They had stayed late in the office, sharing one cold sandwich. They had laughed beside the broken printer. At midnight, she had told him, “You can trust me.”

“You should keep it,” Noah said.

“I tried to,” she answered. “But I found something today. I think that memory may be important.”

She held out her hand.

Noah took it.

The office disappeared.

He was standing inside the old records room. Rain hit the windows. The lights were weak. He saw himself laughing with her beside the printer. Then the door opened.

The manager came in.

Noah watched the scene through his colleague’s eyes. The manager went to a red cabinet and took out a safety report. The report said that a broken alarm had caused an accident. He replaced it with another report.

This one blamed the night guard.

The manager spoke quietly on the phone.

“The company will pay nothing,” he said. “And the guard will take the blame.”

Then he hid a black memory drive inside the broken printer.

The scene changed. His colleague saw the manager looking at her. She pulled Noah behind the door and covered his mouth. They stayed silent until the manager left.

That was the moment they became friends.

The memory ended.

Noah opened his eyes. His colleague was still holding his hand, but her face had changed.

“Did we have a good night?” she asked.

“You don’t remember?”

She shook her head. The memory was gone from her.

Noah ran to the old records room. Inside the broken printer, he found the black drive.

At that moment, the manager appeared in the doorway.

“Give me that,” he said.

Noah stepped back. His hands were shaking, but he did not let go. He sent the files to the company’s safety office and the police.

By the time they came, the manager was gone.

Outside, his colleague waited by the gate.

She looked at Noah carefully. “Why does your face feel familiar?”

Noah wanted to tell her everything.

Instead, he smiled sadly.

“You trusted me once,” he said. “I’ll remember it for both of us.”
