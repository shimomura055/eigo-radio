### The Lion That Rose Again

For three nights, a lion came to our village in East Africa. It killed cattle and disappeared before morning. The villagers were afraid, so I went after it with two local hunters.

We followed its tracks through dry grass. Near sunset, we saw the lion beside a tree. It was about thirty yards away. It looked at us, but it did not move.

I raised my old rifle and fired.

The lion fell. I believed it was dead. While I began to put another bullet into the rifle, the lion suddenly stood up. In one great jump, it reached me.

I fell onto my back. The lion was on top of me. Its teeth closed around my left arm.

Something strange happened then. I was fully awake. I could see the lion’s yellow eyes and hear the hunters shouting. But I felt no pain and no fear. I was calm, almost like a man watching a dream.

The lion threw me aside and ran toward the older hunter. The hunter fired, but he missed. The lion knocked him down and turned to the younger hunter.

The younger hunter tried to run. The lion caught him too. I wanted to help, but my left arm hung uselessly, and I could not lift my rifle.

Then the lion stopped. It took a few slow steps and fell beside the younger hunter. My first bullet had gone deep inside its body. At last, the lion was dead.

The two hunters pulled me away from it. Blood covered my shirt, but only then did I begin to feel pain.

They carried us back to the village. The older hunter and the younger hunter soon recovered. My arm was much worse. The bones were broken, and the muscles were badly torn.

I lived, but my arm never became strong again. Even now, when I remember that evening, I see the lion rising after my shot.

That is why I never again believed that a quiet animal was a dead animal.
