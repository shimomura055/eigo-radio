### Under the Ruined Gate

The city was dying from hunger. Many houses were empty, and the streets were full of dust. The servant had worked for a rich family, but his master had no food and had dismissed him.

For two days, the servant had eaten nothing. He walked through the city in his torn clothes. He wanted to work honestly, but no one could pay him. At last, he thought, *If I do not steal, I may die.*

That evening, he reached a ruined gate. One tall tower still stood beside it. The servant entered the tower to sleep there. The room was dark and cold. Dead bodies lay on the floor. Some were covered with old cloth, but many were not.

Then he heard a sound.

The servant looked behind a broken wall. An old woman was sitting beside a dead woman. She was pulling long hairs from the dead woman’s head. Near her was a small piece of cloth.

“What are you doing?” the servant asked.

The old woman looked at him but did not stop. “I am making a wig,” she said. “Someone will buy it tomorrow. Then I can get bread.”

“You are taking hair from a dead body,” said the servant.

“I know,” said the old woman. “But what else can I do? I must live.”

She pointed at the dead woman. “And do not think she was better than me. When she was alive, she cheated hungry people. She sold them bad food and took their money. She also lied about the weight of her bread. She lived by taking from others.”

The servant looked at the dead woman. Then he looked at the old woman’s warm coat and strong shoes.

The old woman continued pulling hair. “In a famine, people do what they must. If I do not take this hair, I will die. That is all.”

The servant said nothing. A few moments earlier, he had been afraid to steal. Now he began to think in the old woman’s way.

He moved quietly behind her. Then he struck her, took her coat and shoes, and ran out of the tower.

The night swallowed him. He was still hungry, but his fear was gone.

Until that moment, he had been a poor man afraid of becoming a thief. Now he was a thief—and hunger was his excuse.
