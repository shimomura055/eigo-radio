### The Door Before Midnight

At a rich banker’s dinner, the guests argued about prison and death. The banker said, “A quick death is kinder than fifteen years in a small room.”

The young lawyer shook his head. “Any life is better than no life.”

“Would you live alone for fifteen years?” the banker asked.

“Yes,” said the lawyer. “And you will pay me two million rubles.”

Their foolish bet was signed that night. A small lodge stood in the banker’s garden. It had one room, a bed, books, and a piano. The lawyer could ask for food and wine by writing a note. But he could not see or speak to another person. If he left even a few hours early, he would lose the money.

On the first day, the lawyer played the piano. Then he drank wine and read light stories. After a year, he began to read serious books. He learned languages. He read history, science, and philosophy. In the last years, he read religious books. His letters became fewer. His life seemed to move far from the world outside.

The banker’s life moved in the opposite direction. He lost money. He lost his house and most of his friends. As the last night came near, he looked at the two million rubles and felt afraid.

“If the lawyer lives until midnight, I am ruined,” he thought.

Near midnight, the banker took a key and walked to the lodge. Cold rain touched his face. He opened the door. The lawyer lay at the table, thin and still. A letter waited beside his hand.

The banker read it.

The lawyer wrote that he had learned the world’s riches were empty. Money, food, books, and even life itself could not give lasting truth. He would give up the two million rubles. To show this, he would leave the lodge five hours early.

The banker stared at the sleeping man. He had come to kill him. Now shame filled his chest.

Before dawn, the lawyer opened the door and walked away into the dark garden. He did not look back.

The banker could not win the bet. But that was not what hurt most. The lawyer had escaped the room. The banker had discovered that he was the prisoner.
