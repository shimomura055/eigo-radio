### The Red Scarf

Maya worked alone at a small railway station high in the mountains. Her job was to check the signals and the rails. Every night, she walked past her neighbor’s cabin. He repaired the switches, where one rail moved to guide a train left or right.

For three months, the company had refused to pay him. Then they blamed him for a broken machine. He had lost his job, but he still lived beside the railway. He was angry and tired.

“They think I am nothing,” he told Maya one cold evening.

“They treated you unfairly,” Maya said. “But please do not do anything dangerous.”

He looked away. “Maybe danger is the only thing they understand.”

That night, snow began to fall. Maya was checking the line when she saw a metal bar between the rails. Someone had forced a switch into the wrong position. The night train would arrive in ten minutes. It carried workers, families, and two sick children going to the city hospital.

Maya ran to the signal box and lifted the radio. There was no answer. The signal light was dark. Its wire had been cut.

Then she saw her neighbor standing beside the track.

“You did this!” she shouted.

He held a small tool in his hand. His face was pale. “The company will finally listen.”

“If the train comes, people may die!”

He looked at the mountains. “No one cared when my life was destroyed.”

Maya felt anger in her chest. The company had been cruel to him. But she could hear the distant sound of the train. There was no safe plan and no time to call for help.

She remembered the red wool scarf her father had given her before he died. It was the last thing she had from him. She pulled it from her neck, tied it to a long metal pole, and poured oil from her work lamp over it.

Her neighbor stared. “What are you doing?”

“Making a warning.”

Maya lit the scarf. The red flame rose in the snow. She ran toward the bend and stood where the driver could see her. The train came closer, loud and fast.

She waved the burning scarf. At the last moment, the train screamed and stopped. Snow flew around Maya. The front carriage stopped only a few meters away.

Her scarf fell into the snow, black and empty.

Behind her, her neighbor dropped his tool. He had wanted revenge. Now he looked at the frightened faces in the train windows.

Maya did not forgive him at once. But she stayed beside him until help arrived.
