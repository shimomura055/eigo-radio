### Two Plus One

After eleven hours on an overnight bus, I arrived in the city at six in the morning. I was seventeen, tired, and almost out of money.

My aunt was supposed to meet me at the station, but my phone was dead. I sat on my backpack and watched people hurry past. Their voices sounded fast and strange.

Then my stomach made a loud sound.

I went to a small café inside the station. A sign above the counter said:

**BREAKFAST DEAL: 2 + 1 FREE — €2.50**

I understood the words, but not the custom.

“One breakfast, please,” I said.

The woman behind the counter put three warm cheese rolls into a paper bag.

I looked at the rolls, then at my coins. “How much?”

“Two euros fifty.”

“Oh.” My face became hot. I had only two euros and seventy cents.

The woman smiled. “Two paid, one free.”

“Ah,” I said. “So the free one is the third one?”

“Yes.”

“In my town, free means you do not pay for anything.”

She laughed. I laughed too, although I still felt embarrassed. I paid and carried the bag to a seat.

I ate one roll quickly. I saved one for later. The woman was cleaning the tables, and I noticed that she had only a cup of water.

“You can have this,” I said, holding out the third roll.

She shook her head. “No, you need it.”

“I have two. You have none.”

For a moment, she looked at me carefully. Then she took the roll. “Thank you,” she said.

At nine o’clock, my aunt still had not come. At ten, my phone was still dead. I had no money for another bus, so I stayed in the station. By evening, my eyes would not stay open.

I slept in a waiting room with my backpack under my head.

When I woke, the café woman was standing beside me. She had put a blanket over my shoulders. A paper cup of tea was beside my hand.

“Your phone is charging behind the counter,” she said. “Your aunt sent a message. She is coming soon.”

I held the warm cup with both hands.

The city was still noisy. People still spoke too fast. But it no longer felt completely strange.

Sometimes a journey changes because of something very small: one free roll, one kind person, and a blanket in a crowded station.
