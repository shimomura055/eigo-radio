### The Open Gate

I was thirteen when my family moved to a new farm. We took four cows, two calves, and one old ox with us. The ox pulled a small cart with our bags and tools.

It was bigger than the other animals. My father always watched it carefully.

“Stay away from its horns,” he told me. “It is usually calm, but frightened animals can be dangerous.”

We walked along a dirt road beside an empty pasture. The afternoon was hot, and the animals moved slowly. Then a loose metal box fell from the cart.

It hit the road with a loud **bang**.

The ox jumped. It kicked the cart, broke the rope, and ran toward the pasture gate. Dust rose around its feet. Its horns pointed forward.

My father grabbed the broken rope, but the ox pulled him down. My mother shouted, “Move!”

I saw her standing near the gate. For one second, she could not move. The ox was coming straight at her.

I wanted to run away. My heart beat hard. But the gate was only a few steps from me.

“Open it!” my father shouted.

My mother pulled at the gate, but it was stuck. I ran to her and pushed with both hands. The ox came closer. I could hear its heavy steps behind me.

“Again!” my mother cried.

We pushed together. The gate opened a little.

My father stood up and moved to the side. He did not pull the rope. He waved his arms and called loudly. The ox turned toward him. Then he walked backward, slowly, toward the open gate.

“Keep it open!” he shouted.

The ox ran past us and into the empty pasture. I let go of the gate and jumped aside. My mother pulled it shut, and my father locked it with a thick piece of wood.

For a moment, nobody spoke. The ox walked around the pasture, breathing hard.

Then my mother began to laugh. Soon, we all laughed, even my father. My legs were still shaking, but I felt different. I had been afraid, yet I had not run away.

Years later, my father told this story to our family. He always lifted his hands when he described the ox. “That gate saved us,” he said.

I knew the gate helped. But I also knew something else: it opened because we pushed together.
