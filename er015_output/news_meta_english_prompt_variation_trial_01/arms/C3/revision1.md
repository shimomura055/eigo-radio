The AI Called the Store. Plot Twist: A Human Was There.

Hey, listen to this — Meta’s AI may have had a little secret in its phone calls.

Picture the plan. You have a question for a store, but you do not want to make the call yourself. So you ask your personal AI assistant, Muse, to do it.

Muse makes the call. The machine is supposed to speak for you.

Except that, in some tests, the voice on the other end may not have been coming from the AI alone. Meta had been testing a system in which human contract workers acted as “concierges” behind the scenes and handled some calls.

So the futuristic moment — “My AI called the store for me” — could include a very old-fashioned ingredient: a real person doing the talking.

Reuters discovered the trial through internal posts at Meta. The posts showed that employees had started raising privacy concerns. Their worry was simple but serious: what if details from the calls escaped the company?

That matters because a phone call can contain more than just a quick question. It may include personal information or details about what someone is trying to arrange. If a human worker is involved, users may reasonably want to know.

Meta executives said the company had rolled back the feature for now.

And that is the strange part. On the screen, Muse looks like one smooth AI service. You give it a task, and it appears to take care of everything. But behind that clean, magical experience, some of the work was being done by people.

There is nothing unusual about companies using people while they test new technology. But phone calls make the surprise sharper. When you send an AI to speak for you, you may imagine a machine quietly handling the job. Instead, a human contractor could be waiting in the background, ready to step in.

For now, Meta has pulled back this version of the feature. Still, the question is not going away. As AI assistants begin making calls, booking things, and dealing with businesses, we may need to ask one basic question before they act for us:

Who, exactly, is on the line?
