The AI Calling the Store Had a Human Behind It

Hey, listen to this — it’s kind of interesting.

Imagine asking your AI assistant to call a store for you. You expect a machine to handle the conversation. Maybe it will ask a question, listen to the answer, and come back with the details.

But in some tests at Meta, there may have been a person quietly handling the call instead.

Reuters reported that Meta had been testing a phone-calling feature for its personal AI agent, called Muse. The idea was simple: Muse could make calls for users. But during the company’s internal trial, some of those calls were handled behind the scenes by human contractors.

So the “AI call” was not always just an AI call. A human concierge could step in and do the talking.

That detail came to light through internal posts reviewed by Reuters. The posts also showed that Meta employees were worried about privacy. Their concern was about the possibility that details from the calls could leak outside the company.

And that is where this small-looking feature became a much bigger problem.

When you ask an AI to write a message, you may already wonder where your words go. But a phone call feels more personal. It can include your name, your plans, your questions, and information about what you want to buy or arrange. If a person is listening and speaking in the background, users may want to know that.

Meta executives said the company had rolled back the feature for now.

What makes this worth watching is the gap between what a service feels like and what is really happening behind it. An AI assistant can look instant and automatic on the screen. But sometimes, the system may still depend on people quietly doing the work.

That is not completely surprising. Many new AI products are built with human help while companies test them. The surprising part is the phone call: you think you are sending a machine to speak for you, but a real person may be on the line.

For now, Meta has paused this version of the feature. But the question is likely to follow every AI assistant that starts making calls: when a machine acts for you, who is actually speaking?
