The AI Phone Call Had a Human Plot Twist

Here’s a strange one.

Imagine asking an AI to call a store for you. You expect the usual modern miracle: you give the machine a job, and somewhere in the cloud, it handles the conversation.

Now add the plot twist.

In some tests at Meta, the AI may not have been doing all the talking. Meta was trying out a phone feature for Muse, its personal AI agent. The feature allowed Muse to make calls for users.

But some of those calls were handled by human contract workers acting as “concierges” behind the scenes.

So the call looked like an AI task from the outside. Behind the curtain, though, a real person could be handling it.

That discovery came from internal posts at Meta, which Reuters reviewed. The posts showed employees raising privacy concerns about the system. They worried that information from the calls could leak outside the company.

And suddenly, this is no longer just a clever convenience story.

A phone call can carry personal details. It can reveal what someone is asking about, what they are trying to arrange, or information connected to a purchase or service. The more people involved in that call, the more important privacy becomes.

Meta executives said the company had rolled back the feature.

The funny thing is that this sounds like a very futuristic idea until you look closely. “My AI called the store” feels like a scene from the next generation of technology. But the hidden machinery was much more familiar: people doing the work while the company tested the system.

That does not mean every Muse call involved a human. The trial involved only some calls. But it does show how easily an AI service can blur the line between machine and person.

You may think you are giving a task to software. Somewhere in the background, a human may still be helping the software complete it.

For Meta, that human backup became a problem when employees began asking what happened to the information in those conversations. The company then pulled back the feature for now.

So the next time an AI says it can make a call for you, there is a new question to add:

Is the AI really making the call — or is a human quietly holding the phone?
