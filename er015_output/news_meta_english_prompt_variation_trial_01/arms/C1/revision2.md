# The AI Phone Call Had a Plot Twist

Imagine asking an AI to call a store for you.

You hand over the job, sit back, and expect software to do the talking. The AI makes the call, asks its question, and comes back with the answer. Simple, right?

Well, Meta’s Muse came with a small plot twist: sometimes, a real person was doing the work.

Meta had been testing a phone-call feature for Muse, its personal AI agent. The feature was designed to make calls for users. But some of those calls were handled by human contract workers, known as a “human concierge.”

They were working behind the scenes. From the outside, it looked like the AI was handling the call. But in some cases, a person had stepped in and taken over.

Reuters confirmed the test by reviewing internal company posts. The posts also revealed that Meta employees had raised privacy concerns. Phone calls can contain personal information, and employees worried that details from those calls could leak outside the company.

A Meta executive later said that the company had rolled back the feature for now.

And that is where the story gets interesting. The surprise is not simply that human workers helped test a new technology. People often play a role while products are being developed.

The awkward part is the expectation. When someone asks an AI to make a call, they may believe the conversation is being handled by software. They may also feel more comfortable sharing information because they think they are avoiding a human conversation.

Then comes the discovery: a contract worker may have been behind the scenes after all.

To be clear, Reuters did not report that every Muse call involved a person. It reported that some calls in the trial were handled by human workers. Still, the test shows how unclear the word “AI” can become.

A service may look fully automatic while quietly depending on people. That may be acceptable to some users. For others, it could feel like an important detail they should have known before using it.

For now, Meta has put the phone feature on hold. The experiment leaves the company with a very simple question: when a human is helping behind the curtain, should the user be told?

Because if you ask an AI to make a call, you probably want to know who is actually holding the phone.
