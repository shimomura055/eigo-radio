# The AI Calling for You May Have Had a Human Behind It

Say you ask an AI to call a store for you. You expect a machine to do the talking, perhaps checking if an item is available or asking about opening hours. But what if, behind the scenes, a real person was making the call?

That is the unusual idea at the center of a report about Meta’s personal AI agent, Muse.

According to Reuters, Meta had been testing a phone-call feature for Muse. The feature was meant to let the AI make calls for users. But some of those calls were handled by human contract workers, called a “human concierge,” working in the background.

In other words, the AI system was not always doing the job by itself. A person could step in and handle the call, even though the user had asked an AI to do it.

Reuters said it confirmed the test through internal company posts. The posts also showed that Meta employees raised concerns about privacy. A phone call can contain personal details, and employees worried that information from the calls could leak outside the company.

A Meta executive later said the company had rolled back the feature for now.

The interesting part is not simply that humans were involved. Companies often use people to help test new technology. The bigger problem is the gap between what users think is happening and what is really happening.

When someone asks an AI to make a call, they may assume the conversation is being handled by software. They may also share information because they believe they are avoiding a human conversation. Finding out that a contract worker was involved could change how comfortable they feel using the service.

This does not mean every Muse call was handled by a person. Reuters reported that only some calls in the trial used human workers. Still, the test shows how quickly the meaning of “AI” can become unclear. A service may look automatic from the outside while relying on people in ways users do not expect.

For now, Meta has paused this phone feature. The small experiment points to a much bigger question for AI companies: if a person is helping behind the curtain, should users always be told?
