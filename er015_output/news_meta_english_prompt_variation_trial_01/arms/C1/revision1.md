# Plot Twist: The AI Calling for You Had a Human Behind It

Imagine asking an AI to call a store for you. You expect a machine to do the talking while you sit back. Maybe it will ask whether an item is available or check the store’s opening hours.

Now imagine learning that a real person was making the call.

That is the surprising twist in a report about Meta’s personal AI agent, Muse.

Meta had been testing a phone-call feature for Muse. The idea was to let the AI make calls for users. But some calls were handled by human contract workers, known inside the test as a “human concierge.” They worked quietly in the background while the service appeared to be an AI feature.

So, in at least some cases, the AI was not doing the whole job alone. A person could step in and handle the call.

Reuters said it confirmed the test through internal company posts. Those posts also showed that Meta employees had concerns about privacy. Phone calls can include personal information, and employees worried that details from the calls might leak outside the company.

A Meta executive later said the company had rolled back the feature for now.

The strange part is not just that humans were involved. People often help companies test new technology. The more important issue is the surprise: users may think they are dealing only with software, while a human worker may be behind the scenes.

That difference matters. Someone asking an AI to make a call may believe the conversation is being handled by a machine. They may also feel more comfortable sharing information because they think they are avoiding a human conversation. Learning that a contract worker took part could make them see the service very differently.

To be clear, Reuters did not report that every Muse call involved a person. It said only that some calls in the trial were handled by human workers.

Still, the test shows how slippery the word “AI” can be. A service can look automatic from the outside and still depend on people in ways users do not expect.

For now, Meta has put the phone feature on hold. And the experiment leaves Meta, and other AI companies, with a simple question: if a human is helping behind the curtain, should users be told?
