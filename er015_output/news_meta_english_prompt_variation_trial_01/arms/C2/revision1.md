# I Asked an AI to Call the Store. Then I Learned It Might Need Backup.

Picture the perfect AI errand: you hand over a boring phone call, and the machine takes care of it.

It calls the store, asks the question, listens to the answer, and comes back with the details. You never have to wait through hold music or explain yourself to a stranger. Very convenient. Very futuristic.

There is just one small detail you might want to know first: sometimes, the “machine” may actually be a person.

Reuters reports that Meta tested a phone feature for its personal AI agent, Muse, in which human contractors handled some calls behind the scenes. Meta called this arrangement a “human concierge.”

So a user could think an AI was making the call, while a real human was quietly doing the work.

That is a rather different kind of surprise from an AI making a mistake. If the software misunderstands a question, you may sigh and try again. But if a human contractor is secretly handling the call, the awkward question becomes: what did that person hear?

Meta employees raised concerns about privacy in internal posts. They worried that details from calls could be exposed outside the company. A Meta executive said the company had rolled back the feature for now.

The fascinating part is not that humans helped an AI service. People are involved in developing and testing AI all the time. It is the hidden switch that makes this story so strange.

You think you have handed a task to software. In reality, at least for some calls during this test, the task could move quietly from a machine to a person.

That matters because AI agents are supposed to do more than chat. They are meant to act for us—to call businesses, make arrangements, and deal with the little jobs we would rather avoid. The more personal work they take on, the more important it becomes to know who is actually speaking for us.

So before an AI offers to make that phone call, there may be one question worth asking:

Is the AI calling the store—or is it calling in a human?
