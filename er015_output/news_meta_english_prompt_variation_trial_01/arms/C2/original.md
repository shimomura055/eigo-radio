# I Asked an AI to Call the Store. Who Was Really on the Line?

Imagine asking an AI assistant to handle a boring phone call for you.

“Call the store and ask whether they still have that jacket.”

You might expect the AI to make the call, listen to the answer, and report back. That is the whole appeal: you give the task to a machine, and you get your time back.

But with Meta’s AI agent, Muse, there was a strange possibility hiding behind some calls: a real person might have been doing the talking.

Reuters reports that Meta tested a phone service in which human contractors secretly handled some calls for the AI. Meta called the experiment a “human concierge.” The idea was not that every call was passed to a person. But for some calls, the “AI” on the other end may actually have been a human helper working behind the scenes.

That changes the feeling of the service completely.

If a machine makes a mistake, you may find it amusing or frustrating. If a human is quietly listening to your call, the first question is different: What exactly did that person hear, and what happened to the information?

That was the concern raised by Meta employees in internal posts. They worried about private details from calls being exposed outside the company. A Meta executive later said the company had rolled back the feature for now.

The interesting part is not simply that humans were involved. Humans often help build, test, and improve AI systems. The interesting part is the hidden handoff. A user could believe they were speaking with an automated assistant while an actual contractor was handling the conversation.

That matters because AI agents are being designed to act for us, not just answer questions. They may call businesses, make appointments, or deal with small tasks we do not want to handle ourselves. The more they act on our behalf, the more we need to know who—or what—is really speaking.

So the next time an AI offers to make a call for you, there is a new question worth asking first:

“Will it be you calling, or someone else?”
