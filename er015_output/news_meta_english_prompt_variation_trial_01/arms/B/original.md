The Most Human Thing About Meta’s AI Phone Agent Was the Human

Here is a slightly awkward question for the age of artificial intelligence: when an AI answers your phone call, who is really doing the talking?

Meta has been testing a telephone feature for its personal AI agent, Muse. The idea is that Muse can handle calls for users. But Reuters found that, in some cases, the voice on the other end may have belonged to a human contract worker working behind the scenes.

This was not a movie-style robot secretly controlled by a person every time. It was an internal experiment involving only some calls. Still, it gives the whole idea of an “AI phone agent” a surprising twist. The AI may be less like a fully independent machine and more like a small customer-service team wearing an AI costume.

The experiment was revealed through internal posts reviewed by Reuters. Meta employees raised concerns about privacy, especially the possibility that call content could leave the company through outside workers. After those concerns, Meta executives said the company had rolled back the feature for the time being.

That reaction is interesting because the difficult part of AI may not always be making it sound smart. It may be deciding what happens when the AI reaches its limits. If a human steps in, the service might work better. But then the user needs to know: Who can hear the call? Where is the information going? And was the person speaking to an AI, a human, or both?

This is the everyday problem hiding inside the big AI debate. People may enjoy having software make calls for them, but a phone call feels private in a way that a search box often does not. We expect a call to connect two people, not quietly add an unseen third party.

Meta’s test does not mean that every Muse call was handled by a human, nor that the system was launched widely. But it shows how quickly an AI product can turn into a question about ordinary trust. The most impressive technology in the room may still be a human voice. The hardest part is explaining who is behind it.
