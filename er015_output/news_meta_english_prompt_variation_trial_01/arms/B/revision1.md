# Meta’s AI Phone Agent Had a Human Backstage Crew

Imagine buying a ticket to an AI show. The lights come up, the technology sounds impressive—and then you discover that some of the most important work was being done by people behind the curtain.

That is the surprising twist in Meta’s test of Muse, its personal AI agent. Meta was experimenting with a feature that could make phone calls on a user’s behalf. But Reuters found that some of those calls were actually handled by human contract workers working in the background.

In other words, the “AI phone agent” was not always a solo performer. For at least part of the experiment, it had a human backstage crew.

This does not mean that every call was handled by a person, or that Muse was simply a human pretending to be a machine. The test involved only some calls. But it does reveal an entertaining—and important—truth about today’s AI: sometimes the magic comes from a mixture of software and people.

The arrangement was described in internal posts that Reuters reviewed. Meta employees raised concerns about privacy, especially the risk that call content could be exposed outside the company through contract workers. Meta executives then said the company had rolled back the feature for the time being.

That is where the comedy of the situation turns into a serious question. A phone call feels personal. When we ask an AI to make a call, we may imagine software handling the task quietly and automatically. We may not imagine another person listening and doing the work behind the scenes.

The issue is not simply whether a human can do the job better. A human might be useful when an AI gets stuck. The bigger question is whether users know who—or what—is handling their conversation. Is it an AI? Is it a human? Is it both? And who is allowed to hear what was said?

Meta’s experiment was not a wide release, and the company’s decision to roll it back shows that privacy concerns mattered. Still, the episode offers a memorable picture of the AI industry. The future may not be a world of robots replacing every worker. It may be a world where robots take the credit, while humans wait just offstage, ready to pick up the phone.
