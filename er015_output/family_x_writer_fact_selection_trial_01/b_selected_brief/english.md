# The Final Boss of AI Phone Calls Is a Human Who Notices the AI

A future service lets AI make phone calls for you. Meta is testing this convenient idea with a service called Muse.

You can ask Muse to make a haircut appointment, check whether a store has something in stock, or get price estimates from several businesses. In other words, AI makes the phone calls that people may find a little troublesome.

But the world of phone calls had an unexpected problem.

Sometimes, the person on the other end noticed, “This is an AI,” and hung up. The AI tried hard to speak naturally. But the moment its true identity was discovered, the game was over. The other person would not continue the conversation with an AI.

So Meta came up with a surprisingly human solution.

For some requests, it handed the call over to trained human staff. Muse would receive the user’s request, but a human staff member would make the call. It was an AI service, but when things got difficult, a human would come in as a substitute.

This “human concierge” feature had been turned on for about half of Meta’s employees by the middle of September 2026. Employees who did not want to take part could choose to leave the feature.

Still, we should not jump to conclusions. What has been confirmed is mainly an internal test at Meta. We do not know how many calls from ordinary users were actually handed over to humans. Nor does this mean that Muse’s calls were always made by people.

The substitute system also created another problem. When a human staff member handled a call, personal information—such as a person’s name and appointment details—might be shared in a way the person did not expect. Some Meta employees raised concerns about this.

A Meta vice president admitted that starting human-made calls without enough explanation had been a problem. Meta then temporarily returned the feature to its earlier form.

The test for AI phone calls is not only whether AI can speak smoothly. Can people stay on the line after learning that they are talking to AI? And if a human appears during the call, are users clearly told about it? In this future phone service, the final question may be an old-fashioned one: honesty.

## In one line

The hardest problem for AI phone calls may be winning trust from people who know an AI is calling.
