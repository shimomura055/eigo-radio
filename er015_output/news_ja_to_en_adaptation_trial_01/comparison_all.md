## 日本語R2(Original、Meta Muse AI電話代行)

「もしもし、AIです」――その声の裏で、人間が代役を務めていた

AIに電話を任せる。いかにも未来の便利なサービスです。人間は用件を伝えるだけ。あとはAIが電話をしてくれる。そんな電話の舞台が、Metaで作られていました。

主役は、個人向けAIエージェント「Muse」です。MetaはMuseに、電話の代行機能を持たせようとしていました。

ところが、舞台裏をのぞいてみると、少し意外な光景がありました。

社内で行われた試験では、通話の一部をAIではなく、人間の契約スタッフが担当していたのです。Metaはこの役割を「人間コンシェルジュ」と呼んでいました。

つまり、表に出ている看板は「AI電話代行」。でも、その一部では人間が電話を担当していた。AIがひとりで演じているように見える舞台に、実は人間の代役が控えていたわけです。

ここが、このニュースのいちばん面白いところです。AIの電話が本当にすごいのかと思ったら、舞台裏には人間がいる。まるで、ひとりで演奏しているように見えたピアノの中に、別の演奏者が隠れていたような話です。

もちろん、人間が手伝うこと自体は悪いことではありません。AIがまだ苦手な部分を、人間が補う。考え方としては自然です。

ただし、電話には個人的な情報が含まれることがあります。Metaの従業員からは、通話内容が外部に流出するなど、プライバシーへの懸念が出ました。AIに任せたつもりの会話を、実際には契約スタッフが聞いて対応していたと知れば、驚く人がいても不思議ではありません。

Reutersが確認した社内投稿によると、Metaの幹部は、この機能をいったん取りやめたと説明しています。

AI電話代行で大事なのは、電話をかけられるかだけではなさそうです。舞台の上で誰が話しているのか。そして、幕の後ろに誰がいるのか。便利なサービスほど、その説明まで含めて初めて「安心して任せられる」のかもしれません。

## arm1 (Faithful)

“Hello, I’m AI” — A Human Was Acting as a Stand-In Behind the Voice

Let AI handle your phone calls. It sounds like a convenient service from the future. People only have to say what they need. Then AI makes the call for them. A phone-call stage like this was being built at Meta.

The lead role was Muse, a personal AI agent for individuals. Meta was trying to give Muse the ability to make calls on people’s behalf.

But when we looked behind the stage, we found a slightly surprising scene.

In tests held inside the company, human contract staff handled some of the calls instead of AI. Meta called this role the “human concierge.”

In other words, the sign out front said, “AI phone service.” But in some cases, a human was making the call. On a stage that looked as if AI were performing alone, a human understudy was actually waiting backstage.

This is the most interesting part of the news. We may think AI phone calls are truly amazing, but there is a human backstage. It is like a piano that seems to be playing by itself, while another performer is hidden inside.

Of course, there is nothing wrong with humans helping. Humans can fill in the parts that AI is still not good at. As an idea, that is natural.

However, phone calls can contain personal information. Meta employees raised privacy concerns, such as the possibility that call content could leak outside the company. If people learned that a conversation they thought they had left to AI was actually heard and handled by contract staff, it would not be surprising if they were shocked.

According to internal posts reviewed by Reuters, a Meta executive explained that the company had put this feature on hold for the time being.

With an AI phone service, the important question may not be only whether it can make a call. Who is speaking on the stage? And who is behind the curtain? The more convenient the service, the more it may need to explain what is behind the curtain before people can truly feel safe leaving things to it.

## 参考B(NEWS-META-ENGLISH-PROMPT-VARIATION-TRIAL-01、日本語Prompt逐語+英語出力指定)

# Behind Meta’s AI Phone Agent, a Human Plot Twist

There is a classic trick in show business: make the performance look effortless, then hide the people doing the hard work backstage.

Meta appears to have tried a version of that trick with Muse, its personal AI agent. The company was testing a feature that could handle phone calls for users. But Reuters found an unexpected detail in internal posts: some of those calls were handled by human contract workers behind the scenes.

So, for part of the experiment, Muse was not performing a solo act. It had human help waiting in the wings.

That does not mean every Muse call was handled by a person. It also does not mean the system was simply a human pretending to be an AI. The report describes an internal test involving some calls. Still, the setup gives the phrase “AI phone agent” a rather entertaining twist. The futuristic machine had something very familiar behind it: a team of people doing phone work.

The plot thickened when Meta employees raised privacy concerns. They worried that call content could be exposed outside the company through the contract workers. Reuters confirmed these details by reviewing internal company posts.

Meta executives then said the company had rolled back the feature for the time being.

And that is where the story changes from a clever backstage trick into a serious trust problem. A phone call feels private. If software makes a call for us, we might picture a computer quietly handling the task. We might not picture a human worker listening in and taking care of it from behind the curtain.

The key question is not only whether the technology works. It is also who is involved. Is the call being handled by Muse, by a person, or by a combination of both? And who might be able to hear the conversation?

Meta’s experiment was not a wide release, and the company’s rollback shows that privacy concerns mattered. But the episode offers a memorable picture of the AI business. The future may not be a clean battle between humans and machines, with one side replacing the other.

It may be more like a stage show. The AI stands in the spotlight. The humans stay backstage. And just when the performance looks most magical, someone notices that the phone was picked up by a person after all.

# The AI Said It Would Call the Store. Plot Twist: A Human Might.

The dream is simple: I ask an AI to call a store for me, and the machine deals with the whole annoying business.

It waits on hold. It asks whether the item is in stock. It listens to the answer. Then it returns to me with the useful part, while I remain safely away from the phone.

That is the magic trick of an AI agent. It does not just answer me. It acts for me.

But there is a strange catch in Meta’s version of that trick: sometimes, the voice doing the work may belong to an actual person.

Reuters reports that Meta tested a phone feature for its personal AI agent, Muse, in which human contractors handled some calls behind the scenes. Inside Meta, the company called this arrangement a “human concierge.”

In other words, a user could believe Muse was making the call, while a human helper was quietly handling it.

That is a very different surprise from finding out that an AI has misunderstood a question. A confused machine is irritating. A hidden human raises a much more personal question: who was on the line, and what did they learn from the call?

Meta employees raised privacy concerns in internal posts, including worries that call details could be exposed outside the company. A Meta executive said the company had rolled back the feature for now.

The funny thing is that the human was not necessarily there because the AI had become clever. The human was there because the service was being tested. But from the user’s point of view, that distinction may not feel very funny at all.

You thought you had sent a machine to do an errand. Instead, there may have been a person backstage, like a stagehand moving the scenery while the robot took the applause.

That hidden handoff is the part I cannot stop thinking about. AI agents are being built to handle the small tasks we would rather avoid, especially phone calls. But the more confidently they act on our behalf, the more we need to know what—or who—is actually doing the talking.

Before letting an AI make that call, perhaps the first thing to ask is not whether it can help.

It is whether the AI is calling the store—or quietly calling in a human.
