# AI Took the Phone Call—Then a Human Came Out from Behind the Curtain

Calling a business can be a little troublesome. Making a reservation is one thing. But if you need to check whether something is in stock or ask several businesses for price estimates, you may have to explain the same thing again and again.

That is where Muse comes in, Meta’s AI for personal users. Muse can call stores and businesses in the United States for you. It can book a haircut, ask whether a product is in stock, or request estimates from several businesses. The idea is to let AI handle all the trouble of making phone calls.

So far, it sounds like a story from the future. But as the story moves on, an unexpected actor walks onto the stage.

For some of the calls requested through Muse, Meta handed the work to trained human agents. Those people made the actual calls. This was supposed to be an AI phone service, but there was a human on the other end of the line. A human stand-in appeared from behind the AI, which seemed to be the main actor.

Why did this happen? Reuters reported that some people hung up when they learned they were speaking with AI. So, to make the calls succeed, Meta turned on a human concierge feature.

AI was supposed to call people instead of humans. But when people learned that AI was calling, the conversation ended. So Meta brought in humans instead. In the search for convenience, being human became the ticket that allowed the call to continue.

But once humans entered the picture, another problem appeared: information. Calls about reservations and estimates may include names and contact details. Depending on the call, they may also include information that people do not want strangers to hear.

Some Meta employees worried that personal or sensitive information could be shared without meaning to when calls were passed to human contract workers. This does not mean that a leak occurred. The concern was that users might not fully know that a request they thought they had given to AI could also be heard by a human.

A Meta vice president admitted that it was a problem for contract workers to begin calls without clear notice. Meta temporarily stopped the feature. As of September 22, 2026, this did not mean it had ended the feature forever. The step was taken to improve the explanation given to users and the protection of their privacy.

This experiment showed more than a future in which AI pushes humans out. It also showed a future in which humans appear behind the scenes so that AI can work. The next question is not only whether AI can make a call, but also who is on the other end. Only then can users truly say, “I’ll leave it to AI.”

## In one line

When AI made phone calls, humans had to step in—and users needed to know who was really listening.
