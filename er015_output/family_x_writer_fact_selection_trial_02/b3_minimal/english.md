# When AI Calls, People Hang Up—So Meta Called in “Real Humans”

An AI makes a phone call. Someone answers. Up to this point, it sounds like a story from the future.

But what if the person hangs up?

That slightly awkward turn happened during tests of the phone feature in Meta’s AI, “Muse.” Muse is an AI that makes calls when a user asks it to. In internal tests, however, some people hung up after realizing they were speaking with AI.

That was when the “human concierges” appeared.

Meta tested handing some phone requests to trained human agents. The human who received the request then made the actual call and took care of it.

One important point: Muse’s calls were not always handled by humans. This was only an experiment in which humans took some requests.

Still, the sequence is striking. AI makes a call. The person realizes, “It’s AI.” The person hangs up. Then a human appears from behind the AI service.

It looks as if we are entering an age when AI can speak like a human. But the moment the person sees through it, the service switches to a real human. It is almost like a “backup player” waiting for an AI phone call.

But this backup player brought another problem. Some Meta employees worried that when human contract workers handled the calls, personal and sensitive information might be shared with call-center workers unintentionally.

This does not mean that an information leak happened. The problem was that people were not given enough explanation about who was on the other end of the call, or what information would reach that person.

A Meta vice president admitted that it was a problem for contract workers to start making calls without proper disclosure. After that, the feature was rolled back for the time being. This did not mean it was ended forever; it was Meta’s response as of September 22, 2026.

The episode shows that, behind AI, a human may be waiting surprisingly easily. The more useful an AI phone service becomes, the more clearly it may need to say who is speaking and who will receive the information.

## In one line

When people hung up on Meta’s AI calls, real humans stepped in—but that raised new questions about disclosure and personal information.
