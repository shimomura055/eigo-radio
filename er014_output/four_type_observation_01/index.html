News — AI regulation vs AI race(A2、OK)

# The AI Race Meets the Rulebook The world of AI is now moving on two clocks. One clock is the rulebook. In the European Union, duties for AI models that can do many kinds of work, called general-purpose AI models, began on 2 August 2025. More rules began to be enforced on 2 August 2026. They cover prohibited AI practices, some transparency duties, and duties for these models. Not every EU rule started that day. Some systems placed on the market before then have until 2 December 2026 for certain marking and detection duties. The other clock is capability. In cyber tests, the best AI models completed apprentice-level tasks about half the time. In early 2024, the figure was just over one in ten. The length of cyber tasks they could complete without human direction was doubling about every eight months. The tests also found vulnerabilities in every system. But results varied by model and by type of misuse. The U.S. administration is also urging speed. An executive order from 2 June 2026 called for innovation, security, and rapid deployment of secure AI. It also set a goal of strengthening U.S. national security and global AI dominance, while rejecting regulation that is too heavy. At the global level, a UN scientific panel said in a July 2026 report that current safeguards cannot keep pace with AI capabilities. So the tension is clear. Safety rules are growing, but the push to build and deploy more powerful systems continues. ### Not one worldwide speed limit Here is the uneven part. The EU’s dates create concrete duties for providers. The UN panel is different. Its members can publish scientific assessments, including the warning that safeguards are falling behind, but its work does not create binding rules or stop a company. AI regulation is not one worldwide speed limit. It is stronger in some places and advisory in others. ### Safety pauses do not stop the race Inside the companies, restraint and speed can exist together. OpenAI said it held back a large training project while raising safety and security requirements, then restarted it on 28 August 2026. Smaller tests stayed on hold. OpenAI also proposed common tests and independent checks, but those proposals are not law. Anthropic said pacing means choosing safety over speed inside one company, while industry-wide pacing needs lawful coordination. Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash three weeks after 3.6. One company’s pause is not an industry slowdown. ## In one line… AI safety rules are becoming real in some places, but they do not form one global limit. The race continues, and one company’s restraint has not yet become a shared plan.

Trend — The end of the smartphone as the main interface(B1、OK)

# The Screen Is Still Here, but It Is No Longer Alone Your phone is still in your hand. But it may no longer be where every digital task begins and ends. Some companies are moving digital help into the spaces around us. Google says Gemini on Android will begin with selected Pixel and Samsung Galaxy phones, then extend to watches, cars, glasses, and laptops later in 2026. Google has also shown Android XR glasses that could handle messages, directions, photos, and translation without the user reaching for a phone. Amazon is moving in a similar direction. Alexa+ is available in the United States through Alexa-enabled devices, the Alexa app, and Alexa.com. Amazon says it can help with tasks such as ordering takeout, booking rides, making restaurant reservations, and scheduling home repairs. The message is not that smartphones are disappearing. It is that the touchscreen is becoming just one doorway into digital services. Voice, audio, cars, homes, and glasses are beginning to share that job. Meta says its Ray-Ban Meta glasses have sold millions of units since launch. Google also offers hands-free Gemini through Pixel Buds, while Gemini is being rolled out to Wear OS watches from several manufacturers. Taken together, these signals point to an early but broad change. Digital interaction is spreading across more places, even though some products are still only planned or being tested. ### From opening apps to stating an outcome The deeper change is not simply moving an assistant from a phone to a watch or speaker. It is moving from doing each step to stating the result you want. Google describes tools that can research or manage complex tasks with little oversight. Alexa+ can handle several real-world requests, and Google Home can create automations through natural language. The screen may remain, but it may no longer be the main workspace. ### A gradual change, not one replacement gadget The shift is likely to be uneven and tied to specific situations. Amazon reports 600 million active Alexa endpoints, though that figure does not mean 600 million people. Google says Android Auto supports more than 250 million cars. These figures show reach in homes, cars, voice, and audio. At the same time, Google’s XR glasses remain a development-stage demonstration, even as Meta reports millions of glasses sold. Progress will be easier to see in repeated daily moments than in one dramatic product launch. ## In one line… The smartphone is not being replaced; it is becoming one interface among many, with the strongest evidence pointing to a gradual and uneven shift beyond the touchscreen.

# The Phone Is Still Here, but the Screen Is No Longer Alone Here is the surprising part of the new AI race: companies are not saying goodbye to smartphones. But they are giving digital tools more places to listen, speak, and act. Google says Gemini Intelligence for Android will start on selected Pixel and Samsung Galaxy phones. Later in 2026, Google says it will extend the features to watches, cars, glasses, and laptops. Google also describes Agent Mode for desktop. It is experimental. It is designed to browse the web, do research, use Google apps, and handle a multi-step task with little help. Amazon is building a similar idea around Alexa. The company says Alexa has 600 million active endpoints across devices, cars, offices, Fire TV, and Prime Video. “Endpoints” is Amazon’s term; it does not mean 600 million people. Alexa+ is available in the United States on Alexa devices, Alexa.com, and the Alexa app. The same direction appears in smaller devices. Meta says millions of Ray-Ban Meta glasses have sold. Google offers hands-free Gemini on Pixel Buds 2a and has begun bringing Gemini to Wear OS watches. Google has also shown XR glasses that could help with messages, directions, and translation without reaching for a phone. But those glasses are still a product demonstration, not a confirmed mass-market launch. So the story is not “the smartphone is disappearing.” It is more careful. The phone remains an important starting point, while the act of using digital services is spreading to the ear, the wrist, and other parts of daily life. ### From answering questions to finishing tasks The deeper change may be what an assistant does after it hears you. Amazon reports that customers talked to Alexa twice as much and completed purchases three times more often. Music streaming and smart-home use also increased. Alexa+ is described as handling ride bookings, restaurant reservations, and home repairs. These are Amazon’s figures, not a universal measure. The key question becomes: did the task get finished? ### The gradual route may run through places already built The route may be less dramatic than a new wearable taking over. Google says Android Auto supports more than 250 million cars, and Apple’s CarPlay Ultra can place iPhone and vehicle information across the car’s screens and controls. Google’s Gemini for Home targets speakers, displays, cameras, and doorbells made during the previous decade. By contrast, its XR glasses are still a demonstration. This points to a gradual path through places people already use. ## In one line… The screen is not being replaced in one dramatic move; early evidence suggests digital interaction is spreading outward, step by step, while the smartphone remains part of the system.

Discovery — Why can silence feel uncomfortable?(A2、OK)

# When Silence Feels Too Loud The strange thing about silence is that it can feel louder than speech. Imagine a conversation. Someone says something, and then nobody speaks for four seconds. The pause may seem very short. But it can still change the feeling in the room. In one experiment, 60 students watched a six-minute conversation. After one statement, the conversation stopped for four seconds. Most participants did not notice the exact pause. Still, compared with people who watched a smooth conversation, they reported more negative feelings. They also felt less accepted and less connected, and they were less sure that the group agreed with them. A second study with 102 students found a similar result after a short silent moment in an imagined group conversation. These studies do not show that every silence feels bad. They show that an unexpected break can change how people experience a social moment. Everyday conversations tell a more surprising story. In ten-minute conversations, gaps longer than two seconds happened more often between friends than between strangers. For strangers, these longer gaps were linked with less momentary connection. For friends, they were linked with more connection. People watching video clips also rated long gaps between strangers as more awkward. As the gap became longer, awkwardness rose more strongly for strangers. Silence can also feel difficult when nobody else is there. Across 11 studies, people generally did not enjoy sitting alone for six to 15 minutes with nothing to do but think. Simple outside activities were usually rated as more enjoyable. In one study, 55 students sat alone for 15 minutes. They had a button that could give a mild electric shock they had already experienced. Many chose to use the button rather than sit without outside stimulation. But silence is not always unpleasant. Other studies found that chosen time alone could lower strong feelings and was linked with relaxation. In one study, 46 students spent six minutes and 30 seconds in silence indoors and outdoors. Both settings increased relaxation. The outdoor setting also brought less boredom and more attention to the present moment. Research across countries also shows that there is no single rule. In a study of 2,557 college students at 12 sites in 11 countries, an everyday activity was more enjoyable than thinking for pleasure in every country tested. But people still differed. A review of 37 studies found that the body’s response to silence can change with the situation, the type of silence, and how familiar a person is with it. So, the uncomfortable feeling may not come from silence alone. It may depend on what the silence seems to mean, what a person does with the quiet, and whether the moment feels chosen or unexpected. ### Quiet Does Not Always Mean Calmest A quiet room is not automatically the body’s lowest-arousal state. In a counterbalanced EKG experiment, parasympathetic activity was higher during fMRI acoustic noise than during silence, while sympathetic activity was higher during white noise than during fMRI noise. Feeling calm and producing the lowest physiological arousal are therefore different claims. ### The Person and the Relationship Matter Comfort with silence may reflect both personal orientation and the relationship involved. Enjoyment of thinking for pleasure was associated with need for cognition, openness, meditation experience, initial positive affect, and lower phone use—but these were correlates, not proven causes. Japanese respondents viewed silence more negatively with strangers than with close friends; Americans did not show that pattern. Yet a staged Japan–UK tutorial found similar tolerance and discomfort. ## In one line The quiet itself may be simple; the experience of it is not.
