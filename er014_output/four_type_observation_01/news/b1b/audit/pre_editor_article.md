# Two Clocks, One AI Race

## Main Story

AI is moving forward quickly, but the rules are trying to keep up.

In Europe, legal duties for general-purpose AI models started to apply on 2 August 2025. Other parts of the EU AI Act began enforcement on 2 August 2026. These include rules on prohibited AI practices, some transparency duties, and certain duties for general-purpose models. But the timetable is staged. Not every rule began on the same day.

International action is also growing, but it has a different role. The United Nations created an independent scientific panel with 40 members in August 2025. In a preliminary report released in July 2026, the panel warned that current safeguards cannot keep pace with the growth of AI capabilities. But the panel does not make or enforce international law.

The technology itself is also advancing. In cyber evaluations, the UK AI Security Institute said that the best models completed apprentice-level tasks about 50% of the time, compared with just over 10% in early 2024. The length of tasks they could complete without human direction was doubling about every eight months. The institute also found vulnerabilities in every system it tested, while safeguards varied.

This is the central tension. Rules, warnings, and safety work are expanding. At the same time, companies and governments still want faster development and deployment.

### Regulation is a map, not one stop sign

Regulation is not one global brake. In the EU, duties for general-purpose model providers applied from 2 August 2025. Selected enforcement followed from 2 August 2026. Language models trained with more than 10^23 calculation steps are considered general-purpose models; above 10^25, a model is presumed to pose systemic risk, though that line is under review. The UN panel can assess the gap, but it cannot enforce a rule.

### Who decides how fast is fast enough?

Google reported releasing a new Flash model three weeks after its predecessor, with updated safeguards. OpenAI said it held back larger Astra runs while raising safety and security requirements, then restarted the frontier run on 28 August 2026. It called for mandatory US testing and independent assessment. Anthropic said industry-wide pacing would need a lawful, verifiable mechanism. US proposals range from rapid deployment to a temporary pause. These actions do not yet form a common brake.

## In one line…

AI governance is becoming a patchwork of dates, thresholds, company choices, and proposed controls. The race continues, while the argument over who can set a common speed limit remains open.
