# The AI Race Meets the Rulebook

The world of AI is now moving on two clocks.

One clock is the rulebook. In the European Union, duties for AI models that can do many kinds of work, called general-purpose AI models, began on 2 August 2025.

More rules began to be enforced on 2 August 2026. They cover prohibited AI practices, some transparency duties, and duties for these models. Not every EU rule started that day. Some systems placed on the market before then have until 2 December 2026 for certain marking and detection duties.

The other clock is capability. In cyber tests, the best AI models completed apprentice-level tasks about half the time. In early 2024, the figure was just over one in ten. The length of cyber tasks they could complete without human direction was doubling about every eight months.

The tests also found vulnerabilities in every system. But results varied by model and by type of misuse.

The U.S. administration is also urging speed. An executive order from 2 June 2026 called for innovation, security, and rapid deployment of secure AI. It also set a goal of strengthening U.S. national security and global AI dominance, while rejecting regulation that is too heavy.

At the global level, a UN scientific panel said in a July 2026 report that current safeguards cannot keep pace with AI capabilities.

So the tension is clear. Safety rules are growing, but the push to build and deploy more powerful systems continues.

### Not one worldwide speed limit

Here is the uneven part. The EU’s dates create concrete duties for providers. The UN panel is different. Its members can publish scientific assessments, including the warning that safeguards are falling behind, but its work does not create binding rules or stop a company. AI regulation is not one worldwide speed limit. It is stronger in some places and advisory in others.

### Safety pauses do not stop the race

Inside the companies, restraint and speed can exist together. OpenAI said it held back a large training project while raising safety and security requirements, then restarted it on 28 August 2026. Smaller tests stayed on hold. OpenAI also proposed common tests and independent checks, but those proposals are not law. Anthropic said pacing means choosing safety over speed inside one company, while industry-wide pacing needs lawful coordination. Google released Gemini 3.7 Flash three weeks after 3.6. One company’s pause is not an industry slowdown.

## In one line…

AI safety rules are becoming real in some places, but they do not form one global limit. The race continues, and one company’s restraint has not yet become a shared plan.
