Full Story Part 1
voice=Aoede(英語・A2 6%減速)		The most important question in the new space-security story is not simply whether satellites can be attacked. It is this: Is the weapon itself in orbit? On September 14, 2026, U.S. Air Force Secretary Troy Meink said that the United States was deploying space-control weapons in orbit. He said they were meant to protect U.S. forces from hostile actions. An official U.S. Space Force article published the next day described this as the first public acknowledgment that the Space Force had put weapons in orbit.

Full Story Part 2
voice=Aoede(英語・A2 6%減速)		But the announcement was limited. It did not name the system, explain its exact attack method, or identify a target. So, the confirmed news is that the United States has publicly acknowledged deploying weapons in orbit. It is not confirmation that those weapons have attacked a satellite. Other claims need a different label. A 2025 U.S. intelligence assessment said Russia was developing a satellite with a nuclear weapon for anti-satellite use. But the assessment did not say that the satellite had been launched, placed in orbit, or used. That difference matters. Announced, being developed, tested, and deployed are not the same thing.
