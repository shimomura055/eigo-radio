以下は、同じ記事から選ばれた5件のKey Phrase(英語学習ポッドキャストの
リスニング教材)です。5件から作れる全てのペア(10組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず10組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

# The New Space Question: Is the Weapon in Orbit?

The most important question in the new space-security story is not simply whether satellites can be attacked.

It is this:

Is the weapon itself in orbit?

On September 14, 2026, U.S. Air Force Secretary Troy Meink said that the United States was deploying space-control weapons in orbit. He said they were meant to protect U.S. forces from hostile actions.

An official U.S. Space Force article published the next day described this as the first public acknowledgment that the Space Force had put weapons in orbit.

But the announcement was limited. It did not name the system, explain its exact attack method, or identify a target.

So, the confirmed news is that the United States has publicly acknowledged deploying weapons in orbit. It is not confirmation that those weapons have attacked a satellite.

Other claims need a different label.

A 2025 U.S. intelligence assessment said Russia was developing a satellite with a nuclear weapon for anti-satellite use. But the assessment did not say that the satellite had been launched, placed in orbit, or used.

That difference matters. “Announced,” “being developed,” “tested,” and “deployed” are not the same thing.

### Counterspace is wider than weapons in orbit

Counterspace is the wider family. Russia, China, and India have used missiles fired from Earth to destroy satellites, and the 2022 KA-SAT case used a cyberattack to disrupt a satellite network. None of those examples placed an attack weapon in orbit. The 2026 U.S. statement matters because it publicly acknowledges this different kind of deployment, without proving an attack.

### Military purpose is not the same as an attack weapon

Ask two separate questions: What is a system built to do? What rule covers it? HBTSS, GPS, and space monitoring are described as support or protection, not confirmed attack weapons. The treaty bans nuclear and other mass-destruction weapons in orbit, as well as military bases and weapon tests on celestial bodies. But UN votes and a U.S. pledge on destructive missile tests do not ban all conventional space weapons.

## In one line…

The confirmed change is a publicly acknowledged weapon in orbit—not proof that every military satellite is an attack weapon, or that a broad ban has already taken effect.
