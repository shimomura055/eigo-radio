# A Lemon Tart, Pickles, and an AI Suggestion

## Main Story

A lemon tart with chopped pickles sounds like a joke—or at least a strange snack. But Lawson says an AI program suggested the pairing, and it became the starting point for a real convenience-store product.

The idea did not stay on a computer screen. Human product specialists made samples, tasted them, and adjusted the taste and appearance. Lawson then planned a sale of the finished product.

By September 2026, Lawson and FamilyMart had both publicly described AI uses linked to food products or the products stores stock. But this is not a story about computers making food alone. It is about people using a computer to find or check ideas before a product reaches a store.

Lawson used AI to find a surprising idea. FamilyMart used it in another way, connected to earlier sales information. The difference shows that “using AI” can mean more than one thing in a convenience store.

### Surprise was the assignment

The pickle idea was not a computer error. Lawson asked its AI to search for combinations that might make people say, “What is this?” “Lemon Tart (Pickle Flavor)” was planned for September 29, 2026, at about 4,700 stores in parts of the Kanto region, Niigata, Yamanashi, and Nagano. It was a regional launch, not evidence of a permanent nationwide product.

### A different question for AI

FamilyMart used AI differently: it used sales information to help make a sweet-potato canelé that might sell well. “Oimo no Canele — with Caramel Sauce” launched nationwide on September 22, 2026, as a limited-quantity product. Separately, FamilyMart’s AI Recommend Ordering began at 500 stores in late June 2025. It uses past sales, weather, nearby foot traffic, and calendar information to suggest products and order amounts. Staff can change the suggestions.

## In one line

AI can offer a strange idea or a sales-based suggestion, but people still test, adjust, and decide what reaches the shelf.
