# “Cockroach Milk”: Before You Reach for a Cup, Look at How It Stores Food

If you hear “cockroach milk” and the café in your mind shuts down at once, I get it. But today, let’s put the urgent meeting about whether to drink it on hold. The main story is not its taste or how it feels going down. It’s the surprising way cockroach babies store food, just for them.

The Ig Nobel Prize went to research on nutrient-rich crystals that had been published earlier. This is not news that cockroach milk has finally made its debut at the dinner table! No one drank it as part of the study, and the study did not test its effects as a food for humans. You can put the cup back on the shelf.

The study looked at a kind of cockroach that raises its young inside its body. The researchers took out protein crystals containing nutrients from the gut of the unborn young. You may have thought you were heading to the milk aisle, but instead you were shown the food store inside a body.

The researchers used X-rays to study the shape of the protein inside the crystals. Fats were bound to the protein, and sugars were attached too. If you were expecting a fancy latte, what you got was a map of molecules. Suddenly, we’re in a science lab.

And the paper includes a striking estimate. The crystals contain more than three times as much energy as the same weight of milk from several kinds of mammals.

“The final boss of nutrition?”

At this point, the marketing team might be ready to leap into action, but hold on. The comparison was between “crystals” and “milk.” It does not mean that drinking them would make people three times as energetic. Take that number on its own, and before you know it, a science story can turn into a TV shopping show.

What’s more interesting is the idea of turning nutrients into crystals and storing them away. No fridge, no lunch box. If tiny crystals can serve as a food store, that’s a pretty clever way to save space.

The authors also suggest that storing nutrients as crystals may help feed the young. But the role of this process has not been confirmed. Next to “That makes sense!” there should be a sign saying “Still being studied.” The two should be shown together.

When you come across the phrase “packed with nutrition,” try asking, “Compared with what?” and “For whom?” Behind the flashy number, you may find something else that is interesting.

Rather than seeing this as a possible breakfast for people, see it as a way to deliver nutrients to young that have not yet been born. You don’t need the courage to drink it. Today, bring your curiosity, not a cup, and take a look inside this little food store.

## In one line
Researchers studied nutrient-rich protein crystals in unborn cockroach young, not cockroach milk for people. The crystals may help feed the young, but that role has not been confirmed.
