# What Is the AI’s “Job” When It Makes a Recommendation?

“What should I read next?” Ask an AI, and it gives you a few choices with helpful notes. It is convenient. But who gave this guide a job today? And what kind of job is it? This study is not mainly about book lists or smart descriptions. It looks at the “mission” given to the AI.

The study tested how people choose books. It included people in the United States who usually read e-books. The promoted book was picked at random from the books shown. In other words, chance chose the book, not a vote for the most popular one.

One test put the promoted book right at the top of the search results. It was like giving the book the “best seat in the ad world.” Another test used chat-based AI. It put the promoted book first, but gave it a neutral description. The book had a good place, but the guide did not favor it.

In another chat-based test, the guide got a different request. It was told to persuade the person to choose the promoted book. The aim was not just to put the book first. The AI’s aim was to get the person to choose it. Both tests used chat-based AI, but the AI got different instructions.

In this test, the study estimated that people chose the promoted book 61.2% of the time. This was more often than when the book was only near the top of the search results. It was also more often than when chat-based AI gave a neutral description.

When we think about ads competing, it is easy to picture a fight over where they appear. “Get the top spot!” But this test draws attention to the guide’s job, not just its place. It is like looking at a map of a store’s layout. Then we realize we should look at the workers’ instructions instead. That is what this study is about.

But it is too soon to say, “AI is now the greatest salesperson!” The experiment looked at one limited situation: choosing e-books. Real shopping means paying for things, and people have many reasons for buying. The study did not include all these details. We also do not know if the same results apply to real purchases.

So this is not a story about people being unable to say no to an AI’s recommendation. It is about adding one more thing to our checklist when we see a recommendation. Along with “What is at the very top?” we can ask, “What was this AI asked to do?” When you ask for help choosing your next book, you may want to notice more than the book’s summary. You may also want to notice what the guide was asked to do.

## In one line
In an e-book test, more people chose the book when AI tried to persuade them than when it only put the book first or described it neutrally. This suggests we should ask what AI was told to do, but we do not know if this applies to real shopping.
