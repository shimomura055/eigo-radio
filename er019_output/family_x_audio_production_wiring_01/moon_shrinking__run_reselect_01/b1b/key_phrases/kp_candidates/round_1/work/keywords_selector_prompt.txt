# More Than 8,900 Wrinkles on the Moon! The Secret of the “Rock Coat” Hidden Beneath Them

Even the Moon has wrinkles. And the research team counted more than 8,900 of them. The Moon looks smooth in the night sky, but up close it seems to have quite a lot of “wrinkles.” But there’s no need for beauty serum. These wrinkles offer clues to how the Moon shrank and to its surprising way of “dressing.”

The team used camera images from the lunar probe LRO. They searched for cliffs on the Moon’s surface and marked them on a map. A wrinkle is a cliff with one steep side and one gentler side. These cliffs are thought to show where solid rock was squeezed together, with one side pushed up over the other. They are a record of a huge “shoving match” carved into the Moon’s surface.

Using this map to work out how much the Moon has shrunk, the team estimated that its radius had become about 42 to 46 meters smaller. By reading the wrinkles on its surface, scientists are investigating how a huge object in space has shrunk. Taking the Moon’s measurements is quite a detailed job.

But if you’re thinking, “Has the Moon shrunk only that much since it was formed?” hold on a moment. What we are looking at is shrinkage in the later part of the Moon’s history, left by relatively recent faults. This is not the total amount of shrinkage from its birth to today.

On the other hand, models that calculate the Moon’s cooling over its entire history predict much greater shrinkage. Does that mean the calculations are way off? It is too soon to say. The periods being compared and the ways they were studied are different. These aren’t figures measured under the same conditions.

Still, the difference is puzzling. One possible explanation is a layer of broken rock. In a way, it is a “rock coat” the Moon has put on. Just hearing that might make your shoulders ache, but what matters is not its weight; it is how well it keeps heat in.

The research team suggests that a thick layer of rock full of gaps may have worked like insulation. Just as insulation in a house keeps heat from escaping, this layer may have made it harder for heat inside the Moon to escape. As a result, the Moon may have cooled and shrunk more slowly.

In other words, the Moon may have kept warm in its rock coat. But it is too soon to end the mystery by saying, “The coat is the culprit!” For now, it is just one possible explanation for the difference.

Counting cliffs on the Moon led to its inner heat, and then to the coat’s warmth—not how it feels to wear, but how well it keeps heat in. The wrinkles were an important clue to how the Moon cooled. Next time you look up at the Moon, imagine the “rock coat” beneath its round shape, too. The Moon’s way of dressing has more to it than meets the eye.
