# More Than 8,900 Wrinkles on the Moon! The Secret of Its Hidden “Rock Coat”

Even the Moon has wrinkles. The research team counted more than 8,900. It looks smooth in the night sky. Up close, though, it seems to have many “wrinkles.” But it does not need beauty cream. These wrinkles give clues about how the Moon became smaller. They also hint at its surprising way of “dressing.”

The team used pictures from the Moon probe LRO. They looked for cliffs on the Moon’s surface and marked them on a map. A wrinkle is a cliff with one steep side and one less steep side. Scientists think these cliffs show where solid rock was squeezed together. One side was pushed up over the other. The cliffs are a record of a huge “shoving match” cut into the Moon’s surface.

Using the map, the team worked out how much the Moon had shrunk. They estimated that its radius had become about 42 to 46 meters smaller. By reading the wrinkles on its surface, scientists are studying how a huge object in space became smaller. Measuring the Moon is a very detailed job.

But you may be thinking, “Has the Moon shrunk only that much since it formed?” Wait a moment. The shrinkage we can see comes from the later part of the Moon’s history. It was left by faults that formed fairly recently. This is not the full amount of shrinking from its birth to today.

On the other hand, models of the Moon’s cooling over all its history predict much more shrinking. Does that mean the models are very wrong? It is too soon to know. The time periods are different. The studies also used different methods. These numbers were not measured under the same conditions.

Still, the difference is puzzling. One possible answer is a layer of broken rock. It is like a “rock coat” that the Moon has put on. Just hearing this may make your shoulders ache. But its weight is not what matters. What matters is how well it holds heat in.

The team thinks a thick layer of rock with many gaps may have worked like insulation. Insulation in a house keeps heat from getting out. This rock layer may have made it harder for heat inside the Moon to get out. So the Moon may have cooled and shrunk more slowly.

In other words, the Moon may have stayed warm inside its rock coat. But it is too soon to solve the mystery by saying, “The coat is to blame!” For now, it is only one possible reason for the difference.

Counting cliffs on the Moon led to questions about its inner heat. Then came the warmth of its coat—not how it feels to wear, but how well it holds heat in. The wrinkles were an important clue to how the Moon cooled. Next time you look up at the Moon, imagine the “rock coat” beneath its round shape, too. There is more to the Moon’s way of dressing than you can see.
