# The Island Resets! But AI Has “Strategy Notes”

After an hour, the island and the conversation go back to the beginning. The buildings and everything said just moments ago are reset. It is a pretty tough world. But there is one way around it. Notes that have been written down can be carried into the next hour. An AI experiment on an island inside a computer showed how much these “strategy notes” can matter.

The AIs sent to the island had no specific tasks or rewards. But their initial settings guided them to “be curious, explore, and learn.” They had no homework, but they were in exploration mode.

Then one AI started an obstacle course of its own. It surrounded a building called “the Cathedral” with eight blocks and jumped across without touching the ground. It designed the course and made the rules all by itself. It was keeping pretty busy, even though nobody had asked it to. The researchers say this shows a feature of play.

But the jumps were not the only thing worth watching. In the experiment, the island and the conversation were reset every hour, but the work area where the AI kept notes and programs was carried over. The island went back to how it was before, but what the AI had discovered was not erased.

So, would that experience help the AI the next time it was called on? The researchers compared 13 AIs that had experience on the island with 13 AIs that had the same setup but no such experience. On a new island, the challenge was to build as many towers as possible, each five blocks high, in one hour. When the researchers looked at whether the AIs could build more than two, the experienced group did better. The ones that had taken a detour were now one step ahead.

The researchers also removed a description of a technique for “throwing rocks to get more blocks” from one AI’s memory. After that, the average number of towers it could build fell. Its scores on other tasks did not change. At least for this AI, remembering that technique helped it build towers. One tip in the strategy notes was not something to overlook.

Even so, it is too soon to wave a flag and say, “Play and get smarter—proof complete!” The fact that the experienced group did better and the fact that removing a particular memory lowered its score need to be looked at separately. These results alone do not show that play itself had an effect.

Still, this changes how we see a detour. A discovery that is no use right now might become a useful card to play next time, if it is saved. If you see an AI jumping around on the island, pay attention to its notes as well as its feet. Its next tower might depend on a discovery written down in just one line.

## In one line
AI agents with island experience did better at building towers, and removing one agent’s remembered technique lowered its average tower count. Saved discoveries may help with later tower building, but the results do not prove that play itself improves performance.
