リスニング教材)です。10件から作れる全てのペア(45組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず45組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

# The Island Resets! But AI Has “Strategy Notes”

After one hour, the island and conversation start again. The buildings and even words from moments ago are reset. It is a hard world. But there is one way around this. Notes written down can go into the next hour. An AI test on an island inside a computer showed how much these “strategy notes” can matter.

The AIs sent to the island had no set tasks or rewards. But their starting settings led them to be curious, explore, and learn. They had no homework, but they were ready to explore.

Then one AI made its own obstacle course. It placed eight blocks around a building called “the Cathedral.” Then it jumped across without touching the ground. It made the course and its rules all by itself. It kept itself busy, though nobody had asked it to. The researchers say this is a feature of play.

But the jumps were not the only thing to watch. In the test, the island and conversation reset every hour. But the work area with the AI’s notes and programs carried over to the next hour. The island went back to how it was before, but the AI’s discoveries stayed.

So, would this experience help the AI when it was asked to work again? The researchers compared 13 AIs with island experience. They also used 13 AIs with the same setup but no experience. On a new island, the task was to build as many towers as possible in one hour. Each tower had to be five blocks high. The researchers checked which AIs could build more than two towers. The experienced group did better. Those that had taken a detour were now one step ahead.

The researchers also erased a way to “throw rocks to get more blocks” from one AI’s memory. After that, the average number of towers it could build went down. Its scores on other tasks stayed the same. For this AI, at least, remembering that way helped it build towers. One tip in the strategy notes was worth paying attention to.

Even so, it is too soon to wave a flag and say, “Play and get smarter—proof complete!” The experienced group did better, and removing one memory lowered its score. These two results need to be studied separately. By themselves, they do not show that play itself made a difference.

Still, this changes how we think about a detour. A discovery that is not useful now might be a useful card to play next time, if it is saved. If you see an AI jumping around on the island, watch its notes as well as its feet. Its next tower might depend on a discovery written down in just one line.

## In one line
AIs with island experience did better at building towers. Removing one AI’s saved method lowered its average tower count. Saved discoveries may help with later tower building, but these results do not prove that play itself makes AI do better.
