リスニング教材)です。5件から作れる全てのペア(10組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず10組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

【記事本文(参考、文脈確認用)】
# Looking for a “Money-Making Personality”? The Main Character Turned Out to Be Your Former Self

“If you become more serious, will you earn more?”
“Or do you become more serious once you start earning more?”

When you hear about research on income and personality, you might expect to learn the “secret of high earners.” But this time, the people in the spotlight are not some rich people. They are “your former self” and “your later self.”

The research brought together 11 large studies. They looked at people who were working and had reported on their personality and income from work. The number of people varied by analysis, but the researchers used data from as many as 134,515 people. In every study, the same people’s income and personality were measured at least three times.

The key was how the researchers compared people. They analyzed separately the differences between people that tend not to change and changes that happen within each person.

Not, “What’s different between me and that person who earns a lot?”
But, “How are my former self and my later self different?”

In a way, the researchers shifted from comparing yourself with others to keeping track of yourself over time. It’s like putting down your binoculars for looking at your neighbor’s greener grass and turning the pages of your own records.

Then came “diligence.” Think of it as a personality that takes things seriously and works hard. There was a small positive link between changes in a person’s income and later changes in their diligence. In the other direction, there was also a small positive link between changes in diligence and later changes in income.

It wasn’t a one-way link, but a two-way one. But this is important, so let me stress it: the links were “small.”

“So, earning more turns me into a more serious person!”
Hold on before jumping to that conclusion.

This study cannot tell us that income changed a person’s personality, or that their personality increased their income. Something else may have played a part in both. Even if researchers follow the same people and take care in how they compare them, that alone cannot prove what caused what.

In other words, this is not a how-to guide to a “personality that earns more.” It is a study that can give you a reason to reopen the book you were about to close, thinking, “This is just the kind of person I am.”

Rather than ending with “That person is the type who earns a lot,” you can look back and ask, “How have I changed?” You may plan to flip through a book about successful people, then realize you’re reading the sequel to your own story. That is what makes this study interesting.

## In one line
Tracking people over time, researchers found small links in both directions between changes in income and later changes in diligence. The study cannot show what caused what, but it offers a reason to ask how we ourselves have changed.
