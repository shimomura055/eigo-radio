Full Story Part 1 (Aoede)
voice=Charon		“If you become more serious, will you earn more?” “Or do you become more serious once you start earning more?” When you hear about research on income and personality, you might expect to learn the “secret of high earners.” But this time, the people in the spotlight are not some rich people. They are “your former self” and “your later self.” The research brought together 11 large studies. They looked at people who were working and had reported on their personality and income from work. The number of people varied by analysis, but the researchers used data from as many as 134,515 people. In every study, the same people’s income and personality were measured at least three times. The key was how the researchers compared people. They analyzed separately the differences between people that tend not to change and changes that happen within each person.
Comment 2 (Charon)
voice=Charon		The researchers looked at how each person changed over time, rather than how people differed from one another. What did they find about changes in income and how seriously people worked?
Full Story Part 2 (Aoede)
voice=Charon		Not, “What’s different between me and that person who earns a lot?” But, “How are my former self and my later self different?” In a way, the researchers shifted from comparing yourself with others to keeping track of yourself over time. It’s like putting down your binoculars for looking at your neighbor’s greener grass and turning the pages of your own records. Then came “diligence.” Think of it as a personality that takes things seriously and works hard. There was a small positive link between changes in a person’s income and later changes in their diligence. In the other direction, there was also a small positive link between changes in diligence and later changes in income. It wasn’t a one-way link, but a two-way one. But this is important, so let me stress it: the links were “small.” “So, earning more turns me into a more serious person!” Hold on before jumping to that conclusion.
Comment 3 (Charon, Bridge to Part 3)
voice=Charon		The study compared each person with their earlier self and found small links between changes in income and changes in how seriously they worked. As you listen next, pay attention to what these links mean.
Full Story Part 3 (Aoede)
voice=Charon		This study cannot tell us that income changed a person’s personality, or that their personality increased their income. Something else may have played a part in both. Even if researchers follow the same people and take care in how they compare them, that alone cannot prove what caused what. In other words, this is not a how-to guide to a “personality that earns more.” It is a study that can give you a reason to reopen the book you were about to close, thinking, “This is just the kind of person I am.” Rather than ending with “That person is the type who earns a lot,” you can look back and ask, “How have I changed?” You may plan to flip through a book about successful people, then realize you’re reading the sequel to your own story. That is what makes this study interesting.
Comment 4 (Charon)
