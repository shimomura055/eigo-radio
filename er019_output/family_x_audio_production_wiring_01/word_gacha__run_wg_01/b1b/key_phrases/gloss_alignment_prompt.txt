以下の各英語key_phraseは、教材として最終確定した英語表現です。current_japanese_glossは、確定前の候補表現(display_phrase_before_canonicalization)を基準に付けた日本語グロスで、確定したkey_phrase全体とは一致していない可能性があります。added_content_wordsは、確定時に候補表現へ新しく加わった内容語です(空の場合があります)。

各項目について、次を行ってください。
1. current_japanese_glossが、最終的なkey_phrase全体(目的語・補語・修飾語を含むすべての語)の意味と過不足なく一致しているかを判定し(current_gloss_consistent)てください。「～」「〜」を含むかどうかは判定の基準ではありません。key_phrase全体の意味が日本語に表れているかどうかだけで判定してください。
2. 一致している場合は、japanese_glossにcurrent_japanese_glossをそのまま返してください。一致していない場合は、最終key_phrase全体に一致する日本語グロスを返してください。current_japanese_glossが最終key_phrase全体の意味を既に表している場合は、より自然・より適切と思える言い換えがあっても書き換えず、current_japanese_glossをそのまま返してください。書き換えるのは、key_phraseの語の意味が欠けている・誤っている場合だけです。
3. added_content_wordsの各語について、japanese_glossの中でその語の意味を表している部分を、japanese_glossからそのまま切り出してadded_word_mappingsに書いてください(english_wordには対応させる英語の語、複数語をまとめた句でもかまいません。japanese_spanにはjapanese_glossに含まれない文字列や、「～」などの記号だけを書かないでください)。複数の英語語が一つの日本語表現でまとめて表されている場合は、同じ部分を複数の語に対応させてかまいません。added_content_wordsが空の項目は、added_word_mappingsを空配列にしてください。
判定の参考例:
- key_phrase「based on」にcurrent「～をもとにした」: 辞書的に自然なので一致です。
- key_phrase「based on interviews」にcurrent「～をもとにした」: interviewsの意味が欠けているので不一致です。「インタビューをもとにした」なら一致です。
- key_phrase「fall into」にcurrent「～に落ちる」: 意味は表れているので一致です(「～に陥る」へ書き換えません)。

key_phrase単体で他の文脈でも再利用できる自然な訳にし、本文固有の固有名詞・文脈の追加はしないでください。key_phrase全体の意味を反映できている場合のみqa_reflects_all_key_phrase_wordsをPASSにしてください。

選ぶ表現は必ず本文中の実表現(source_span)に対応させ、短く自然な日本語グロスを付けてください。

日本語グロスは、辞書的に正しいだけでなく、日本人の英語学習者が聞いてすぐ意味を理解できる、自然で平易な現代日本語にしてください。直訳調や、「常態」「是正」のような過度に硬い報道語・漢語、一般的な学習者には伝わりにくい表現は避けてください。

# I Checked the Rarity Before Learning the Words: “Rare-Word Hunting” Starts with an English-Word Gacha

If you’re going to learn English words, start with the ones people use a lot. That’s obviously right. There’s no arguing with it. And yet, the moment a gacha appears in front of you, the good student inside you steps away. “That may be true, but what’s the rarest one?” Wait. You came here to study today, didn’t you?

The “Dopa English Word Gacha” is just the thing to send your mind off track. English word cards have seven rarity levels. The more often a word is used, the lower its level; the less often it is used, the higher. The words you’d want to learn first for study end up on the dull side in the gacha. Practical value and star power, surprisingly, live apart.

Normally, you might think, “Could I use this in conversation?” But now you might lean forward and think, “What? This word is hardly ever used!” You check the rarity before reading the meaning. You thought you were opening a vocabulary book, but in your mind, a send-off for the rare-word hunters is already underway.

What’s more, when you draw a card rated “UR” or higher, the bonus “Jackpot” begins, and you can keep opening packs. You’re happy about one rare card, and then you get more time to open packs. Your willpower is just about to stand up and say, “All right, time to review,” when “Just one more” tugs at its sleeve.

What might happen here is not that you lose interest in English words. It’s that the reason for your interest changes. You go from “I draw a card because I want to learn” to “I look at words because I want to draw a card.” Even if you’re looking at the same cards, the person in charge inside your mind has changed from the study worker to the treasure hunter.

But here’s something worth underlining in red. Just because a word is rare doesn’t mean it should be high on your study list. Rarity is simply the game’s way of sorting words by how often they are used. “I’m happy I drew it” and “I should learn it first” are two separate things. Even if it shines, it doesn’t get special treatment in your study order.

But is this feeling limited to English? Wanting a mark of achievement more than the content. Wanting a reward more than reaching your goal. If you’ve felt that way, you may recognize a little of yourself in the urge to chase rare words. Oh, please don’t look at me.

Of course, this system alone can’t tell us whether people really get more interested in opening packs than in memorizing words. Still, if “It’s rare!” makes you stop and look at a word you would have passed by because it seemed useful, that’s an interesting way to spark curiosity. It’s unexpected to find a gacha at the entrance, though.

“What did you learn today?”
“More importantly, ask me what I drew!”

If that’s what you feel like saying, maybe you have what it takes to be a rare-word hunter. Now, before we move on to the next card, shall we look at the word’s meaning too?

## In one line
An English-word gacha makes rare, less-used words the prize, even though common words are usually better to learn first. Rarity reflects word frequency, not what learners should study first. The game alone cannot show whether players will focus more on opening packs than memorizing words.
