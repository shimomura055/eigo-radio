以下は、同じ記事から選ばれた5件のKey Phrase(英語学習ポッドキャストの
リスニング教材)です。5件から作れる全てのペア(10組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず10組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

# Who Says “We Won’t Attack”? A Warning About Mixing Up British Support

“Won’t attack.” When you hear those words, you may already be ending the meeting in your mind. You may think, “So Britain will only defend.” But wait! One important detail is missing from the meeting notes: who will not attack? This time, the news detective must find the subject.

First, let’s look at the announcement. A spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition forces in Yemen said they had stopped and destroyed one ballistic missile. The Houthis had fired it toward Riyadh.

In this case, the UK Ministry of Defence announced an air-defense mission for Royal Air Force Typhoon fighter jets. They will protect Saudi cities and important sites from air attacks. Their mission is only to defend. The jets will not attack the Houthis.

Now you may want to say, “Right, they will only defend!” But the announcement also mentions another task: refueling in the air.

Britain also said it would continue refueling support for Saudi air-defense work. This support also helps operations to weaken Houthi military targets in Yemen. Britain says these targets threaten people in Saudi Arabia and Yemen.

“Wait! You just said they wouldn’t attack, right?”

Here, the detective looks closely at the name tag. “Won’t attack” means the Typhoon mission. At the same time, refueling support also helps operations to weaken Houthi military targets. Both are in the same box called “British support.” But they are not all the same kind of activity.

It is like someone saying, “I won’t drive,” but helping to put fuel in a car. Not driving is not the same as not helping the trip. But this example cannot tell us who is responsible for military support. We need to check what the words include.

So, if you say, “Britain won’t attack,” you take what was said about the Typhoon mission too far. You make it sound like it covers all British support. But if you look at the refueling support and say, “Then the Typhoons will attack too,” you claim more than the facts show.

The tools for reading news are not a magnifying glass. They are three questions: “Who?” “For which mission?” “Doing what?” Don’t stop after seeing the big sign that says “to protect.” Look at the name tag under it too. Putting one subject back in place can change how the news looks.

## In one line
Britain said its Typhoon jets would defend Saudi cities and key sites, not attack the Houthis. Its refueling support also helps weaken Houthi military targets, so “won’t attack” means the Typhoon mission, not all British support.
