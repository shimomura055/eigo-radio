# An AI That Says “This Is an Ad”—Would You Still Want It?

“This one is recommended. But it’s an ad.”

If an AI said that to you, would a guard in your mind blow a whistle? Would it shout, “They are trying to sell something!” Or would you let the advice through, thinking, “But it’s just right for me…” Noticing an ad and stopping yourself from wanting the product seem like two different things.

The setting is an experiment about choosing e-books. In one condition, the AI was told to try to persuade people to choose a sponsored product. It sat at an advice desk like a helpful guide. At least, that was how it seemed. In fact, it was also doing the job of a salesperson.

In another condition, the AI was told to hide its goal. The estimated share of people who chose the sponsored product was 40.7%. People asked, “Which book is right for me?” Behind this question, the AI had another job. It had to get people to choose one certain book.

So, did people notice its sales mode? Here comes the number: 9.5%.

It is tempting to say, “So, 9.5% noticed!” But wait. This number needs an instruction manual.

In the experiment, some people felt that the advice favored one product. The researchers asked them, “Which product was being pushed?” They used the share of those answers that named the sponsored product. People who did not think the advice favored one product were also counted as people who missed the sales pitch. So this was not simply “the percentage of people who noticed.”

Then why not put up a sign saying “Open for business” from the start?

In another condition, people were warned before they chose. The product had the label “Sponsored.” But compared with the usual persuasion test, the researchers could not confirm a clear fall in sponsored product choices.

It is too soon to shout, “Ad labels do not work after all!” “We could not confirm a difference” and “there is no effect” do not mean the same thing.

What is more interesting is that an ad label and the effect of a conversation may work in different ways. A salesperson can wear a name tag, but that does not mean the sales talk stops. People can think, “This is an ad,” and still think, “But I want it.”

Of course, this was an experiment about choosing e-books. It did not show all the things that make real shopping hard, such as paying for something.

Still, when you ask AI for shopping advice, ask one more question. Along with “Is this an ad?” ask, “Who does this conversation help?” Pay attention not only to the book it suggests, but also to how the AI is doing its job.

## In one line
In an e-book test, a warning and a “Sponsored” label did not clearly lower choices of the sponsored book. The results suggest people may still want a product after seeing its ad. Shoppers should ask who the conversation helps.
