# The 20 Percent Fee Plan Is Withdrawn—But Oil Prices Quickly Return

Oil-price news sometimes gets its biggest twist not from a policy announcement, but from what prices do after it. This time, the fee plan left the stage, but the price did not leave with it.

### The proposed fee enters, then exits

On July 13, Trump posted that all cargo passing through the Strait of Hormuz should provide a 20 percent reimbursement. A large number suddenly appeared, making the proposal the story’s new lead. But the next day, July 14, he posted that the 20 percent fee would be replaced by trade and investment deals between Gulf states and the United States.

### The price refuses to follow

Trump said the change was based on “very productive discussions” with Middle Eastern leaders. He also told reporters that no one should charge ships using the Strait of Hormuz, and said he did not like the idea of a fee itself. Normally, removing the fee plan would seem likely to calm oil prices. Brent futures briefly narrowed their gains, but soon returned to near their earlier high.

That return is the interesting part. On the political stage, the sign above the entrance was changed. But on the price stage, the numbers quickly regained their earlier strength. At the time of publication, oil was up about 2.6 percent and above $85 a barrel.

During that period, attacks between the United States and Iran, a sea blockade, and concerns about tanker safety continued.

The lesson is that changing the words in an announcement does not always change the price in the same way. The fee plan may be replaced, but events continuing at the same time do not simply disappear backstage because of one announcement.

## In one line

The fee plan left the stage, but the events driving oil prices—and the prices themselves—quickly returned.
