# AI Pets Are Free. But Their Owners Still Have Only 24 Hours a Day

Work messages, news, videos, and that app you keep opening without thinking. Inside your smartphone, it’s already like a packed commuter train. What if someone said, “An AI pet is getting on, too”? And it’s free. Your wallet nods. But your calendar looks away.

Meta’s “Waddle World” is an AI pet app offered in the Philippines. It is free in the app stores, and its official website says there is no subscription either. The stamps you earn in the app cannot be bought with money. It’s certainly nice not to have to pore over a price list.

But anyone thinking, “I’ll let my child play with it,” should pause here. The descriptions in both app stores say users must be 18 or older. This time, it’s adults who are being asked whether they want to welcome a pet.

The age labels are a little confusing. Apple lists it as 18+, while Google Play gives it a content rating of 3+. However, the latter is not a rule about who can use the app. It does not mean “OK from age three!” A content rating and the age required to use it are two different things. Don’t skip over this just because the pet has a cute face.

So, if it costs nothing, the next thing to check might be how much storage space your phone has. Or maybe how much free time you have. The question is not, “Would I want it even if I had to pay?” but “Do I want to spend a few minutes with this pet today, too?” Even though it’s free, the test for moving in is surprisingly tough.

When it comes to usefulness, you can compare features side by side. But “I kind of want to see it” is hard to put in a chart. The appeal of AI pets for adults may lie less in how many things they can do and more in whether you want to make room for one in the gaps of a busy day.

Still, it’s too early to get excited about predictions of the future. When it will become available in Japan and other places had not been announced by the time of reporting. TechCrunch has suggested that it could be an early step toward future pet-like devices, but it does not say that any specific link has been confirmed.

Whether it will become a craze on the scale of Tamagotchi is still an open question. Will “It’s free, so I’ll try it” turn into “I want to see this pet for a little while today, too”? A place to try it out and a seat reserved every day are different. What is an adult’s smartphone missing? A new resident? Or a little room in the day to care about someone?
