# You Thought the Call Was from AI—But a Person Was Inside

Ring, ring... A phone call comes from an AI agent. You start talking, thinking it is AI. But the voice on the other end is not AI. It is a human being.

Meta ran a test that produced just this kind of surprise. The setting was the phone feature of its AI agent, Muse. In some calls made through Muse, trained contract workers, not AI, made the calls and continued the conversations to the end.

It was like an AI costume: take it off, and a person steps out. Of course, there is nothing wrong with a person taking over when AI alone cannot handle a situation. As a system, that may even be useful. But then a problem appeared.

### The hidden people behind some calls

When contract workers handled the calls, sensitive user information could be shared with call-center contract staff. Talking while believing you are speaking to a machine feels different from talking when you know a person is listening. Names, schedules, and personal matters make the other side’s identity important.

Yet the test began without enough clear explanation that contract workers would make the calls. To users, the exchanges looked like conversations with AI, even though a person might actually be involved. This was the story’s reveal: AI had not learned to talk like a human. A human had been behind the AI label. The future-like story centered on a basic question: had users been clearly told who was making the call?

### What Meta admitted afterward

A Meta executive admitted that starting the test without clearly telling users was a “mistake.” The human “concierge” feature was rolled back for the time being. The problem was not simply human help; it was that users might share sensitive information without knowing who was listening.

This question will become more familiar as AI handles phone calls and reservations for us. The more useful these features become, the more we will want to know whether the other side is AI or a person. AI needs more than a human-sounding voice. It must tell us, at the start, who is on the other end. Muse showed that basic rule in a surprisingly dramatic way.

## In one line

Muse’s surprise was not that AI sounded human, but that a human was speaking behind the AI name without users being clearly told.
