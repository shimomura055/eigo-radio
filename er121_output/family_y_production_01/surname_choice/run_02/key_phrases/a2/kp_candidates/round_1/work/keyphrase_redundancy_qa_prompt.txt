それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

【記事本文(参考、文脈確認用)】
If your last name changed when you married, how would you feel? A husband may choose his wife’s last name on the marriage form. Or he may join her family as an adopted son. He and both families have their own feelings. They may care about different things. How can they choose something they can all accept? Here are three people with different lives and situations.

The first speaker is a father. He took his wife’s last name and joined her family as an adopted son. He lives with his family at his wife’s parents’ home. I became an adopted son in my wife’s family and took their last name. Changing my last name did not bother me at all. My parents accepted it easily, even though my hometown is far out in the country. They were not old-fashioned about it. My wife’s parents were the ones who worried. They asked me again and again if I was sure. I was thankful that both families accepted my choice and were not upset. I still find it a little hard to have little time by myself at my wife’s parents’ home. But learning how each family does things was harder than changing my last name. When I accepted that our families were different, I felt much more at ease.

The next speaker is a mother. Her son will marry next year. She felt sad when she heard he would take his wife’s last name. Honestly, I was sad. When I heard my son would marry next year and take his wife’s last name, I did not like it. He will live near her parents’ home, far from us. When he said he would change his last name too, I felt my son was moving away. I was very upset. But was the last name change the only thing I disliked? I think I really wanted him to stay close. Someone said that was an old-fashioned way to think. I think they were right. I need to let my son go, don’t I? I still feel sad, but I want to accept his choice. I want to be kind to his wife without getting too close, and care about their happiness.

The last speaker is a parent. A relative took his wife’s last name. The parent’s own child is not married yet. I think it is fine for a man to take his wife’s last name. If my son chose that, I would not be against it. But I cannot stop thinking about a relative. He took his wife’s last name without talking to his parents. I know his mother, my aunt, was so shocked that she became ill. But if it were my son, I would still feel bothered if his partner’s parents thought it was only natural. I want them to feel at least a little thankful to my son. I say they should decide for themselves, but it still bothers me. Even so, I do not want to stop my son because of my feelings as a parent. If he talked to me before choosing a last name, I could tell him how I feel too. I hope I could have that kind of talk with his partner’s parents as well.

The man who joined his wife’s family as an adopted son said he was thankful that both families accepted him. A parent who respects a son’s choice said they wanted the other parents to feel at least a little thankful to their son. In a talk about last names, perhaps a person’s choice meets a parent’s feeling that they are giving something up. Along with a couple’s choice about their last name, parents may have feelings they want to share with the other parents. If your son chose his wife’s last name, what would you want to hear from her parents?
