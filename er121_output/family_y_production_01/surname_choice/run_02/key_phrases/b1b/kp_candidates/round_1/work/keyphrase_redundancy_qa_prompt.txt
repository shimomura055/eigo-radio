それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。

If your last name changed when you got married, how would you feel? Whether a husband chooses his wife’s last name on the marriage registration form or joins her family as an adopted son, he and each of their families have their own feelings. When they care about different things, how can they choose something they can both accept? Here are the voices of three people with different situations and lives.

The first speaker is a father who took his wife’s last name when he joined her family as an adopted son. He lives with his family at his wife’s parents’ home. I became an adopted son in my wife’s family and took their last name. I had no problem at all with changing my last name. My parents accepted it easily, even though my hometown is way out in the country. They weren’t old-fashioned about it. It was my wife’s parents who worried, asking me again and again if I was really sure. I was grateful that both sets of parents accepted my decision without taking offense. I still find it a little hard that I don’t get much time to myself while living at my wife’s parents’ home, but adjusting to the way each family does things was harder than changing my last name. Once I could accept that of course we were different, I felt much more at ease.

The next speaker is a mother whose son is getting married next year. She felt sad when she heard he would take his wife’s last name. Honestly, I was sad. When I heard that my son, who is getting married next year, would take his wife’s last name, I didn’t like it. He’ll live near her parents’ home, far from us. When he said he would change his last name too, I felt like my son was moving away, and I got very upset. But was it only the change of last name that I didn’t like? I think the truth is that I wanted him to stay close. Someone told me that was an old-fashioned way to think, and I think they were right. I need to let go of my son, don’t I? I still feel sad, but I want to accept his choice to take his wife’s last name. I want to treat his wife without getting too close, and care about their happiness.

The last speaker is a parent whose relative took his wife’s last name. Their own child has not married yet. I think it’s fine for a man to take his wife’s last name. If my son decided to do that, I wouldn’t oppose it, but I can’t help thinking about a relative. He took his wife’s last name without discussing it with his parents, and I know that his mother, my aunt, was so shocked that she became ill. But if it were my son, I would still be bothered if his partner’s parents thought it was only natural. I want them to feel at least a little grateful to my son. I say that they should decide for themselves, but that still bothers me. Even so, I don’t want to hold my son back because of my feelings as a parent. If he talked to me before deciding on a last name, I could share how I feel, too. I hope I could have that kind of conversation with his partner’s parents as well.

The man who joined his wife’s family as an adopted son said he was grateful that both sets of parents accepted him. A parent who respects a son’s choice said they wanted the other parents to feel at least a little grateful to their son. In a conversation about last names, perhaps a person’s own choice overlaps with the feeling that a parent is giving something up. Alongside the couple’s decision about their last name, there are feelings that parents want to share with the other parents. If your son chose his wife’s last name, what would you want to hear from her parents?
