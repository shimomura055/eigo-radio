# Different Voices When a Husband Chooses His Wife’s Last Name

If your last name changed when you got married, how would you and your family feel? Choosing the wife’s last name means that the couple decide on their marriage papers to use her name. It does not involve adoption. When the couple and their families have feelings about this, what should they value as they decide? Here are the voices of three people with different situations and lives.

First is a father who was adopted into his wife’s family and took their last name. He lives with his family at his wife’s parents’ home.
I was adopted into my wife’s family, and I use their last name. I had no hesitation at all about changing my last name. My parents’ home is in a pretty rural place, but they don’t have old-fashioned views, and they accepted it easily. It was my wife’s parents who were more careful. They checked with me many times to make sure I was really sure. I was grateful that both sets of parents accepted my decision without being upset. Living at my wife’s parents’ home, I still find it a little hard that I have so little time to myself. But fitting in with each family’s idea of what is normal was harder than changing my last name. Once I could accept that it was normal for things to be different, I felt much more at ease.

Next is a mother whose son is getting married next year. She felt lonely when she heard that he would take his wife’s last name.
Honestly, I felt lonely. When I heard that my son, who is getting married next year, would take his bride’s last name, I didn’t like it. They’ll live near her parents’ home, far from us. Then he said he would change his last name too, and I felt like my son was moving away. I got really upset. But was it only the change of last name that I didn’t like? I think what I really felt was that I wanted him to stay close. Someone pointed out that I was thinking in an old-fashioned way, and I thought they were right. I need to let my son go. I still feel lonely, but I want to accept his choice to take his wife’s last name. I want to be kind to his wife without getting too close, and to care about their happiness as a couple.

Last is someone from the parents’ generation whose relative chose his wife’s last name. Their own child’s marriage is still in the future.
I think it’s fine for a man to choose his wife’s last name. If my son made that choice, I wouldn’t oppose him, but I keep thinking about what happened in my family. I know a relative who took his wife’s last name without first talking it over with his parents. The relative’s mother, my aunt, was so shocked that she became ill. But if it were my son, it would still bother me if his partner’s parents thought it was only natural. I want them to feel at least a little grateful to my son. I say the couple should decide for themselves, but that still bothers me. Even so, I don’t want my feelings as a parent to hold my son back. If he talked to me before deciding on a last name, I could tell him how I feel. I hope we can have that kind of time with his partner’s parents too.

The man who was adopted into his wife’s family says he was grateful that both his parents and his wife’s parents accepted his decision. The parent who says they don’t want to hold their son back wanted his partner’s parents to be grateful to their son. Thanks for accepting his decision and thanks for changing his last name go in opposite directions. That difference may also show who they think the choice to take the wife’s last name is for. If your son chose his wife’s last name, what would you want his partner’s parents to say to you?
