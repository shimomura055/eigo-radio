# Package Redelivery: Different Voices

What if you had to pay to have a package delivered again when you weren’t home? Charging for redelivery means the person receiving the package pays a fee on top of the regular delivery charge. How should we decide whether to charge a fee or reduce redeliveries in some other way? Let’s hear from three people with different roles and different lives.

First is a delivery driver who delivers packages every day, going back to homes where no one was home.
When I leave a missed-delivery notice and head off, I do feel a little down. But then I go on to the next house. I do hope there will be fewer redeliveries, if possible, and they really are hard work. Even so, I can’t just say that customers should pay that cost. I think it makes more sense to cover the cost through the overall delivery charge than to charge each customer who couldn’t receive a package for that redelivery. For that to happen, I’d also like the companies to think about whether free delivery for online shopping should stay as it is. If they review delivery charges, I want them to review how those charges are set, too, so that the money actually goes to the people who deliver the packages.

Next is someone who receives packages while both they and their spouse work and are often away from home.
Paying for redelivery just doesn’t feel right to me. My husband and I are both away from home for work, so for packages where I can choose a delivery time, I choose a day and time when I’m sure I’ll be home. But once, I chose a morning time slot and the package didn’t come. I went out to run an errand, and when I got back, there was a missed-delivery notice. There are also many packages where I can’t choose a delivery time, so I don’t know in advance when they’ll arrive. Before charging people, I want them to first work on coming at the time we chose and letting us know when a package will arrive. The delivery workers are probably having a hard time because there aren’t enough of them, so maybe it can’t be helped. But I can’t accept having to pay for that. Am I wrong to feel this way?

Last is someone who takes the trouble to pick up packages at a delivery office or locker to avoid redelivery.
I try to receive packages at a delivery office or locker so I don’t cause trouble by needing redelivery. It’s convenient to pick them up when it suits me, but I can’t choose a locker for some items. And even when I ask for a package to be sent to a delivery office, it can arrive late, so sometimes I still end up receiving it in person. Even so, I go to the trouble of thinking about how to receive my packages, and honestly, I think it’s wrong that I pay the same fee as people who are always away and ask for redelivery. Since redelivery takes up the delivery workers’ time, it’s fine to charge for it. I’d be okay with a system where the first redelivery is free and people who are repeatedly not home pay. But maybe that’s easy for me to say, since the delivery office is nearby and I can choose how to receive my packages.

A missed-delivery notice was left at the home of someone who had chosen a morning delivery time. For people who can choose how to receive packages, a redelivery fee can be a signal to find another way. But simply making the person receiving the package pay will not change delivery delays or the way people are told when packages will arrive. The person receiving the package also can’t change how much the delivery fee is or how much of it goes to the people who deliver the packages. When you couldn’t be home and it caused a problem, could you choose how to receive the package? Did you know when it would arrive? Can charging the person receiving the package change that problem?
