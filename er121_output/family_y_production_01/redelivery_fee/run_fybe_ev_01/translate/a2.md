# Package Redelivery: Different Voices

What if you had to pay for a second delivery when you were away from home? A redelivery fee means the person getting the package pays extra. They pay it on top of the usual delivery cost. Should we charge a fee, or find another way to reduce redeliveries? Here are three people with different jobs and lives.

First is a delivery driver who delivers packages every day. They return to homes where no one was home.  
When I leave a missed-delivery note and go, I feel a little sad. But then I go to the next house. I hope there will be fewer redeliveries, if possible. They really are hard work. Still, I can’t just say customers should pay that cost. I think it is better to include the cost in the total delivery price. That is better than charging each customer who could not get a package. For that to happen, I want companies to think about keeping free delivery for online shopping. If they review delivery prices, I want them to check how they set them, too. That way, the money can really go to the people who deliver packages.

Next is someone who works. Their spouse works too, and both are often away from home.  
Paying for another delivery just doesn’t feel right to me. My husband and I are both away from home for work. When I can choose a delivery time, I pick one when I know I’ll be home. But once, I chose a morning time, and the package did not come. I went out to do an errand. When I got back, there was a missed-delivery note. Many packages do not let me choose a delivery time. So I don’t know when they will come. Before charging people, I want them to first work on coming at the time we chose. I also want them to tell us when a package will come. There may not be enough delivery workers, so they are probably having a hard time. Maybe it can’t be helped. But I can’t accept having to pay for that. Am I wrong to feel this way?

Last is someone who picks up packages at a delivery office or locker. They do this to avoid causing trouble with another delivery.  
I try to get packages at a delivery office or locker so I don’t cause trouble with another delivery. It’s convenient to pick them up when it suits me. But I can’t choose a locker for some items. Even when I ask for a package to go to a delivery office, it can arrive late. So sometimes I still have to receive it in person. Even so, I take care to think about how to get my packages. Honestly, I think it is wrong that I pay the same fee as people who are always away. They ask for another delivery. Since another delivery takes up delivery workers’ time, it is fine to charge for it. I’d be okay with a system where the first redelivery is free. People who are not home again and again would pay. But perhaps that is easy for me to say. The delivery office is nearby, and I can choose how to get my packages.

A missed-delivery note was left at the home of someone who chose a morning delivery time. For people who can choose how to get packages, a fee for another delivery may be a sign to find another way. But making the person who gets the package pay will not fix late deliveries. It will not change how people are told when packages will arrive. The person who gets the package also cannot change the delivery fee. Nor can they change how much of it goes to the people who deliver packages. If you could not be home and this caused a problem, could you choose how to get the package? Did you know when it would arrive? Can making the person who gets the package pay change that problem?
