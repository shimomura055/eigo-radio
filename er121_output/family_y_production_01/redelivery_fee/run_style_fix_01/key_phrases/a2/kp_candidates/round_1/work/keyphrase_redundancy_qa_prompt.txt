それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」

- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず6組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

【記事本文(参考、文脈確認用)】
What if you had to pay when a package came again because you were not home? A redelivery fee is extra money paid by the person getting the package. It is added to the usual delivery cost. How should we decide whether to charge this fee or cut repeat deliveries another way? Let’s hear from three people with different lives and views.

First, hear from a delivery driver who visits homes every day. When I leave a note saying no one was home, I feel a little sad, too. But then I go to the next house. I really want fewer redeliveries. They are hard on us, too. Even so, I can’t simply say customers should pay the cost. I think the cost should be part of the general fee for carrying packages. That makes more sense than charging each person who was not home for their redelivery. I want the company to think about whether free delivery for online orders should stay as it is. If it changes delivery fees, I want it to look at how it sets them, too. Then the money can really reach the people who deliver packages.

Next, hear from a woman who gets packages and is often away. She and her husband both work. Having to pay for a redelivery just doesn’t feel right to me. My husband and I are both away at work. So, when we can choose a delivery time, we choose a day and time when we know we’ll be home. But once, I chose a morning time, and the package did not come. I went out to do an errand. When I came back, there was a note saying they had tried to deliver it. There are also many packages where we cannot choose a time. We do not know when they will come, either. Before they charge us, I want them to fix two things: come at the time we chose and tell us when the package will arrive. Delivery workers must have a hard time because there are not enough of them. I guess it can’t be helped. But I can’t accept having to pay for that. Am I wrong to feel this way?

Lastly, hear from someone who picks up packages at a locker or another place to avoid another delivery. I try to pick up packages at a delivery office or a locker so I don’t cause extra work with redelivery. It is handy to pick them up when it suits me. But I can’t choose a locker for some things. Even when I ask for a package to go to a delivery office, it may arrive late. So sometimes I end up getting it in person after all. Still, I take the time to think about how to get my packages. Honestly, I think it’s wrong that I pay the same fee as people who are always away and ask for redelivery. Redelivery takes up the delivery worker’s time, so charging for it is fine. I’d be happy if the first redelivery were free, and people who were away again and again had to pay. But maybe that’s just my view. The delivery office is nearby, and I can choose how to get my packages.

A note saying delivery was missed was left at the home of someone who had chosen a morning time, too. For people who can choose how to get packages, a redelivery fee can be a sign that encourages them to make an effort. But making the person who gets the package pay will not fix late deliveries or the way people are told when packages will arrive. People who get packages cannot change the delivery cost. They also cannot decide how much of it goes to delivery workers. When you could not be home and had a problem, could you choose how to get your package? Did you know when it would arrive? Can charging the person who gets the package fix that problem?
