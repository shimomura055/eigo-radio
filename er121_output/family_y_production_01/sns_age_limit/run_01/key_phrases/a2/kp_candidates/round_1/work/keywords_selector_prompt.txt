【記事本文】
If a child says they want to use social media, how much should you let them use? Setting limits until a certain age is different from stopping them from having an account at all. First, should Japan set an age limit for children using social media? Let’s hear from three people with different views and lives.

First, a parent with a young child speaks. They think about social media while looking after their child each day. My young child picked videos that were too much for them, and things in the app that could cost money, just because they were interested. When I talk to them, they often say, “Then I won’t do it.” I think they understand when I explain it. But maybe they can’t see what’s wrong before they choose. I don’t think it’s okay to let them use anything they happen to like. Seeing this, I feel safer if there are rules for using social media at different ages. But honestly, on a long trip, I want them to be quiet. It helps if they watch something. Limits are hard on parents too. Even so, I still can’t say how far I want the rules to go.

Next, a high school student speaks. Social media is a normal way for him to keep in touch with friends. He talks about children’s use as a user. I don’t want people to stop me using social media just because of my age. If even high school students couldn’t have accounts, it would be hard to keep in touch with friends. I’m against a ban. I want a say in what I can use and how I use it. I want to decide for myself, but I can’t handle everything that happens on my own. If people say bad things about me or threaten me, I’d still want to go to an adult right away. Maybe that’s selfish of me. Instead of giving everyone the same limits because of their age, I think we can do other things, like set safety filters. I want the way we deal with this to fit how we use it. But we should still be able to keep in touch with friends.

Last, a parent who lives with a middle school child speaks. They must decide at home how their child uses social media. As a parent of a middle school child, I want to choose how much they use social media, based on what they can handle. Rather than draw a line based only on age, I think it would be better, if possible, to let them use more features step by step as they learn to handle them. I don’t want the only choices to be letting them use it or banning it. I want different steps. If my child started using social media with no preparation as soon as they reached the age when it had been banned, I would worry. I don’t think reaching the age when it’s okay to use it means they’ll suddenly know how to keep themselves safe. Just as you need to practice swimming before going into the sea, it’s still scary when a child who isn’t ready is suddenly thrown into it.

The high school student said that wanting adults to help might be “selfish.” It is hard to put two things together: choosing how to use social media, and going to adults when something goes wrong. The parent of a middle school child said children won’t suddenly know how to keep themselves safe, even when they are old enough to use it. Setting an age when children can use social media also brings up another question: how much can they turn to adults after they start? If you let a child in your life use social media, which parts of their use would you let them decide? And what kinds of trouble would you help them with?
