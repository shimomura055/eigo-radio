# Is the “Brain” AI’s Only Star? Let’s Applaud the Work of “Connecting” Behind the Scenes

AI news today is once again a showcase for a star’s latest tricks. “It’s gotten smarter again!” “It can do even that?” The audience is cheering. But this time, let’s move the spotlight a little to one side. I’d like to pay some attention to the work of “connecting,” whose name was called alongside the star.

The trigger was Broadcom’s earnings report. Sales of AI semiconductors came to $16.7 billion. According to the CEO, that was up 221% from the same period the year before. That number is too striking to simply read past. It makes you want to clap and say, “AI is booming!” But there’s more to see.

The CEO said demand remained very strong for “custom AI accelerators” and “networking.” When two technical terms appear side by side, it can suddenly feel like the audience has been left behind. For now, let’s keep two ideas in mind: “for AI” and “connecting equipment.”

What catches my interest is the latter: “connecting.” If AI is the star of a live show, we tend to focus on its dazzling performance. But when we look at the earnings report, we hear a voice from backstage, too: “There’s strong demand over here as well!” Oh, so things are lively over there, too.

Of course, the word “connecting” sounds plain. Unlike a new feature, it does not easily make you say, “Wow!” at first glance. Even so, the CEO named it as an area with strong demand. If we follow only the flashy answers, we might miss this part of the show.

But it is too soon to write a headline in our minds saying, “The real culprit behind the sales jump is connecting technology!” This explanation does not tell us how much each area sold. And “very strong demand” is the CEO’s own view. Before handing out the lead actor award in the excitement, let’s separate what we know from what we don’t.

The company also expects its total sales to rise next quarter. But this is a forecast about the future. Unlike results that have already come in, the actual results may be different. “Sales have already happened” and “sales may rise from here” are two different things. The more excited we get about strong numbers, the more important it is to keep this difference in mind.

The next time a new AI trick surprises you, try looking at the side that supports it, too. Along with asking, “What can it do?” ask, “What is in demand in businesses related to AI?” Starting with $16.7 billion, this time we’re looking at “connecting.” When we look at the backstage as well as the stars, the earnings report, like a show, can look a little different.

## In one line
Broadcom reported $16.7 billion in AI semiconductor sales, while its CEO said demand remained very strong for custom AI accelerators and networking. That puts networking’s behind-the-scenes role in focus, though the report does not show how much revenue came from it.
