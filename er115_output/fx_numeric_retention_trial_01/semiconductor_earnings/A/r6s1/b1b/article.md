# The AI Theater: The One Who Connects Things Beside the Star Is Getting Attention, Too

“How much smarter has AI become?”

Whenever we open the news, we check how the star is improving. It answered a hard question. It learned a new skill. Great, let’s clap! But today, I want to point the microphone at the quiet worker beside it. It seems things are lively backstage, too.

First, the numbers Broadcom announced are impressive. Sales of AI chips were $16.7 billion, up 221% from the same period last year. The number alone is so big, you can almost hear entrance music and see smoke rising behind it.

But hold the confetti for now. The $16.7 billion is not the company’s total sales; it is sales of AI chips alone. The flashier the number, the more we should check its name tag. In a quick-buzzer quiz, this could be a trick question.

This time, what we should listen closely to is the CEO’s explanation. Demand for custom AI accelerators and networking remains very strong.

There is strong demand for AI chips made for specific uses. That makes sense. But there was someone else, too.

“Wait, you too, the one who connects things?”

Networking is the system that lets devices share information with one another. If we imagine AI as a theater, the one we watch is the star who can deliver even difficult lines smoothly. Meanwhile, this is the crew member who keeps communication backstage going.

If we were interviewing the lead star, a voice from next door might say, “We’re getting orders over here, too.” We turn around and find ourselves wondering about the backstage crew’s schedule, too. That’s the fun of this story.

Still, shouting, “Networking was the real star all along!” would be a little hasty. We shouldn’t start the awards ceremony just yet.

This information alone does not tell us how much custom AI accelerators and networking each added to sales. The statement that demand is strong and the amount each contributed to sales growth are two different things.

Even so, we shouldn’t miss the fact that the CEO mentioned both. AI news can be interesting not only for how smart the “brain” is, but also for what is connected to what, and how.

We usually see the answers that appear on the screen. When we admire how good they are, let’s sometimes take a look backstage, too. What devices are there, and how are they connected? There are questions there that would not occur to us if we only watched the star.

This isn’t about replacing the lead. We can clap while simply moving the microphone a little to the side.

“By the way, the one who connects things, are you busy over there, too?”

## In one line
