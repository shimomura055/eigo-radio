# The AI Theater: The One Who Connects Things Beside the Star Is Getting Attention, Too

“How much smarter has AI become?”

When we open the news, we check how the star is getting better. It answers a hard question. It learns a new skill. Great, let’s clap! But today, I want to point the microphone at the quiet worker beside it. Things seem busy backstage, too.

First, the numbers Broadcom announced are impressive. Sales of AI chips reached $16.7 billion, up 221% from the same time last year. The number is so big, we can almost hear its entrance music. We can almost see smoke rise behind it.

But don’t throw the confetti yet. The $16.7 billion is not all of the company’s sales. It is only sales of AI chips. The bigger a number looks, the more we should check its name tag. In a fast quiz show, this could be a trick question.

This time, we should listen closely to what the CEO says. Demand for custom AI accelerators and networking remains very strong.

There is strong demand for AI chips made for special jobs. That makes sense. But the CEO mentioned someone else, too.

“Wait, you too, the one who connects things?”

Networking is the system that lets devices share information. If we imagine AI as a theater, the one we watch is the star. The star can say even difficult lines smoothly. Meanwhile, this is the crew member who keeps messages moving backstage.

If we were interviewing the lead star, a voice from next door might say, “We’re getting orders over here, too.” We turn around and start wondering about the backstage crew’s schedule, too. That is the fun of this story.

Still, saying, “Networking was the real star all along!” would be too quick. We should not start the awards ceremony yet.

This information alone does not tell us how much custom AI accelerators added to sales. We also do not know how much networking added. Saying demand is strong is not the same as saying how much each added to sales growth.

Even so, we should notice that the CEO mentioned both. AI news is interesting not only because the “brain” is smart. It also matters what is connected to what, and how.

We usually see the answers on the screen. When we admire how good they are, let’s sometimes look backstage, too. What devices are there? How are they connected? These are questions we might not think of if we only watched the star.

This isn’t about replacing the lead. We can clap and simply move the microphone a little to the side.

“By the way, the one who connects things, are you busy over there, too?”

## In one line
Broadcom reported $16.7 billion in AI chip sales, with strong demand for custom chips and networking. We look beyond AI’s “brain,” but do not know each part’s share of sales growth.
