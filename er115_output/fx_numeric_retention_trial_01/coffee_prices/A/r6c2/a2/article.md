# Coffee prices have fallen, so why do the price tags still look so proud?

When you hear “coffee prices have fallen,” your mind may already hear music for a big sale. But when you reach the store, prices are still high. They stand their ground. “Um, haven’t you seen the news?”

Actually, the price tags have not missed the news. They follow a different calendar from ours.

The main world guide to coffee prices fell on average during the month. It was lower than the month before. At the same time, the US consumer price index for coffee in cities was 7.1% higher that month than in the same month a year earlier. It was also higher than the month before.

Coffee prices and price tags going separate ways? Did they have a fight?

The key to this mismatch is “a time lag.” Today’s price tag is not a live report of today’s market price. It shows the cost of beans from some time ago, arriving late. You could call it “a bill from the past.”

Experts estimated this in a report Reuters shared earlier. A change in green coffee bean prices takes at least about nine months to reach store prices. The beans have to be roasted and packed first. So the change takes time to reach the price tag.

We may get excited and say, “I hear beans have got cheaper!” But the price tag says, “We’re still using costs from when prices were high.” No wonder they are not on the same page.

What’s more, the news agency said at the time that most of the rise in US store prices for coffee came from past shortages in coffee supplies and higher prices for green coffee beans. If you look only at recent news to explain a price rise, you may miss the alibi the timeline gives.

“I see. So there’ll be a sale in nine months?”

Sorry, that conclusion is too quick. Experts said it takes at least about nine months. This is only an estimate. It is not a set time for every company or product. It also does not promise that prices will definitely fall then.

A time lag is one reason why lower market prices do not reach price tags right away. Even if coffee stays expensive, that does not always mean the tags go against today’s news.

Next time you sigh at the store, ask this after “That’s expensive!”: “When were those beans bought?” It may be hard on your wallet, but the price tag may still be receiving a delivery from the past.

## In one line
World coffee prices fell, but US store prices kept rising because tags still showed bean costs from months earlier. Experts estimate changes can take nine months or more to reach stores. The wait can differ, and lower prices are not certain.
