# Coffee prices have fallen, so why are the price tags still so smug?

When you hear “coffee prices have fallen,” your mind is already playing the music for a big sale. But when you get to the store, the prices are still high, standing their ground. “Um, haven’t you seen the news?”

Actually, the price tags are not out of touch with the news. They are looking at a different calendar from the one we are.

The international benchmark for coffee prices fell on average over the month, compared with the month before. At the same time, in the US that month, the consumer price index for coffee in cities was up 7.1% from the same month a year earlier. It was also up from the month before.

Coffee prices and price tags going their separate ways? Did they have a fight?

The key to understanding this mismatch is “a time lag.” Today’s price tag is not a live broadcast of today’s market price. It is a place where the cost of beans from some time ago arrives late. You could call it “a bill from the past.”

Experts estimated, in a report Reuters shared earlier, that it takes at least about nine months for a change in the price of green coffee beans to reach store prices. The beans have to be roasted and packed, so there is a wait before the change reaches the price tag.

We may get excited and say, “I hear beans have got cheaper!” But the price tag says, “We’re still bringing you the cost from when prices were high.” No wonder they are not on the same page.

What’s more, the news agency reported at the time that most of the rise in US store prices for coffee was due to past supply shortages and higher prices for green coffee beans. If you look only at recent news to find the cause of a price rise, you may miss the alibi provided by the timeline.

“I see. So there’ll be a sale in nine months?”

Sorry, that’s too quick a conclusion. “At least about nine months” was an estimate by experts. It is not a set period that applies to every company and every product. Nor is it a promise that prices will definitely fall at that time.

A time lag is one reason why a fall in market prices does not reach price tags right away. Even if coffee stays expensive, that does not always mean it goes against today’s news.

Next time you sigh at the store, after “That’s expensive!” ask, “When were those beans bought?” It may be hard on your wallet, but the price tag may still be taking in a delivery from the past.

## In one line
Coffee prices fell on the international market, but US store prices kept rising because price tags reflect bean costs from months earlier. Experts estimate it takes around nine months or more for price changes to reach stores, but the timing varies and lower prices are not guaranteed.
