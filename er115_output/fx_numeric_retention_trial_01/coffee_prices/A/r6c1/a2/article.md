# Coffee Price Tags Are Still Jet-Lagged

“We’ve ended the tariffs.” Hearing this, I lowered the coffee price in my mind. But when I got to the store, the real price tag had not changed at all. I wanted to ask the shelf, “Haven’t you heard?”

But price tags have their reasons. This number seems to show news from a little while ago, not the latest news.

In the United States, the average store price of ground coffee rose in September. It was 41% higher than in the same month a year earlier. This is not about a special cup ordered at a café. It is about the coffee you make at home. It should wake you up, but its price is enough to do that.

Tariffs seem like a possible cause. You might want to point and say, “You did it!” But don’t decide too soon. This story gets complicated if we mix up when each character comes in.

Reuters says there were two main reasons for the price rise. A bean shortage had been going on before the tariffs. Raw coffee beans were also getting more expensive. Tariffs also made the beans used to make coffee cost more. But when the report came out, this effect had not yet fully reached the prices shoppers paid.

So, the earlier bean shortage now affects store prices. Higher costs for raw beans affect them too. The tariff effect came later, and it has not fully arrived yet. The news may come out now, but it takes time to reach the price tag.

So, if tariffs are taken away, can this delivery be canceled? We might hope for news like, “Yes, prices will be lower starting tomorrow!” But it is not that simple.

It was reported that even after the United States removed tariffs on coffee and other goods, changes in raw-material prices would take time to reach each step. These steps are roasting, packing, and selling beans in stores.

Experts think the wait is about nine months or longer. We want to buy a cup today, but the clock on the price tag moves very slowly.

Still, it is too soon to take out the calendar. This does not mean prices will definitely fall in about nine months. It is an estimate of how long changes in raw-bean prices take to affect store prices. It does not work the same way for every company or product.

“Tariffs are gone” and “coffee costs less” do not always happen at the same time. Price tags do not have a switch that flips as soon as a policy is announced.

Next time you look twice at a price in a store, you might also ask, “When is this number’s news from?” A price tag may look like it belongs to today, but tell a story about what was happening a little while ago.

## In one line
In September, average U.S. ground coffee prices were 41% higher than in September a year earlier, mainly because of earlier bean shortages and higher raw-bean prices. Tariff effects had not yet fully reached shoppers; after tariffs ended, experts estimated raw-bean price changes might take about nine months or longer to reach stores, but this is not a promise of lower prices, and timing varies by company and product.
