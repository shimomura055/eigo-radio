# Coffee Price Tags Are Still Jet-Lagged

“We’ve ended the tariffs.” Hearing that news, I lowered the coffee price in my mind. But when I got to the store, the real price tag had not moved at all. I felt like asking the shelf, “Haven’t you heard?”

But price tags have their reasons. It seems this number is reading news from a little while ago, not the latest news.

In the United States, the average retail price of ground coffee in September was 41% higher than in the same month a year earlier. This isn’t about a special cup ordered at a café. It’s about the coffee you make at home. It’s supposed to wake you up, but the price alone is enough to do that.

Tariffs come to mind as a suspect. You might want to point and say, “You did it!” But don’t jump to conclusions. This story gets complicated if we mix up when each character enters.

According to Reuters, the main reasons for the price rise were a shortage of beans that had been going on before the tariffs, and rising prices for raw coffee beans. Tariffs also made the beans used to make coffee more expensive, but at the time of the report, that effect had not yet fully reached the prices consumers paid.

In other words, what is making its presence felt on store shelves now is the earlier shortage of beans and higher costs for raw beans. The effect of tariffs, which came later, has not fully arrived yet. The news may be breaking, but delivery to the price tag takes its time.

So, if tariffs are withdrawn, can this delivery be canceled? We might hope for a story like, “Yes, prices will be lower starting tomorrow!” But it isn’t that simple.

It was reported that even after the United States withdrew tariffs on coffee and other goods, it would take time for changes in the price of raw materials to reach prices at the stages where beans are roasted, packed, and sold in stores.

Experts estimate that the wait is at least about nine months. We want to buy a cup today, but the price tag’s clock moves very slowly.

Still, it’s too soon to take out the calendar. This is not a promise that “prices will definitely fall in about nine months.” It is an estimate of how long it takes for the price of raw beans to affect retail prices, and it does not apply in the same way to every company or product.

“Tariffs are gone” and “coffee is cheaper” are not always shown at the same time. Price tags don’t have a switch that flips the moment a policy is announced.

Next time you look twice at a price in the store, you might also ask, “When is this number’s news from?” The price tag may look like it belongs to today while telling a story about what was happening a little while ago.

## In one line
U.S. ground coffee prices rose 41% year over year, mainly because of earlier bean shortages and higher raw-bean prices. Tariff effects had not fully reached shoppers; after tariffs were withdrawn, experts estimated raw-bean price changes could take about nine months or longer to reach stores. That is only an estimate, not a promise of lower prices, and timing varies by company and product.
