# Cockroach Milk: The Way It’s Stored Is More Amazing Than Drinking It

If the café in your mind closed the moment you heard “cockroach milk,” don’t worry. We’re not drinking any today. No glass or straw needed. All you need is a little extra curiosity.

This study won this year’s Ig Nobel Prize. Its stars are not a new product for people, but baby cockroaches growing inside their mothers—and nutrient-rich protein crystals in their guts.

Milk, but a crystal. Suddenly, we’ve been led from the “drinks” aisle to a warehouse tour. And the location? Inside the young cockroaches’ guts. It could hardly be more unexpected.

Researchers studied a type of cockroach whose young grow inside the mother. They removed crystals from part of the young cockroaches’ intestines and used X-rays to analyze their three-dimensional structure.

They found that substances related to fats were attached to the proteins, and that some parts had chains of sugars, too. It is called a “protein crystal,” but it is not a simple box containing only protein.

The paper also includes a number that makes you read it twice. For the same weight, these crystals are estimated to contain more than three times as much energy as milk from several kinds of mammals.

“So, could this be the next super drink?”

Wait. We can’t put that on a menu yet. This study did not look at the nutritional effects on people who eat them, or whether they are safe as food. To conclude from the energy figure alone that they would be amazing to drink is like opening a restaurant just because a warehouse tour got you excited.

The point of this study is not “they’re amazing to drink,” but “who knew nutrients could be stored this way?” A crystal of nutrients appears just when you were imagining a liquid. That surprise alone is plenty to feed our curiosity.

The authors suggest that storing nutrients as crystals may help feed young before they are born. But that role has not yet been confirmed. We can see what the warehouse looks like, but there is still more to learn about what it does.

When we look at food, we tend to wonder what is in it. But the way living things store food can also be fascinating.

Cockroach milk. Even if you have no plans to order it, doesn’t its storage trick make you want to lean in a little?

## In one line
Researchers found nutrient-rich crystals in baby cockroaches’ guts, estimated to contain over three times the energy of the same weight of several kinds of mammal milk. The study reveals a surprising way to store nutrients, but did not test the crystals’ effects or safety as food for people.
