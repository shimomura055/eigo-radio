# Cockroach Milk: How It Is Stored Is More Amazing Than Drinking It

If the café in your mind closed when you heard “cockroach milk,” don’t worry. We are not drinking any today. You do not need a glass or a straw. You only need a little more curiosity.

This study won this year’s Ig Nobel Prize. Its stars are not a new product for people. They are baby cockroaches growing inside their mothers, and protein crystals full of nutrients in their guts.

Milk, but a crystal. Suddenly, we have left the drinks aisle for a warehouse tour. And where is it? Inside young cockroaches’ guts. It is hard to think of a more surprising place.

Researchers studied a kind of cockroach whose young grow inside the mother. They took crystals from part of the young cockroaches’ intestines. Then they used X-rays to study the crystals’ 3D shape.

They found substances related to fats attached to the proteins. Some parts also had chains of sugar. It is called a “protein crystal,” but it is not a simple box with only protein inside.

The paper also gives a number that makes you read it twice. For the same weight, these crystals are thought to contain over three times as much energy as milk from several kinds of mammals.

“So, could this be the next super drink?”

Wait. We cannot put that on a menu yet. This study did not look at how the crystals affect people who eat them. It also did not test if they are safe to eat. The energy number alone cannot show that they would be amazing to drink. That is like opening a restaurant just because a warehouse tour got you excited.

The point is not, “They are amazing to drink.” It is, “Who knew nutrients could be stored this way?” A crystal full of nutrients appears just when you imagine a liquid. That surprise alone is plenty to feed our curiosity.

The authors suggest that storing nutrients as crystals may help feed young before they are born. But that role has not been confirmed yet. We can see what the warehouse looks like. But we still have more to learn about what it does.

When we look at food, we tend to wonder what is in it. But the way living things store food can also be fascinating.

Cockroach milk. Even if you have no plans to order it, doesn’t its storage trick make you want to lean in a little?

## In one line
Researchers found nutrient-rich crystals in baby cockroaches’ guts, thought to hold over three times the energy of the same weight of milk from several kinds of mammals. The study shows a surprising way to store nutrients, but did not test the crystals’ effects or safety as food for people.
