# Follow the Moon’s “Wrinkles”! A “Coat” Idea Appears

Almost nobody looks up at the Moon and thinks, “It’s wearing warm clothes again tonight.” But a study of the Moon’s surface wrinkles suggests it may have a kind of “coat.” This coat may make it harder for heat inside the Moon to escape. A study of wrinkles has somehow become a clothing check. What is going on?

First, let’s look at the wrinkles themselves. They are cliffs with one steep side and one gentle side. People think they formed when the ground was pushed together. Then rock on one side rose up over rock on the other. With clothes, you might reach for an iron. But here, we are talking about rock. These wrinkles are on a very different scale.

The team used pictures from a probe’s camera to map the cliffs. They looked at how the cliffs were spread out. From this, they estimated that the Moon’s radius had shrunk by about 42 to 46 meters. They did not wrap a huge tape measure around the Moon. Instead, they read changes in the ground to work out how much its size had changed.

But don’t quickly decide, “The Moon has shrunk that much since it was born!” This number estimates how much it shrank during a later part of its history. A fairly young fault recorded it. Also, we still do not know when that fault began to form. The wrinkles’ age is a mystery, too.

Meanwhile, some models calculate how the Moon cools. They say the Moon’s total shrinkage may be greater than this estimate. You may ask, “Were the calculations wrong?” But wait before deciding. The periods being compared are different. The ways they were studied are different. Putting the numbers side by side does not settle the question.

One clue may help explain the difference: a thick layer of broken rock. It matters that the rock is broken, but that is not all. There are also gaps in the layer. If this layer worked like insulation, it may have kept heat from escaping. This may have slowed the Moon’s cooling and shrinking.

Rough rocks might act like warm clothes. It is hard to tell just by looking. We think about extra clothes on a cold day or insulation in a house. Then the Moon’s far-off heat story feels much closer. Of course, the Moon is not really wearing clothes. Still, it has not been shown that the “coat” is the cause. It is only one possible explanation. The wrinkles may show how much the Moon has shrunk. They may also show how quickly it has been giving off heat. Next time you look at the Moon, imagine the “coat” hidden under its patterns. You may see the Moon a little differently.

## In one line
Maps of the wrinkles suggest the Moon’s radius shrank about 42 to 46 meters later in its history. The wrinkles may show how fast it lost heat; a thick layer of broken rock with gaps may have slowed its cooling like insulation.
