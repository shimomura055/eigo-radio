# We Asked About the Moon’s “Wrinkles”—But Got an Unexpected Silence?

The Moon looks cool and calm in the night sky. But its surface has “wrinkles,” and they are more than patterns. They may show that the Moon has shrunk.

“So if we look at all the wrinkles, can we find out how much it shrank?”

Even if you question the Moon like a famous detective, it will not share its secrets so easily.

The main wrinkles in this case are land shapes like cliffs. One side is steep, and the other is gentle. They may have formed when blocks of rock pushed together. One block then moved up over the other. They are huge marks from a pushing game on the Moon’s surface.

These land shapes suggest the Moon’s radius shrank by about 42 to 46 meters.

“Case closed! That’s how much the Moon has shrunk since it was born!”

But it is too soon to close the report with that answer.

This number shows how much the Moon shrank during a later time in its history. It comes from faults that formed relatively recently. We still do not know for sure when these faults first began to form.

So this is not the Moon’s “account book” for its whole life. It is a book that starts partway through. It cannot tell us what happened before its first page.

But some models show how the Moon cools. They predict that it may have shrunk more in total than this estimate.

“Wait, the numbers from the models and the evidence don’t match!”

Detective, let’s calm down for a moment here, too. The models and the wrinkles cover different periods. So we cannot compare their amounts directly, like account books for different times.

Also, the Moon may have had a way to “shrink without showing it right away.”

The research team points to strong rock that can spring back. This may have made faults near the surface less likely to form. The faults may not have appeared until enough pushing force had built up.

If so, the wrinkles do not show every bit of shrinking as it happens. They are more like a video that started recording only when the conditions were right. That video cannot show what happened before it began.

But this is only one possible answer. We have not decided that the rock caused this. We still do not know why the model’s prediction is different from the estimate based on the faults.

The Moon’s wrinkles are important witnesses. But they do not tell us everything.

Next time you look up at the Moon, try asking:

“How much have you shrunk? And before that, when did you start keeping records?”
