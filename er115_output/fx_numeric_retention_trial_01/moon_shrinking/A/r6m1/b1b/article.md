# We Questioned the Moon’s “Wrinkles”—But Got an Unexpected Silence?

The Moon looks cool and calm in the night sky. But its surface has “wrinkles.” And these are not just patterns. They may be evidence that the Moon has shrunk.

“So if we examine all the wrinkles, we can find out how much it has shrunk, right?”

Even if you press the Moon like a famous detective, it will not give up its secrets so easily.

The key wrinkles in this investigation are landforms like cliffs, steep on one side and gentle on the other. They are thought to have formed when blocks of rock pushed against each other, with one riding up over the other. They are the huge marks left by a game of pushing against one another on the Moon’s surface.

Based on these landforms, the Moon’s radius is estimated to have shrunk by about 42 to 46 meters.

“Case closed! That’s how much the Moon has shrunk since it was born!”

But it is too soon to close the report with that conclusion.

This number shows how much the Moon shrank during a later period in its history, as recorded by relatively recent faults. And it is still not certain when those faults first began to form.

In other words, what we found is not the Moon’s “lifetime account book.” It is a book that starts partway through. It alone cannot tell us what happened before the first page.

On the other hand, some models that calculate how the Moon cools predict that its total shrinkage may be greater than this estimate.

“Wait, the calculations and the evidence don’t match!”

Detective, let’s take a moment to calm down here, too. The periods covered by the models and recorded by the wrinkles are not the same. You cannot check the amounts directly by comparing account books that cover different periods.

Also, there may have been a reason the Moon could “shrink without showing it right away.”

The research team points to strong, elastic rock. It may have made faults near the surface less likely to form, so the faults may not have appeared until a certain amount of pushing force had built up.

If so, the wrinkles are not a live report of every bit of shrinking. They are more like a video that only started recording once the conditions were right. That does not mean it shows what happened before recording began.

However, this is only one possibility. It has not been decided that the nature of the rock was the cause, and we still do not know why the model’s prediction differs from the estimate based on the faults.

The Moon’s wrinkles are important witnesses. But they do not tell us everything.

The next time you look up at the Moon, try asking:

“How much have you shrunk? And before that, when did you start keeping records?”

## In one line
The Moon’s surface faults suggest its radius shrank about 42 to 46 meters during a later period, not across its whole history. Cooling models cover a different timespan, so their larger predictions cannot be directly compared with this estimate, and the gap remains unexplained.
