# The Moon’s Wrinkle Case File: There Is Evidence It Shrunk, but the Record Is Not Complete

The Moon has “wrinkles,” too. This is not about beauty. People think these wrinkles show that the Moon itself got smaller. If so, studying them should tell us how much it has shrunk so far. We might say, “Case solved!” But the Moon does not give up its secrets so easily.

At the scene, a cliff remains. One side is steep, and the other slopes gently. People think a pushing force moved the rock along a fault. One side rose over the other. It is like a message cut into the Moon’s surface: “I was pushed.”

Later in its history, the Moon’s radius may have shrunk. The amount was about 42 to 46 meters. It is a big story about space. Still, the numbers seem oddly familiar. But we should not assume the Moon shrank only this much in total.

This record comes from a fairly recent fault. It does not tell the whole story, from the Moon’s birth to today. We do not even know when the fault first started to form. There is a pile of evidence. But we cannot tell when this record begins.

Meanwhile, some models show how the Moon cooled and shrank. They suggest its total shrinkage was greater than this estimate. “Did the calculations get it wrong?” No. Do not decide too quickly. Models and landforms cover different times. They are also studied in different ways. They are not report cards for the same period. So the numbers alone cannot solve the case.

One possible story is a thick layer of loose rock near the Moon’s surface. It was full of gaps. The research team thinks it may have worked like a warm cover. It may have slowed the cooling and shrinking deep inside the Moon. The Moon may have been surprisingly good at “keeping warm.”

The other possibility is strong rock that could bend and spring back. Faults may have been hard to form near the surface. They may have formed only after enough pushing force built up. Changes could still happen inside. But wrinkles may not have appeared on the surface right away.

Did the Moon cool slowly? Or was its surface hard to wrinkle? Both are still possible. The case is not settled.

The Moon’s wrinkles are not a complete album of its whole shrinking story. We read the pages that remain. We look for the time that is missing. This uncertainty is what makes the case interesting. Tonight, the Moon is not simply quiet. It may still be in the middle of giving its statement.
