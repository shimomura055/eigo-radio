# The Moon’s Wrinkle Case File: There Is Evidence It Shrunk, but the Record Is Incomplete

The Moon has “wrinkles,” too. And this is not about beauty. They are thought to be traces of the Moon itself shrinking. If so, studying the wrinkles should tell us how much it has shrunk so far. We might want to say, “Case solved!” But the Moon does not give up its secrets so easily.

What remains at the scene is a cliff that is steep on one side and slopes gently on the other. It is thought to be a mark left when pushing forces shifted the rock, with one side riding up over the other. In a way, it is a statement carved into the Moon’s surface: “I was pushed.”

The estimated shrinkage in the Moon’s radius during its later history is about 42 to 46 meters. It is a grand story about space, yet the numbers feel oddly familiar. But we must not rule that the Moon’s total shrinkage was only this much.

This is a record left by a fairly recent fault, not a full account from the Moon’s birth to the present. It is not even clear when that fault first began to form. There is a pile of evidence. But we cannot tell when the record begins.

Meanwhile, some models that calculate how the Moon cooled and shrank suggest that its total shrinkage would be greater than this estimate. “Did the calculations get it wrong?” No, don’t jump to conclusions. The models and the landforms cover different periods and use different ways of studying them. Since they are not report cards for the same period, the numbers alone cannot settle the case.

Two possible stories come to mind. One is a thick layer of loose rock, full of gaps, near the Moon’s surface. The research team suggests that it may have acted like insulation and slowed the cooling and shrinking of the interior. The Moon may have been surprisingly good at “keeping warm.”

The other possibility is strong, elastic rock. Faults may not have formed easily near the surface until enough pushing force had built up. In other words, even when changes happened inside, wrinkles may not have appeared on the surface right away.

Was the Moon slow to cool? Or was it hard to wrinkle? Both are still possibilities, and the case is not settled.

The Moon’s wrinkles are not a complete album of its entire shrinking history. We read the pages that remain and look for the time that is missing. That very uncertainty is what makes this case interesting. Tonight, the Moon is not simply quiet. It may still be in the middle of giving its statement.

## In one line
Faults on the Moon suggest its radius shrank by about 42 to 46 meters in its later history. But they record only part of the story, so the Moon’s total shrinkage remains unknown.
