# Questioning the Moon’s Wrinkles! The Past a Shrinking Moon Won’t Tell

The Moon has wrinkles, too. So let’s ask: “Mr. Moon, you’ve shrunk quite a bit, haven’t you?” But this witness will not reveal the whole story so easily. This study is a space detective story, following clues carved into the Moon’s face.

First, let’s examine the scene. The wrinkles are cliffs with one steep side and one gentle side. They are thought to be marks left when pieces of bedrock pushed against each other and one rode up over the other. The research team mapped these cliffs from camera images and worked backward to estimate how much the Moon had shrunk.

The figure that emerged was about 42 to 46 meters of shrinkage in the Moon’s radius. The Moon’s wrinkles are surprisingly specific witnesses.

But it is too soon to run the headline “The Moon’s Shrinkage Fully Solved!” These numbers show shrinkage in a later period of the Moon’s history, recorded by relatively young faults. They are not the total amount from the Moon’s birth to today. The diary we seized did not contain a complete record going back to the very beginning.

The difference from the calculations made by models makes the investigation even more complicated. This estimate is lower than what a model of the Moon cooling and shrinking predicts. But the model shows the total shrinkage built up over time. The numbers were not measured over the same period or in the same way. So we cannot say, “The model is our confirmed suspect!”

What might explain the difference? One possibility raised by the research team is a thick layer of loose rock, with gaps in it, covering the Moon’s surface. It may have acted as insulation, making it harder for heat inside the Moon to escape. Could the Moon have been wearing a thick coat, after all? Its “coat” of loose rock may have slowed the rate at which it cooled and shrank.

Another possibility is that the Moon’s surface held back its report. Strong, springy bedrock may have stopped faults from forming near the surface until enough force had built up. In other words, shrinking did not always mean wrinkles appeared right away. “There was a change, but it did not show on the surface.”

Did the Moon cool in a different way? Or was something happening before its shrinkage turned into wrinkles? The reason for the difference is still unknown.

The Moon’s wrinkles are important witnesses, but they cannot speak for every era. We should value their testimony, but also pay attention to their silence. To investigate the Moon’s face, it seems we need not only a great detective’s flash of insight, but also careful checks of the evidence.

## In one line
Young lunar faults suggest the Moon’s radius shrank by about 42 to 46 meters during a later period, not over its entire history. The wrinkles reveal only part of the story, and scientists still do not know why the estimate differs from models.
