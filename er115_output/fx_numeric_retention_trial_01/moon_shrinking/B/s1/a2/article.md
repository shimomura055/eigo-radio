# Moon, Are You Slow to Cool Because You Wear So Many Layers?

The Moon has wrinkles, too. When you hear that, you may ask, “Moon, are you tired too?” But researchers studied those wrinkles and found a surprising question about keeping warm. Moon, what are you wearing underneath?

First, what are these wrinkles? They are shapes like cliffs. One side is steep, while the other is gentle. Researchers think pressure pushed one block of deep rock up over another. That movement showed on the surface. In other words, they are marks from a huge fight between rock beds.

Using these shapes, researchers estimated the Moon’s shrinking. Its radius shrank about 42 to 46 meters during a later time in its history. This is not all the shrinking since it formed. Some studies say the shapes formed about 300 to 400 million years ago. But we still do not know exactly when.

Now another number comes in. Some models calculate how the Moon cools. They predict that its radius shrank by at least about 750 meters in all.

“About 45 meters and 750 meters? Are those numbers much too far apart?”

You may want to use a calculator, but do not answer too quickly. The models show the Moon’s total shrinking over time. The wrinkles show shrinking during a later time. They are like records from different times. The numbers cover different times, and researchers found them in different ways. So we cannot simply compare them and say, “The calculation was wrong!”

With this difference in mind, the team suggested one possible answer to the gap. It may be a layer of broken rock around the Moon. This thick layer has many gaps. It may have worked like a thick coat. It could have slowed the Moon’s inside from cooling and shrinking.

Rock, but doing the job of keeping things warm. It looks rough and hard, but it works more like a blanket. In this idea, the word “rock” matters less than two things. How thick is the layer? How many gaps does it have? We thought we were following the Moon’s shapes. Somehow, now we are talking about how to keep a house warm.

But it is too soon to say, “The Moon stopped shrinking by dressing warmly!” No one has proved that the rock layer caused this. We still do not know why the difference appeared. The idea that it kept heat in is only one possible answer. In a mystery, even when a promising character appears, we have not found the answer yet.

Still, there is more to enjoy when we look at the Moon. First, we can ask, “How much did it shrink?” Then we can ask, “How hard was it for the Moon to cool?” Tonight, the Moon hanging in the sky may look like someone who will not give up a blanket.
