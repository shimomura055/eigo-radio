# Moon, Are You Slow to Cool Because You’re Wearing So Many Layers?

The Moon has wrinkles, too. When you hear that, you may want to ask, “Are you tired, too, Moon?” But when researchers looked into those wrinkles, what they found was an unexpected question about keeping warm. Moon, what are you wearing underneath?

First, what are the wrinkles? They are cliff-like landforms, steep on one side and gentle on the other. They are thought to have formed when pressure pushed one slab of bedrock up over another, with that movement showing on the surface. In other words, they are the marks left by a huge “rock-bed pushing match.”

From these landforms, researchers estimated that the Moon’s radius shrank by about 42 to 46 meters during a later period in its history. This is not the total amount it has shrunk since it formed. Some studies say the landforms formed about 300 to 400 million years ago, but the timing is still uncertain.

Now another number enters the picture. Some models that calculate how the Moon cools predict that its total shrinkage adds up to at least about 750 meters in radius.

“About 45 meters and 750 meters? Aren’t those numbers way too far apart?”

You may want to grab a calculator, but don’t rush to an answer. The models show the total shrinkage over time. The wrinkles, on the other hand, record shrinkage during a later period. They are like records covering different periods. Since the numbers were not measured over the same period or in the same way, we cannot simply compare them and say, “The calculation was wrong!”

With that difference in mind, the research team suggested a layer of broken rock around the Moon as one possible way to explain the gap between the two numbers. The thick layer, with many gaps in it, may have worked like insulation and slowed the cooling and shrinking of the Moon’s interior.

Rock, yet doing the job of keeping things warm. It looks rough and solid, but its job is more like a blanket. What matters in this idea is not so much the word “rock” as how thick the layer is and how many gaps it has. We thought we were following the Moon’s landforms, but somehow this has turned into a discussion about insulating a house.

But it is too soon to conclude, “The Moon stopped itself from shrinking by dressing warmly!” The rock layer has not been proven to be the cause, and we still do not know why the difference arose. The insulation idea is only one possible explanation. In a mystery, even when a promising character appears, we have not reached the solution yet.

Still, there is more to enjoy when looking at the Moon. After asking, “How much did it shrink?” we can ask, “How hard was it for the Moon to cool?” Tonight, that Moon hanging in the sky may look a little like someone who does not want to give up their blanket.
