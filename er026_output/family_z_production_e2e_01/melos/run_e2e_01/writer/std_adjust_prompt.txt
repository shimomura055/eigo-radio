# Before Sunset

The guards went still when Melos spoke.

“You kill people without a fair trial,” he told the ruler. “A man only has to frighten you, and you order his death.”

Dust covered the shepherd’s sandals. He stood alone before the ruler, trying not to show his fear.

The ruler’s eyes narrowed. “Then you shall die as well.”

Melos’s stomach tightened.

“Give me three days,” he said. “My sister is getting married. Let me attend her wedding. Then I will return.”

“To your own execution?” The ruler laughed.

“My friend will stay in my place.”

His friend stepped forward without a word. The ruler studied them both, as though searching for a trick.

“Before sunset on the third day,” he said at last. “If you are late, your friend dies.”

His friend took Melos’s hand. His fingers were warm and steady.

“Go. I’ll wait.”

At the wedding, Melos smiled whenever his sister smiled. He watched her join hands with her husband, but every shadow crossing the floor drew his eyes toward the door. Time was passing.

Before dawn on the third day, he set out for the city.

At the river, he stopped.

The bridge was gone.

Brown water rushed through the empty crossing, carrying broken branches. Rain had turned the river into a flood. Melos searched along the banks, but there was no other way across.

He stepped into the water.

The current swept his feet away. Muddy water filled his mouth. He fought toward the far bank, reached for a passing branch, and caught it. Holding tight, he dragged himself ashore.

He rose, shaking, and ran.

Beyond the river, attackers blocked the road. One grabbed his arm. Another swung a stick. Melos struck out, broke free, and stumbled past them. Their angry shouts followed him, but he did not look back.

By afternoon, pain filled every step.

Then his legs gave way.

He lay with his face against the earth. The city was still far away. He could not reach it. He could not even stand.

He could stay here.

No one would find him before sunset. His friend would die, but he would live.

Then he remembered those warm fingers around his hand.
