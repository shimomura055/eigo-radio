【B1 Article】
Melos called across the hall, “You kill men from fear. You never give them a chance to speak.”

Silence came, and guards grabbed the young shepherd. The ruler ordered his death.

“Give me three days to see my sister’s wedding,” Melos said. “Then I will return to you.”

The ruler smiled and said, “Your friend must stay here. Return late, and he will die in your place.”

Melos turned to his friend, who offered his hands for the chains. Melos had no time to speak.

“Go now, because I know you will come back.”

At the wedding, his sister’s laugh rose above everyone’s voices. Melos listened closely to remember its exact sound.

He left for the city before daylight on day three.

The river stopped him; its bridge was gone. Rain made the water brown and dangerously fast. He stepped back, ran, and jumped into the flood.

The water pulled him under, but he fought toward the light. He grabbed a branch and pulled himself onto the far bank.

Later, three men blocked his way, and a knife flashed. He hit the man holding it with his walking stick. He ran uphill as their shouts followed behind him.

By afternoon, each step hurt, and his feet were bloody. He fell beside a rock and tried to stand. But his legs would not lift him.

He could wait here, and someone might find him. Or he could return home.

But his friend would lose his life instead.

For one awful moment, that felt easier than standing up.

He remembered the hands held out for the chains. His friend trusted him without asking for another promise.

Water came slowly through a crack in the rock. Melos drank, got onto his knees, and stood up.

He managed one step, then took another.

At the city gates, the bell rang for his friend’s death.

He raced to the square, where the sword was already raised above his friend.

“Stop now! I’m back!”

His friend turned around, and Melos fell against him.

“I almost left you to die,” he said softly.

“I almost lost my trust in you,” his friend replied.

They held each other while the last sun lit the walls.

The ruler kept his eyes on them.

Finally, he said, “Put the sword down and free both men.”

More softly, he then asked, “How can a man win such trust?”

For once, he waited for a reply instead of demanding one.
