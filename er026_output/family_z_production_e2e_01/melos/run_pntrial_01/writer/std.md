# Before Sunset

His friend offered his hands before the guards asked him.

He held them steady.

An hour before, Melos shouted at the ruler, “You’re afraid of people, so you kill them! You never let them answer you!”

The ruler ordered his death.

Melos begged for three days to attend his sister’s wedding. His friend would stay in prison until Melos returned.

“Come back before sunset on the third day, or your friend will die,” the ruler warned.

“I will come back.”

His friend gave a nod as the guards tied his wrists.

At the wedding, Melos smiled each time his sister did. He could hardly swallow his food. Before dawn, he left for the city.

He reached the river by noon.

There was no bridge now.

Brown water raced between broken bridge posts. He searched the bank but found no crossing. As the sun climbed, he walked into the river.

Water pushed him sideways and filled his mouth. He caught a branch, but it broke. Then his fingers caught a root on the other bank. Coughing, he pulled himself onto the mud.

Further on, men carrying sticks stood across the road.

“Give us your money.”

“I don’t have any!”

They still attacked, and he pushed past one man. A stick hit his shoulder, sending pain down his arm. Somehow, he got away.

By late afternoon, his legs hardly worked. He fell beside the road and lay still, watching an ant cross his finger.

He had tried, so surely he had done enough.

He could return home, hide, and stay alive.

But his friend would lose his life.

Melos shut his eyes and saw those hands again. His friend had held them out without a fight.

He heard water running nearby and dragged himself towards it. He drank and washed his face, but his legs shook when he stood up.

Still, he started running.

At the city gate, people rushed towards the square. Above their heads, Melos could see a wooden platform.

His friend was standing under the hanging rope.

“Wait!”

The crowd’s noise covered the sound of his voice.

Melos pushed through the crowd, shouting, “I’ve come back!”

A guard stopped moving his hand at the knot.

Melos fell forward against his friend’s body.

“I nearly stopped trying,” he said softly.

His friend held his injured shoulder, then quickly relaxed his grip.

“You came back, though.”

The ruler stared at them, then finally told the guards to remove the rope.

“I thought everyone would choose to save their own life.”

Both friends stayed silent.

The guards stayed behind as the ruler came down from his chair.

“Could you trust me one day?”

For the first time, the ruler sounded afraid to Melos.
