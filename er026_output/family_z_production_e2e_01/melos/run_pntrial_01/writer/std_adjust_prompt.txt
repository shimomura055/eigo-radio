# Before Sunset

His friend held out his hands before the guards even asked.

They did not shake.

An hour earlier, Melos had shouted at the ruler, “You kill people because you fear them! You never let them answer!”

The sentence was death.

But Melos had begged for three days to attend his sister’s wedding. His friend would remain a prisoner until he returned.

“Before sunset on the third day,” the ruler warned. “Or he dies instead.”

“I’ll return.”

His friend nodded as the guards tied his wrists.

At the wedding, Melos smiled whenever his sister smiled, though he could hardly swallow his food. Before dawn, he started back towards the city.

By midday, he reached the river.

The bridge was gone.

Brown water rushed between the broken posts. He searched along the bank. No crossing. With the sun climbing above him, he stepped into the river.

The current swept him sideways. Water filled his mouth. He grabbed a branch, but it snapped. Then his fingers caught a root on the opposite bank. He dragged himself onto the mud, coughing.

Farther along, men with sticks blocked the road.

“Your money.”

“I have none!”

They attacked anyway. He pushed past one man, but a stick struck his shoulder. Pain shot down his arm. Somehow, he escaped.

By late afternoon, his legs could barely move. He collapsed beside the road. An ant crawled over his finger, and he watched it without moving.

He had tried. Surely that was enough.

He could go home. Hide. Live.

His friend would die.

Melos closed his eyes. There were those hands again, held out without a struggle.

Water trickled nearby. He dragged himself towards it, drank, and washed his face. When he stood, his legs shook.

He ran anyway.

At the city gate, people were hurrying towards the square. Over their heads, he saw a wooden platform.

His friend stood beneath a hanging rope.

“Stop!”

His voice disappeared into the noise.

Melos forced his way through the crowd. “I’m here!”

A guard’s hand froze at the knot.

Melos fell against his friend.

“I almost gave up,” he whispered.

His friend gripped his injured shoulder, then quickly loosened his hold.

“But you came.”

The ruler stared at them. At last, he ordered the rope removed.

“I thought every man would save himself.”

Neither friend answered.

The ruler stepped down from his chair. His guards stayed behind.

“Could you ever trust me?”

For the first time, Melos heard fear in his voice.
