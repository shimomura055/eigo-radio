# Before Sunset

His friend held out his hands before the guards even asked.

Later, Melos would remember how steady they were.

An hour earlier, he had faced the ruler and shouted, “You kill people because you fear them! You never let them answer!”

The sentence was death.

Melos had begged for three days to attend his sister’s wedding. His friend would stay as a prisoner until he returned.

“Be here before sunset on the third day,” the ruler said, “or he dies instead.”

“I’ll return.”

His friend nodded as the guards tied his wrists.

At the wedding, Melos smiled whenever his sister smiled, but could hardly eat. Before dawn, he set off for the city.

By midday, he reached the river—and stopped.

The bridge was gone.

Brown water tore between the broken posts. He searched along the bank, but there was no crossing. He stepped into the river.

The current swept him sideways. Muddy water filled his mouth. He caught a branch; it snapped. Then his fingers closed around a root on the opposite bank. Coughing, he dragged himself onto the mud.

Farther along, men carrying sticks blocked the road.

“Your money.”

“I have none!”

They attacked anyway. Melos pushed past one, but a stick struck his shoulder. Pain shot down his arm as he escaped.

By late afternoon, his legs would barely carry him. He collapsed beside the road and watched an ant crawl over his finger.

He had tried. Surely no one could ask for more.

He could go home. Hide. Live.

His friend would die.

Melos closed his eyes. Again he saw those hands, held out without a struggle.

Water trickled nearby. He dragged himself towards it, drank and washed his face. His legs shook as he stood.

He ran anyway.

At the city gate, people were rushing towards the square. Above the crowd stood a wooden platform. Beneath a hanging rope waited his friend.

“Stop!”

No one heard.

Melos forced his way through. “I’m here!”

A guard’s hand froze at the knot.

Melos fell against his friend. “I almost gave up,” he whispered.

His friend gripped his injured shoulder, then quickly loosened his hold.

“But you came.”

The ruler stared. At last, he ordered the rope removed.

“I thought every man would save himself.”

Neither friend answered.

The ruler stepped down from his chair, leaving his guards behind.

“Could you ever trust me?”

For the first time, Melos heard fear in his voice.
