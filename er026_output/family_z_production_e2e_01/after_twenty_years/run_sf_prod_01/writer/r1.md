# The Promise

Bob stood in the doorway of a closed hardware store, watching rain shine on the New York pavement. Almost ten. He moved his unlit cigar from one corner of his mouth to the other and looked along the street again.

A patrolman stopped beside him.

“I’m waiting for a friend,” Bob said. “There was a restaurant here once. Twenty years ago tonight, we had our last dinner together.”

“They pulled it down five years ago.”

Bob struck a match and lit his cigar. For a moment, the flame lit his face, the small scar near his eyebrow, and the diamond pin in his scarf.

“My best friend, Jimmy. We grew up like brothers. I went West, but he wouldn’t leave New York. We promised to meet here at ten tonight, whatever happened to us.”

“And you haven’t seen him since?”

“No. We wrote for a while, then lost touch.” Bob smiled. “But Jimmy will come. You could always depend on him.”

The patrolman listened calmly.

“Will you wait long?”

“Half an hour. If he’s alive, he’ll be here.”

“I hope your friend turns up. Good night.”

The patrolman walked on. Bob settled back into the doorway. Every approaching footstep made him look up.

At half past ten, a tall man in a long overcoat hurried towards him.

“Is that you, Bob?”

“Jimmy!”

The man took both his hands. Bob laughed, and for a moment the twenty years between them seemed to disappear.

They walked away arm in arm. Bob talked eagerly about the West, his business, and the money he had made. There was so much to tell that he hardly knew where to begin.

Then they reached a drugstore. Its bright windows threw light across both their faces.

Bob stopped. He pulled his arm away.

“You’re not Jimmy,” he said. “Twenty years can change a man, but not the shape of his nose.”

“They can change a good man into a bad one,” the tall man said. “You’re under arrest, Bob. You’re wanted in Chicago.”

Bob’s mouth opened, but no words came.

“Before we go, there’s a note for you.”

Bob unfolded it beneath the drugstore light.

“Bob, it’s Jimmy. I was at our meeting place on time. When you struck that match, I recognized your face as the man wanted in Chicago. I came to keep our promise, but I couldn’t bring myself to arrest my old friend, so I found a plain-clothes officer to do it for me.”
