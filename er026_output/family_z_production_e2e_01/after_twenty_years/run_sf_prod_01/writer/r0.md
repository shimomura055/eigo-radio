# The Promise

Bob sheltered in the doorway of a closed hardware store. Cold rain shone on the New York pavement. It was almost ten, and he held an unlit cigar between his teeth.

A patrolman stopped beside him.

“I’m waiting for a friend,” Bob explained. “Twenty years ago tonight, we had dinner here. There used to be a restaurant.”

“They pulled it down five years ago,” the patrolman said.

“We promised to meet here again at ten, no matter where we were or how our lives had turned out.”

Bob struck a match. Its light showed a square face, a small scar near one eyebrow, and a large diamond pin in his scarf.

“Jimmy and I grew up like brothers. I went West to make my fortune. He wouldn’t leave New York.” Bob smiled. “I’ve done well. I wish he could see what I’ve made of myself.”

“Have you kept in touch?” the patrolman asked calmly.

“For a year or two. Then we lost each other. But Jimmy won’t forget. He was the truest friend a man could have.”

“Will you leave if he isn’t here at ten?”

“I’ll give him half an hour. He’ll come if he’s alive.”

“I hope your friend turns up. Good night.”

The patrolman walked on.

At half past ten, a tall man in a long overcoat hurried towards the doorway.

“Is that you, Bob?”

“Jimmy!”

The tall man caught both his hands. Bob laughed, feeling suddenly young again.

They set off arm in arm. Bob began telling him about the West, talking quickly, eager to fit twenty years into a few streets.

At the corner, a drugstore flooded the pavement with light. Bob stopped and pulled his arm away.

“You’re not Jimmy. Twenty years can’t change a man’s nose that much.”

“But they can change a good man into a bad one,” said the tall man. “You’re under arrest, Bob. Chicago wants you.”

Bob stood still, his mouth slightly open.

“Here’s a note you should read.”

Bob unfolded the paper beneath the drugstore light.

“Bob, I was at our meeting place on time. When you struck the match, I recognized you as the man wanted in Chicago. Somehow I couldn’t arrest you myself, so I went and found a plain-clothes man to do the job. —Jimmy”
