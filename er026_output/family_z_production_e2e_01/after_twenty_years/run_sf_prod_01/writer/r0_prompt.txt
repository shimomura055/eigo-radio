[Story Seed -- inspired by a real, rights-cleared source, freely reimagined;
this is NOT a summary to reproduce, just raw material]
- A man waits in the dark doorway of a closed hardware store on a cold, drizzly night, with an unlit cigar, and explains to a passing patrolman that he is keeping a promise made twenty years ago: to meet his best friend on this exact spot at ten o'clock, whatever their fortunes.
- The restaurant where they said goodbye is gone; the man tells how the two young men grew up together like brothers, how he left for the West to make his fortune while his friend refused to leave New York, and how they lost touch; he is proud, a little boastful about his success, and sure his friend will come.
- The patrolman listens calmly, asks a few friendly questions, learns the man will wait half an hour, wishes him luck and walks on along his beat; the details (a pale square-jawed face, a small scar near the eyebrow, a large diamond scarfpin) are noticed in the match light.
- About twenty minutes later a tall man in a long overcoat, collar turned up, hurries over and says 'Is that you, Bob?'; the waiting man joyfully answers 'Jimmy Wells!', and the two walk off arm in arm while the man from the West starts to tell his story.
- Under the bright light of a drugstore the man from the West stops: this tall man's nose is not Jimmy's ('not long enough to change a man's nose from a Roman to a pug'); the tall man says it sometimes changes a good man into a bad one, and that Bob has been under arrest for ten minutes as 'Silky' Bob, wanted by Chicago.
- He hands over a short note from Patrolman Wells: Jimmy was there on time, saw by the match light that it was the man wanted in Chicago, could not do it himself, and so got a plain clothes man to do the job; the story ends with the note, signed JIMMY.

[How to convert it]
Keep the New York street setting at night and the original order of events; do not modernize the setting. Write it mainly in natural third-person English at about 400 words, with the waiting man (Bob) as the main character, the patrolman on the beat and the tall man in the overcoat described by roles (the patrolman, the tall man) and Jimmy mentioned by name only as Bob's old friend. Preserve the central story, the relationship between the two friends, the cause-and-effect chain and the ending: the twenty-year promise told to the patrolman; the patrolman's calm, friendly manner and his walking on; the tall man's greeting and the warm walk arm in arm; the discovery under the drugstore light that this is not Jimmy; the arrest of the wanted man; and the note from Patrolman Wells, which is the original's closing reveal. Keep the quiet suspense and the bittersweet twist: Jimmy kept the promise and also did his duty. Do not explain the trick, hint that the patrolman is Jimmy, or comment on the twist before the note, and do not add a moral or an explanation of your own after the note. The note itself is not such an explanation: it is the closing line of the story and must be kept; the surprise is the point. Compress by trimming the street description, the boasting and the small talk, not by cutting the foreshadowing in the patrolman's conversation with Bob.
Level and length override: write natural upper-intermediate (B1-B2) English, about 400 words (roughly 330-470); ignore any earlier A2 / word-count instruction.
