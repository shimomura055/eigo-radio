# The Eye in the Dark

You fancy me mad. I am only nervous—very nervous. The illness has sharpened my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen, and judge how calmly I can tell you what happened.

I loved the old man. He had never hurt me. I wanted none of his money. It was his eye: pale blue, with a cloudy film over it. A vulture eye. When it looked at me, my blood went cold. I had to be free of it.

For seven nights, at midnight, I lifted his latch. Slowly, slowly, I opened the door. I put my covered lantern inside and let a single thin ray fall across his eye. Always closed. You see, I could not act. It was the eye I hated, not the old man. Each morning, I asked kindly how he had slept.

On the eighth night, I almost laughed at my own care. Then my thumb slipped against the lantern’s fastening.

“Who’s there?” the old man cried.

I did not move. For a whole hour, we stayed like that. He sat in bed, listening. I stood at the door, listening to him. At last he gave a small groan. I knew that sound. It was terror.

I opened the lantern a little. The thin ray fell on the eye.

Wide open.

Then I heard a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart. It grew faster. Louder. Surely a neighbour would hear it!

I rushed in. The old man shrieked once. I dragged him down and pulled the heavy bed over him. The beating continued, then stopped. I checked. No pulse.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and replaced them without leaving a trace.

At four, someone knocked.

Three officers. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I smiled. The cry was mine, I said. A dream. The old man was away in the country. I showed them every room. Then I brought chairs into his bedroom and placed mine directly above the hidden body.

They sat chatting. Quite satisfied.

But my head began to ache. There was a ringing in my ears. No—not ringing. A low, quick beating.

I spoke louder. It grew louder still.

You understand, I had to cover it. I paced. I scraped my chair across the boards. The officers kept chatting and smiling.

Louder. Louder.

They heard it. They knew. Those smiles were meant to mock me. I could not bear them.

“I did it!” I screamed. “Tear up the planks! Here! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
