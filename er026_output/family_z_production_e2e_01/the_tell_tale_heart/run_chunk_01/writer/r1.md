# The Eye in the Dark

You think I am mad. No—merely nervous. My illness has sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Would a madman speak as calmly as I do? Listen, and judge for yourself.

I loved the old man who lived with me. He had never harmed me, and I wanted none of his money. Only his eye troubled me: pale blue, covered with a cloudy film. A vulture eye. Whenever it turned towards me, my blood went cold. I had to escape it.

For seven nights, at midnight, I lifted his latch. You should have seen my care! I opened the door so slowly that he never stirred. Then I uncovered just enough of my lantern to send a thin ray towards his eye.

Always closed.

What could I do? It was the eye I hated, not him. Each morning, I kindly asked how he had slept.

On the eighth night, I almost laughed at how cleverly I worked. Then my thumb slipped against the lantern’s fastening.

“Who’s there?” he cried.

I stood perfectly still. For a whole hour, he sat in bed, listening. Then came a quiet groan—not pain, but terror. I knew the difference.

Carefully, I uncovered the lantern. The ray found his eye.

Wide open.

A low, quick beating reached me, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart! It beat faster, louder. Surely a neighbour would hear it!

I rushed forward. He shrieked once before I dragged him down and pulled the heavy bed over him. The beating stopped at last. I checked his pulse. Nothing.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and fitted them back exactly. You would have found no trace.

At four in the morning, someone knocked. Three police officers stood outside. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I welcomed them. The cry was mine, I explained—a bad dream. The old man was away in the country. Smiling, I showed them every room.

Finally, I brought chairs into his bedroom. I placed mine directly above the body. You see how little I had to fear?

They sat chatting, quite satisfied.

Then my head began to ache. A ringing filled my ears. No—not ringing. That low, quick beating again.

I spoke louder. The sound grew.

I paced and scraped my chair across the boards. Still they chatted and smiled.

Louder. Louder.

You understand? They heard it! They knew everything! Those pleasant smiles were mocking me. I could not bear another moment.

“I did it!” I screamed. “Tear up the planks! Here! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
