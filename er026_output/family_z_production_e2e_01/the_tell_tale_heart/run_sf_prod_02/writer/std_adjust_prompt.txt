# The Eye in the Dark

You call me mad. No. Nervous, perhaps, but perfectly sane. My illness sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen to my calm voice, and judge for yourself.

I loved the old man who shared my home. He had never hurt me; I wanted none of his money. But his eye! Pale blue, covered by a cloudy film—a vulture’s eye. Whenever it turned towards me, my blood went cold. I had to be free of it.

For seven nights, at midnight, I lifted his latch. You should have seen my patience! I eased the door open so slowly he never stirred, then let one thin ray escape my lantern.

The eye was always closed.

So I could do nothing. You understand: I hated the eye, not the man. Each morning I kindly asked how he had slept.

On the eighth night, I nearly laughed at my cleverness. Then my thumb slipped against the lantern’s fastening.

“Who’s there?” he cried.

I stood motionless. For an hour he sat in bed, listening. At last he groaned softly. Not pain—terror. I knew that sound.

I uncovered the lantern. Its narrow beam found the eye.

Wide open.

A low, quick beating reached me, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart! Faster it went, and louder. What if a neighbour heard?

I rushed forward. He shrieked once. I dragged him down and pulled the heavy bed over him. Finally, the beating stopped. I checked his pulse. Nothing.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and fitted them back exactly. Not a mark! You could have searched all night.

At four in the morning, three police officers knocked. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I welcomed them. A bad dream, I explained; the cry was mine. The old man was away in the country. I showed them every room, then brought chairs into his bedroom. I sat directly above the body. You see how little I feared?

They chatted pleasantly. I smiled.

Then my head began to ache. A ringing filled my ears—but no. That low, quick beating again.

I spoke louder. It grew louder too.

I paced and scraped my chair across the boards. Still they chatted. Still they smiled.

Louder. Louder!

Could you have borne it? They heard! They knew! Their smiles were mocking me. I could bear no more.

“I did it!” I screamed. “Tear up the planks! Here! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
