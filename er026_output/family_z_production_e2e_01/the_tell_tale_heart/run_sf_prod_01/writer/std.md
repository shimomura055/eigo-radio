# Listen Carefully

I was only nervous, not mad, and I knew it. My illness made all my senses stronger, especially my hearing. I could hear everything in heaven and on earth. Judge how calmly I tell you this whole story.

I loved the old man who shared my home. He never hurt me, and I wanted none of his money. But his pale blue eye had a thin covering, like a vulture’s. It froze my blood. I would kill him and close that eye forever.

For seven nights, I slowly opened his door at midnight. I put my head inside, carrying a covered lantern. I let a thin line of light reach his eye.

The eye was closed every time.

I hated the eye, not him, and could not act. Every morning, I spoke to him warmly.

On night eight, my thumb slipped on the lantern’s metal catch.

“Who is in here?” the old man called out.

I stood still for an hour while he sat listening. A small cry of fear came from his bed.

At last, I let a thin line of lantern light fall across his eye.

This time, his eye was wide open.

Then I heard a low, fast beat, like a watch inside cotton. His heart was beating faster and louder. What if a neighbour heard it?

I shouted and jumped forward, and he screamed once. I dragged him onto the floor and pulled the heavy bed over him. His heart kept beating for a little longer.

At last, there was no sound.

I hid his body under three wooden floorboards. I put them back carefully, leaving no mark.

At four in the morning, three police officers knocked because a neighbour had heard a scream.

I smiled and welcomed them inside. I explained that my scream came from a bad dream. The old man, I said, was away in the country.

After showing them the house, I brought chairs into his bedroom. I sat right above the body, calm and sure of myself. The officers sat down and chatted.

My ears began ringing, so I spoke more loudly. Now the sound was clear: a low, fast beat, like a watch inside cotton.

I got up, walked around, and dragged my chair across the floor. Yet the beating only grew louder.

The officers smiled and talked as if they heard nothing.

But they knew, and their friendly smiles were laughing at me! How could they sit there? I could not bear another second.

“Don’t pretend anymore!” I screamed. “I killed him! Pull up the floorboards here, under my feet! It is his horrible heart beating!”
