# Listen Carefully

You fancy me mad. But I am only nervous—very, very nervous. The illness has sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen, and judge how calmly I can tell you the whole story.

I loved the old man. He had never hurt me. I wanted none of his gold. It was his eye: a pale blue vulture eye, covered with a thin film. When it looked at me, my blood went cold. I decided to kill him and close that eye forever.

For seven nights, at midnight, I lifted his latch and slowly opened his door. I put my covered lantern inside, then my head. One thin ray of light fell on the eye.

Always closed.

So I could do nothing. You understand? It was not the old man I hated. Every morning I greeted him kindly and asked how he had slept.

On the eighth night, I almost laughed at my own care. Then my thumb slipped on the lantern’s metal fastening.

“Who’s there?” cried the old man.

I stood still. For a whole hour, neither of us moved. He sat in bed, listening. At last he gave a little groan. I knew that sound. It was terror.

I opened the lantern a little. A thread of light fell on the eye.

Wide open.

Then I heard a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart. It grew faster. Louder. Surely a neighbour would hear!

I rushed in with a yell. He shrieked once. I pulled him to the floor and dragged the heavy bed over him. The beating continued beneath it. Then it stopped.

He was dead.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and replaced them, leaving no trace.

At four, someone knocked.

Three officers stood outside. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I smiled. The shriek was mine, I explained—a bad dream. The old man was away in the country. I showed them every room. Then I brought chairs into his bedroom and sat directly above the hiding place.

They chatted pleasantly. I answered with ease.

Then a ringing troubled my ears.

I spoke louder. The sound grew clearer: low and quick, like a watch wrapped in cotton.

I stood. I paced. I scraped my chair across the boards.

Still louder.

The officers kept chatting. Smiling.

Could you have borne those smiles? They heard it. They knew. They were making a game of my terror.

“Stop pretending!” I screamed. “I did it! Tear up the planks! Here, under my feet! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
