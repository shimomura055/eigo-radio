# The Gift of the Magi

One dollar and eighty-seven cents. Della counted it three times, but the little pile of coins refused to grow. Tomorrow was Christmas. How could she buy Jim a present with so little?

She sank onto their shabby couch and cried. She had saved every penny she could, yet she still could not afford anything worthy of her husband.

There were two treasures in their small home: Jim’s gold watch, passed down from his grandfather to his father and then to him, and Della’s long brown hair.

Della rose and stood before the mirror. She loosened her hair, letting it fall below her knees. For a moment she touched its shining waves. Then she reached for her coat.

Before her courage failed, she hurried downstairs and along the cold street to a hair-goods shop.

“Will you buy my hair?” she asked.

The owner lifted it and examined it.

“Twenty dollars.”

“Please, cut it quickly.”

For the next two hours, Della searched the shops. At last she found a simple platinum watch chain. It was beautiful without trying to attract attention—just like Jim. The price was twenty-one dollars.

No more worn leather strap for his wonderful watch! She could almost see his smile.

At home, she curled what remained of her hair. The mirror showed a face that looked strangely young.

“Please let him still think I’m pretty,” she whispered.

At seven, dinner was nearly ready. Footsteps sounded on the stairs.

Jim opened the door and stopped. His eyes rested on her short hair, and Della’s heart began to beat faster.

“Jim, darling, I sold it. I couldn’t let Christmas come without a present for you. It will grow again!”

He stepped forward and held her close. Then he placed a package in her hands.

Della tore away the paper. Inside lay the tortoise-shell combs she had admired so often in a shop window. She held them against her chest, smiling through tears.

“My hair grows quickly, Jim.”

Then she remembered her own gift and held out the chain.

“Isn’t it perfect? Give me your watch!”

Jim sat down and smiled gently.

“I sold the watch to buy your combs.”

Della sat beside him and took his hand. The combs had no long hair to hold; the chain had no watch to carry. Neither had been able to afford a gift, so each had given up their greatest treasure.

The magi were the wise men who brought gifts on the first Christmas. Yet these two foolish young people, whose gifts they could not use, were the wisest of all gift-givers. They were the magi.
