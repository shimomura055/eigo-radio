# The Gift of the Magi

One dollar and eighty-seven cents. Della counted it three times, pushing the pennies across the table. Tomorrow was Christmas, and that was all she had for Jim’s present.

She dropped onto the shabby couch and cried. Outside their little New York flat, footsteps hurried through the cold.

There were two things she and Jim were proud of. One was Jim’s gold watch, which had belonged to his father and grandfather. The other was Della’s shining brown hair.

Della stood before the mirror and let her hair fall. It flowed below her knees, soft and bright. She watched it for a moment. Then, before she could change her mind, she pinned it up, pulled on her old coat and hat, and hurried downstairs.

At a hair-goods shop, she stopped, breathing hard.

“Will you buy my hair?”

The owner lifted the heavy brown waves.

“Twenty dollars.”

“Give me the money quickly,” Della said.

For two hours she searched the shops. At last she found it: a simple platinum watch chain. Nothing showy—just quiet beauty, like Jim. It cost twenty-one dollars.

Now he could throw away the old leather strap he used for his wonderful watch.

Back home, Della curled her short hair. The face in the mirror looked younger, almost like a schoolboy’s.

“Please,” she whispered, “let him still think I’m pretty.”

At seven, with dinner nearly ready, she heard Jim on the stairs.

He opened the door and stopped. His eyes stayed on her hair. Della could not understand his expression.

“Jim, darling, don’t look at me like that. I sold my hair. I couldn’t let Christmas come without giving you something.”

He stepped forward and held her. Then he took a package from his coat pocket.

Della tore off the paper.

Inside were the tortoise-shell combs she had so often admired in a shop window. She pressed them to her chest, laughing through sudden tears.

“My hair grows quickly, Jim.”

Then she remembered.

“Look what I bought you!”

She held out the platinum chain.

Jim sat down and smiled gently.

“I sold the watch to buy your combs.”

Della looked from the chain to the combs. Then she sat beside him and took his hand.

The magi were the wise men who brought gifts to the Babe in the manger and invented the art of giving Christmas presents. These two foolish young people had given up their greatest treasures for each other. Yet, of all who give gifts, they were the wisest. They are the magi.
