# The Woman in White

One winter night in Japan, a snowstorm drove two woodcutters into a hut beside a river. Minokichi was eighteen; the old man was his master. They barred the door and lay beneath their straw coats. Outside, snow struck the walls. Soon the old man was asleep.

Minokichi woke with snow blowing across his face. The door stood open. In the pale light, a woman dressed in white bent over his master. Her breath flowed over the old man like bright white smoke.

Then she turned towards Minokichi. He tried to move, but his body would not obey. Her beautiful face came close to his.

“I meant to kill you too,” she whispered. “But you are young, so I will spare you. If you ever tell anyone what you have seen tonight, even your own mother, I will know. Then I will kill you. Remember.”

The woman went out into the snow. Minokichi barred the door and touched his master’s face. It was icy cold. The old woodcutter was dead.

After that night, Minokichi told nobody what had happened. He kept silent even about his master’s death.

The next winter, on the road home, Minokichi met a beautiful girl called Yuki. She had white skin and a gentle voice. He took her home, where his widowed mother welcomed her and persuaded her to stay.

Minokichi and Yuki married and had ten fair-skinned children. The years passed, but Yuki seemed never to grow old.

Years later, one evening at home, Minokichi sat watching Yuki sew by the lamp. Their children were asleep. The light fell softly across her face.

“There was a woman who looked very like you,” Minokichi said.

“Where did you see her?” Yuki asked.

He told her the whole story: the night in the hut, the white breath that had killed his master, and the woman who had spared him and warned him to keep silent.

Yuki dropped her sewing. She rose and bent over him.

“It was me!” she cried. “I told you I would kill you if you ever said one word about it! Only those sleeping children keep me from killing you now. Take very good care of them. If they ever have reason to complain of you, I will treat you as you deserve!”

Her voice grew thin, like the crying of the wind. She melted into white mist, rose towards the roof beams, and slipped away through the smoke-hole.

She was never seen again.
