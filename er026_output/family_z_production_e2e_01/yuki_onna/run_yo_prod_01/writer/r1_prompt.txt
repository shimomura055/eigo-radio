# The Woman in White

One stormy winter night, two woodcutters sheltered in a riverside hut in Japan.

Minokichi was eighteen; the old woodcutter was his master. They barred the door and lay under their straw coats. The old woodcutter soon slept.

Minokichi woke with snow in his face. The door stood open. A woman in white bent over the old woodcutter. Her breath flowed over him like bright white smoke.

Then she leaned towards Minokichi. He could not move. Her beautiful face came close to his.

“I meant to kill you too, but I will spare you because you are young,” whispered the woman in white. “If you ever tell anybody, even your own mother, what you have seen tonight, I will know it, and I will kill you. Remember!”

The woman in white went out. Minokichi barred the door, then touched his master’s icy face. The old woodcutter was dead.

Minokichi told nobody about the woman in white.

The next winter, Minokichi met a girl called Yuki on the road home.

She was beautiful, with white skin and a gentle voice. He brought her home. Minokichi’s widowed mother welcomed Yuki and persuaded her to stay. Minokichi and Yuki married. They had ten fair-skinned children, but Yuki seemed never to grow old.

Years later, Minokichi watched Yuki sewing by the lamp at home.

The children were asleep. The paper lamp cast a soft light on Yuki’s face.

“You remind me of someone,” said Minokichi. “I saw a woman once who looked very like you.”

“Where did you see her?” asked Yuki.

Minokichi told Yuki everything: the night in the hut, the white breath that killed his master, and the beautiful woman who had spared him with a warning.

Yuki dropped her sewing. She stood and bent over him.

“It was me!” cried Yuki. “I told you then that I would kill you if you ever said one word about it! Only those sleeping children keep me from killing you now. Take very good care of them. If they ever have reason to complain of you, I will treat you as you deserve!”

Her voice grew thin, like the crying of the wind. She melted into white mist, rose towards the roof beams, and slipped away through the smoke-hole.

She was never seen again.
