# The Thicket

“I found him this morning, in a thicket off the mountain road near Kyoto,” said the woodcutter. “One stab in the chest. The blood was dry. No, I found no sword or bow—only rope beneath a cedar, and a comb. The bamboo was broken, the earth trampled. There had been a struggle.”

“I passed them yesterday, about noon,” said the monk. “He carried a sword and bow. His wife rode beside him, her face behind a veil. I hardly looked at them. Now I wish I had.”

“I caught Tajomaru by the stone bridge,” said the constable. “Yes, the famous bandit. He’d fallen from a horse, but he still had the dead man’s bow. I know his kind. Find the woman. She’ll tell you what he did.”

“Takehiro was my daughter’s husband, a gentle samurai,” said the old woman. “Masago is proud, but she’s only nineteen. I beg you, find her. Please. Don’t send me home without news.”

“Yes, I killed him,” said Tajomaru. “But don’t call me a coward. A breeze lifted her veil, and I wanted her. I promised him buried treasure, led him into the thicket and tied him to the cedar. She fought me with her dagger, but I took her by force.

“Then she caught my arm. One of us must die, she said; she would go with the survivor. So I freed him. We fought fairly, sword against sword. Twenty-three strokes! No other man had lasted twenty. When he fell, she was gone. I took his weapons and left.”

“After the bandit left, I crawled towards my husband,” said Masago at the temple. “He was still tied up. I begged him to die with me, but he looked at me with cold contempt. My own husband, looking at me like that!

“I held my dagger. His lips moved: ‘Kill me.’ I stabbed him. I couldn’t bear those eyes.

“Afterwards, I tried to drown myself. I couldn’t even do that. Where can I go now?”

“I heard the bandit comforting her,” said Takehiro’s spirit through the medium. “Then my wife said, ‘Take me anywhere.’ She pointed at me. ‘Kill that man.’

“He pushed her away and asked me whether she should live or die. For that, I almost forgave him. But she fled. He cut one rope and left.

“I rose from the cedar and picked up the small dagger she had dropped. I stabbed myself in the chest.

“The thicket fell silent. Then someone crept towards me on soft feet. An unseen hand quietly drew the dagger from my chest.

“I sank for ever into the darkness.”
