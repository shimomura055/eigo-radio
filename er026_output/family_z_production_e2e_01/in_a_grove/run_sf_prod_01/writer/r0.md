# The Thicket

“I found him this morning, off the mountain road near Kyoto,” said the woodcutter. “One stab in the chest. The blood was dry. No sword or bow, only rope beneath a cedar and a comb. I saw the broken bamboo and trampled earth. There had been a struggle.”

“I passed them yesterday, about noon,” said the monk. “He carried a sword and bow. His wife rode beside him, her face behind a veil. I hardly noticed them. Now I wish I had looked more closely.”

“I caught Tajomaru beside the stone bridge,” said the constable. “He had fallen from a horse, but he still had the dead man’s bow. I know his kind. Find the woman; she will tell you what he did.”

“Takehiro was my daughter’s husband, a gentle samurai,” said the old woman. “Masago is proud, but she is only nineteen. I beg you, find her. Please don’t make me go home without news.”

“Yes, I killed him,” said Tajomaru. “A breeze lifted her veil, and I wanted her. I promised him buried treasure, led him into the thicket and tied him to the cedar. She fought me with her dagger, but I took her by force. Then she caught my arm. One of us must die, she said; she would go with the survivor. So I freed him. We fought fairly, sword against sword. Twenty-three strokes! No other man had lasted twenty. When he fell, she was gone. I took his weapons and left.”

“After the bandit left, I crawled towards my husband,” said Masago at the temple. “He was still tied up. He looked at me with such cold contempt. I begged him to die with me, but his eyes never changed. I held my dagger. His lips moved: ‘Kill me.’ I stabbed him because I could not bear that look. Afterwards, I tried to drown myself. I could not even do that. Where can I go now?”

“I heard the bandit comforting her,” said Takehiro’s spirit through the medium. “Then my wife said, ‘Take me anywhere.’ She pointed at me. ‘Kill that man.’ The bandit pushed her away and asked me whether she should live or die. For that, I almost forgave him. But she fled. He cut one rope and left.

“I rose from the cedar and picked up the small dagger she had dropped. I stabbed myself in the chest. The thicket fell silent. Then someone crept towards me on soft feet. An unseen hand quietly drew the dagger from my chest. I sank for ever into the darkness.”
