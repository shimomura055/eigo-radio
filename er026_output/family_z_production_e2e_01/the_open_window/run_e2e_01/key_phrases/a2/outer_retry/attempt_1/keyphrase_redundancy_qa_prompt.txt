以下は、同じ記事から選ばれた3件のKey Phrase(英語学習ポッドキャストの
リスニング教材)です。3件から作れる全てのペア(3組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず3組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

【記事本文(参考、文脈確認用)】
Framton Nuttel felt nervous, even in this quiet sitting room. He kept turning his letter of introduction between his fingers. His doctor wanted him resting quietly in the country, not visiting strangers.

“My aunt will come downstairs in a minute,” said the fifteen-year-old sitting opposite him. “How well do you know her?”

“I don’t really know anything about her.”

The girl’s eyes turned towards the French window, open onto the lawn.

“Doesn’t that open window seem strange at this time in October?”

“Well, the weather’s still quite warm,” Framton answered.

“Her husband and two young brothers went shooting three years ago today. They went outside through that very window.”

For a moment she stopped speaking, and Framton waited.

“They never came back from the marsh, where the ground seemed firm. All three sank into deep mud with their little brown spaniel. Nobody ever found their bodies.”

Framton’s fingers stopped turning the letter.

“She expects them back and leaves it open until dark. Her husband wore a white waterproof coat over his shoulders. Her youngest brother sang, ‘Oh, brother, why are you jumping?’ He always sang that to annoy her.”

The girl swallowed hard.

“When it starts getting dark, I sometimes almost feel they really will—”

Then someone opened the door.

His host rushed in, saying how sorry she was.

“I hope you’ve enjoyed talking to my niece. Is the open window all right with you? My husband and brothers will soon return from shooting. They’ll get my carpets so dirty!”

Framton tried to find a safe subject and explained his doctor’s orders. He needed complete rest, without any excitement. She listened, but kept looking past him towards the lawn.

Suddenly, she looked happy.

“They’re finally back!”

The girl watched the window with her mouth slightly open. Framton turned, feeling cold inside.

Three men with guns were crossing the lawn as darkness came. One wore a white coat over his shoulders, and a tired brown spaniel followed them.

A young man’s voice called in the evening light.

“Oh, brother, why are you jumping?”

Framton took his hat and stick and ran through the hall. He rushed out of the gate, forcing a passing cyclist into the hedge.

The men entered through the open window.

“Who’s the man who just ran away?” the husband asked as he cleaned his muddy boots.

“Such a strange visitor!” she replied. “He looked like he’d seen a ghost.”

“The spaniel frightened him,” the girl said calmly. “He told me he was terribly afraid of dogs. Once, a pack chased him into a cemetery. He spent the night in an empty grave, with the dogs looking down at him.”

She looked over at the sleepy dog.

“He told me they took turns.”
