# The Open Window

Framton Nuttel sat forward, holding a letter with wet fingers. He had come to the countryside to calm his nerves. But this quiet sitting room did not help him relax.

“My aunt will come down shortly,” said fifteen-year-old Vera. “Do you know much about her?”

“I know almost nothing about her.”

Vera looked over at the large, open window.

“Three years ago today, her husband and two young brothers went shooting. Their brown spaniel followed them through that window. After a very wet summer, they sank into deep mud on the open land. No one ever found their bodies.”

Framton’s fingers became still on the letter.

“She still believes they’ll come home. Every evening, the window stays open until dark. Her husband carried a white coat over his arm. Her youngest brother sang, ‘Bertie, why do you bound?’ He often sang that song to annoy her.”

Her voice shook as she spoke again. “Sometimes, as it gets dark, I almost think—”

She stopped speaking, and only the clock’s loud ticking continued.

Her aunt came quickly into the room.

“I hope Vera has been talking to you! Is the open window all right? My husband and brothers will soon come back from shooting. Their boots will carry half the countryside into this room!”

Framton felt cold as he heard her happy voice. She was still waiting for them! He explained his doctor’s advice: rest and no excitement. She hardly listened, and kept looking across the grass.

Suddenly, she smiled and said, “Look, they’ve finally come home!”

Vera looked out, with terrible fear on her face.

Framton looked round. Three men with guns crossed the grass in the half-light. One carried a white coat over his arm. Their tired brown spaniel walked behind them.

A young man called out, “Bertie, why do you bound?”

Framton struggled to breathe as those dead men walked towards him.

He grabbed his hat and almost fell leaving the room. Seconds later, he was running down the drive.

The men came through the window, looking normal but muddy.

“Why did that man run off?” the aunt’s husband asked.

“A strange man came to visit. He spoke about his nerves, then rushed away. He looked as if he’d seen a ghost.”

“The spaniel frightened him,” Vera replied in a calm voice. “He told me he’s terribly afraid of dogs. In India, some dogs chased him into a graveyard. He hid all night in an empty grave. The dogs stood above him, making low, angry noises.”

Vera was very good at inventing believable stories in seconds.
