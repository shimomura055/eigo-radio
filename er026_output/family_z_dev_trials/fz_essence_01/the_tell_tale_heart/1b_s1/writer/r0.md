# The Quiet House

You fancy me mad. But I am only nervous—very, very nervous. My illness has sharpened my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen, and judge how calmly I can tell you this.

I loved the old man. He had never hurt me. I wanted none of his money. It was his eye: pale blue, with a cloudy film over it. A vulture eye. Whenever it looked at me, my blood went cold. I had to get rid of it.

For seven nights, I went to his room at midnight. I turned the latch slowly and opened the door. Then I let one thin ray from my lantern fall across his face. Always, the eye was closed. I could do nothing. You understand: it was the eye I hated, not the old man.

On the eighth night, I smiled at my own care. Then my thumb slipped against the lantern fastening.

The old man sat up.

“Who’s there?”

I stood still for a whole hour. He did not lie down. In the darkness, I heard a small groan of terror. I knew that sound. I had made it myself, alone at night.

At last, I opened the lantern a little. The thin ray fell straight upon the eye.

Wide open.

Then came a low, quick sound, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart. It beat faster. Louder. Surely a neighbour would hear it!

I threw open the lantern and rushed in. The old man shrieked once. I dragged him down and pulled the heavy bed over him. At last, the beating stopped. I checked. He was dead.

I hid the body beneath three floorboards and replaced them without leaving a trace.

At four, someone knocked at the street door.

Three officers stood outside. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I welcomed them. The cry was mine, I explained—a bad dream. The old man was away in the country. I showed them every room.

Then I brought chairs into his bedroom. I placed my own directly above the hidden body. You see how little I feared?

The officers sat chatting.

Soon my ears began to ring. I spoke louder. But the sound grew clearer: low, quick, steady.

I rose and paced. I scraped my chair across the boards. Louder. Louder still.

The officers kept talking and smiling.

They heard it. They knew! Why else would they smile? They were mocking me. I could not bear another moment.

“I did it!” I screamed. “Tear up the planks! Here! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
