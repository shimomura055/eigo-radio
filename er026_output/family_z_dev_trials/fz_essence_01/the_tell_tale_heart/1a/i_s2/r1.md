# Listen Carefully

He was not mad, he insisted, only nervous. Illness had sharpened his senses; he heard everything in heaven and earth.

“Listen carefully,” he said. “Judge how calmly I can tell the story.”

He loved the old man who shared his home. The old man had never hurt him, and he wanted none of his gold. Only the eye mattered: pale blue, covered with a thin film. A vulture’s eye. He had to close it forever.

For seven nights, at midnight, he eased open the bedroom door and slipped his head and covered lantern inside. A narrow beam searched for the eye.

Always closed.

So he could not act. Each morning, he kindly asked how the old man had slept.

On the eighth night, his thumb slipped on the lantern’s metal fastening.

“Who’s there?”

He froze. For an hour, the old man sat in bed, listening. Neither moved.

He heard a soft groan and recognised its meaning. Terror.

Slowly, he opened the lantern. The ray fell on the eye.

Wide open.

Then came a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. The old man’s heart. Faster. Louder. Soon a neighbour would hear!

With a yell, he rushed forward. The old man shrieked once. He pulled him onto the floor and dragged the heavy bed over him. Beneath it, the beating continued, then stopped.

Dead.

He hid the body beneath three floorboards and carefully replaced them. No trace remained.

At four in the morning, three police officers knocked. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

He welcomed them with a smile. The cry was his, he explained—a bad dream. The old man was away in the country. Calmly, he showed them every room.

In the bedroom, he brought out chairs and sat directly above the hidden body. The officers sat chatting pleasantly. He answered easily. Surely this proved how calm and sane he was.

Then a ringing troubled his ears.

He spoke louder, but the sound became clearer: low and quick, like a watch wrapped in cotton.

His fingers tightened around his chair. The beating grew louder. He raised his voice again.

The officers kept chatting, still smiling. They seemed to hear nothing.

But they knew. They must know! Those smiles were a game, a cruel joke at his expense. He could bear it no longer.

“Stop pretending!” he screamed. “I did it! Tear up the planks! Here, beneath my feet! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
