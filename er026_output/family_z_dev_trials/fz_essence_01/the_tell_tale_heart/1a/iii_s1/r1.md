# Listen Carefully

You think I am mad. I am nervous, not mad. Would a madman speak so calmly? My illness sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen carefully, and judge for yourself.

I loved the old man I lived with. He had never harmed me, and I wanted none of his gold. Only his eye troubled me: pale blue, covered with a thin film. A vulture’s eye. Whenever it turned towards me, my blood grew cold. That eye had to close forever.

For seven nights, exactly at midnight, I lifted his latch and eased his door open. Slowly, you understand. I slipped my covered lantern inside, then my head, and let one thin ray fall on his eye.

Always closed.

How could I act? The eye, not the man, was my enemy. Each morning I greeted him warmly and asked about his sleep. Such care! Such patience!

On the eighth night, I almost laughed at my own cleverness. My thumb slipped on the lantern’s metal fastening.

“Who’s there?” he cried.

I did not move. For an hour, he sat in bed, listening. Then came a small groan of terror. I knew that sound well.

I opened the lantern a little. Light fell across the eye.

Wide open.

Now I heard a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart! Faster it went, and louder. Surely a neighbour would hear!

I rushed in with a yell. He shrieked once. I dragged him to the floor and pulled the heavy bed over him. The beating continued, then stopped.

Dead. And the eye would trouble me no more.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and replaced them perfectly. You would have found nothing.

At four in the morning, three police officers knocked. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I welcomed them with a smile. A bad dream, I explained; the shriek was mine. The old man was away in the country. I showed them every room, then brought chairs into his bedroom. I sat directly above the hidden body.

They chatted pleasantly. I answered calmly. Why should I fear?

Then a ringing troubled my ears.

I spoke louder. The sound became clear: low and quick, like a watch wrapped in cotton.

I stood, paced, scraped my chair against the boards.

Louder. Louder!

The officers kept chatting and smiling. How could you bear such smiles? They heard it! They knew, and they were laughing at my terror!

“Stop pretending!” I screamed. “I did it! Tear up the planks! Here, beneath my feet! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
