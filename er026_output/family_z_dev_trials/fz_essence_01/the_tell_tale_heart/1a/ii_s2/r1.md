# Listen Carefully

You think I am mad. But I am only nervous. My illness has sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen carefully, and judge how calmly I can tell you the story.

I loved the old man who shared my home. He had never harmed me, and I wanted none of his money. Only his eye troubled me: pale blue, with a thin film over it. A vulture’s eye. Whenever it turned towards me, my blood went cold. That eye had to close forever.

For seven nights, at midnight, I opened his bedroom door, inch by inch. I slipped my covered lantern inside and let one narrow ray fall on his eye.

Always closed.

How could I act? I hated the eye, not the man. Each morning, I smiled and asked how he had slept.

On the eighth night, my thumb slipped on the lantern’s metal fastening.

“Who’s there?” he cried.

I froze. For an hour, I stood in the darkness while he sat up in bed, listening. Then he gave a small groan—not pain, but terror. I knew that sound.

I opened the lantern a little. The thin light struck his eye.

Wide open.

Then came a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart! It beat faster, louder. I could scarcely breathe. What if a neighbour heard?

With a yell, I rushed in. He shrieked once. I dragged him to the floor and pulled the heavy bed over him. Beneath it, his heart kept beating.

Then silence.

He was dead.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and carefully replaced them. Not a mark remained.

At four in the morning, someone knocked.

Three police officers stood outside. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I welcomed them with a smile. A bad dream, I explained; the cry had been mine. The old man was away in the country. I showed them through the house.

In his bedroom, I brought out chairs and sat directly above the hidden body. They chatted pleasantly. I answered easily. Was this the behaviour of a madman?

Then a ringing troubled my ears.

I talked louder, but the sound grew clearer: low and quick, like a watch wrapped in cotton.

I rose and paced. I scraped my chair across the boards.

Louder. Louder!

Still the officers smiled and talked, as though they heard nothing.

But they knew! Those smiles were a game. They were enjoying my terror.

I could bear it no longer.

“Stop pretending!” I screamed. “I killed him! Tear up the planks! Here, beneath my feet! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
