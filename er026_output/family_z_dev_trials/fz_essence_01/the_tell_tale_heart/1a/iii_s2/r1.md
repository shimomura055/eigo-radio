# Listen Carefully

You think I am mad. But listen carefully. My illness has sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and on earth. Judge for yourself how calmly I can tell you what happened. Would a madman speak like this?

I loved the old man who shared my house. He had never harmed me; I wanted none of his money. Only his eye troubled me: pale blue, covered with a thin film. A vulture’s eye. Whenever it turned towards me, my blood went cold. That eye had to close forever.

For seven nights, exactly at midnight, I lifted his latch and opened the door. Slowly, you understand—so slowly! I slipped my covered lantern inside, then my head, and let one narrow ray fall on his eye.

Always closed.

So I could not act. It was the eye I hated, not the man. Each morning I greeted him warmly. You see how careful I was?

On the eighth night, I almost laughed at my own skill. My thumb slipped against the lantern’s metal fastening.

“Who’s there?” he cried.

I did not move. For an hour, he sat in bed, listening. Then came a soft groan of terror. I knew that sound well.

I opened the lantern a little. The light fell straight upon the eye.

Wide open.

Then I heard a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart! Faster, louder—surely a neighbour would hear!

I rushed in with a yell. He shrieked once. I dragged him to the floor and pulled the heavy bed over him. The beating continued, then stopped.

Dead.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and replaced them perfectly. You would have found nothing.

At four in the morning, three police officers arrived. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I smiled. A bad dream, I explained. The old man was away in the country. I showed them through the house, brought chairs into his bedroom, and sat directly above the body.

They chatted pleasantly. I answered easily. You can imagine my confidence.

Then a ringing troubled my ears.

I spoke louder. It became clearer: low and quick, like a watch wrapped in cotton.

I stood and paced. I scraped my chair across the boards.

Louder. Louder!

Still they chatted and smiled.

Could you have borne those smiles? They heard it! They knew! They were enjoying my terror!

“Stop pretending!” I screamed. “I did it! Tear up the planks! Here, beneath my feet! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
