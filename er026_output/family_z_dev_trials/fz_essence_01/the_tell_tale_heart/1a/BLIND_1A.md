## コード A

# Listen Carefully

You think I am mad. No—only nervous, terribly nervous. My illness has sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen carefully, and judge how calmly a sane man can tell you his story.

I loved the old man who lived with me. He had never harmed me, and I wanted none of his gold. But his eye—that pale blue vulture eye, covered by a thin film—made my blood turn cold. I had to close it forever.

For seven nights, at midnight, I eased open his door and slipped my covered lantern inside. Slowly, slowly, I let one narrow ray fall on his eye.

Always closed.

So I could not act. It was the eye I hated, not him. Each morning, I smiled and asked how he had slept.

On the eighth night, my thumb slipped against the lantern’s metal fastening.

“Who’s there?” he cried.

I froze. For an hour he sat in bed, listening, while I stood in the darkness. Then he gave a soft groan. I knew that sound: pure terror.

I opened the lantern a little. A thin ray caught the eye.

Wide open.

A low, quick beating reached me, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart! It grew faster, louder. A neighbour would hear it!

With a yell, I rushed in. He shrieked once. I dragged him to the floor and pulled the heavy bed over him. The beating continued, then stopped.

I waited. He was dead.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and replaced them carefully. Not a mark remained.

At four in the morning, someone knocked. Three police officers stood outside; a neighbour had heard a shriek.

I welcomed them with a smile. A bad dream, I explained. The shriek was mine. The old man was away in the country.

I showed them every room, then brought chairs into his bedroom. I placed mine directly above the hidden body and sat down, perfectly calm.

They chatted pleasantly. I answered easily.

Then a ringing troubled my ears.

I spoke louder, but it became clearer: a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton.

I rose and paced. I scraped my chair across the boards.

Louder. Louder!

Still they talked and smiled, as if they heard nothing.

Nothing? No! They heard it. They knew everything. Those smiles—they were laughing at my terror! How could anyone bear it?

“Stop pretending!” I screamed. “I killed him! Tear up the planks! Here, beneath my feet! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
