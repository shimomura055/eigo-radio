# Listen to Me

Listen to how calmly I speak before you call me mad. My illness made my senses stronger, especially my hearing. I hear every sound in heaven and on earth.

I loved the old man who lived with me. He never hurt me, and I never wanted his gold. But his pale blue eye had a thin cover. That vulture eye made my blood cold. You see, only that eye had to go.

For seven nights, I carefully opened his door at midnight. My lantern’s thin light always found his eye closed. I could do nothing, but I greeted him warmly every morning. You can see how clever I was, can’t you?

On night eight, I smiled at my clever work. Then my thumb slipped on the lantern’s metal lock.

“Who is there?” the old man called out loudly.

For a whole hour, I stayed still as he sat up listening. Then he made a low, frightened sound I knew well.

I opened the lantern a little, shining its light onto his wide-open eye.

I felt angry and heard a soft, fast beating. It sounded like a watch wrapped in cotton: his heart. It grew louder, and I feared a neighbour would hear.

I jumped forward, and he gave one sharp cry. I pulled him down and covered him with the heavy bed. The beating stopped; I checked carefully and found no heartbeat.

I hid his body under three floorboards and put them back. There wasn’t one mark left for anyone to see. You would have been proud of my work.

At four that morning, three police officers came because a neighbour heard a cry.

I smiled and told them my bad dream caused the cry. I said the old man was away in the country. Then I showed them every room; I had nothing to hide.

I brought chairs into his bedroom, placing mine directly above the body. Could a mad person feel so sure of himself? The officers seemed satisfied and sat talking.

My head started hurting, and my ears began ringing. But no, I was hearing that soft, fast beating again.

I spoke louder, walking around and dragging my chair. The beating grew louder, but they just smiled and talked.

Of course they heard, and they knew what I had done! Behind those smiles, they were laughing at me. The beating kept growing louder until I could stand no more.

“Don’t pretend anymore!” I shouted. “I killed him! Pull up the floorboards, here below my chair! That is his terrible heart beating!”
