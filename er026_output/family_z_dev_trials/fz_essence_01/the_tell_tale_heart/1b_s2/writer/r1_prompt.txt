# Listen to Me

I am nervous, yes. But I am not mad. My illness sharpened my senses, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Listen, and judge how calmly I can tell you this.

I loved the old man. He never hurt me. I wanted none of his gold. It was his eye: pale blue, covered with a film. A vulture eye. Whenever it turned towards me, my blood grew cold. I had to destroy it.

For seven nights, at midnight, I gently turned his latch. I opened the door and let one thin ray from my lantern fall on the eye. Always closed. So I could do nothing. Each morning I greeted him kindly. You fancy me mad? But consider my care.

On the eighth night, I smiled at my own skill. Then my thumb slipped on the lantern’s tin fastening.

“Who’s there?” the old man cried, sitting up.

I stood still. For a whole hour he sat listening in the dark. Then I heard his low groan of terror. I knew that sound.

At last I opened the lantern a little. A thin ray fell on the eye. Wide open. The sight filled me with fury.

Then I heard a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart. It grew louder. Surely a neighbour would hear!

I rushed in. He shrieked once. I dragged him down and pulled the heavy bed over him. The beating stopped at last. I checked. No movement. No pulse.

I hid his body beneath three floorboards and replaced them without leaving a trace.

At four in the morning, someone knocked at the door.

Three officers. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I welcomed them. The cry was mine, I explained. A bad dream. The old man was away in the country.

I showed them every room. Then I brought chairs into his bedroom and sat directly above the hiding place. You see how little I feared? The officers chatted pleasantly. They were satisfied.

But my head began to ache. There was a ringing in my ears. It grew clearer. No—not ringing. A low, quick beating.

I talked louder. The sound grew louder still. I paced and scraped my chair across the boards. The officers went on chatting, smiling. How could they sit there?

They heard it. They knew. Those smiles were meant for me.

I could not bear another moment.

“Stop pretending!” I screamed. “I did it! Tear up the planks! Here, here! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
