# Listen to Me

Listen to me before you judge. I am not mad. My illness made my senses sharper, especially my hearing. I hear everything in heaven and earth. Notice how calmly I explain it all.

I loved the old man who shared my house. He had never hurt me, and I had no wish for his gold. But his pale blue eye, covered with a thin film, chilled my blood. A vulture’s eye! You understand: only that eye had to go.

For seven nights, at midnight, I opened his door with wonderful care. A narrow ray from my lantern touched his eye. Always closed! So I could do nothing. Each morning, I greeted him warmly. Was that not clever?

On the eighth night, I smiled at my own skill. Then my thumb slipped on the lantern’s tin fastening.

“Who’s there?” he cried.

I stood perfectly still. For an hour, he sat up, listening. Then he gave a low groan of terror. I knew that sound.

I opened the lantern a little. The light struck his eye—wide open.

Anger filled me. Then I heard a low, quick beating, like a watch wrapped in cotton. His heart! Louder, louder. Suppose a neighbour heard it?

I rushed forward. He shrieked once. I dragged him down and pulled the heavy bed over him. Soon the beating stopped. I checked carefully: no movement, no pulse.

Then I hid his body beneath three floorboards and fitted them back. Not a mark remained. You would have admired my work.

At four in the morning, three police officers knocked. A neighbour had heard a shriek.

I smiled and welcomed them. The cry was mine, I explained: a bad dream. The old man was away in the country. I showed them every room. Nothing to hide, you see.

Finally, I brought chairs into his bedroom and placed mine directly above the body. How could a madman be so confident? The officers sat chatting, quite satisfied.

But my head began to ache. There was a ringing in my ears. No—a low, quick beating.

I spoke louder. The sound rose. I paced and scraped my chair across the floor. Still they smiled and chatted. Could they hear nothing?

Of course they heard! They knew everything. Their smiles mocked me. The beating grew louder, louder. I could not bear it.

“Stop pretending!” I screamed. “I did it! Tear up the planks! Here, under my chair! It is the beating of his hideous heart!”
