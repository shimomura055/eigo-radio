[Story Seed -- inspired by a real, rights-cleared source, freely reimagined;
this is NOT a summary to reproduce, just raw material]
- A self-possessed fifteen-year-old girl keeps a nervous visitor company while her aunt is late, and the visitor, who has come only to deliver a letter of introduction for his nerve cure, knows nothing about the household.
- The girl tells him a calm, tragic tale about the large French window left open: three years ago to the day, the aunt's husband and two young brothers walked out through it to go shooting and were lost in a bog, their bodies never found, and the aunt still waits for them every evening.
- The girl's story is built on small convincing details (the husband's white waterproof coat, the youngest brother singing 'Bertie, why do you bound?' to tease his sister, the little brown spaniel) and a sudden break in her voice, which makes the visitor believe her and makes the open window feel eerie.
- The aunt then enters, cheerful and busy, speaks of the men coming home any moment, keeps looking toward the open window, and barely listens to the visitor's long talk about his illness, so everything she does seems to confirm the girl's story.
- At twilight, three figures with guns, one with a white coat over his shoulders and a tired brown spaniel at their heels, walk silently across the lawn toward the window; then a young voice chants 'I said, Bertie, why do you bound?' and the terrified visitor flees past a cyclist on the road.
- The men come in, ordinary and muddy; the aunt says the visitor must have seen a ghost, and the girl calmly says he was afraid of the spaniel, backing it up with a vivid invented tale about his past; the story ends with the narrator's own last line ("Romance at short notice was her speciality."), which shows that the girl invents stories on the spur of the moment.
