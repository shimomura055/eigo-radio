# The Gift of the Magi

One dollar and eighty-seven cents. Della counted it again, but the coins refused to become anything more. Tomorrow was Christmas, and she wanted to give Jim something wonderful. Instead, she had enough money to make her cry.

So she did, on their worn little couch.

Their flat held two treasures: Jim’s gold watch, which had belonged to his father and grandfather, and Della’s long brown hair. Their furniture might not impress anyone, but those two things could.

Della stood before the mirror and let her hair fall below her knees. For a moment she watched it shine. Then she pinned it up, pulled on her old coat, and hurried downstairs before her courage could change its mind.

At the hair-goods shop, the owner lifted a thick handful.

“Twenty dollars.”

“Cut it quickly,” said Della.

For two hours afterward, she searched the shops. Then she found it: a plain platinum chain, strong and beautiful without trying to attract attention. Just like Jim. It cost twenty-one dollars.

His fine watch would no longer need its old leather strap.

At home, Della curled her short hair and prepared dinner. She looked in the mirror again. A schoolboy seemed to be looking back.

“Please let him still think I’m pretty,” she whispered.

At seven, Jim’s footsteps sounded on the stairs. He entered in his old overcoat and stopped. Della could not understand the look on his face.

“I sold my hair,” she said quickly. “I couldn’t let Christmas come without a present for you. It’ll grow again. You don’t mind, do you?”

Jim put his arms around her. She pressed her face against his coat.

Then he handed her a package.

Della opened it. Inside were the tortoise-shell combs she had admired so often in the shop window.

Her delighted cry turned into tears.

“My hair grows fast,” she said, holding them close.

Then she sprang up.

“But you haven’t seen your present!”

The platinum chain shone in her hand.

“Give me your watch, Jim. Let’s see how it looks.”

Jim sat down and smiled gently.

“Let’s put our presents away for a while. I sold the watch to buy your combs.”

Della sat beside him.

No hair for the combs; no watch for the chain. Each had sold their greatest treasure to buy the other a gift that could not now be used.

The magi were wise men who brought gifts to the baby in the manger. These two foolish young people had given away their treasures because they loved each other more. Of all who give gifts, they were the wisest. They were the magi.
