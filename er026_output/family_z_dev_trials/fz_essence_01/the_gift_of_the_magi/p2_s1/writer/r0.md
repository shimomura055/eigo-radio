# The Gift of the Magi

On Christmas Eve, Della emptied her purse onto the table in their small New York flat. One dollar and eighty-seven cents. She counted it twice more, pressing each penny flat with her finger. Then she dropped onto the worn couch and cried.

She and Jim owned two things worth being proud of. His gold watch had belonged to his father and grandfather. Her brown hair shone as it fell below her knees. Everything else was old or cheap, but those two treasures could make them stand a little taller.

Della stood before the mirror and let down her hair. For a moment, she held its heavy ends. Then she pinned it up, pulled on her old coat and hurried into the street.

At the hair-goods shop, the owner lifted a thick section.

“Twenty dollars.”

“Please cut it quickly,” Della said.

Two hours later, she found a plain platinum watch chain. It was strong and beautiful without trying to attract attention—just like Jim. It cost twenty-one dollars. Now he could put away the old leather strap he used for his watch.

Back home, Della curled her short hair and prepared dinner. The mirror showed her someone who looked like a schoolboy.

“Please let him still think I’m pretty,” she whispered.

At seven, his footsteps sounded on the stairs.

Jim entered in his old overcoat and stopped. He stared at her hair. Della could not understand his expression.

“I sold it,” she said quickly. “I couldn’t have Christmas without giving you something. It will grow again, Jim.”

He put his arms around her. She pressed her face against his coat.

Then he handed her a small package.

Inside were the tortoise-shell combs she had so often admired in a shop window. She gave a cry of joy, then touched her short curls. Tears filled her eyes as she held the combs against her chest.

“My hair grows fast,” she said.

Suddenly she reached for his gift.

“Look! Give me your watch. I want to see it with the chain.”

The platinum slipped through her fingers, bright in the lamplight.

Jim sat down and smiled gently.

“Della, let’s put our presents away for a while. I sold the watch to buy your combs.”

She sat beside him, still holding the chain.

The magi were the wise men who brought gifts to the Babe in the manger and invented the art of giving Christmas presents. These two foolish young people had given up their greatest treasures for each other. Yet of all who give gifts, they were the wisest. They are the magi.
