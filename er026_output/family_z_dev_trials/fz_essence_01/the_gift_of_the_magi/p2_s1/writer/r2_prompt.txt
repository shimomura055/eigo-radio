# The Gift of the Magi

On Christmas Eve, Della counted her money for the third time. One dollar and eighty-seven cents. The pennies lay neatly on the table, quite unwilling to become dollars. Tomorrow was Christmas, and that was all she had for Jim’s present.

She sat on the worn couch and cried.

Their little New York flat held two treasures: Jim’s gold watch, passed down from his grandfather and father, and Della’s brown hair, which fell below her knees. They had little else to be proud of, but those two things were enough to make them stand straighter.

At the mirror, Della let down her hair. It flowed over her shoulders like a dark waterfall. She held the ends a moment, then pinned it up and hurried out in her old coat.

The owner of the hair-goods shop lifted a thick section.

“Twenty dollars.”

“Cut it quickly,” Della said.

Two hours later, she found the chain: plain platinum, strong and quietly beautiful, just like Jim. It cost twenty-one dollars. At last, his fine watch would have something better than an old leather strap.

Back home, she curled what remained of her hair and prepared dinner. The girl in the mirror looked rather like a schoolboy.

“Please let him still think I’m pretty,” she whispered.

At seven, footsteps sounded on the stairs.

Jim came in wearing his old overcoat. He stopped at the sight of her.

Della’s heart seemed to stop with him.

“I sold my hair,” she hurried to explain. “I couldn’t let Christmas come without buying you something. It’ll grow back. You don’t mind, do you?”

He put his arms around her, and for a moment she hid her face against his coat.

Then he gave her a small package.

Inside lay the tortoise-shell combs she had so often admired in the shop window.

Her cry of delight broke into tears. She held them against her chest.

“My hair grows fast,” she said.

Then she remembered.

“Your present! Look, Jim!”

She held up the platinum chain, shining in the lamplight.

“Give me your watch. Let’s see how it looks.”

Jim sat down and smiled gently.

“Let’s put our presents away for a while. I sold the watch to buy your combs.”

Della sat beside him, the chain still in her hand.

No hair for the combs; no watch for the chain. Each had sold their greatest treasure to buy the other a gift.

The magi were wise men who brought gifts to the baby in the manger. These two foolish young people had given away what mattered most because someone else mattered more. Of all who give gifts, they were the wisest. They were the magi.
