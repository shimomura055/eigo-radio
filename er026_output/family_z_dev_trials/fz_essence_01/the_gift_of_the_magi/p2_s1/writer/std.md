# The Gift of the Magi

Della counted her money again: one dollar and eighty-seven cents. The coins refused to grow. Tomorrow was Christmas; she wanted a wonderful gift for Jim. Instead, she had only enough money to make her cry.

So she cried on their little, worn couch.

Their flat held two treasures, but neither was furniture. Jim’s gold watch had been his father’s and grandfather’s. The other was Della’s beautiful, long brown hair.

Before the mirror, Della let her hair fall past her knees. She watched it shine, then pinned it up again. She hurried downstairs in her old coat before her courage left.

At the hair-goods shop, the owner lifted a thick handful of Della’s hair.

“I’ll give you twenty dollars for it.”

“Then cut it now, please,” Della said.

She searched shops for two hours and found the right gift. It was a simple platinum chain, strong and quietly beautiful. It was like Jim, and cost twenty-one dollars.

Now his fine watch could lose its old leather strap.

Back home, she curled her short hair and made dinner. In the mirror, she looked like a schoolboy.

“Please, let him still find me pretty,” she whispered.

At seven, she heard Jim’s steps on the stairs. He entered in his old coat and stopped. She could not understand his expression.

“I sold my hair to buy you a Christmas gift. I couldn’t leave you without one, and it will grow back. Do you mind?”

Jim held her, and she pressed her face into his coat.

Then he gave her a present wrapped in paper.

Opening the package, she found the tortoise-shell combs she loved. She had often looked at them in the shop window.

Her happy cry soon changed to tears.

“My hair will grow quickly,” she said, holding them close.

Then, all at once, she jumped to her feet.

“But there’s still your present for you to see!”

The platinum chain was bright and shiny in her hand.

“Now, give me your watch, Jim, and let’s try it.”

Jim sat down, smiling gently at his wife.

“Let’s keep our presents for later,” he told her gently. “I sold my watch so I could buy your combs.”

Della came over and sat down next to her husband.

There was no hair for the combs, and no watch for the chain. Each had sold their greatest treasure for a gift neither could use.

The magi were wise men who took gifts to the baby in the manger. These two foolish young people had given up their treasures for love. Of all people who give presents, these two are the wisest. They are the true magi.
