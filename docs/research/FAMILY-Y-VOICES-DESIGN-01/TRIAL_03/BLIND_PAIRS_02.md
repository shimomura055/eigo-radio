## P1(日本語・3人の語り(Voice部分のみ))

### Text 1

俺は、学校が朝にスマホを預かって、下校の時に返すルールに賛成だ。現場から遅く帰って、高校に通ううちの娘と毎晩スマホで揉めるのはしんどい。学校でもそう決まってると言えたら、俺だけが悪者にならずに済む。居間の充電カゴに入れると決めたのに守られず、取り上げた翌朝の食卓では、娘と口をききづらかった。「私だけ返事が遅れると外される」とも言われた。俺は言い返せなかった。決めたことは守ってほしいんだ。親父みたいに怒鳴るのも嫌だ。ただ、昼間預けさせても夜の家は変わらないのに、学校に頼んで俺が楽になりたいだけな気もする。家じゃ段取りも組めない現場監督だ、俺は。

朝いちばん、保健室へ来る子の顔を、私は毎朝見ている。眠い、って。頭が痛い、って。この前も、ベッドで休む子の枕元でスマホの画面が光っていた。明け方まで起きていたと聞くと、どう声をかけようかと迷う。昼間にスマホを取り上げられた翌日から、顔を見せなくなったあの子も気になる。来ないのと、つらくないのは同じなんだろうか。学校が朝にスマホをまとめて預かって下校まで返さないと決めたら、それで済んだと思ってしまわないかな。それが、どうにも怖い。私は保健室では取り上げず、夜に何があったかを話せる場所を残したい。預かったあと、あの子の話は誰が聞くんだろう。

僕は、朝にスマホを集めて下校まで預かるのには反対だ。昼は倉庫で働いて、夜は定時制に通っているから、学校にいる間ずっと連絡を切る約束はできない。休憩中にシフト変更の連絡が来る。返事が遅いと、仕事はほかの人に回される。前に授業中、職場から着信が続いても出られず、あとで連絡がつかないのは困ると言われた。シフトも減らされかけた。僕はここで卒業したい。家に入れるお金も要る。授業が分かるようになったのに、仕事か学校かなんて、選びたくない。僕は授業中、鞄の奥にしまって触らないと約束する。でも、まとめて預かると決める前に、僕の働き方も聞いてほしい。

### Text 2

俺は、娘の高校で、休み時間や昼休みもスマホを使わせない決まりに賛成だ。授業中だけじゃ足りない。現場から遅く帰って、毎晩スマホで揉めるのはしんどい。学校でもそう決まってると言えたら、俺だけが悪者にならずに済む。居間の充電カゴに入れる約束を、娘は守らなかった。取り上げた翌朝の食卓で、娘と口をききづらかった。「私だけ返事が遅れると外される」とも言われた。俺は言い返せなかった。決めたことは守ってほしいんだ。うちの親父みたいに怒鳴るのも嫌だ。ただ、昼間の学校で使えなくしても、夜の家は変わらない。学校に頼んで俺が楽になりたいだけな気もする。家じゃ段取りも組めない現場監督だ、俺は。

朝いちばん、保健室へ来る子の顔を、私は毎朝見ている。眠い、って。頭が痛い、って。この前も、ベッドで休む子の枕元でスマホの画面が光っていた。明け方まで起きていたと聞くと、どう声をかけようかと迷う。昼間にスマホを取り上げられた翌日から、顔を見せなくなったあの子も気になる。来ないのと、つらくないのは同じなんだろうか。先生に「スマホを禁止にしたら、保健室に来る子は減る？」と聞かれて、私は答えに詰まった。学校が休み時間まで使えなくして、それで対策した気になってしまわないかな。それが、どうにも怖い。私は保健室では取り上げず、夜に何があったかを話せる場所を残したい。使えなくしたあと、あの子の話は誰が聞くんだろう。

僕は、休み時間までスマホを使えなくする決まりには反対だ。昼は倉庫で働いて、夜は定時制に通っている。だから、学校にいる間ずっと連絡を切る約束はできない。授業の合間の休み時間に、シフト変更の連絡が来る。返事が遅いと、仕事はほかの人に回される。前に授業中、職場から着信が続いても出られず、あとで連絡がつかないのは困ると言われた。シフトも減らされかけた。僕はここで卒業したい。家に入れるお金も要る。授業が分かるようになったのに、仕事か学校かなんて、選びたくない。僕は授業中、鞄の奥にしまって触らないと約束する。でも、休み時間まで使えなくすると決める前に、僕の働き方も聞いてほしい。

## P2(英語(Standard)・3人の語り(Voice部分のみ))

### Text 1

I support the rule that the school takes students’ phones each morning. It gives them back when school ends. I get home late from the worksite. It’s tiring to argue with my high school daughter about her phone every night. If I could say, “The school has the same rule,” I would not be the only bad guy. We agreed she would put her phone in the charging basket in the living room. But she did not stick to it. The morning after I took it away, it was hard to talk to her over breakfast. She also said, “If I’m the only one who replies late, I’ll be left out.” I couldn’t argue back. I want her to stick to what we decide. I don’t want to yell like my dad did. Still, even if the school keeps her phone during the day, things at home won’t change at night. Maybe I just want to ask the school to make things easier for me. I’m a site supervisor who can’t even make a plan at home.

I see the faces of children who come to the nurse’s office first thing every morning. “I’m sleepy,” they say. “My head hurts,” they say. The other day, I saw a bright phone screen beside the pillow of a child resting on a bed. When I hear they were awake until dawn, I wonder what I should say. I also worry about a child who stopped coming to the nurse’s office. This happened the day after the school took their phone away. Does not seeing them mean they are not having a hard time? If the school decides to collect all the phones in the morning and keep them until the end of the day, will we think that takes care of it? That is what scares me. I don’t take phones away in the nurse’s office. I want to keep a place where students can talk about what happened at night. After we take their phones, who will listen to that child?

I’m against collecting phones in the morning and keeping them until school ends. I work in a warehouse during the day. At night, I go to evening high school. So I can’t promise to be out of touch the whole time I’m at school. During breaks, I get messages about changes to my shift. If I reply late, the work goes to someone else. Once, calls kept coming from work during class, but I couldn’t answer. Later, they told me it was a problem that they couldn’t reach me. My shifts almost got cut, too. I want to graduate from here. I need to give money to my family, too. Now that I’ve started to understand my classes, I don’t want to choose between work and school. I promise to keep my phone deep in my bag and not touch it during class. But before deciding to collect everyone’s phones, please hear about how I work, too.

### Text 2

I am for a rule at my daughter's high school. It would keep phones off during breaks and lunch, too. Only banning them in class is not enough. I get home late from the job site. Fighting about phones every night makes me tired. If the school has the same rule, I won't be the only bad guy. My daughter did not keep our agreement. She was supposed to put her phone in the living-room charging basket. I took her phone away. The next morning, it was hard to talk to her at breakfast. She also said, “If I am the only one who replies late, I will be left out.” Her words left me speechless. A promise is a promise, and I want it kept. Shouting like my father is not what I want, either. But if she can't use her phone at school, nothing changes at home at night. Maybe I just want the school to help so my life is easier. I am a site supervisor who can't even manage things at home.

First thing every morning, I see the kids who come to the nurse's office. “I'm sleepy,” they say. “My head hurts,” they say. The other day, a student was resting on the bed. Their phone screen glowed beside the pillow. When I hear they stayed up until morning, I don't know what to say. I also think about one student. After their phone was taken away at school, they stopped coming to see me. If they don't come, does that mean they are okay? A teacher asked me, “If we ban phones, will fewer kids come to the nurse's office?” I could not answer. If the school bans phones during breaks too, will it think the problem is solved? That scares me. I don't take phones away in the nurse's office. I want to keep a place where kids can talk about what happened at night. After the phones are off-limits, who will listen to that child?

I'm against a rule that bans phones during breaks, too. I work at a warehouse during the day. At night, I go to evening high school. So I can't promise to stop all contact while I'm at school. Between classes, I get messages about my shifts. If I reply late, the work goes to someone else. Once, my workplace kept calling during class. I couldn't answer. Later, they told me it was a problem that they could not reach me. They almost cut my shifts, too. I want to graduate from this school. I also need money to give to my family. Now I can understand my classes. I don't want to choose between work and school. I promise to keep my phone at the bottom of my bag. I won't touch it during class. But before you decide, please hear how I work, too.

## P4(日本語・最後のまとめ(1パート)※初回稿のまとめは抽象語が多い版(it1)、修正稿はit2の具体的な構成を新設定に合わせた版)

### Text 1

三人とも、スマホのことで暮らしや通学が苦しくなるのを避けたい。夜の居間のカゴ、朝の保健室の枕元、休憩中のシフト連絡では、困る時も場所も違う。守りたいのは、親子の会話、つらさを話せる場所、働きながら通う日々だ。預ける決まりは、親子の助けになっても、話や連絡を途切れさせるかもしれない。父親も先生も、昼に預かるだけで夜の困りごとが消えるとは思っていない。あなたが今日、スマホを手放すと困るのは、いつ、どこにいるときだろう。

### Text 2

## P5(英語(Standard)・最後のまとめ(1パート))

### Text 1

At breakfast, beside the pillow in the nurse's office, and through work messages during breaks, people want to keep talking with someone. Longer phone-free time may mean fewer fights about the basket at night. But a student whose phone is taken away may stop talking at the bedside. He may follow the rule in class. But if his shift messages stop during breaks, his workplace may lose trust in him. The father who asks the school for help also does not want another silent morning after taking the phone away. They may disagree about how long phones can be used. But they all want someone they can still talk to. What message do you want to answer right away? When does it come, and who sends it?

### Text 2

All three want to stop phone problems from making life or school harder. The trouble comes at different times and places: the basket in the living room at night, the pillow by the bed in the nurse’s office in the morning, and shift messages during breaks. What they want to protect is talk between parent and child. They want a place to talk about what is hard. They also want to keep daily life going while they go to school and work. A rule about handing in phones may help parents and children, but it might also cut off talks and contact. Neither the father nor the teacher thinks taking phones during the day alone will make problems at night go away. When and where today would it be hard for you to give up your phone?
