# The Trial of “Leaving Things Out” Is Now in Session

A supplement sits alone in the kitchen. It is here again today. It was here yesterday, too. “Left out again!” A lawyer speaks for the “put it away” side. They quickly ask for it to be removed. But the court says, “Not so fast.” This supplement may need a lawyer to speak for it.

The charge is that it makes the kitchen look bad. But the defense says, “If I put it away, I might forget to take it.” So it does more than take up space. When someone can see it, it helps them remember. In a way, it is a “kitchen reminder.”

This comes from a study of homes in New Zealand. Researchers used photos while talking with people in 22 homes. Several people lived together in each home. One person kept supplements where they could see them, so they would not forget to take them.

For the person who wants it put away, it is “something in the way.” For the person who left it there, it is “a sign that helps me remember.” They see the same thing, but in different ways. One sees something to remove. The other sees a worker still doing its job. No wonder the kitchen meeting gets heated.

If you say, “Straight into the cupboard!” and make the person put it away, the kitchen will look neat. But you might also remove the reminder the person relied on. Storage: a success. Its help in daily life needs another look. It is not that simple.

The study gives us another way to look at clutter. It is not always a sign that someone has failed. It can also help people in daily life. A scorecard might say, “The fewer things you can see, the better.” But that could take points away from the ways each home has found to make life work.

Do you have any “things left out that are still doing a job” at home? If you ask, “Why don’t you put that away?” it can feel like you are questioning someone in court. Ask instead, “What does having it there help with?” That can turn the talk into a planning meeting about how to make daily life work.

But don’t rush to say, “Science proves that if you leave it out, you won’t forget!” The person in the study gave that reason. The study did not test whether keeping it in sight works. Many people in the study had a high level of education, too. So we cannot say these results fit every home.

So the verdict is not, “Clutter is completely innocent!” First, ask, “What job is that thing doing there?” Don’t call it wrong just because it is out. Before you bring out the storage box, hear it out. That supplement may be taking its job quite seriously.
