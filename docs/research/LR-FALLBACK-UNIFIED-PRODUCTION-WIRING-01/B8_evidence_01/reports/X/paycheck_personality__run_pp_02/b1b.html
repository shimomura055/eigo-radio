“Are people who earn more always more hardworking?” When people talk about income and personality, they often start looking for “traits that successful people share.” But this time, let’s ask the high earner next door to step offstage for a moment. The person in the spotlight is none other than you. The research team brought together and analyzed data from 11 studies that followed the same people. The participants were working people who reported their personality and income from work. Their personality and income were each surveyed at least three times. So this was not a single snapshot, but a series of photos showing changes over time. The direction of the camera matters here. The researchers separated the differences between people—“That person is more hardworking than me and also earns more”—from changes within the same person: “How did my personality change after my income changed?” They moved from ranking people by yearly income to tracking one person over time. It’s a different contest altogether.

本文 / full_story_part2

LR 発動なしFallback 発動なし

The personality trait they focused on was conscientiousness: the tendency to work seriously and steadily. Their analysis found that, within the same person, higher income was linked to higher conscientiousness later on. Wait—we thought this was about personality affecting income, but income got a part too! And the arrow doesn’t point just one way. A rise in conscientiousness was also weakly linked to higher income later on. They found a weak connection not only between “personality and later income,” but also between “income and later personality.” Hearing this, it’s tempting to ask, “So does a pay raise update your personality too?” But hold on before reaching that conclusion. This was an analysis of data that tracked changes, and it cannot prove that income changed personality or that personality increased income. Something else may have affected both.

本文 / full_story_part3

LR 発動なしFallback 発動なし

“Came first” and “was the cause” are two different things. In a drama, the person shown first at the scene is not always the criminal. The order of events can be a clue, but it does not solve the case by itself. So when you look back on your income or the way you work, you don’t have to end the story with “That’s just my personality.” Try asking, “What has changed in me lately?” and take a closer look. Rather than judging other people, look for changes in yourself. When it comes to income and personality, simply changing who you compare yourself with can show you a very different picture.
