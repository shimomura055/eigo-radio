本文 / full_story_part1

A stone. Still a stone. Surely something will happen soon—but no, it’s a stone. Nothing happens on the screen. This goes on for five minutes. But you, the viewer, can still do one thing: make someone else’s reward smaller. This suddenly sounds dark. But it comes from an online experiment with people in the United States. They were put into groups at random. One group watched a five-minute video of a stone, where nothing happened. The other group watched a magician. During the video, they had three choices. They could make another person’s reward smaller, make it bigger, or do nothing. They could change it by 10 cents each time, up to 50 cents in all. The stone on the screen stayed quiet, while the viewers were given “a little power.” So, did boredom become fuel for being mean?

本文 / full_story_part2

LR 発動なしFallback 発動なし

People who strongly liked seeing others suffer were more likely to make the other person’s reward smaller after watching the stone video than after watching the magician video. For people who felt this less strongly, there was no clear difference. You may want to say, “So, it really depends on personality.” But there is more to the story. In the next experiment, people were again put into groups at random. But this time, they could not make the reward bigger. They could only make it smaller. This time, it did not matter how strongly people liked seeing others suffer. People who watched the stone video were more likely to make another person’s reward smaller than people in the other groups. If we look only at personality, we may miss something. We need to ask not only what kind of person someone is, but also what they can do in that situation. We thought we were reading a profile of the person. Now we also notice how the stage is set.

本文 / full_story_part3

But it is too soon to say, “Add a button to make rewards bigger and bring world peace!” The two were separate experiments. We cannot say the choices alone caused the different results. We do not know if giving people a choice to make rewards bigger can stop meanness. The experiment looked only at brief boredom. It also looked only at actions that made another person’s reward smaller. We cannot use its results directly to explain everyday meanness or boredom that lasts a long time. Even so, we see boring moments a little differently now. We can ask not only, “What mustn’t I do?” but also, “What else can I do instead?” Before we look at someone’s personality, we can look at the menu in front of us. The stone may not move for five minutes. But the question we ask changes a little.
