“If you take things more seriously, will you earn more?” “Or do you start taking things more seriously after you earn more?” When you hear about studies on income and personality, you may expect to learn the “secret of people who earn a lot.” But this time, the people in the story are not rich people. They are “your past self” and “your later self.” The research brought together 11 large studies. They looked at people who had jobs and shared information about their personality and work income. The number of people was not the same in every study. The researchers used data from up to 134,515 people. Each study measured the same people’s income and personality at least three times. The important thing was how the researchers compared people. They looked separately at differences between people that usually stay the same. They also looked at changes inside each person.

Not, “What’s different between me and that person who earns a lot?” But, “How are my past self and my later self different?” In a way, the researchers stopped comparing people with others. Instead, they followed each person over time. It is like putting down binoculars you use to look at your neighbor’s greener grass. Then you turn the pages of your own records. Then came “diligence.” This means taking things seriously and working hard. When a person’s income went up, their diligence tended to rise later too. This link was small. The link also went the other way. When diligence rose, income tended to rise later. That link was small too. It was not a link in only one direction. It went both ways. But this is important, so let me say it clearly: the links were “small.” “So, earning more makes me more serious!” Wait before you jump to that conclusion.

本文 / full_story_part3

LR 発動なしFallback 発動なし

This study cannot show that higher income changed a person’s personality. It also cannot show that their personality made their income rise. Something else may have played a part in both. Even when researchers follow the same people and compare them carefully, this alone cannot show what caused what. In other words, this is not a how-to guide for a “personality that earns more.” This study can give you a reason to open again a book you were about to close. You may have thought, “This is just the kind of person I am.” Instead of ending with, “That person is the kind who earns a lot,” you can look back and ask, “How have I changed?” You may plan to flip through a book about successful people. Then you may realize you are reading the next part of your own story. That is what makes this study interesting.
