Here is the key point. At the time of that report, the tariff-caused rise had not yet passed through the whole supply chain. It had not fully reached the prices shoppers paid. When bean costs rise, the store price tag does not jump up at once and say, “Got it!” Price rises take time to appear. In other words, a price tag is not live news. It is like a letter that arrives a little late. You may open it today, but it may not be about something that happened today. It may tell you about an earlier drought, a bean shortage, or high market prices. If you look only at the latest headline, you may name the wrong sender. Next time, take a second look at a store price tag. Ask, “Why is it expensive?” Then add, “When did this happen?” Coffee may taste bitter, but we want our detective work to be clear. Before that first cup, turn the clock back a little.
