If you’re going to learn English words, start with the ones people use a lot. That’s obviously right. There’s no arguing with it. And yet, the moment a gacha appears in front of you, the good student inside you steps away. “That may be true, but what’s the rarest one?” Wait. You came here to study today, didn’t you? The “Dopa English Word Gacha” is just the thing to send your mind off track. English word cards have seven rarity levels. The more often a word is used, the lower its level; the less often it is used, the higher. The words you’d want to learn first for study end up on the dull side in the gacha. Practical value and star power, surprisingly, live apart. Normally, you might think, “Could I use this in conversation?” But now you might lean forward and think, “What? What’s more, when you draw a card rated “UR” or higher, the bonus “Jackpot” begins, and you can keep opening packs. You’re happy about one rare card, and then you get more time to open packs. Your willpower is just about to stand up and say, “All right, time to review,” when “Just one more” tugs at its sleeve. What might happen here is not that you lose interest in English words. But here’s something worth underlining in red. Just because a word is rare doesn’t mean it should be high on your study list. Rarity is simply the game’s way of sorting words by how often they are used. “I’m happy I drew it” and “I should learn it first” are two separate things. Even if it shines, it doesn’t get special treatment in your study order.
