# When Tip Screens Ask for More: The Quiet Power of the First Number

At the end of a meal, the bill is ready. A screen turns toward you and offers a few tip choices. The first suggested amount may be higher than the tip you planned.

In that moment, the screen is not only doing math. It is helping set the starting point for your decision.

A 2014 study looked at more than 13 million credit card payments in New York City taxis. Before payment, passengers saw a small menu of suggested tip rates.

The important detail was the $15 fare line. When a fare moved from just below $15 to just above it, the suggested menu changed suddenly. The researchers used the fare threshold to compare passengers who saw different menus.

Passengers who saw the higher suggested rates left more money. This shows that the first number on the screen can change the final tip.

But there is a twist.

If the suggested rate is too high, some customers do not simply choose a smaller amount. Instead, they choose zero. The study found that a high first suggestion could increase tips from some passengers, while pushing others to stop tipping by card.

This issue also appears in restaurant tipping. A 2022 policy brief says restaurant tipping in the United States rose from about 10 to 15 percent in the early 1970s to about 20 percent in the late 1990s. Some digital checkout screens may show tips as high as 35 percent.

### From a private choice to a public moment

With a cash tip jar, the choice was relatively private. On a digital screen, the customer may choose while an employee is standing nearby. The report calls this pressure “guilt tipping.” It is an explanation of the situation, not a direct measure of how strong the pressure is.

### Customers are starting to push back

A survey released on April 8, 2026, found that 78 percent of respondents thought tipping had become ridiculous. Forty-four percent said they had reduced their tips from the year before. Also, 36 percent said they chose a custom amount instead of a suggested one. These are survey answers, not proof that screens caused the change, but they show clear tiredness with the system.

## In one line…

The first tip number can move a payment higher, but if it feels too demanding, some customers may choose nothing at all. The real issue is not only the amount shown, but how the choice is presented.
