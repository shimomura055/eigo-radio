# The Tip Screen Is Not Just a Suggestion

At checkout, the tip screen can look like a simple suggestion. But the first number it shows can become the number a customer thinks around, even when it is higher than planned.

A peer-reviewed study found this effect. Published in 2014, it examined more than 13 million New York City taxi credit-card transactions.

Just below and above a $15 fare, the screen suddenly changed to a different set of default tip rates. Because the fares were so close, researchers could compare rides that saw different menus. Passengers who saw higher suggested rates left more. In this setting, the first number changed actual tips.

But higher is not always better. When the starting rates were too high, some passengers did not choose a smaller custom amount. They left no credit-card tip at all.

A 2022 policy brief offers one business explanation. It argues that high default tips may raise store income while having little measurable effect on customer satisfaction or repeat visits. So the screen creates a real trade-off: it can lift a payment, but an aggressive request can also lead to zero.

### From a tip jar to a public choice

U.S. restaurant tipping rose from about 10 to 15 percent in the early 1970s to about 20 percent by the late 1990s. Today, digital payment screens can show tips of up to 35 percent. The change is not only about math. A cash tip jar was relatively private, while choosing a rate in front of an employee can feel public. One policy brief calls this pressure “guilt tipping.”

### Customers are pushing back

In a U.S. consumer survey released in April 2026, 78 percent said tipping customs had become ridiculous, and 44 percent said they had reduced their tips from the previous year. The feeling of obligation also fell, from 66 percent in September 2025 to 59 percent in 2026. Meanwhile, 36 percent said they chose a custom amount instead of a preset rate. These are self-reported results, not proof that screens caused the change. But they may suggest some frustration with tipping practices and pressure around digital screens, without directly measuring growing frustration with the screens themselves.

## In one line

The tip screen can raise the amount people leave by setting the first number. But when that number feels too high or too public, the same screen can turn a gentle nudge into resistance.
