# Why the Tip Screen Starts Higher Than You Planned

## Main story

You finish a meal, a coffee, or a taxi ride. Then a screen turns toward you.

It shows several tip choices. The first choices may be higher than the amount you planned to give. Suddenly, a simple payment can feel like a test.

A peer-reviewed study looked at more than 13 million credit-card payments in New York City taxis. Before payment, the screen showed a small set of suggested tip rates.

Near a $15 fare, the screen changed its suggestions suddenly. Rides just below that price saw one menu. Rides just above it saw another. Because the fares were very close, researchers could compare similar payments with different suggested rates.

The result was clear: passengers who saw higher suggested rates usually left more money. The first number on the screen became a starting point for the final choice.

But there was also a limit. When the suggested rate was too high, some passengers did not enter a smaller custom amount. They left no credit-card tip at all.

So the screen creates a difficult balance. A higher suggestion can increase the tip from some customers. But it can also make other customers reject the tip completely.

This question is now common outside taxis. A Rutgers University policy brief says digital payment screens can show tips as high as 35 percent. In 2026, a Popmenu survey also reported strong consumer frustration. Many people said they were giving smaller tips than before.

### From a private jar to a public choice

Tipping in American restaurants rose from about 10 to 15 percent in the early 1970s to about 20 percent in the late 1990s, according to the Rutgers brief.

Cash made the choice more private. A digital screen can make the choice visible to the worker standing nearby. Michael Lahr calls the pressure from this moment “guilt tipping.” The screen is not only showing numbers. It may also change how the customer feels.

### The pushback is growing

In Popmenu’s 2026 survey, 78 percent said tipping had become ridiculous. Forty-four percent said they had reduced their tips from the year before.

The feeling of “I must leave a tip” fell from 66 percent in September 2025 to 59 percent in 2026. Also, 36 percent said they chose a custom amount instead of a preset choice. These answers do not prove what caused the change, but they show growing tiredness with tip screens.

## In one line…

The first number on a tip screen can move the final payment, but setting it too high is not a guaranteed win. It may bring in more money from some customers while pushing others toward zero.
