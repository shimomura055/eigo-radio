# 💳 Why the Tip Screen Often Starts Higher Than You Planned

At the end of a taxi ride or a restaurant meal, the final screen asks a simple question:

**“How much would you like to tip?”**

But the screen does not show a blank space. It shows a few suggested percentages first.

That first number can quietly change your decision.

A peer-reviewed study by Kareem Haggag and Giovanni Paci looked at more than **13 million** credit-card payments in New York City taxis. The researchers found a useful moment around a **$15 fare**.

When the fare moved from just below $15 to just above $15, the suggested tip menu suddenly changed. The rides were almost the same in price, but passengers saw different first suggestions.

Passengers who happened to see the higher suggestions left more money.

This showed that the first number on the screen was not just information. It had a real effect on the final tip.

But there was also a warning.

When the suggested amount became too high, some passengers did not simply choose a smaller custom tip. They chose **zero** for their card tip.

So, for businesses, a higher suggestion can bring in more money from some customers. But it can also make other customers reject the request completely.

That is the strange power of the tip screen. It does not only ask for a decision. It helps set the starting point for that decision.

### From a private jar to a public choice

In the past, a cash tip jar allowed people to decide more privately. A digital screen puts the choice in front of the worker. Michael Lahr of Rutgers University calls this pressure **“guilt tipping.”** At the same time, U.S. restaurant tipping rose from about **10–15%** in the early 1970s to about **20%** in the late 1990s. Some digital screens now show tips of up to **35%**.

### The pushback is now visible

A 2026 Popmenu consumer survey points to growing fatigue. **78%** said tipping had become ridiculous, and **44%** said they tipped less than the year before. The feeling that they “must” tip fell from **66%** in September 2025 to **59%**. Also, **36%** chose a custom amount instead of a preset one. These are self-reported answers, so they show a mood, not proof of cause.

## In one line…

The first tip number is a quiet starting point: it can raise some payments, but if it feels too high, it can turn the whole request into a no-tip moment.
