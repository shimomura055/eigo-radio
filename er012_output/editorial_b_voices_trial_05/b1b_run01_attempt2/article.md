# One Office, Two Ways to See a Desk

## The Question

Each morning, an employee arrives at the office and looks across the desks for one that is still empty. A bag goes under the chair. Yesterday’s keyboard may be today’s keyboard. There is nowhere to leave papers overnight. Some offices are assigning desks again, while others still let people choose. The question is not simply which system is better. It is why the same desk can mean comfort to one person and an unused cost to another.

### One Voice: The employee looking for a place

Every morning, the rule says, “Choose any desk.” But the worker still has to search for one. There is no drawer for papers or personal things.

Employees interviewed in Japan said they felt unsettled without their own desk and struggled to store their belongings. Workers with assigned desks were also more likely to say they felt they belonged at work and could concentrate.

Even a free system can create a regular seat. About 37 percent of people in free-address offices said their seating tended to become fixed. The choice exists on paper, but a dependable place to focus may still be missing.

### Another Voice: The workplace leader watching the whole floor

On the other side of the office, Linda Foggie looks across the whole floor. As a workplace leader at a bank, she must reckon with a broader workplace reality: many desks may sit empty on some days, while people who come in may still want a sense of place and team connection.

She must explain space costs to senior leaders and answer for different ways of working. Too many assigned desks can leave space unused. Too many flexible desks can weaken belonging.

Foggie described “a slow, quiet shift” toward more assigned seating and less flex seating. The pressure is to answer for both the empty floor and the people who use it.

## Why They See It Differently

These two people are measuring different things. The employee measures a good office by the start of the day: Can I sit down, put my papers somewhere, and focus?

The workplace leader measures it across sites and workers: How much space is empty, and can different groups still feel connected?

That is why Amazon planned assigned desks at offices that had used them before the pandemic, while keeping shared desks in European locations that already used them. One company can use both models without treating one as a universal answer.

## What This Tells Us

Before these voices, the desk debate sounds like a vote: fixed or free. After them, it becomes a question about what is being counted.

One person counts belonging and calm. The other must count empty space, cost, and the needs of a whole workforce. The deeper issue is not one winning desk system, but which responsibility the workplace is trying to carry.
