以下は、同じ記事から選ばれた5件のKey Phrase(英語学習ポッドキャストの
リスニング教材)です。5件から作れる全てのペア(10組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず10組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

# The Same Feed, Two Different Mornings

## The Question

Each morning, a phone lights up on a crowded train. A news page opens, and the first stories seem to know what the reader follows. There is only a short ride to work. For one reader, this is a helpful shortcut. For another, the same screen raises a quiet question: what important story may be missing? The system is the same. The experience is not.

### One Voice: The commuter who needs a fast morning

On the train, I open the “For you” page and see my followed topics first. I have only the ride to catch up, so I do not want to sort through unrelated stories. Google News uses topics and sources I follow. It can also use my past activity on Google services and YouTube. I can follow or unfollow topics, ask for more or less, and hide a source. These controls make the feed feel partly mine. I still worry an important story could be left out, but this quick start protects my morning time.

### Another Voice: The reader who sees the feed narrowing

Each time I scroll, I see familiar arguments and sources. I begin to wonder about stories outside that circle. I want to know why one article comes before another, but I cannot see all the signals behind the order or how much control I have over the recommendations. Clicks, likes, shares, and search history can shape what appears. The service may also be tuned for engagement and advertising income. That makes me uneasy. A hidden order may be choosing what I notice first. I want to protect my chance to meet views I would not normally open.

## Why They See It Differently

Both readers want the same thing: the feed shapes what they understand. Neither is wrong to care about that. But they protect different stakes. One wants a manageable morning and control. The other wants views outside the usual feed. The first can follow, hide, or change display levels. The second cannot reach the ranking rules, which may shape the view without showing how. This is an uneven process, not two equal controls.

## What This Tells Us

The deeper question is not simply whether personalization helps or harms. It is who can shape the path to news, and who can see that path. A feed may feel personal because it answers to a reader’s choices, while part of the decision stays out of view. That boundary is the real tension.
