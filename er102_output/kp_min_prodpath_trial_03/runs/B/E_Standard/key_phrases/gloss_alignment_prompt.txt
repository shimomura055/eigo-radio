# A Human Joined an AI Phone Call. The Problem Was the “Cast Change” Notice

I was impressed: “AI can even make phone calls. The future is here!” But the person taking the request at the other end was human. Wait—what a sudden change, from science fiction to a workplace drama. Still, the main point is not, “So it was a person after all!” The question is whether people were clearly told that a new person had joined.

Meta was testing some calls made through Muse. Trained contract workers made these calls and finished the conversations with the other person. They tested a setup where Muse passed the work to a human helper. People did not handle every call. So it is too soon to put up a caption saying, “Actually, people did it all!”

But this test missed one important notice. The vice president of Meta’s Superintelligence Labs division wrote a post for Meta staff. There, the vice president called it a “mistake.” The mistake was starting a test where contract workers made calls without clearly telling people. The vice president also said Meta had paused its human concierge feature for now.

Let’s check the breaking-news caption once more. What Meta pulled back was the human concierge feature. It did not stop all calls through Muse. Canceling a whole show and pausing one part are different things.

So, is it wrong by itself to bring in a human helper? Sometimes, passing the conversation to a person can help it move forward. But saying, “A person will take over from here,” is very different. Quietly changing the cast behind the scenes is not the same. In a movie, you might clap when a mysterious person is revealed. You might say, “Ah, so that’s who it is!” But you may not want that surprise in a conversation you are part of.

Some people may speak or feel differently if they think they are talking to AI. Knowing they are talking to a person may change that. Even if the request goes well, we cannot ignore the missing notice. We cannot just say, “It all worked out in the end.” That was exactly what Meta admitted it had got wrong.

Convenience matters. But some people care just as much about knowing who they are talking to. If AI passes the baton to a person, it should say so. That one line is part of the service, too. The person taking over does not have to be a secret guest.
