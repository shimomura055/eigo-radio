リスニング教材)です。5件から作れる全てのペア(10組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず10組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

【記事本文(参考、文脈確認用)】
# If You Had to Watch a Stone for Five Minutes, Would Your “Bad-Guy Switch” Turn On?

A stone. Still a stone. Surely something will happen soon—but no, it’s a stone. Nothing happens on the screen. This goes on for five minutes. But you, the viewer, can still do one thing: make someone else’s reward smaller.

This suddenly sounds dark. But it comes from an online experiment with people in the United States. They were put into groups at random. One group watched a five-minute video of a stone, where nothing happened. The other group watched a magician.

During the video, they had three choices. They could make another person’s reward smaller, make it bigger, or do nothing. They could change it by 10 cents each time, up to 50 cents in all. The stone on the screen stayed quiet, while the viewers were given “a little power.”

So, did boredom become fuel for being mean?

People who strongly liked seeing others suffer were more likely to make the other person’s reward smaller after watching the stone video than after watching the magician video. For people who felt this less strongly, there was no clear difference.

You may want to say, “So, it really depends on personality.” But there is more to the story.

In the next experiment, people were again put into groups at random. But this time, they could not make the reward bigger. They could only make it smaller. This time, it did not matter how strongly people liked seeing others suffer. People who watched the stone video were more likely to make another person’s reward smaller than people in the other groups.

If we look only at personality, we may miss something. We need to ask not only what kind of person someone is, but also what they can do in that situation. We thought we were reading a profile of the person. Now we also notice how the stage is set.

But it is too soon to say, “Add a button to make rewards bigger and bring world peace!” The two were separate experiments. We cannot say the choices alone caused the different results. We do not know if giving people a choice to make rewards bigger can stop meanness.

The experiment looked only at brief boredom. It also looked only at actions that made another person’s reward smaller. We cannot use its results directly to explain everyday meanness or boredom that lasts a long time.

Even so, we see boring moments a little differently now. We can ask not only, “What mustn’t I do?” but also, “What else can I do instead?” Before we look at someone’s personality, we can look at the menu in front of us. The stone may not move for five minutes. But the question we ask changes a little.

## In one line
Stone-watchers were more likely to make another person’s reward smaller. In one test, this was true only for people who strongly liked seeing others suffer; in another, it was true at every level. The tests suggest choices may matter along with personality, but they do not show that offering a choice to make rewards bigger stops meanness or applies to everyday life.
