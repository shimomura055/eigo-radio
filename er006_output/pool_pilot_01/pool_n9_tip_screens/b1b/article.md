# Why Tip Screens Often Ask for More Than You Planned

At checkout, an awkward moment may arrive before payment is finished. The screen offers a few tip rates, with one number shown first. That number can change the question in your mind—from “What did I plan to give?” to “Which option should I choose?”

A peer-reviewed study published in 2014 examined more than 13 million credit-card taxi transactions in New York City. Around a $15 fare, the tip menu changed suddenly. At fares just below and just above that price, passengers saw different default tip menus.

Passengers who saw the higher suggested rates left more money. In this taxi setting, the screen itself changed the final tip. The first number became a starting point for the decision.

But there was a limit. When the suggested rate was too high, some customers did not choose a smaller custom amount. They left no credit-card tip at all.

A 2022 policy brief describes high suggested tips as a business choice. It says they can increase store revenue, while having little measurable effect on customer satisfaction or return visits. That offers one reason a screen may show a number higher than a customer’s first plan.

Today, digital payment screens can sometimes suggest tips as high as 35 percent. The small box at the end of a meal is no longer just recording a decision. It is helping shape the decision before it is made.

### The old tip jar worked differently

A 2022 policy brief places today’s screen in a longer change. In the US, restaurant tipping rose from about 10 to 15 percent in the early 1970s to about 20 percent by the late 1990s. The report argues that digital screens also create “guilt tipping.” The choice feels less private when a worker is nearby and can see it.

### The pushback is becoming visible

A 2026 US survey found signs of fatigue. Seventy-eight percent called tipping ridiculous, and 44 percent said they had reduced their tips. The share who felt they had to tip on a screen fell from 66 percent in September 2025 to 59 percent in 2026. Thirty-six percent chose a custom amount. These are self-reported answers, not proof of cause.

## In one line…

The tip screen starts the conversation with a number, but it does not control the final answer. A stronger suggestion may bring in more money from some customers—and bring a zero from others.
