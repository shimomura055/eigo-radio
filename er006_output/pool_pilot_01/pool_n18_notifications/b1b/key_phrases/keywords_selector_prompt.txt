# Why Is It So Hard to Ignore a Notification?

Imagine trying to focus when a familiar phone alert sounds.

You do not look at the screen. You do not touch the phone. But your attention may still move toward it.

A 2022 study tested this idea with 73 university students. They completed 960 rounds of a visual letter task. Some rounds included a notification sound. Others included a neutral comparison sound.

The result was clear. People responded more slowly when the notification sound played. Brain-wave recordings also showed a stronger signal during those rounds. The researchers interpreted this as a sign that extra mental control was needed to keep working.

Then came an even quieter finding.

A second study, published in 2023, tested 42 university students. Their phones were switched off, and no notifications arrived. In one condition, the phone was on the desk. In the other, it was placed in another room.

Even without a sound or a screen, performance was lower when the phone was nearby.

Together, the studies suggest that distraction can begin in two ways. A notification sound can interrupt an active task. And the physical presence of a phone may affect attention before any alert arrives.

### Silence is not the same as absence

The second study gives the quiet effect a clearer shape. With the phone on the desk, the average attention score was 99.71, compared with 108.95 in another room. Processing speed showed the same direction: 98.48 on the desk, compared with 108.57 away. The researchers suggested that people may keep part of their attention ready for the phone, but they did not directly test that explanation.

### A habit that starts early

A 2018 U.S. survey of teens aged 13 to 17 found a familiar morning pattern. Seventy-two percent said they often or sometimes checked messages or notifications soon after waking. A majority felt a need to reply quickly. About four in ten felt anxious, upset, or lonely without their phone nearby. Most did not call this addiction; the report described it as a hard-to-resist habit.

## In one line...

Your phone does not have to be opened to become part of the task. In these studies, attention was pulled by the sound and affected by the silent device, which helps explain why ignoring a notification can feel harder than it looks.
