# The Small Bench and the Big Question: Who Can Stay?

A public bench looks like a small thing. But for an older person, it can change a walk. It can offer a short rest. It can also create a place to meet someone.

A 2016 qualitative study by Ottoni and colleagues looked at people aged 60 and over in three nearby neighborhoods in Vancouver, Canada. Researchers used one-to-one seated interviews and walk-along interviews. The study included 28 participants in 2012 and 22 in 2014.

Older adults said benches helped them use and enjoy green and waterfront spaces. They also said benches supported social contact. The researchers described this idea with a simple image: “benches become like porches.”

A separate review by Boavida and colleagues in 2023 examined 28 articles published from 2020 to 2023. It looked at public parks, active aging, and well-being. The review reported that older park users wanted more benches, and this preference was linked with relaxation.

But benches are not always treated as invitations. Some cities have used defensive, or hostile, design. This can mean sloped seats, center armrests, or removing benches. These changes may make it harder to sleep or lie down.

So one small object now sits inside a larger city question:

“Is public space only for moving through, or is it also for stopping and staying?”

### A bench can act like a porch

The Vancouver study gives the bench a social meaning. It is not only street furniture. It can be a semi-public place between a private home and a busy street. People may pause there, see familiar faces, and have casual contact. The study does not prove that a bench creates friendship. It shows how older adults experienced the bench as part of neighborhood life.

### Design can welcome—or push people away

The review says design should be tested with users, not only judged from a plan. Interviews, group talks, observation, and comfort checks can show what works. It also reminds us to be careful with examples. At Malmö’s Triangeln station, officials said a bench was too steep by mistake and later corrected. In New York, a 2021 post about removing subway benches to stop sleeping was deleted; the MTA called it an error.

## In one line…

A bench is a small place, but it can support movement, rest, and everyday connection—or tell some people they should not stay.

Maybe the future of public space begins with a simple question: Who is this seat really for?
