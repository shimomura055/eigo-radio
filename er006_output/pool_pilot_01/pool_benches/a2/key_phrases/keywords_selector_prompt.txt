# A Small Seat, a Bigger City: Why Public Benches Matter

A bench does not move. But it can help a person keep moving.

For an older adult, a long walk can become several shorter steps. There is time to stop, sit, and rest before walking again.

A study in three neighboring areas of Vancouver listened to adults aged 60 and older. Researchers used seated interviews and walk-along talks. They met 28 participants in 2012 and 22 in 2014.

The older adults said benches helped them enjoy green spaces and places by the water. The seats gave them rest during walks. They also supported short talks and a feeling of connection.

The researchers described this with a warm image:

**“Benches become like porches.”**

A porch is between private life and public life. A neighborhood bench can play a similar role.

A 2023 review gives a wider view. It studied 28 articles published from 2020 to 2023. The review reported that older park users preferred more benches, linked to their wish to relax.

Parks with seating, pleasant paths, toilets, cafés, and good care were also reported to encourage older visitors.

But public space can move in the opposite direction.

Some benches are tilted or divided by middle armrests. Other benches are removed. When such changes are used to stop sleeping or other activities, they are called **“hostile architecture”** or **“defensive design.”**

In 2021, an NYC Transit social media post said subway benches were being removed to stop homeless people from sleeping on them. The post was later deleted. The MTA said it had been posted in error. It was not proof of an official removal policy.

So, the bench debate is not only about street furniture. It is about who can rest, who can stay, and how easily older adults can move through their community.

### A porch shared by strangers

“Like a porch” is more than a nice phrase. A person can rest alone, greet a neighbor, or watch daily life. These small contacts may help a local place feel familiar and shared, especially for people who spend more time close to home.

### The shape is not the whole story

At Malmö’s Triangeln station, a tilted bench brought public complaints. A formal complaint was reported in March 2011. But a city explanation said the bench had arrived steeper than planned and was later fixed. The review recommends listening to users, watching real use, and measuring comfort.

## In one line…

A bench is a small object, but it can open or close a city. A place to sit can also be a place to keep moving—and to stay connected.
