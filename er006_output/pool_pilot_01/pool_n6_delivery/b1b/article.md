# Why We Keep Refreshing the Delivery Tracking Page

A package is on its way, but its next step is unclear.

So we open the tracking page. Then, a few minutes later, we check it again. Nothing may have changed. Still, the update button pulls us back.

What looks like simple impatience may be part of a wider response to uncertain waiting.

A 2025 longitudinal study by Howell and Sweeny, published in *Emotion*, followed three groups over periods ranging from several weeks to several months. They included voters waiting for the 2020 United States presidential election result, people waiting for their California bar exam results, and academic job applicants waiting for hiring decisions.

The pattern was clear: as people became more worried, they searched more often for news and updates.

This result challenges the common idea that anxiety makes people avoid information. In these waiting periods, the opposite pattern appeared. When worry was strongest, people looked for more clues.

The researchers suggest that checking may create a fragile and temporary feeling of control. A person cannot control the election result, an exam result, or a hiring decision. But checking gives them something they can do.

The studies did not directly test delivery tracking pages. Even so, repeated package checks may be an everyday example of the same wider pattern. When the result is unknown, information can feel like a small handle on an uncontrollable situation.

### A small action in a powerless wait

Waiting is not always passive. When the outcome is outside our control, searching can become a small action that feels available. But that control is fragile. An update may calm uncertainty for a moment without changing the result. This is a possible mechanism, not proven causation.

### The lab version of “check again”

In a 2021 *Scientific Reports* experiment, 28 healthy people aged 19 to 29 watched moving-dot displays and could replay them before deciding. Harder, less certain displays led to more checks. The reported link was **b = 0.90, P < 2 × 10⁻¹⁶**. Conscious awareness of uncertainty predicted checking, while an unconscious measure did not.

## In one line…

Repeated delivery checks may be a small, everyday form of a larger human response: when the future feels uncertain, we search for clues to regain a little control—even if that feeling lasts only a moment.
