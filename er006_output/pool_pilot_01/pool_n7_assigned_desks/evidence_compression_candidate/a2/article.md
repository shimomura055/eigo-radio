# The Quiet Return of the Desk with a Name on It

For a while, many modern offices had no home base.

After the pandemic, **hot-desking** became more common. In this system, workers do not have their own desks. They choose an empty desk when they arrive.

Now, that system is facing complaints.

Some employees do not like searching for a desk each morning. Others do not enjoy sitting beside different people every day. They also want a work area that feels more stable and predictable.

So, in some offices, the assigned desk is coming back.

Companies such as Scotiabank and iCapital Network are quietly reducing their use of hot-desking. For some teams, one desk is once again kept for one worker.

But this is not a full return across the business world.

Desk sharing is still common. A 2024 office survey found that the share of companies with at least one desk for each worker fell from 56% in 2023 to 40% in 2024. The companies surveyed expected that figure to fall to about one third by 2026.

The office is changing in two directions at once.

### A small desk can create a big feeling

A workplace survey found that workers with assigned desks were more likely to say they felt they belonged at work. They were also more likely to say they could focus well. This shows a connection, but it does not prove that the desk itself caused these better feelings.

### A mixed model is taking shape

The wider trend still favors shared space. At the same time, some companies are bringing back fixed seats for selected workers or teams. This suggests that offices may not choose one system for everyone. They may use shared desks in some areas and assigned desks in others.

## In one line…

The assigned desk is quietly returning in some offices, but hot-desking is still part of the larger workplace trend. The future office may be a careful mix of personal space and shared space.
