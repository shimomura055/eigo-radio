# The Quiet Return of the Assigned Desk

For a while, the modern office moved toward shared desks. Now, some offices are quietly moving back.

Hot-desking means that no desk belongs to one person. Workers choose an empty desk when they arrive. An assigned desk is different: each worker has a regular place.

Today, some companies are bringing assigned desks back. Others are using hot-desking less than before. The change is not happening everywhere. It is a choice being made in some offices.

The change comes after workers complained about shared desks. For some people, choosing a new desk again and again makes the workday feel less settled. They want a place that feels familiar and ready for work.

Still, this is not a full return to the old office. Across the wider market, companies continue to share desks and keep fewer desks for their workers.

So the office story is divided:

“Some desks are becoming personal again. But desk sharing is still common.”

### A small space can change the workday

One survey gives a clue about the appeal. Workers with assigned desks were more likely to say they felt they belonged at work and could focus well. This shows a connection, not proof that the desks caused these feelings. Other complaints are practical. Hot-desking can feel less attractive than working at home, and sitting near strangers can be uncomfortable.

### The office is still sharing

The bigger office picture points the other way. In one survey, the share of companies with at least one desk for every worker fell from 56 percent in 2023 to 40 percent in 2024. The survey’s respondents expected that figure to fall to about one third by 2026. The return of assigned desks is selective, not a new rule for every office.

## In one line…

Assigned desks are making a quiet comeback because some workers want a stable place to belong and focus. But the wider office is still moving toward shared desks, so the future may be a mix of both systems.
