# The Office Desk Has a Name Again

For a time, the office desk stopped belonging to anyone. Hot-desking became more common after the pandemic. Workers were expected to choose any free desk instead of using one assigned to them.

Now, in some offices, that idea is being quietly rolled back.

Companies including Scotiabank and iCapital Network are among those bringing assigned desks back, at least in some parts of their workplaces. For the workers affected, the morning search for an open desk is replaced by a familiar place.

This is not a return everywhere. It is a selective change in some companies and teams. The move comes as some employees have voiced dissatisfaction with hot-desking.

The system can offer flexibility, but it also changes the daily experience of work. A desk is no longer just a surface for a laptop. For some workers, it is part of knowing where they belong and how they will begin the day.

Survey data show a link between assigned seating and two workplace feelings: belonging and the ability to focus. But the wider office market is still moving toward shared space.

So the desk has returned for some workers—but it has not become the new rule for everyone.

### A desk can shape the daily experience

Among workers with assigned desks, 87% reported a sense of belonging, compared with 74% in environments without assigned desks. For effective focus, the figures were 80% and 67% for assigned and hot-desking workers. The pattern is meaningful, but it is not proof that the desk caused the result.

### A small reversal inside a larger shift

A 2024 survey found that companies with at least one desk per employee fell from 56% in 2023 to 40% in 2024. Respondents expected the share to reach about one-third by 2026. This suggests that assigned desks are returning in selected places while desk sharing remains the wider trend.

## In one line…

The desk is getting its name back—but only in some corners of the office. For now, assigned seats and shared desks are living side by side.
