# 🪑 The Quiet Return of the Assigned Desk

After the pandemic, many offices changed the way people sit at work.

Instead of giving each person a desk, they introduced **hot-desking**. A worker comes in, finds an empty desk, and uses it for the day. There is no personal desk waiting for that worker.

Now, some offices are changing again. Reports from the office world show companies such as Scotiabank and iCapital Network bringing back **assigned desks** for some workers or teams. In this system, a person or a team has a regular place.

The change is quiet. It is not a return to fixed desks across every office. It is a response in some workplaces to complaints about shared seating and the daily search for a place to work.

So the office story is not simply, “Shared desks are gone.” Some companies are adding fixed seats, while companies overall still have fewer desks for their workers.

The result is a mixed picture. One team may choose any free desk each morning. Another team may have its own regular area.

The question is now simple:

**Is maximum flexibility always the best way to work?**

### A desk can feel like a home base

Why are some workers pushing back? Korn Ferry says hot-desking may feel less attractive than home, and sitting beside unfamiliar colleagues can be uncomfortable. Workers may also value a predictable, consistent place. Gensler’s data found stronger feelings of belonging with assigned desks: 87% versus 74%. These are links, not proof the desk caused the difference.

### Flexibility is still the larger trend

Still, this is not a full return to one desk for every person. CBRE found that the share of companies with at least one desk per employee fell from 56% in 2023 to 40% in 2024. Respondents expected about one-third by 2026. Sharing remains common, even as some teams choose assigned seats.

## In one line…

Assigned desks are making a quiet comeback as some workplaces respond to the wish for a stable place to work. But shared seating is still the wider trend, so the future may be a mix of both systems.
