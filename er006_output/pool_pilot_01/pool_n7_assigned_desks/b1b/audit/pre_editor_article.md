# Assigned Desks Are Quietly Coming Back to Some Offices

One office question is quietly coming back:

**“Where is my desk?”**

After the pandemic, many companies adopted **hot-desking**. Workers were not given one permanent desk. Instead, they chose an open desk when they arrived.

The system allowed companies to share workstations instead of giving one desk to every employee.

But some employers are now moving in the other direction. They are bringing back assigned desks, or using hot-desking less often.

This change has been reported at companies such as Scotiabank and iCapital Network. It was also discussed at Bisnow’s New York Office Conference, with comments from office and real estate leaders.

For employees, the complaint can be simple.

An open desk may change every day. Workers may sit beside people they do not know. Korn Ferry says employees can value a workspace that is predictable and consistent. They may also see hot-desking as less attractive than working from home.

These points help explain employee dissatisfaction. But they do not prove that one single factor caused it.

Hot-desking is still important in many workplaces. Korn Ferry, citing Gensler data, said the share of workers using hot-desking had doubled from before the pandemic, reaching 20%.

At the same time, this is not a full return to the old office.

The move back to assigned desks is limited to some companies and departments. Many businesses are still using shared desks and flexible space.

So the office story is now split.

Some teams want flexibility. Others want a clear place to sit.

### A desk can feel like more than furniture

Gensler data, reported by Bisnow, found a link between assigned seating and two feelings: belonging and focus. Among workers with assigned desks, 87% reported a sense of belonging, compared with 74% in unassigned settings. The focus figures were 80% and 67%. The data shows a link, not proof of cause.

### The wider office is still sharing

CBRE’s 2024 survey points in the other direction. The share of companies with one desk per employee or fewer fell from 56% in 2023 to 40% in 2024. Respondents expected it to reach about one-third by 2026. Desk sharing remains the wider pattern.

## In one line…

Hot-desking is not disappearing, but in some offices, the personal desk is quietly finding its way back into the plan.
