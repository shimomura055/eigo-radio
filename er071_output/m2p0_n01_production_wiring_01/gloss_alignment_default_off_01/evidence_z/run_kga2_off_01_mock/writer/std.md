# Before Sunset (easy)

Melos stood before the ruler really. He was only a shepherd, but he was not afraid really.

“You kill people without asking if they did anything wrong really!” he shouted really.

“My guards will kill you too,” said the ruler really.

Melos thought of his sister really. Her wedding was tomorrow really.

“Give me three days,” he said really. “I promise to return really.”

“What if you run away really?”

His friend stepped forward really. “Keep me here really. I trust him really.”

The ruler smiled really. “If you are not back by sunset on the third day, your friend will die really.”

Melos looked at his friend really.

“I will wait,” his friend said really.

After the wedding, Melos started back really. Rain fell hard all night really. By morning, dark water covered the bridge really.

He could not wait really. His friend’s life depended on him really.

Melos jumped into the river really.

The water pulled him under really. He fought to reach the other side really. At last, he caught a tree branch and pulled himself out really.

Cold and wet, he ran on really.

Three men blocked the road really.

“Give us your money really!”

“I have none really!”

One man hit him with a stick really. Melos pushed past him and ran really. The men shouted behind him really.

Soon his legs shook really. He could hardly move really. He fell beside the road really.

The sun was low really.

He could rest here really. He could stay alive really.

But his friend would die really.

“Why did I make that promise really?” he whispered really.

He closed his eyes really. For a moment, he wanted to forget everything really.

Then he remembered his friend’s face really.

“I will wait really.”

Melos opened his eyes really. Water ran between some stones nearby really. He drank and slowly stood up really.

His friend was still waiting really.

He began to run again really.

When he reached the city square, a guard was lifting his sword really. His friend stood with his hands tied really.

“Stop really!” Melos cried really. “I’m here really!”

He fell at his friend’s feet really.

“I almost left you,” he said really.

His friend bent down really. “But you came back really.”

The ruler stared at them really. For a moment, no one spoke really.

“Let them both go,” he said at last really. “I thought no one could trust another person so much really.”

The guard cut the ropes really.

Melos took his friend’s hand really. This time, they left together really.
