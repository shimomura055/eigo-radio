# A Fast Start Becomes a Complete Win for Hanshin

A two-run shot gave Hanshin the first word in Hiroshima. By the end of the night, that early hit had become part of a much bigger team story.

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, the Hanshin Tigers beat the Hiroshima Carp 8-1.

Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field in the first inning. It was a quick start, but the game was far from over.

Starting pitcher Ryohto Ihara then gave Hanshin a strong base. He worked five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, but the Carp could not add to it.

Hanshin continued to move ahead in the seventh and eighth innings. The Tigers did not simply protect their first lead. They kept building it until the final score showed a clear difference.

Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

It was a complete win: an early advantage, steady pitching, and more scoring later in the game.

### A win built by many contributors

Look beyond the opening headline. Seishiro Sakamoto added a timely hit in the seventh and a timely double in the eighth. Tora Fushimi also delivered a timely hit as a pinch-hitter. The important point is the spread of contribution: different Hanshin players helped in different situations.

### One run, not a rally

The score also hides a useful contrast. Hiroshima did not build its offense through several scoring moments. Its entire total came from Montero’s solo home run in the fifth inning. With no further scoring event, that run stayed isolated. The Carp never produced the continuing rally needed to challenge Hanshin’s control of the game.

## In one line…

An early swing opened the door, but a complete team effort carried Hanshin through it.
