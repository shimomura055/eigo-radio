# A Fast Start, Then a Firm Finish: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima

Some baseball games turn on one big swing. This one began that way, but Hanshin did not leave the result to that moment.

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run homer to center in the first inning. Hanshin moved in front early against Hiroshima.

The next task was to protect that lead. Rito Ihara, Hanshin’s starting pitcher, worked five innings. He allowed two hits and one run. Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

With Hiroshima limited to one run, Hanshin had room to keep building. The Tigers added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The early advantage had become a wide gap by the end.

Hanshin won 8-1.

### An attack in two phases

Hanshin’s offense had two different shapes. Sato’s home run created the first separation. Later, timely hits by Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Taii Fushimi, followed by Sakamoto’s timely double, made the margin much larger. The message is not just that Hanshin started well. It also finished its work with several important hits.

### A breakthrough that stayed small

Montero’s home run was Hiroshima’s only scoring play. Ihara’s line was five innings, two hits, and one run. Together, those facts help explain why the home team’s breakthrough did not become a longer attack. It cut the lead by one run, but never produced a multi-run threat. The interruption was real, but it stayed isolated.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned a quick two-run start into a complete team win: its starter kept Hiroshima’s scoring to one solo homer, and its hitters kept adding pressure later. At Mazda Stadium, the final 8-1 score told the story clearly.
