# A Fast Start, Then a Firm Finish: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima

Some baseball games turn on one big swing. This one started that way, but Hanshin did not leave the result to it.

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run homer to center in the first inning, putting Hanshin in front early against Hiroshima.

The next task was to protect the lead. Hanshin starter Rito Ihara worked five innings, allowing two hits and one run. Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

With Hiroshima scoring only once, Hanshin kept building. The Tigers added runs in the seventh and eighth innings, turning their early advantage into a wide gap.

Hanshin won 8-1.

### An attack in two phases

Hanshin’s offense had two phases. Sato’s home run created the first separation. Later, timely hits by Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Taii Fushimi, followed by Sakamoto’s timely double, made the margin much larger. Hanshin started well and finished with several important hits.

### A breakthrough that stayed small

Montero’s home run was Hiroshima’s only scoring play. Ihara allowed two hits and one run in five innings. Those facts help explain why the home team’s breakthrough did not become a longer attack. It cut the lead by one run, and Hiroshima scored no more runs. The interruption was real, but it stayed isolated.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned a quick two-run start into a complete team win: its starter limited Hiroshima to one solo homer, and its hitters kept adding pressure later. At Mazda Stadium, the final 8-1 score told the story clearly.
