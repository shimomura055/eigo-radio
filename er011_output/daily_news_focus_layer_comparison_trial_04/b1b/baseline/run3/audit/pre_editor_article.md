# A Two-Run Start, a Complete Finish: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

Some wins are decided by one swing. Others show their strength in what happens after that swing. Hanshin’s 8-1 victory over Hiroshima on August 16 had both parts.

At Mazda Stadium, Teruaki Sato sent a two-run home run to center field in the first inning. Hanshin quickly moved in front, 2-0.

The game then took a quieter shape. Hanshin’s starting pitcher, Rihito Ihara, gave the team a steady start. He pitched five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, cutting the gap to one run. But Hiroshima could not add to that score.

Hanshin did not leave the result to its early lead. The team scored again in the seventh and eighth innings, slowly turning a close game into a comfortable one.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

What began with one powerful swing ended as a complete win. Hanshin took the lead early, protected it, and then made the difference much larger late in the game.

### Two home runs, two very different results

The two home runs did not carry the same weight. Sato’s shot brought two runs, while Montero’s later drive brought only one and represented all of Hiroshima’s scoring. This was not simply a battle of home runs. The number of runners on base changed what each swing could do.

### A win built by more than one hitter

Ihara’s steady outing gave Hanshin a base to build on, but the finish belonged to several players. In the seventh and eighth innings, Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs, and pinch-hitter Towa Fushimi added another. Hanshin turned an early advantage into a late cushion through different contributors.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first with power, then finished the job with steady pitching and help from across the lineup. It was an 8-1 win that never let Hiroshima find its way back.
