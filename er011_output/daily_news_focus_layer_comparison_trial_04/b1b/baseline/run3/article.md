# A Two-Run Start, a Complete Finish: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

Some wins are decided by one swing. Others show their strength in what comes after. Hanshin’s 8-1 victory over Hiroshima on August 16 had both.

At Mazda Stadium, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field in the first inning. Hanshin quickly took a 2-0 lead.

The game then became quieter. Hanshin’s starting pitcher, Rihito Ihara, gave the team a steady start. He pitched five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, cutting the lead to one run. But Hiroshima could not score again.

Hanshin did not rely only on its early lead. The team scored in the seventh and eighth innings, turning a close game into a comfortable one.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

What began with one powerful swing ended as a complete win. Hanshin took the lead early, protected it, and then made it much larger late in the game.

### Two home runs, two very different results

The two home runs did not carry the same weight. Sato’s shot brought in two runs, while Montero’s later drive brought in only one and accounted for all of Hiroshima’s scoring. This was not simply a battle of home runs. The number of runners on base changed what each swing could do.

### A win built by more than one hitter

Ihara’s steady outing gave Hanshin a base to build on, but several players helped finish the game. In the seventh and eighth innings, Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs, and pinch-hitter Towa Fushimi added another. Hanshin turned an early advantage into a late cushion through different contributors.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first with power, then finished the job with steady pitching and help from across the lineup. It was an 8-1 win that never let Hiroshima find its way back.
