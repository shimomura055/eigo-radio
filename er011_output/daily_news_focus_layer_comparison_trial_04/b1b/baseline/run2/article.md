# One Swing, Then Complete Control: Hanshin Wins 8-1

An early home run can change a baseball game. But an early lead still needs to be protected. On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin did both.

The Tigers moved first in the opening inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field, giving Hanshin a 2-0 lead before the game had fully settled.

From there, starter Rihito Ihara gave Hanshin a steady base. He pitched five innings and allowed two hits and one run. Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

Hanshin also scored in the seventh and added more in the eighth. The lead grew, and the game moved away from a close finish.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8. Hanshin started with one powerful swing, then kept that advantage under control until the end.

### A lead protected by preventing pressure

The key detail is not simply that Hiroshima scored once. It is how that run arrived: one solo home run, with no multi-run response. Ihara’s work kept the early lead from becoming a high-pressure situation. Those first two runs had lasting value before the later insurance arrived.

### More than one source of offense

The late cushion also had a clear shape. Seishiro Sakamoto produced a timely hit in the seventh, then an RBI double in the eighth. Pinch hitter Torai Fushimi added another timely hit in the seventh. The margin was built not only by Sato’s power, but by different roles delivering when chances came.

## In one line…

Hanshin did not rely on one big moment alone. A fast start, steady pitching, and contributions from different parts of the lineup turned an early lead into a complete 8-1 victory.
