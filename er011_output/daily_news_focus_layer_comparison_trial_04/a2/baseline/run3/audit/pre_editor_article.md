# One Big Swing, Then a Steady Climb: Hanshin Wins 8–1

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, the Hanshin Tigers beat the Hiroshima Toyo Carp 8–1.

The story began with one powerful swing. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin took a 2–0 lead before the game had time to settle.

But an early lead alone was not enough. Starting pitcher Ryoto Ihara kept the game quiet for Hanshin. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

That one Hiroshima run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. It was Hiroshima’s only score of the night. After that, Hanshin stayed in front.

Then Hanshin made the gap larger. The Tigers scored again in the seventh and eighth innings. By the end, the early lead had become an 8–1 victory.

### A reply without a second act

The scoring pattern is the hidden story. Hiroshima answered Hanshin’s two-run start with one solo home run, but there was no follow-up. Hanshin had a different pattern: it scored early, then added runs in separate late innings. Montero changed the number on the board, but Hiroshima could not turn that moment into lasting pressure.

### The late push had several voices

The final push was not carried by Sato alone. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in a run in the seventh inning, then hit a double that brought in another in the eighth. Tora Fushimi, sent in to hit as a substitute, also drove in a run in the seventh. Different hitters helped turn the lead into a complete win.

## In one line…

One big swing started the night, but Hanshin finished it with steady pitching and contributions from more than one hitter. It was an 8–1 win built step by step.
