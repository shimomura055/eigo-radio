# One Swing Started It, but Hanshin Finished Strong at 8-1

One swing started the night. The later innings made sure the game would not stay close.

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, the Hanshin Tigers beat the Hiroshima Toyo Carp 8-1.

Hanshin took the lead in the first inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. The score was 2-0 before the game had fully settled.

Hanshin’s starting pitcher, Ryoto Ihara, then gave the team five solid innings. He allowed two hits and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. That brought Hiroshima closer, but it was the home team’s only score of the game.

Hanshin added more runs in the seventh inning. The team scored again in the eighth. The lead grew, and Hiroshima could not close the gap.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

It was a win that began quickly, stayed under control, and became larger as the game went on.

### From a lead to a big gap

Sato’s home run gave Hanshin a 2-0 start, but the final seven-run difference came later. After Montero brought Hiroshima within one run, Hanshin added runs in the seventh and eighth. The first hit created the chance, but the later scoring made the win much clearer.

### One score, not a long attack

Ihara’s start was not a shutout, and it did not need to be. He allowed two hits in five innings, while Hiroshima scored only on Montero’s solo home run. In simple terms, one scoring blow did not become a longer attack. Hanshin stayed in control without needing perfect pitching.

## In one line…

Hanshin used one early swing, steady starting pitching, and a late push to turn a close moment into a complete 8-1 win.
