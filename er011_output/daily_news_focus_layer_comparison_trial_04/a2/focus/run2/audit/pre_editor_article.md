# An Early Blast Became an 8-1 Hanshin Win

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin did not wait for the game to settle. They struck first, and that early blow shaped the night.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin took a 2-0 lead.

Then Rinto Ihara gave that lead a strong base. He pitched five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, making the score 2-1. But Hiroshima could not add another run.

Hanshin did not leave the game to one early swing. They added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew late, and Hiroshima could not catch up.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

This was a win built in stages: an early home run, a steady start from Ihara, and more runs late. Hanshin took control early and kept the gap growing.

### More than one player finished the job

The late scoring had a wider shape than the opening home run. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs in both the seventh and eighth innings. Pinch-hitter Fushimi also brought in a run. The margin grew through several players, including help from the bench.

### What the fifth-inning home run really tells us

Montero’s home run can sound like the moment Hiroshima turned the game around. But the record supports a smaller conclusion. It shows a one-run interruption, not a proven change in momentum or team feeling. No comeback followed, and the game record does not confirm what either side was thinking.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned one early swing into a complete team win: a steady start, late help from different players, and a lead that kept growing.
