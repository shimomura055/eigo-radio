# A Small Refrigerator Slider With a Big Job

At first glance, a crisper drawer is just a box for fresh food. But many refrigerators give it two different settings: low humidity and high humidity.

That small choice creates a different environment inside the drawer.

A high-humidity drawer is more closed. It helps keep moisture around foods that lose water quickly. Leafy greens, such as kale, can stay fresh longer instead of wilting quickly. Broccoli and other produce that needs moisture can also benefit from this setting.

A low-humidity drawer allows more air to move through. This matters for foods that release ethylene as they ripen. If that gas stays around other fruits and vegetables, they may ripen too quickly and go bad sooner. With more ventilation, the gas can escape more easily.

So the crisper drawer is doing more than keeping food cold. One setting helps manage moisture. The other helps manage the air around foods.

### Think about how foods affect one another

The useful question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?”

Apples, pears, bananas, and tomatoes release a lot of ethylene. In a ventilated, low-humidity drawer, that gas can leave more easily. The benefit may reach beyond the item itself, because nearby produce is less exposed to a gas that can push it toward early ripening and spoilage.

The drawer can therefore help manage relationships between foods, not just store them side by side.

### First decide whether the drawer is needed

Humidity is not always the first question. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage.

Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic are different. They are best kept in a cool, dry place. They should not all be treated as one identical “leave it on the counter” group.

That means the first question is sometimes not, “Which humidity setting should I use?” It is, “Does this food belong in the crisper system at all?”

## In one line…

The crisper drawer is not a simple fruit-versus-vegetable box. It is a small decision about how food behaves, how nearby food may be affected, and where each item belongs.
