# The Small Fridge Drawer With Two Very Different Jobs

A refrigerator crisper drawer can look like a small detail. But behind its simple slider are two different ways to store fresh food.

Many models offer a low-humidity setting and a high-humidity setting. The low setting lets more air move through the drawer. The high setting is closer to sealed, so it helps hold moisture inside.

Used with the right food, these settings can help produce stay fresh longer.

But the familiar shortcut — fruit in one place and vegetables in the other — does not tell the whole story.

The better question is what problem the food faces in storage. Some foods can make nearby produce ripen too soon. Others mainly lose water and wilt. That is where the small control becomes useful.

There is one more catch. The drawer is not the right place for every item. A setting cannot fix food that belongs somewhere else.

### The slider is a cause-and-effect tool

Think of the control as a way to respond to a food’s main storage problem, not as a simple fruit label. If ethylene from the food may affect nearby produce, the low setting gives that gas a way out. Apples, pears, and bananas fit this side. If quick water loss is the bigger risk, the high setting helps hold moisture. Leafy greens and broccoli are examples. This approach also helps with unfamiliar produce.

### First ask whether the drawer is right

Before choosing a setting, ask whether the item belongs in the drawer at all. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic are better kept in a cool, dry place, so they should not be treated as identical counter items. Strawberries and oranges show why one rule can mislead: commercial 90–95% humidity figures do not automatically become a household high-humidity recommendation, and appliance guidance differs.

## In one line…

The crisper drawer is not one answer for every food. First check whether the item belongs there, then ask whether its bigger problem is nearby ripening or its own water loss.
