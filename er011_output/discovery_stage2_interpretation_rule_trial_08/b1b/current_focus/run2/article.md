# The Fridge Drawer With Two Very Different Jobs

A crisper drawer can look like a place where all fresh produce goes. But the small control on the drawer can change the air around the food. Many refrigerators offer a low-humidity setting and a high-humidity setting, sometimes through a slider.

The low-humidity drawer lets more outside air move through it. The high-humidity drawer is more closed off. That difference matters because fresh food does not all lose quality in the same way.

Some produce gives off ethylene gas as it matures. If other fruits and vegetables stay exposed to that gas, they can ripen too quickly and spoil sooner. A vented low-humidity drawer lets more of the gas escape, which can help protect nearby produce.

Other foods lose water quickly and begin to wilt. Keeping them in the high-humidity drawer helps hold moisture around them. So the real question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?” It is, “Does this food give off a lot of ethylene, or does it lose water easily?”

That is the useful shift: the two settings are not just labels on a refrigerator. They are two different ways to manage the air around fresh food. But the simple rule has limits, especially when advice for home refrigerators does not match advice for other kinds of storage.

### Sort by behavior, not by name

Use the drawer as a quick sorting tool. Apples and pears fit the vented low-humidity drawer. Leafy greens, such as kale, and broccoli fit the high-humidity drawer. Tomatoes and bananas are a separate case: one guide recommends keeping them on the counter, rather than putting them in either drawer. That small exception keeps a simple rule from becoming a bad habit.

### When the rule stops being simple

Strawberries and oranges show why one universal rule can mislead. A commercial-storage guide lists very high humidity for these foods after harvest, but that does not automatically tell you which household drawer to use. One refrigerator maker places both foods in its low-humidity fruit bin, while another places strawberries in high humidity. When guidance differs, check your refrigerator’s manual instead of forcing a category rule.

## In one line…

The right crisper setting begins with how the food behaves, not with the word fruit or vegetable. And for disputed foods, the smartest choice may be to follow the guidance made for your own refrigerator.
