# The Small Refrigerator Choice That Changes How Fresh Food Is Stored

A small slider in the refrigerator can change what happens to fresh produce.

Many refrigerators offer two crisper drawer settings: low humidity and high humidity. The difference is more than a label. A low-humidity drawer lets more air pass through. A high-humidity drawer is more tightly closed.

That creates two different storage environments.

The reason matters because fresh food does not all change in the same way. Some fruit releases ethylene gas as it ripens. If nearby produce stays around too much of this gas for too long, it may ripen too quickly and begin to spoil.

Other produce loses water quickly. It can become wilted before it is eaten.

So the crisper drawer is not simply a place for “fruit” on one side and “vegetables” on the other. It is a small system for dealing with different problems. Used with care, the two settings can help fresh produce stay in good condition for longer.

### Find the problem before choosing the setting

Use the food’s first problem as a quick guide. If it is losing water and becoming wilted, the more closed high-humidity setting is the better match. Leafy greens and broccoli fit this pattern. If ethylene is the concern, the more ventilated low-humidity setting can help the gas move away. Apples and pears are examples. This is more useful than memorizing simple fruit and vegetable labels.

### The drawer is not the whole storage plan

Before choosing low or high humidity, ask a more basic question: does the food belong in the crisper at all? Iowa State guidance recommends keeping tomatoes and bananas at room temperature. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration; they should be kept in a cool, dry place. A humidity setting cannot fix the wrong storage place.

## In one line…

The best crisper habit is a two-step check: identify what is harming the food, then make sure the crisper is the right place for it.
