# Two Refrigerator Drawers, Two Different Jobs

Open the refrigerator, and you may find a small choice hiding in the produce drawers.

One setting says low humidity. The other says high humidity. They may look almost the same, but they are not doing the same job.

The low-humidity drawer lets more air move through it. The high-humidity drawer is closer to closed. This difference matters because fresh food can have different problems while it is stored.

Some foods release a gas called ethylene as they become ripe. If other produce stays near that gas for too long, it may ripen too quickly and spoil sooner.

Other foods lose water quickly. Their leaves become soft and wilted.

So the best setting is not always decided by one simple rule: fruit in one drawer, vegetables in the other. The better question is what problem the food is most likely to have.

### Look at the food’s main risk

For food that releases a lot of ethylene, the low-humidity drawer is the better match. Apples, pears, bananas, and tomatoes are examples of foods that release more of this gas. The open airflow helps the gas move away.

For food that loses water quickly, high humidity is more useful. Kale and other leafy vegetables need help staying fresh. Broccoli is another example. Here, keeping moisture in the drawer matters more than letting air move out.

This gives you a simple sorting test: is the main risk gas, or is it water loss? That question is more useful than simply asking whether the food is a fruit or a vegetable.

### Sometimes the right drawer is no drawer

There is one more question to ask before choosing a setting: does this food need to go in the refrigerator at all?

Iowa State University Extension recommends keeping tomatoes and bananas on the counter rather than in the refrigerator. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration either, but they should be kept in a cool, dry place. That is not exactly the same as counter storage.

This is the practical boundary of the crisper system. It is not a universal produce box. Some foods should stay outside it.

## In one line…

The crisper works best when you match the drawer to the food’s real risk—and first check whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all.
