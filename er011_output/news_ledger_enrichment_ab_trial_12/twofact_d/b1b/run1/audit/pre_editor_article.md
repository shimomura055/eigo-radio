# One Big Swing, Then a Complete Win for Hanshin

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin quickly took control against the Hiroshima Carp.

The first blow came in the opening inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin moved ahead 2-0 before the game had time to settle.

That early lead needed someone to protect it. Starter Rihito Ihara did exactly that. He pitched five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run. Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

Hiroshima had an answer, but not a second one. Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The important point was not just the first lead. It was the way Hanshin kept adding to it.

By the end, the gap had become too large for Hiroshima to close. Hanshin beat Hiroshima 8-1. The game followed a clear path: an early home run created the advantage, Ihara held the middle of the game, and the offense finished the job late.

For Hanshin, this was a win built step by step. For Hiroshima, one home run was all the offense could produce. The result was a night in which Hanshin kept moving forward, while Hiroshima never found a way to build a real reply.

### Ihara made an impact in two ways

Ihara also made an impact at the plate. He singled in the third and fifth innings, going 2-for-2. It was his first multi-hit game as a professional. His contribution therefore reached beyond his work on the mound and into Hanshin’s attack.

### A milestone inside a difficult result

Montero’s swing carried a different meaning for him. It was his first time reaching double-digit home runs in a season since coming to Japan. He had finished 2025 with nine. The moment did not change Hiroshima’s result, but it marked a clear season milestone for the hitter.

## In one line…

A fast start became an 8-1 finish: Hanshin controlled the game, while Ihara and Montero each reached a personal milestone in different ways.
