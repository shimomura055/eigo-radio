# One Big Swing, Then Calm Control: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8–1

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin found the perfect way to start a game: one big swing, followed by steady work.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin was ahead 2–0 before Hiroshima could settle into the game.

That early lead had someone to protect it. Hanshin pitcher Taketo Ihara gave up only two hits and one run over five innings. Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

But one run was not enough to change the game. Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. Each new run made a comeback harder. Hiroshima could not close the gap.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

This was not a close escape. Hanshin took control early, then kept the game moving in its direction until the end.

### Ihara made his mark twice

Ihara had a second story in this game. He went 2-for-2 as a batter, with two hits in two at-bats. It was the first multi-hit game of his professional career. His night included a personal first at the plate, not only a strong performance on the mound.

### A milestone in defeat

Montero also reached a personal mark. His home run gave him 10 for the season. It was his first time reaching double figures in Japan. He had finished 2025 with nine. Hiroshima lost the game, but this was still an important step in Montero’s own story.

## In one line…

One early swing opened the door, and steady pitching and late runs carried Hanshin through it. Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin: a clear win from start to finish.
