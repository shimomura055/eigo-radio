# Hanshin Starts Fast and Finishes with a Clear Win

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin took control early.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin led 2-0, sending an early message.

But an early lead is only a beginning. Starting pitcher Rito Ihara kept Hiroshima from building a strong reply. He pitched five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

That run came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. It was Hiroshima’s only score.

Hiroshima could not score again. Hanshin added runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew, and the home team had no second answer.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

The game had a simple shape: Hanshin took control early, faced one response, and then moved away from danger. The result was a complete win, not just a good start.

### The one reply that did not grow

Hiroshima’s only scoring moment made the game 2-1, but it did not become a longer push. Ihara’s five-inning work limited the damage to two hits and one run. After Montero’s home run, Hiroshima added nothing. Hanshin passed the game’s one real test without letting it spread.

### A margin built by several contributors

The wide finish did not belong to Sato alone. In the seventh and eighth innings, Seishiro Sakamoto hit balls that brought in runs. Tora Fushimi, used as a substitute batter, also delivered one. Hanshin kept adding with different players instead of asking one early swing to carry the whole night.

## In one line

Hanshin turned an early strike into a complete victory: Hiroshima answered once, but the response stayed small, and several Hanshin players helped finish the job.
