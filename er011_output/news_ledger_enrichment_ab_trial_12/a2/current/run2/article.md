# One Early Swing, Then a Complete Hanshin Win

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin struck first before Hiroshima could settle in.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly took a 2-0 lead.

The lead also had strong support from the mound. Hanshin starter Ryohto Ihara pitched five innings, allowing only two hits and one run.

Hiroshima scored in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. But that was Hiroshima’s only run of the game.

Hanshin kept adding to its early lead, scoring more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. Hiroshima had no answer.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

It was a clear win built in two parts: an early hit opened the game, and steady play kept the advantage safe.

### One breakthrough, not a comeback

The key defensive detail was the lack of follow-up pressure. In Ihara’s five innings, Hiroshima managed only two hits and one run. Montero’s home run was a single break, not the start of a lasting attack. That is why the game never became a true comeback battle.

### More than one name on the scoresheet

Sato had the headline moment, but more than one Hanshin hitter finished the job. Seishiro Sakamoto brought in runs with hits in the seventh and eighth innings. Torai Fushimi also came in as a pinch-hitter and delivered a hit that brought in a run. This was a team hitting story, not a one-swing story.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first, kept Hiroshima quiet, and turned an early lead into an 8-1 victory with help from the whole team.
