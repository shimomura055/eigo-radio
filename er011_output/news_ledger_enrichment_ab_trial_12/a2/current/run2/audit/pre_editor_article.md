# One Early Swing, Then a Complete Hanshin Win

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin made the first move before Hiroshima could settle into the game.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly moved ahead, 2-0.

The early lead also had strong support from the mound. Hanshin starter Ryohto Ihara pitched five innings. He allowed only two hits and one run.

Hiroshima scored in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. But that was Hiroshima’s only run of the game.

Hanshin did not stop after its early start. The team added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew, and Hiroshima had no answer.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

It was a clear win built in two parts: an early hit that opened the game, followed by steady play that kept the advantage safe.

### One breakthrough, not a comeback

The bigger defensive detail was the lack of follow-up pressure. Across Ihara’s five innings, Hiroshima managed only two hits and one run. Montero’s home run was a single break, not the start of a lasting attack. That is why the game never became a true comeback battle.

### More than one name on the scoresheet

The headline moment belonged to Sato, but the finish belonged to more than one Hanshin hitter. Seishiro Sakamoto brought in runs with hits in the seventh and eighth innings. Torai Fushimi also came in as a pinch-hitter and delivered a hit that brought in a run. This was a team hitting story, not a one-swing story.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first, kept Hiroshima quiet, and turned an early lead into an 8-1 victory with help from the whole team.
