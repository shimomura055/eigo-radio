# Hanshin Turns a Fast Start into a Complete Win in Hiroshima

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, the Hanshin Tigers took control early and never gave the game back.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin led 2–0.

But the home run was only the opening scene.

Starting pitcher Rihito Ihara gave Hanshin a steady base. He worked five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. After that, the Carp could not add to their total.

Hanshin kept building, adding runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The Tigers widened the gap and did not allow Hiroshima back into the game.

By the end, the result was clear:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin.

It was a complete win, built from an early lead, steady pitching, and late scoring.

### A quiet finish from the bullpen

The key late detail was Hanshin’s relief pitching. From the sixth through the ninth innings, Kinoshita Rito, Rafael Dolis, Yu Iwasaki, and Masaki Oikawa shared the final four innings.

Together, they allowed just one hit, gave up no walks, struck out five, and allowed no runs. Their steady work left Hiroshima little room for a late comeback. The lead was protected to the final out.

### More than one big swing

The team numbers show a wider story. Hanshin had 13 hits and made no errors. Hiroshima had three hits and one error, recorded on a bad throw by shortstop Kaito Kozono in the ninth inning.

Those figures show repeated pressure at the plate and clean work in the field. The result was not explained by one swing alone. It was a strong team performance in both attack and defense.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned a fast start into a full-team victory, controlling the game with steady pitching, continued pressure, and clean execution.
