# A Fast Start, a Lone Reply, and a Firm Finish

What happens when a team starts with a big swing and then keeps the game under control? Hanshin showed it on August 16, at Mazda Stadium.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center. Hanshin moved in front 2-0 right away. But that lead was only the beginning.

Starting pitcher Rito Ihara kept the game calm. He worked five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima's only run came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. Hiroshima answered once but could not build on it.

Hanshin stayed in control, adding more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The game never became close; it kept moving in Hanshin's direction.

By the end, Hanshin had beaten Hiroshima 8-1. The early home run opened the game, but steady pitching and more runs later secured the victory.

### Control across the whole team

The team numbers show a wider difference. Hanshin had 13 hits and no errors. Hiroshima had three hits and one error. This showed that Hanshin's control reached across the lineup and the field, rather than resting on one narrow advantage.

### A quiet finish on the mound

After the starting pitcher left, four Hanshin pitchers shared the next four innings: Rito Kinoshita, Rafael Dolis, Yu Iwasaki, and Masataka Oikawa. Together, they allowed one hit, gave up no walks, struck out five, and allowed no runs. That finish kept Hiroshima from finding a way back.

## In one line…

One big swing started the story, but steady work from beginning to end made it an 8-1 Hanshin win.
