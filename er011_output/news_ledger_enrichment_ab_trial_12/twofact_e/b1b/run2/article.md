# A Two-Run Start, Then Total Control in Hiroshima

On August 16, Hanshin turned a road game into a statement win.

At Mazda Stadium, the Tigers struck first. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run, an early sign that Hanshin would not let Hiroshima control the night.

But the game was not won by one swing alone.

Starter Rito Ihara gave Hanshin a strong foundation. He worked five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run. Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

That was all the Carp could produce.

Hanshin added to its lead late, scoring in the seventh and again in the eighth to create more distance when Hiroshima needed a way back.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

It was a complete win: an early blow, steady pitching, and enough offense to finish the job.

### Sato’s home run had an extra layer

Sato’s shot came with two outs and a runner on first. With the count at one ball and one strike, he hit the third pitch, a two-seam fastball from Shohei Mori, into center field.

It was Sato’s 28th home run of the season, putting him just one behind teammate Shota Morishita, who had 29. So the opening blast was more than the first two runs of the game. It was also a clear moment in Hanshin’s home-run race.

### A win that steadied the season

This result mattered beyond one night. Hanshin ended a two-game losing streak and stayed in first place, two games ahead of Yomiuri.

Hiroshima also saw its two-game winning streak end. The Carp missed a chance to win three straight, something they had not done since the opening series.

So, for Hanshin, this was a stabilizing win. The team did not simply win by seven runs; it stopped a short slide while keeping its place at the top.

## In one line…

Hanshin opened the door with Sato’s bat, locked it with Ihara’s pitching, and finished with an 8-1 win that restored momentum.
