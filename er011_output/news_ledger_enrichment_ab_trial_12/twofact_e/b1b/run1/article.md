# Hanshin Takes Control Early and Finishes with an 8–1 Win

On August 16, the Hanshin Tigers turned a close-looking game into a one-sided victory at Mazda Stadium.

The turning point came in the first inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field, giving Hanshin an early lead and putting pressure on the Hiroshima Carp from the start.

Hanshin’s starting pitcher, Ryoto Ihara, gave the team exactly what it needed. He allowed only two hits and one run over five innings. Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

That was the only time Hiroshima scored.

Hanshin did not let the game stay close. The Tigers added more runs in the seventh and again in the eighth. With the early lead protected and the offense adding to it late, Hanshin completed an 8–1 victory.

It was a game built on two clear moments: a powerful start and steady control afterward.

### Sato’s home run had a second meaning

Sato’s opening shot was also his 28th home run of the season. That put him one home run behind teammate Shota Morishita, who had 29.

So the first big play was important beyond this game. It also tightened Hanshin’s home-run race. One swing gave the team a lead and brought Sato closer to the top of the club’s power list.

### A win that stopped the slide

The victory ended Hanshin’s two-game losing streak. The Tigers also stayed in first place, keeping a two-game lead over the Yomiuri Giants.

For Hiroshima, the result ended a two-game winning streak before it could become three. That gives the 8–1 result a wider meaning on August 16: Hanshin halted its slide without losing its position at the top, while Hiroshima missed a chance to build more momentum.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first, trusted Ihara to steady the game, and finished the job with an 8–1 victory that brought both control and renewed momentum.
