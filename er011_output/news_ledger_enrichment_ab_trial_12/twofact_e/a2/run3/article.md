# A Fast Start, Then a Firm Finish: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin gave Hiroshima very little room to breathe.

The game began with a big swing. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly took the lead, changing the shape of the game at once.

Starting pitcher Rihito Ihara gave the team a strong base. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. It cut the lead, but Hiroshima could not score again.

Hanshin kept building its advantage later in the game, scoring in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew, and Hiroshima could not come back.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

It was a complete win for Hanshin. A powerful start, steady pitching, and more runs late in the game all pointed in the same direction.

### A swing with a story of its own

Sato’s home run also marked a personal milestone. With one ball and one strike, he hit the third pitch from Shohei Mori, a two-seam fastball. It was Sato’s 28th home run, leaving him just one behind teammate Shota Morishita at that time. One swing brought both power and a place near the top of Hanshin’s home run race.

### Two streaks, one clear turn

This win changed the short-term picture for both teams. Hanshin had lost its previous two games, but that slide ended here. Hiroshima had won two in a row, but its chance for a third straight win disappeared. The result gave Hanshin relief and stopped Hiroshima’s recent run.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned one early big swing into a full-team win, while Ihara’s steady outing helped keep Hiroshima from finding a way back.
