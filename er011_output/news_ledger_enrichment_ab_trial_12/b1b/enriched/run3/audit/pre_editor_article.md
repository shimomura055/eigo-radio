# One Early Blow, Then Complete Control: Hanshin Beat Hiroshima 8-1

At Mazda Stadium on August 16, Hanshin turned one powerful swing into a comfortable victory.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly took control, and Hiroshima was forced to chase the game from the start.

Hanshin’s starting pitcher, Rihito Ihara, then kept that advantage safe. He allowed only two hits and one run over five innings.

Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. It was a brief answer, but it did not start a comeback.

Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew, and the game moved further away from Hiroshima. By the final out, Hanshin had completed an 8-1 win.

It was not just a game won by an early home run. Hanshin scored first, received a strong start from Ihara, and kept adding pressure later in the game.

### A pitcher who also hit

Ihara’s contribution was not limited to the mound. He collected hits in both the third and fifth innings, going 2-for-2. It was his first multi-hit game as a professional. That gave the victory an unusual extra layer: the starter also supported Hanshin’s offense directly.

### The finish was built on control

The late innings showed why Hiroshima could not build momentum. After Ihara left, four Hanshin relievers combined for four scoreless innings, allowing just one hit, with no walks and five strikeouts. Hanshin also finished with 13 hits and no errors, while Hiroshima had three hits and one error. The numbers point to a game controlled from start to finish.

## In one line…

Hanshin used one early home run to open the door, then closed the game with steady pitching, timely runs, and a complete team performance.
