# One Swing, Then Complete Control: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

One big swing opened the game, but it did not tell the whole story.

On August 16 at Mazda Stadium, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center in the first inning. Hanshin quickly took the lead over Hiroshima.

The Carp found one answer in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, giving Hiroshima its only run of the night. But the game did not become a back-and-forth fight.

Hanshin kept control. The Tigers added runs in the seventh and eighth innings, and the gap continued to grow. Hanshin won 8-1.

### An early lead needs protection

An opening home run can disappear if the other team answers right away. Here, Hanshin had something more useful than a quick lead: time. Starter Rito Ihara allowed only two hits and one run over five innings. That kept Hiroshima from turning the first two runs into a larger rally while Hanshin waited for more chances to score.

### The margin tells a different story

The final score also shows a clear difference in how the teams scored. In the seventh and eighth innings, Hanshin produced several run-scoring hits. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs in both innings, and pinch-hitter Tora Fushimi also delivered an RBI. Hiroshima, by contrast, had only Montero’s one solo home run. Hanshin spread its scoring across several hits, while Hiroshima had one isolated answer.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned an early two-run advantage into an 8-1 victory by keeping Hiroshima quiet and continuing to add pressure late.
