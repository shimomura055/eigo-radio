# A Fast Start That Never Let Go

Some wins are built on one big moment. Others come from making that moment last. Hanshin did both on August 16 at Mazda Stadium.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly took a 2–0 lead.

Starting pitcher Ryoto Ihara gave that lead a strong base. He worked five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, but the Carp could not turn it into a bigger attack.

Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew, and Hiroshima had no answer before the game ended.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8. It was a clear win built on an early advantage, steady pitching, and more offense later in the game.

### The shape behind the score

The final score was shaped by when the runs came, not only by the total. Hanshin opened with two runs and added more late. Hiroshima had only one-run answers. The gap did not close and reopen; it kept moving in Hanshin’s direction.

### Not perfect, but enough

Ihara did not need to throw a shutout. His line was five innings, two hits, and one run. Because that run came on a solo home run, Hiroshima never turned one successful swing into a larger rally. His job was not to erase every threat, but to stop one hit from becoming a bigger problem.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned a quick two-run lead into an 8–1 victory, while Ihara made sure Hiroshima’s only scoring moment stayed only one moment.
