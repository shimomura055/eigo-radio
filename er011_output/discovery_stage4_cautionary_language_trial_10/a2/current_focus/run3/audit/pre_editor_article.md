# The Refrigerator Slider That Helps Fresh Food Last Longer

A small slider inside the refrigerator may look unimportant.

It often has two choices: low humidity and high humidity. Some refrigerators use a slider to change between them.

The two settings work in different ways. The low-humidity drawer allows more air to move through. The high-humidity drawer is almost closed, so it holds more moisture.

Fresh food does not all behave in the same way.

As some fruit ripens, it gives off a gas called ethylene. If other produce is exposed to this gas for a long time, it may ripen too fast and spoil. The low-humidity setting helps the gas move away.

Other foods lose water quickly. Then they can wilt. The high-humidity setting helps them keep more moisture.

So the useful question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?” The better question is, “What does this food do?”

### The clear cases are about behavior

For the clear cases, think about behavior, not labels. Apples and pears fit the low-humidity drawer because they release a lot of ethylene. Leafy greens, such as kale, and broccoli fit the high-humidity drawer because they lose water quickly. Bananas and tomatoes are different: they are recommended for room-temperature storage, not as refrigerator-drawer examples.

### When the number does not settle the question

Some foods do not have one clear setting at home. A source about commercial storage after harvest gives strawberries and oranges a moisture level of 90 to 95 percent. That does not automatically mean “use high humidity” in a household drawer. GE puts both on its low-humidity fruit side, while Samsung puts strawberries on the high-humidity side. When advice conflicts, check your refrigerator’s own guide instead of trusting one number.

## In one line…

The smart use of a crisper drawer is not about memorizing “fruit” or “vegetable.” Match the setting to the food’s behavior—and be careful when household advice is not the same everywhere.
