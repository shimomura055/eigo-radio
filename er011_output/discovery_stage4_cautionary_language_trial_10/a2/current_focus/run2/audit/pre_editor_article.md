# The Refrigerator Drawer That Has Two Different Jobs

## Main Story

A small switch inside the refrigerator can change how fresh food is stored.

Many refrigerators have a crisper drawer with two choices: low humidity and high humidity. The low setting lets more air move through the drawer. The high setting is closer to closed.

This is more than a small label on a drawer. It creates two different spaces for fresh food.

At first, the rule may seem easy: put fruit in one place and vegetables in another. But that rule is too simple. The important question is not only what a food is called. It is how that food behaves after it enters the drawer.

Some produce gives off ethylene, a gas released as it ripens. If other food stays near that gas for too long, it may ripen too fast and spoil sooner. Other produce loses water quickly and starts to wilt.

So, one drawer does not have just one job. Used in the right way, its settings can help fresh food last longer.

### Sort by behavior, not by name

Now picture apples and pears beside leafy greens. Apples and pears give off more ethylene, so the air-moving, low-humidity setting helps that gas leave the drawer. Leafy greens lose water quickly, so the high-humidity setting is a better choice.

The useful idea is to separate foods that affect one another. Do not treat the crisper as one shared box. This can help reduce early ripening nearby and wilting in the greens.

### Check the refrigerator first

Before choosing a setting, ask a more basic question: should this food be in the refrigerator at all?

Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage, so they should not be used as low-humidity drawer examples. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic need a cool, dry place instead.

This two-step check matters. First choose the right home for the food. Then choose the right drawer setting.

## In one line…

The best use of a crisper drawer starts with food behavior: give each item the right setting, but first make sure the refrigerator is the right place for it.
