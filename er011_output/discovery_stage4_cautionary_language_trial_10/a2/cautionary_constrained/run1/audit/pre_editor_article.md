# The Fridge Drawer With Two Jobs

Open the refrigerator, and you may see a small slider on the produce drawer.

It can look unimportant. But this slider changes how the drawer works.

Many refrigerators have two settings: low humidity and high humidity. The low-humidity setting lets more air move through the drawer. The high-humidity setting is closer to closed.

So, the drawer is not simply for “fruit” or “vegetables.” It has two different jobs.

One setting helps a gas leave. The other helps water stay.

As fruit ripens, it gives off a gas called ethylene. If other produce stays around too much of this gas, it may ripen too fast and spoil sooner. A ventilated, low-humidity drawer helps the gas move away.

Other foods lose water quickly. Their leaves or surfaces can become soft and wilted. A more closed, high-humidity drawer helps them keep moisture.

That is why using the right setting can help produce stay fresh longer. The important question is not only what the food is called. It is what kind of problem the food has.

### Sort by what the food needs

Ask two simple questions: Does this food give off a lot of ethylene? Will it wilt quickly? Apples and pears fit the first question, so low humidity is a useful choice. Leafy greens and broccoli fit the second, so high humidity is useful. The drawer works best when it matches the food’s weak point, not its supermarket label.

### Check the refrigerator first

Before choosing a setting, ask whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage, so the low-humidity drawer is not a reason to chill them. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic need a cool, dry place. That is not exactly the same as counter storage.

## In one line…

The crisper drawer is not a fruit-versus-vegetable contest. First decide whether the food belongs in the fridge, then match the drawer to the way it loses freshness.
