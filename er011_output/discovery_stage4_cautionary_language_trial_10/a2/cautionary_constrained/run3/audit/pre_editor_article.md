# The Small Refrigerator Setting That Can Help Produce Last Longer

A small choice inside your refrigerator can make a real difference.

Many crisper drawers have two settings: low humidity and high humidity. These are not just labels. The low setting lets more air move out. The high setting keeps the drawer more closed.

When food is in the wrong setting, it may lose water and wilt. Or it may ripen too quickly. Choosing the right setting can help produce stay fresh longer.

But the best choice is not simply “fruit” or “vegetable.” Different foods face different problems. There is also one question to ask before choosing a setting:

Does this food belong in the refrigerator at all?

That is where simple drawer advice can go wrong.

### Choose the setting by the food’s main risk

Think of the two settings as two airflow choices.

If a food may lose water and wilt, high humidity is usually the better match. Leafy greens, such as kale, and vegetables such as broccoli fit this pattern.

If the bigger risk is ethylene gas, choose the more open, low-humidity setting. Apples and pears release a lot of this gas. With more airflow, the gas can move away more easily.

This test is more useful than sorting food only by its name as a fruit or a vegetable.

### The drawer is not always the right place

Before choosing a drawer setting, first ask whether the food belongs in the refrigerator.

Bananas and tomatoes are recommended for counter storage, so a low-humidity drawer is not the answer for them. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration. They are better suited to a cool, dry place.

This step matters because even the best drawer setting cannot fix the wrong storage location.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer is really a two-step choice: first find the right place, then choose the right airflow for the food’s main risk.
