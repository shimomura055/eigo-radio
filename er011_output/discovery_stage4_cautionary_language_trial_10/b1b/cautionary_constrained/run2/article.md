# The Small Refrigerator Setting That Can Help Produce Last Longer

A refrigerator produce drawer may look like one simple box. But many refrigerators give it two jobs: low humidity and high humidity.

The difference is in the air. A low-humidity drawer lets more air move through. A high-humidity drawer is more closed, so it holds more moisture around the food.

The best choice is not simply “fruit” or “vegetable.” A better question is: what problem does this food have?

Some fruit, including apples and pears, gives off ethylene gas as it matures. If other produce stays around that gas, it may ripen too quickly and spoil sooner. A low-humidity drawer lets the gas move away more easily.

Other foods lose water quickly and begin to wilt. Leafy greens, such as kale, and vegetables such as broccoli can benefit from the more closed conditions of a high-humidity drawer.

So the simple story is not about putting all fruit on one side and all vegetables on the other. It is about either letting gas escape or helping food hold on to water.

### Decide on the refrigerator first

Before choosing a humidity setting, make a more basic decision: does this food belong in the refrigerator at all?

Apples and pears are examples that can fit the low-humidity drawer. But bananas and tomatoes are not good refrigerated-drawer examples. They are recommended for counter storage. The order matters: first decide “fridge or not,” and then choose a drawer setting.

That avoids using the humidity slider to solve a problem that should instead be solved by keeping the food out of the refrigerator.

### When storage science meets a home drawer

A commercial storage guide may give strawberries or oranges an ideal humidity of 90 to 95 percent. But that number does not automatically mean “use the high-humidity setting” in a home refrigerator.

Household guidance is not always the same. One appliance maker places both strawberries and oranges in its low-humidity Fruit Bin guidance. Another places strawberries on the high-humidity side.

The lesson is not that the science is useless. It is that a commercial storage number and a household drawer label are not always the same instruction. For disputed foods, guidance for the specific appliance matters more than a universal fruit-versus-vegetable rule.

## In one line

A smart crisper habit is not to memorize one simple rule: first ask whether the food belongs in the refrigerator, then ask whether it needs air to escape or moisture to stay.
