# The Small Fridge Slider That Can Help Produce Last Longer

Some of the most useful controls in a kitchen are easy to ignore. One is the small slider on a refrigerator’s crisper drawer.

Many crisper drawers offer two settings: low humidity and high humidity. The low-humidity side has more ventilation. The high-humidity side is closer to closed.

Used well, these settings can help fresh produce stay in good condition for longer. But the simple rule of “fruit here, vegetables there” is not enough.

As fruit ripens, it releases a natural gas called ethylene. If other produce is exposed to that gas for too long, it may ripen too much and spoil sooner. A vented drawer can let some of that gas escape.

Other foods have a different problem. They lose water quickly and begin to wilt. For them, holding more moisture inside the drawer can be helpful.

So the crisper drawer is not just a box with a mysterious slider. It is a small storage system with two different jobs. But before changing the setting, there is one more question to ask: does the food belong in the refrigerator at all?

### Match the drawer to the food’s main risk

Use the food’s biggest storage problem as your test. If the main concern is ripening gas, apples and pears are examples for the low-humidity side. If the main concern is water loss, leafy greens and broccoli fit the high-humidity side.

This is more useful than memorizing a fruit-versus-vegetable list. Ask what is most likely to make the food deteriorate first, then choose the setting that matches that risk.

### Check the refrigerator first

The slider is not the first step for every food. Bananas and tomatoes are recommended for counter storage, rather than in the refrigerator’s low-humidity drawer.

Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration. They are suited to a cool, dry environment. These foods should not be casually treated as crisper-drawer items.

That means an ethylene-producing food does not automatically belong in the low-humidity drawer. First decide whether it belongs in the refrigerator. Then choose between the two settings.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer works best when you first check where the food should be stored, and then match its main risk—escaping gas or lost moisture—to the right setting.
