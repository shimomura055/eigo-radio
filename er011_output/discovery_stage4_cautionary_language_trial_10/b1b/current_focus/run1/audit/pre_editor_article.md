# The Small Refrigerator Choice That Can Help Fresh Produce Last Longer

At first glance, a crisper drawer looks like one simple box. But many refrigerators give it two different jobs: low humidity and high humidity.

The low-humidity setting has more ventilation. The high-humidity setting is closer to sealed. That small difference can change how fresh produce is stored.

Used correctly, the drawer can help food stay fresh longer. Used without thinking, it may not protect the food as well as it could.

The important point is that this is not simply a choice between fruit and vegetables. Different foods face different problems. Some may ripen too soon. Others may lose water and wilt.

### Ask what the food is losing

The drawer works better as a problem-solving tool than as a fruit-and-vegetable sorter. Ask one question: “Am I trying to let ethylene escape, or hold on to water?” Apples and pears fit the low-humidity side. Leafy greens such as kale, and broccoli, fit the high-humidity side. This gives you a simple way to match the setting to the food’s main risk.

### First decide whether it belongs in the fridge

But there is an earlier question: should the food be in the refrigerator at all? Tomatoes and bananas are not suited to refrigeration and are recommended for counter storage. So putting them in a low-humidity drawer is still the wrong choice. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic are different. They need a cool, dry place. “Not refrigerated” does not automatically mean “store on the counter.”

## In one line

The quiet lesson is that freshness begins with the right question: does this food belong in the refrigerator, and if so, does it need air to escape or moisture to stay? That small pause can help the drawer’s two settings work as intended.
