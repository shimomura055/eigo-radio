# The Small Refrigerator Slider With Two Different Jobs

## Main Story

That small slider in a refrigerator drawer may look minor. But it controls the air around fresh produce, which can affect how long food stays usable.

Many refrigerators offer two choices: low humidity and high humidity. The low-humidity setting lets more air move through the drawer. The high-humidity setting is more sealed, so it holds in more moisture.

At first, the difference may seem simple. But fresh produce can lose quality in different ways.

As some fruit ripens, it releases ethylene gas. When nearby produce is exposed to that gas, it may ripen too quickly and spoil earlier. Other foods lose water quickly and begin to wilt.

The drawer is designed to respond to these different problems. One setting gives more ventilation. The other helps reduce moisture loss. Used in the right way, the two settings can help fresh food last longer.

But there is one important detail: the choice is not simply about whether something is a fruit or a vegetable. It is about what the food needs from the air around it.

### The slider controls air, not just labels

Low humidity does not mean “make the air dry.” It mainly means more ventilation, so ethylene can escape. High humidity slows water loss instead. Think of the slider as a choice between gas buildup and wilting. Apples and pears fit the first side; leafy greens such as kale and broccoli fit the second.

### The choice comes before the drawer

Before touching the slider, first decide whether the item should be in the refrigerator at all. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration and are recommended for a cool, dry place. A drawer setting cannot fix the wrong first choice. The routine is simple: refrigerator or not, then which setting.

## In one line…

The crisper is not just a fruit-and-vegetable box. First choose the right place for the food, then choose whether it needs more ventilation or more moisture.
