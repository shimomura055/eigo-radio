# The Small Fridge Choice That Helps Produce Last Longer

A small detail in the refrigerator may deserve more attention.

Many refrigerators have a produce drawer with two settings: low humidity and high humidity. It may look like a simple slider, but the settings give fresh food different storage conditions.

The high-humidity setting keeps the drawer more closed, so food can hold on to its moisture. The low-humidity setting lets more air move through.

This matters because fresh produce can have different storage problems. Some foods lose water quickly and wilt. Some fruit releases ethylene gas as it ripens. If other produce stays near that gas, it may ripen too fast and spoil sooner.

A low-humidity drawer can help that gas move away. A high-humidity drawer can help protect food that dries out easily.

So the drawer is not simply a place for “fruit” or “vegetables.” It is a small tool for matching food with the problem most likely to damage it.

### Match the setting to the food’s problem

When food loses water quickly, choose high humidity. Leafy greens and broccoli are examples. When refrigerated food gives off a lot of ethylene, low humidity may be the better match. Apples and pears fit this group. The useful question is not “fruit or vegetable?” It is “what does this food need protection from?”

### Check the storage place first

The drawer setting is not the first decision for every food. Bananas and tomatoes can release ethylene, but some guidance recommends keeping them at room temperature, such as on the counter, rather than in the refrigerator. So not every food that makes this gas belongs in the low-humidity drawer. First choose the right place. Then choose the setting.

# The Fridge Drawer With Two Jobs

Open the refrigerator, and you may see a small slider on the produce drawer.

Other foods lose water quickly. Their leaves or surfaces can become soft and wilted. A more closed, high-humidity drawer helps them keep moisture.
