# One Swing Started It, But Hanshin Finished the Job

Some wins are built from one big moment. This Hanshin win began that way, but it did not end there.

On August 16, 2026, Hanshin played Hiroshima at Mazda Stadium.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly moved ahead, 2–0.

Starting pitcher Rikuto Ihara then gave Hanshin a strong base to work from. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

That one run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run for Hiroshima. It made the score 2–1, but Hiroshima could not score again.

Hanshin kept adding runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew, and the game moved away from Hiroshima.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

This was a complete win: an early lead, steady pitching, and more scoring late in the game.

### Hiroshima answered once, but could not build a comeback

After Sato’s home run gave Hanshin a 2–0 lead, Montero brought Hiroshima within one run. But that was Hiroshima’s only score of the night.

So, the home team changed the score, but not the shape of the game. There was no long rally and no inning with several Hiroshima runs. Hanshin’s lead was tested briefly, but it was never turned into a real comeback.

### The win belonged to more than one bat

Sato opened the game with the biggest early hit. But other Hanshin players made the final margin possible.

Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs in both the seventh and eighth innings. Pinch-hitter Taii Fushimi also delivered an RBI in the seventh. The message is clear: Sato opened the door, but several players helped Hanshin walk through it.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first with Sato’s home run, then used steady pitching and shared hitting to turn an early advantage into an 8–1 win.
