# Hanshin Builds an 8-1 Win One Important Moment at a Time

## Main Story

One swing gave Hanshin a fast start. Then came the harder part: keeping the lead after Hiroshima answered.

On August 16, 2026, Hanshin played Hiroshima at Mazda Stadium. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly led 2-0.

Hiroshima answered in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, making the score 2-1. The game was close again.

Hanshin’s starting pitcher, Rihito Ihara, helped keep the game under control. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs, and pinch-hitter Torai Fushimi also delivered an RBI hit.

The lead grew, and Hiroshima could not close the gap. Hanshin won 8-1.

The game began with a big play, but ended with several smaller pieces working together.

### A win built in two different ways

Hanshin scored in two different ways. Sato’s two-run homer created the opening. After Montero brought Hiroshima closer, Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Fushimi delivered timely hits in the later innings. That contrast matters: Hanshin did not need to repeat its first-inning power play. It found another way to widen the gap.

### What the one run really means

The 8-1 score may look easy, but Hiroshima did score. Montero’s solo home run was its only run. Ihara allowed just two hits and one run in five innings. The useful detail is that Hanshin did not erase every scoring moment. It kept one moment from becoming a bigger attack.

## In one line…

Hanshin opened with power, added timely hits, and kept Hiroshima from building on its one run.
