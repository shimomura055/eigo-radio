# A Fast Start, Then a Careful Finish: Hanshin Beat Hiroshima 8–1

## Main Story

On August 16 at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin beat Hiroshima 8–1. The game began with one big swing, but its shape came from what followed.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center. Hanshin took control early.

The next question was simple: could the team protect that lead?

Starter Rihito Ihara gave Hanshin the answer. He pitched five innings, allowing only two hits and one run. His work kept Hiroshima from making the game close.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, cutting the score to 2–1 for a moment.

But Hanshin stayed on course. The team added runs in the seventh and eighth innings, and the lead grew again. This was not a game decided by home runs alone. It was a win built on an early advantage, steady pitching, and timely hits late in the game.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

### More than one bat finished the job

Sato created the early headline, but several players drove Hanshin’s later scoring. Seishiro Sakamoto delivered an RBI hit in the seventh and an RBI double in the eighth. Pinch-hitter Torai Fushimi also added an RBI hit in the seventh.

That gives the final score a useful meaning. Hanshin did not depend on one more huge swing. Different players made the needed plays, so the margin grew step by step.

### One home run, but no lasting chase

Montero’s home run briefly made the game 2–1, so Hiroshima did create a moment of pressure. But that was the home team’s only run of the night.

The difference matters when reading the score. Hiroshima changed the game once, but could not continue that threat. The result was a tense moment inside a game Hanshin controlled overall.

## In one line…

Hanshin opened the game with power, then won it through steady pitching and several timely contributions. It was an 8–1 victory shaped by control, not just one spectacular swing.
