# From an Early Home Run to a Complete Win

## Main Story

Some games begin with one big hit. Others are won by building an advantage step by step. Hanshin’s 8–1 win over Hiroshima on August 16 had both: a fast start and a steady finish.

At Mazda Stadium, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field in the first inning. Hanshin led 2–0 before the game settled. That early lead gave starting pitcher Rihito Ihara room to work.

Ihara made good use of it. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run. Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. The shot made the game closer, but Hanshin kept its advantage.

Hanshin added runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew again, and Hiroshima could not answer.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

This win began with power and continued through steady pitching and a growing lead.

### Power first, timely hitting later

Hanshin’s offense changed as the game moved on. Sato created the first advantage with a home run. Later, Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Taii Fushimi added runs with timely hits. The final margin came from more than one kind of attack.

Hanshin did not depend on another big swing. It turned its early advantage into a wider lead through different kinds of hits.

### One answer, not a lasting rally

Montero’s home run changed the score, but it did not lead to a continuing wave of offense. Hiroshima finished with only one run, while Ihara allowed two hits and one run in his five innings.

The fifth-inning shot made the game closer without creating sustained pressure. It was a response, but not the beginning of a larger comeback.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned an early lead into an 8–1 road win, while Hiroshima’s single reply remained just that: a single reply.
