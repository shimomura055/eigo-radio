# Japan’s Young Travelers Want a Slower Trip—But the Calendar Has Not Changed Yet

As of September 2026, young travelers in Japan are sending two messages at once.

They want more control over their travel time. But the trips they plan are still short.

Several independent surveys point in this direction. Young men show interest in solo travel and trips built around their hobbies. In a government survey, about nine in ten Japanese people aged 19 to 25 said they wanted free time during an overseas tour. About eight in ten of them wanted at least half a day.

But this is where the story becomes interesting.

The wish is moving faster than the length of the trip. People planning autumn travel still reported short stays. Japan’s Tourism White Paper also says that making stays longer is still an important task.

So the evidence does not show a complete move from famous sightseeing to slow travel. It shows an early change in what people want, while travel plans remain short.

### The trip is becoming more personal

Young men under 30 are not simply leaving sightseeing behind. JTBF’s 2025 survey found that 25.2% chose solo trips and 24.3% chose hobby-focused trips. A separate survey also put solo travel first for men aged 18 to 29. Young women still showed strong interest in famous destinations, at 44.7%. The key change may be who controls the day, not whether landmarks disappear.

### A wish for one week, a plan for two nights

Put two survey questions side by side, and the gap becomes clear. If people could take a full month off, the most common wish was a trip of about one week, at 24.1%. Yet autumn travel planners expected only 1.8 nights on average. The middle answer was 2.0 nights. The next question is practical: can an ideal wish become extra nights in real plans?

## In one line…

The direction points toward more personal and slower travel, especially among young men—but the evidence shows an early change in preference, not a completed change in stay length.
