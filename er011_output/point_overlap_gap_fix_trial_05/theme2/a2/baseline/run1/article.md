# Young Travelers Want Slower Trips—but Their Stays Are Still Short

As of September 2026, a new wish is becoming clearer among young people in Japan, especially young men.

They want travel to move at their own speed.

But there is a surprising gap. The wish for a slower trip is growing, while actual stays are still short.

One survey asked people to imagine having one month off. The most common answer was a trip of about one week, chosen by about 24% of people.

That sounds like a long break from normal life. But a separate survey looked at people planning an autumn trip. Their average stay was about two nights, and the middle answer was also two nights.

So the picture is not “young people have already stopped sightseeing and started taking long trips.”

It is more interesting than that.

Their idea of a good trip may be changing before their travel plans do. Japan’s Tourism White Paper also lists longer stays as an important policy goal. In other words, the official picture says this change is still unfinished.

### Slow travel can begin with control

A slow trip does not always need many nights. It can also mean having private time, space for a hobby, or freedom from a fixed schedule. About 90% of Japanese Gen Z respondents said they wanted free time during an overseas tour, and most wanted at least half a day. For travel businesses, this suggests that flexible plans may matter even before stays become longer.

### One generation, different travel maps

The change is also stronger in some groups than in others. Among men under 29, solo travel was chosen by about 25%, and hobby-focused travel showed a similar level. But among women under 29, about 45% still showed interest in visiting famous tourist spots. Young travelers are not one market moving in exactly the same direction.

## In one line…

Japan’s young travelers may be moving toward more personal, slower travel—but the strongest evidence so far is a change in what they want, not yet a clear change in how long they stay.
