# Japan’s Young Travelers Want More Freedom, but Their Trips Are Still Short

As of September 2026, a new travel mood is becoming easier to see in Japan.

Many young people, especially young men, seem to want trips with less pressure and more personal time. But there is a surprising gap between that wish and the length of their actual trips.

Among men aged 29 and under, solo travel was one of the most popular choices, at about 25%. Travel that helps people explore a hobby more deeply was almost as popular. In another survey of Japanese Gen Z respondents aged 19 to 25, about 90% said they wanted free time during an overseas tour, and about 80% of them wanted at least half a day.

Together, these results point to a stronger wish to travel at one’s own pace. The full schedule is losing some of its appeal.

But this does not automatically mean that people are taking long vacations. When asked what they would do if they could take one month off, the most common answer was a trip of about one week, chosen by about 24%.

In a separate autumn survey of people planning a trip, stays averaged about two nights. The median was also two nights.

Japan’s Tourism White Paper also lists longer stays as a policy challenge. So the country has not fully moved from sightseeing tours to slow, long stays. The clearer story is this: the desire for a more personal rhythm is growing, while measured trip lengths remain short.

### Slow travel can be about the schedule, not the calendar

A slower trip does not always require a week in one place. It can mean leaving part of the day open, choosing one hobby, or skipping activities that do not matter to you. This helps explain how a two-night trip can still feel more personal. A preference for more personal, flexible itineraries may coexist with a short number of nights.

### Young travelers are not moving in one direction

The shift is especially visible among young men. For men aged 18 to 29, solo travel was the top companionship style in another survey. Young women showed a different pattern: about 45% were interested in visiting famous tourist sites, while about half favored gourmet travel. This is not one youth revolution. It is an uneven change, with different travel styles growing side by side.

## In one line…

Japan’s young travelers are showing stronger interest in freedom and personal pace, but the evidence points to an early, uneven shift—not yet a complete move to long stays.
