# The Refrigerator Drawer With Two Different Jobs

At first glance, a crisper drawer looks like a simple box for fresh food. But many refrigerators have two settings: low humidity and high humidity. Some models use a slider to adjust them.

That small choice changes the air inside the drawer. The low-humidity setting allows more air to move through. The high-humidity setting is closer to sealed.

Used well, these settings can help fresh produce keep its quality longer. But the right choice is not simply “fruit here, vegetables there.”

Fresh food can lose quality in different ways. Some foods release a gas as they ripen. That gas can make nearby produce ripen too quickly. Other foods lose water quickly and begin to wilt.

So the useful question is not only, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?”

It is also, “What problem am I trying to prevent?”

### Sort by the risk, not by the food group

A practical way to choose the setting is to look at the food’s main risk.

Apples and pears are good low-humidity examples. They release more ripening gas, and extra airflow can help move it away.

Leafy greens, such as kale, and vegetables that lose water easily, such as broccoli, fit the high-humidity side better. Keeping more moisture around them can help slow wilting.

This gives you a simple rule for foods you may not know well: think about whether you are trying to limit early ripening or prevent moisture loss. That is more useful than memorizing a long list of fruits and vegetables.

### The drawer is not the first decision

There is one important boundary. Choosing low or high humidity only matters after you decide that the food belongs in the refrigerator.

Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage rather than refrigeration. So they should not go in the low-humidity drawer simply because they release ripening gas.

Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration. They are recommended for a cool, dry environment. That is not exactly the same as keeping them on the counter with tomatoes and bananas.

The larger lesson is simple: first choose the right storage place, then choose the right drawer setting.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer works best when you match both the food and its main risk to the right home: choose the storage place first, then choose the humidity.
