リスニング教材)です。5件から作れる全てのペア(10組)について、
それぞれ以下4つの観点で意味重複を判定してください(それぞれPASS=重複なし/
FAIL=重複あり)。

- meaning_overlap: 2つのkey_phraseが表す意味内容が、実質的に同じか(例:
  「注意を引く」と「注意の一部を奪う」は表現は違っても同じ「注意を奪われる」
  という概念を指しており、meaning_overlap=FAILとすべきです)
- usage_context_overlap: 2つの表現が使われる典型的な場面・文脈が、実質的に
  同じか
- grammatical_teaching_value_overlap: 2つの表現が学習者に教える文法・構文上の
  価値(例: 句動詞の型、比喩の型、コロケーションの型)が、実質的に同じか
- conceptual_role_overlap: 記事の中でこの2つの表現が担っている概念・役割が、
  実質的に同じか

is_near_duplicateは、上記4観点のうち2つ以上がFAILの場合、または1つでも
明確にFAILで「単なる言い換え、または同じ内容を別の比喩・表現で表しただけ」
と判断できる場合にtrueにしてください。reasoningには判定理由を1〜2文の
日本語で書いてください。

単に同じ記事・同じ話題について書かれているために表面的な語彙(記事固有の
固有名詞やトピック語)が共通することは、重複とはみなさないでください。
判定対象はあくまで、Key Phraseそのものが教える意味・用法・学習価値です。

pairsには、必ず10組全ての組み合わせを含めてください(rank_a < rank_b)。

# The Small Refrigerator Slider with a Big Job

A small slider inside the refrigerator may be easy to ignore.

But many crisper drawers have two settings: low humidity and high humidity. They are not simply two names for the same space.

The low setting lets more air move through the drawer. The high setting is more closed.

That small difference can matter after fresh food comes home from the store.

Some foods give off a gas as they ripen. Other foods lose water quickly and become soft or limp. The two drawer settings are meant for these different problems.

There is also one important surprise. The best choice is not always as simple as “fruit goes here” and “vegetables go there.”

And before choosing a setting, there is another question: does this food belong in the refrigerator at all?

### Ask what the food is trying to lose

A useful way to use the slider is to ask one question: “Am I trying to keep water in, or let a gas out?”

If the main problem is wilting, high humidity is the better match. Leafy greens, such as kale, and broccoli can lose water quickly, so a more closed drawer can help.

If the main problem is gas, low humidity may be the better match. Apples and pears give off ethylene, a gas that can make nearby produce ripen too quickly. More air movement helps that gas escape.

This turns the slider into a simple diagnosis tool. You do not have to memorize a fruit-and-vegetable rule. You can look at the food’s main storage problem.

### The drawer is not always the first step

Humidity settings cannot help food that is not suited to refrigerator storage.

Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage, not for a low-humidity drawer. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration and are better kept in a cool, dry place.

So even the low-humidity advice for apples and pears should not be copied to every food that gives off ethylene. First choose the right storage place. Then choose the setting.

## In one line…

The crisper drawer works best when you match the setting to the food’s problem—and remember that some foods should not go in the refrigerator at all.
