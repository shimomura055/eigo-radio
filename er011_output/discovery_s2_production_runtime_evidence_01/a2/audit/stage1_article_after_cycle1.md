# Why Do We Wake Up Just Before the Alarm?

The alarm has not rung. Then your eyes open. You check the time, and it is close to the time you were supposed to wake up.

This small morning surprise may be linked to the body’s own daily clock. Light helps set this clock. Regular times for sleeping and waking can also help keep it close to a 24-hour day.

In one laboratory study, healthy adults could follow a 24-hour schedule even under very weak light, when their rest and activity times were kept regular. The same setup usually did not make them follow a shorter 23.5-hour day or a longer 24.6-hour day.

Researchers also study a related event called self-awakening. This means waking near a planned time without an outside signal. A review found 17 studies with 715 people. But the studies were not all alike. Many counted waking within 30 minutes of the planned time as a success.

That makes the morning puzzle harder, not easier. Some experiments found changes in the body before people woke. But most participants knew the target time and were asked to wake then. So these studies show that people can sometimes prepare for a planned wake-up. They do not show that the body can read the exact time on an alarm clock.

A 2025 home study of 201 healthy adults adds another piece. On average, a body chemical called cortisol began rising about three hours before waking and reached its highest level about half an hour after waking. But the study found no clear faster rise during the final hour before waking. The people also differed greatly from one another.

So what happens when you wake just before the alarm? Your daily clock and your usual sleep schedule may help set the stage. The body may also begin preparing to wake before your eyes open. But research still describes this as a possible preparation, not a perfect inner alarm.
