# The Small Refrigerator Setting That Helps Produce Stay Fresh

That small slider in the refrigerator can look like a label nobody needs to read. But the crisper drawer has two different storage spaces.

One setting lets more air move through the drawer. The other keeps it more closed and humid.

That difference matters because fresh produce does not all react the same way. Some foods lose water quickly and wilt. Others release a gas as they ripen. If the setting does not match the food, it may lose quality sooner. The right setting can help it stay fresh longer.

So the useful question is not simply, “Is it a fruit or a vegetable?”

A better question is: what is the food doing inside the refrigerator? Is it releasing a gas that may affect nearby produce? Or is it losing water and drying out?

### Look beyond the produce aisle

Ask two questions: Does this food release a lot of ethylene, or does it lose water and wilt quickly? Apples, pears, bananas, and tomatoes generally fit the ventilated, low-humidity drawer. Leafy greens and broccoli generally fit the high-humidity drawer. Strawberries and citrus remind us that some fruits prefer high humidity.

This helps you think about unfamiliar produce. The food’s behavior matters more than whether it is a fruit or a vegetable.

### Some produce belongs somewhere else

Before choosing a humidity setting, ask whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration; they are recommended for a cool, dry place.

The practical lesson is simple: choose the right storage location before choosing the drawer setting.

## In one line…

The crisper drawer works best when you match it to how food releases gas or loses water—not simply to whether it is a fruit or a vegetable. And sometimes, the best setting is no drawer at all.
