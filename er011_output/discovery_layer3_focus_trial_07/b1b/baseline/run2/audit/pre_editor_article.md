# The Two-Setting Secret Inside Your Crisper Drawer

The next time you open your refrigerator, look at the crisper drawer. That small slider can change how long fresh food stays in good condition.

Many crisper drawers have two settings: low humidity and high humidity. A low-humidity setting allows more air to move through the drawer. This can help ethylene gas escape.

Some fruits release a lot of ethylene as they mature. Apples, pears, bananas, and tomatoes are examples. If other produce stays near this gas for too long, it may ripen too quickly and spoil. A more open drawer can help reduce that buildup around nearby food.

A high-humidity setting is more enclosed. It helps keep moisture around foods that lose water quickly. Leafy greens, such as kale, and vegetables such as broccoli can wilt as they dry out.

So the best question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?” Instead, ask what the food is likely to do in storage. Will it release gas that affects nearby food? Or will it lose water and wilt? That is the basic guide for choosing the setting.

### Fruit does not always mean low humidity

Here is the surprising part: fruit does not automatically belong in the low-humidity drawer. Strawberries and citrus fruits, including oranges, can favor high humidity. UC Davis lists an ideal humidity of 90 to 95 percent for both strawberries and oranges.

That means a simple “fruit goes low” rule can be misleading. Before choosing a setting, ask whether the food quickly loses water and needs help staying moist.

### The drawer is not the first decision

There is an even earlier question: does this food belong in the refrigerator at all?

Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage, rather than either crisper setting. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration. They are better kept in a cool, dry environment.

So the humidity slider is not a universal answer. It becomes useful only after you know that the food belongs in the refrigerator.

## In one line

The crisper drawer is less about fruit versus vegetables than about gas, moisture, and the food’s proper storage place. Read the food’s needs before moving the slider.
