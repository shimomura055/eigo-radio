# The Small Refrigerator Setting That Helps Produce Last Longer

At first glance, the crisper drawer may look like just another box in the refrigerator. But many refrigerators offer two settings: low humidity and high humidity.

The difference is more than a small label. A low-humidity drawer lets in more outside air. A high-humidity drawer is more sealed. These settings create different conditions for fresh produce.

That matters because food does not all spoil in the same way. Some fruits release ethylene gas as they mature. If other fruits or vegetables are exposed to that gas for too long, they may become overripe and spoil sooner.

For foods that release a lot of ethylene, such as apples and pears, the low-humidity setting can let the gas escape. This may help nearby produce last longer.

Other foods lose water quickly and become wilted. Leafy vegetables and broccoli are better suited to a high-humidity setting, where the drawer helps them retain moisture.

So the main question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?” A better question is, “Does this food release a lot of ethylene, or does it lose water quickly?”

### The fruit rule has important exceptions

The fruit rule breaks first. Strawberries and oranges are fruits, but they can favor high humidity. A food guide lists an ideal range of 90–95% for both. So “fruit” is not enough information. Ask instead whether the food loses water quickly. That can help prevent moisture-loving produce from drying out in a ventilated drawer.

### Check the refrigerator question first

Before choosing a humidity setting, decide whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic need a cool, dry place and do not need refrigeration. These foods should not all be treated as if they belong in the same place.

## In one line…

The crisper drawer is not a simple fruit-versus-vegetable test. Match the setting to the food’s main problem—ethylene or water loss—and first check whether the food should be refrigerated at all.
