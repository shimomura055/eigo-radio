# The Small Refrigerator Setting That Can Keep Produce Fresh Longer

A produce drawer can look like a simple box. But in many refrigerators, it offers a small choice with a real effect: low humidity or high humidity.

The low-humidity setting is more ventilated. The high-humidity setting is more closed. That difference creates two different storage routes for fresh produce.

The reason is simple. As fruit ripens, it gives off a gas called ethylene. If other fruits and vegetables stay around that gas, they may ripen too quickly and spoil. A ventilated drawer helps the gas escape.

Other foods have a different problem. They lose water quickly and begin to wilt. A more closed, high-humidity drawer helps them hold on to moisture.

So the important question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?” The better question is, “What is most likely to damage this food?” That is the key to using the drawer correctly.

### Read the setting as a spoilage test

Start with the food’s main weakness. If it loses water quickly, use the high-humidity drawer. Leafy greens and broccoli fit this pattern. If it releases a lot of ethylene, use the ventilated low-humidity drawer, where the gas can escape more easily. Apples and pears are examples.

This gives the drawer a practical purpose. It is not only storing food. It is responding to the reason that food may spoil.

### Some produce breaks the simple rule

Fruit does not always mean low humidity. Strawberries and citrus fruits, such as oranges, prefer a high-humidity environment.

Some produce should not be placed in a crisper drawer at all. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic need a cool, dry place instead. That is not exactly the same as keeping tomatoes and bananas on the counter.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer is not simply a fruit drawer or a vegetable drawer. It is a choice between letting ethylene escape and helping food keep its water—with some produce needing a different storage place altogether.
