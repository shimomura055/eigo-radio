# The Tiny Fridge Slider With a Big Job

Open the refrigerator, and the produce drawer may look like a simple box. But a small slider can give it two different jobs.

Many drawers have low- and high-humidity settings. Humidity means the amount of moisture in the air. At low humidity, a vent lets more air move through. At high humidity, the vent is almost closed, so more moisture stays inside.

That small change matters because fresh food keeps changing after we bring it home.

As some fruit ripens, it releases a natural gas called ethylene. If other fruits or vegetables stay near this gas for a long time, they may ripen too quickly and spoil sooner.

Water matters too. Some fresh foods lose water quickly and begin to wilt. Keeping more moisture inside the drawer can help with that problem.

So the slider is not just a label. It changes the air around the food. The important question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?” The food’s own needs matter more.

### The food’s behavior is the clue

Ask what the food needs. For foods that do go in the drawer, apples and pears release a lot of ethylene, so the vented, low-humidity setting generally fits. Leafy greens and broccoli lose water quickly, so high humidity helps. Strawberries and oranges are the surprise: they are fruits that favor about 90–95% humidity.

### Some foods need a different place

There is a boundary to the slider advice. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration; they are best kept in a cool, dry place. For these foods, the first step is not choosing low or high humidity. It is choosing the right place outside the crisper.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer is a small air-control tool, not a universal storage box. Match the setting to the food’s behavior, and remember that some foods should stay out of the drawer.
