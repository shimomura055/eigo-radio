# The Small Fridge Slider With a Big Job

Open a refrigerator, and you may see a small slider inside a fresh-food drawer. It may say “low humidity” or “high humidity.” Many people leave it alone. But this slider is not just a label. It changes how air moves around the food.

On the low setting, more air moves through the drawer. On the high setting, the drawer is more closed, so moisture stays around the food.

This matters because fresh food can face different problems. Some fruit gives off ethylene, a natural gas, as it ripens. If other food stays around that gas too long, it may ripen too quickly and spoil. Other foods lose water quickly and become limp.

So, the drawer is not simply for fruit on one side and vegetables on the other. Its two settings are designed for two different food problems.

### Read the label as a clue about air

Use the setting as a decision, not decoration. Low humidity is the vent: it lets ethylene escape. High humidity is the cover: it holds moisture in. Ask which risk is stronger for the food in front of you. Apples and pears fit the first pattern. Leafy greens and broccoli fit the second. This check is more useful than sorting everything as fruit or vegetables.

### The fruit rule has important exceptions

A simple fruit rule can still lead you wrong. Strawberries and oranges are fruits, but they favor high humidity, around 90 to 95 percent. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic need a cool, dry place. They should not all be treated like counter produce.

## In one line

The small fridge slider is really a choice between letting gas out and holding moisture in. Choose by what the food needs, not only by whether it is a fruit or a vegetable.
