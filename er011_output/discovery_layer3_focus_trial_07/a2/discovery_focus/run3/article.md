# The Small Refrigerator Slider That Can Help Fresh Food Last Longer

Inside many refrigerators, a small slider may look unimportant.

But the crisper drawer often has two choices: low humidity and high humidity. These labels affect how air and moisture move around the food.

The low-humidity setting lets more air through. The high-humidity setting is more closed, so more moisture stays near the food.

That difference matters because fresh food does not all change in the same way. Some food loses water and wilts. Other food gives off a natural gas as it ripens. The right setting can give each kind of food a better place to stay fresh.

So the real question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?” It is, “What does this food need most?”

### Ask what the food is likely to do

A simple sorting rule is: Is the food losing water quickly, or is it releasing a natural gas called ethylene that may make nearby food ripen too fast?

Apples, pears, bananas, and tomatoes fit the second pattern. Leafy greens and broccoli fit the first. Strawberries and oranges are important exceptions. Although they are fruits, they favor high humidity, around 90 to 95 percent.

This question is more useful than putting every fruit or vegetable in the same setting.

### Not every item belongs in a drawer

The crisper drawer is not the whole storage map. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage rather than refrigeration.

Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic are different. They do not need refrigeration and are recommended for a cool, dry place.

So “not refrigerated” does not always mean “leave it on the kitchen counter.” Before changing a drawer setting, first check whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer can help when you match the food’s main risk—not just its name—to the setting, and first check whether it belongs in the refrigerator.
