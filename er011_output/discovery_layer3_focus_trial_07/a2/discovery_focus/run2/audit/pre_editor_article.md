# The Refrigerator Drawer With Two Very Different Jobs

Inside many refrigerators, a small drawer has a choice to make. It may offer two settings: low humidity and high humidity.

Low humidity allows more air to move through. High humidity keeps the drawer more closed.

Fresh food does not all change in the same way. Some fruits give off ethylene gas as they ripen. If other produce stays around that gas, it may ripen too fast and spoil sooner.

Other produce may wilt as it loses water. A more humid drawer can help with that problem.

So, the drawer is not simply for “fruit” on one side and “vegetables” on the other. The same drawer can help different foods in different ways. Used well, it can help fresh produce stay useful for longer.

### Ask what the food may lose first

Here is a simple way to use the setting: ask what the food is likely to lose first.

If nearby ethylene gas is the problem, the low-humidity, more open drawer can let the gas escape. If the food may lose water and wilt, the high-humidity, more closed drawer can hold moisture.

This gives the setting a reason, not just a name.

### The produce aisle can give the wrong hint

Strawberries and oranges are fruits, but they can prefer high humidity.

Tomatoes and bananas are different. They should stay on the counter, not in a crisper. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic need a cool, dry place.

The better question is not, “What aisle was this from?” It is, “Where does this food keep best?”

## In one line…

The crisper drawer is less about fruit versus vegetables and more about matching food with its first storage need. Sometimes, the right place is no drawer at all.
