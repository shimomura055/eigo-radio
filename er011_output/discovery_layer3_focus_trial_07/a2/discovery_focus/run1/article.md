# The Small Refrigerator Slider That Changes How Fresh Food Is Kept

Open the refrigerator, and one small slider may look unimportant. But it changes how the crisper drawer handles fresh food.

Many refrigerators offer two choices: low humidity and high humidity. They are not just two labels for the same space. They are two ways to store produce, and the better setting can help food stay fresh longer.

That sounds like an easy rule: put fruit one way and vegetables the other. But that shortcut can lead you astray. The key choice depends on the kind of food, not just its name.

A crisper drawer is more than a simple box. It gives different foods different conditions. The question is not just, “Is this a fruit?” or “Is this a vegetable?”

It is, “What does this food need while it waits in the refrigerator?”

That change in thinking can help you use the drawer with more care, instead of moving the slider without knowing what it does.

### Read the slider as a two-problem tool

The useful way to read the slider is to look for two different problems. Apples and pears release a natural gas called ethylene. Low humidity gives that gas a way out. Leafy greens and broccoli lose water quickly, so high humidity helps them keep moisture. For unfamiliar food, ask: what does it release or lose first?

### Some foods break the usual rule

Here is where the simple shortcut breaks. Strawberries and oranges are fruits, but they favor high humidity. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage, not the drawer. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration and are best kept in a cool, dry place.

## In one line…

The crisper drawer is a small tool for two problems: what food gives off and what it loses. Choose the setting for the food, and remember that some produce belongs somewhere else.
