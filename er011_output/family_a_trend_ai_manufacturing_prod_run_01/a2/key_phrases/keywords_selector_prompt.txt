# The AI Factory Story Has Two Different Clocks

## Main story

As of September 2026, the factory AI story has two clocks.

Investment plans are moving fast. But broad use inside factories is moving more slowly.

Worldwide, factories installed 542,000 industrial robots in 2024. That was more than twice the number from ten years earlier. The robotics industry says AI and machines that can do more by themselves are the top robotics trend for 2026.

Business plans point in the same direction. In a survey of manufacturing leaders, 80 percent said they plan to put at least 20 percent of their improvement budget into software, sensors, and other tools for smarter factories.

That sounds like a quick move toward AI-run factories. But plans are not working systems. Across available reports, newer machines are still in limited use in real production.

There is another part of the story. Some manufacturing growth linked to AI is happening around factories, not inside their production tasks.

So this is not yet a story about a finished AI factory. It is a story about a strong push toward one, while different parts of manufacturing change at different speeds.

### Where the money is really going

One important split is hidden in the investment story. One report identifies more than $500 billion in private commitments for U.S. semiconductor manufacturing. It also says that some makers of power equipment for data centers have orders for several years. Those orders support manufacturing in some areas. This is manufacturing for AI, not necessarily AI in manufacturing.

### The first real test is the daily job

To see real progress, watch specific jobs, not big robot shows. Preliminary U.S. figures show food-industry installations up 30% in 2025, while car making, the largest sector, fell 1%. Another survey found current use of more independent robots at 9%, with 22% planned within two years. In China, human-shaped robots remain mainly in demos and pilot projects. The clearest sign is a repeatable job in a real plant.

## In one line…

AI is pulling more investment into manufacturing, but the evidence is still stronger for a growing push than for a broadly AI-run factory.
