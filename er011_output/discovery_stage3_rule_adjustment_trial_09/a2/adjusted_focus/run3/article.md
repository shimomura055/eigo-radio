# One Refrigerator Drawer, Two Very Different Jobs

Most refrigerator crisper drawers look like simple boxes. But many have two settings: low humidity and high humidity.

That small choice can change how fresh food keeps.

With low humidity, the drawer lets more air move through. With high humidity, it stays more closed.

At first, this may sound like a small detail. But it changes what happens inside the drawer.

As fruit ripens, it releases ethylene gas. Nearby produce can ripen too fast if exposed to it. Some foods also lose water quickly and wilt.

So the drawer is not simply a fruit box or a vegetable box. The setting should match the food’s behavior.

That leads to a useful kitchen rule: first ask whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all. Then choose the drawer that fits what the food needs.

Used correctly, these two settings can help produce stay fresh longer.

### Sort before you slide

Before choosing a drawer, ask a simpler question: should this food be chilled? Bananas and tomatoes are recommended for room-temperature storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic need a cool, dry place, not necessarily the same counter. For produce that does belong in the refrigerator, apples and pears fit the low-humidity side. Leafy greens and broccoli fit the high-humidity side. This two-step check keeps the drawer rule from being used on the wrong foods.

### A storage number needs context

One careful detail can prevent confusion. Commercial storage guides list 90 to 95 percent humidity as the best range for strawberries and oranges. But that number does not automatically tell you which setting to use in a home refrigerator. Appliance advice differs: one maker puts both on its low-humidity fruit side, while another puts strawberries on the high-humidity side. For these foods, the guidance for your refrigerator matters.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer is not a simple fruit-versus-vegetable box. First decide whether food belongs in the refrigerator, then use the food’s behavior—and, for some foods, your appliance’s own guidance—to choose the setting.
