# The Tiny Refrigerator Slider With Two Different Jobs

Inside many refrigerators, the produce drawer often has two choices: low humidity and high humidity. Some models use a small slider to make this choice.

At low humidity, more air can move through the drawer. At high humidity, the drawer is more closed, so it holds more moisture.

Why does that matter?

Fresh produce can go bad in different ways. Some fruits release a natural ripening gas called ethylene. If other produce stays around this gas for a long time, it may ripen too fast. Other foods lose water quickly and begin to wilt.

So the two settings are not just different labels. They create different conditions inside the same drawer. The goal is to match the condition to the problem the food is facing.

### Choose by the food’s first problem

The better question is: what problem comes first? Food that quickly loses water and wilts may suit high humidity; leafy greens such as kale and broccoli are examples. Food that releases a lot of ripening gas may suit low humidity; apples and pears fit this example. The choice follows food behavior, not food names.

### Some foods should stay out of the drawer

But there is one step before choosing a setting: decide whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all. Bananas and tomatoes are recommended for room-temperature storage, such as on a counter, rather than in a crisper. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic are recommended for a cool, dry place. So low humidity is not a destination for every food that releases gas.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer is not simply a fruit-and-vegetable box. It is a small storage choice: protect food from extra gas, protect it from water loss, or keep it out of the refrigerator when that is the better fit.
