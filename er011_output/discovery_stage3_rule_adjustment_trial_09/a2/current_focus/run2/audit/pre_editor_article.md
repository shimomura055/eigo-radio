# The Two-Way Secret of Your Crisper Drawer

At first glance, the crisper drawer looks like a simple box. But many refrigerators give it two choices: low humidity and high humidity.

Used the right way, these settings can help fresh food stay in good condition longer. The important point is that one setting does not fit every food.

The low setting lets more air move through the drawer. The high setting helps keep moisture inside.

So, the best choice is not decided by one easy rule, such as “fruit goes here” or “vegetables go there.”

Leafy greens and broccoli can lose water and wilt quickly. They may do better in a high-humidity drawer. Apples and pears give off ethylene, a gas that can make nearby produce ripen too fast. A low-humidity drawer lets more of that gas move away.

The drawer is not just a place to put food. It is a small control for air and moisture.

### Check the fridge before the slider

Before choosing low or high, ask a more basic question: should this food be in the refrigerator? Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration and are better in a cool, dry place. Low humidity is not a reason to refrigerate everything.

### When a precise number is not a simple answer

Some guides for storing produce after harvest give strawberries and oranges an ideal humidity of 90 to 95 percent. But that number is not a direct instruction for a home drawer. GE puts both foods on its low-humidity fruit side, while Samsung puts strawberries on the high side. For these foods, follow your refrigerator maker’s guidance.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer works best when you match the setting to the food’s real need, not simply its name. First check whether the food belongs in the refrigerator, then choose the setting that fits.
