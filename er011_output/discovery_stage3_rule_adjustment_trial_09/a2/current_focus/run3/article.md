# The Small Refrigerator Setting That Helps Fresh Food Last Longer

Inside many refrigerators, the crisper drawer has a small choice: low or high humidity.

It is easy to leave the slider alone. But this setting changes the air around fresh food.

That matters because fresh food does not all lose quality in the same way. Some food releases a gas as it ripens. Other food loses water and wilts quickly. The drawer is not just a box. It matches the air to the food.

The simple message is this: do not choose only by the words “fruit” or “vegetable.” Look at what the food does. The right setting can help food stay in better condition.

### Match the drawer to the food

Think of the drawer as a matching system.

The low-humidity side lets more air move through. Apples and pears fit this setting because they release a lot of ethylene gas as they ripen.

The high-humidity side is more closed. It helps foods that lose water quickly, such as leafy greens like kale and vegetables like broccoli.

This match also matters to nearby food. When ethylene can escape, it is less likely to make other produce ripen too quickly. So, one correct setting can help more than one food in the drawer.

### Why the fruit-and-vegetable rule fails

Now for the trap: “Fruit means low humidity, and vegetables mean high humidity” is too simple.

Bananas and tomatoes are recommended for counter storage, not as examples for a refrigerator drawer.

There is also no single household answer for strawberries and oranges. Different appliance makers give different advice. A 90 to 95 percent humidity figure from commercial storage does not settle the best choice for a home drawer.

For unclear foods, check your own refrigerator’s guide instead of relying on a simple category rule.

## In one line

A crisper drawer works best when you match its air to the food’s behavior—not just to whether the food is called a fruit or a vegetable.
