# One Drawer, Two Different Jobs: How to Use a Refrigerator Crisper

At first glance, the crisper drawer looks like a simple box for fresh produce.

But in many refrigerators, it has two settings: low humidity and high humidity. Some drawers use a slider to adjust the setting.

These labels are not just decoration. A low-humidity drawer allows more air to move through it. A high-humidity drawer is closer to sealed.

That means the same refrigerator can offer two different storage spaces. The best choice depends on what may happen to the food while it is inside.

The easy shortcut is to think, “Fruit goes here, and vegetables go there.” But that does not tell the whole story.

Some produce gives off a gas as it ripens. Other produce loses water quickly and begins to wilt.

So the useful question is not only what the food is called. It is what kind of storage problem it may create.

### Ventilation for ripening gas

A low-humidity drawer is best understood as a ventilation tool, not simply a fruit box. Apples and pears release ethylene, a gas that can speed ripening and spoilage nearby. In a vented drawer, some of that gas can escape. But bananas and tomatoes are recommended for counter storage, so they are not refrigerator examples.

### Protecting water and texture

Leafy greens such as kale can lose water quickly and wilt. Broccoli is another example of produce that benefits from high humidity; its listed best humidity is about 90 to 95 percent. A more enclosed drawer helps hold moisture around these foods. When limp leaves or lost crispness is the concern, moisture—not the word “vegetable”—guides the choice.

## In one line

The crisper drawer is less about sorting food into fruits and vegetables than about matching storage to the problem: let ripening gas out, or hold water in. A small change in the slider can make the drawer work more thoughtfully for the food inside.
