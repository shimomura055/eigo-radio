# One Drawer, Two Different Jobs: How to Use a Refrigerator Crisper

At first glance, the crisper drawer looks like a simple box for produce.

But many refrigerators have two settings: low humidity and high humidity. Some drawers use a slider to adjust the setting.

These labels matter. A low-humidity drawer lets more air move through it. A high-humidity drawer is more sealed.

That means the same refrigerator can offer two different storage spaces. The best choice depends on what may happen to the food inside.

The easy shortcut is to think, “Fruit goes here, and vegetables go there.” But that is not the whole story.

Some produce gives off a gas as it ripens. Other produce loses water quickly and starts to wilt.

So the useful question is not just what the food is called. It is what kind of storage problem it may create.

### Ventilation for ripening gas

A low-humidity drawer is best seen as a ventilation tool, not simply a fruit box. Apples and pears release ethylene, which can speed ripening and spoilage nearby. In a vented drawer, some of that gas can escape. Bananas and tomatoes are recommended for counter storage, so they are not refrigerator examples.

### Protecting water and texture

Leafy greens such as kale can lose water quickly and wilt. Broccoli also benefits from high humidity; its listed best humidity is about 90 to 95 percent. A more enclosed drawer helps hold moisture around these foods. When limp leaves or lost crispness is the concern, moisture—not the word “vegetable”—guides the choice.

## In one line

The crisper drawer is less about sorting food into fruits and vegetables than about matching storage to the problem: let ripening gas out, or hold water in. A small change in the slider can help the drawer work better for the food inside.
