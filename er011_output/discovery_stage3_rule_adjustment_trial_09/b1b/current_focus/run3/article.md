# Two Settings, Two Jobs: Getting More from the Crisper Drawer

A small slider in the refrigerator can look unimportant. But the crisper drawer is not always just one box.

Many refrigerators offer two settings: low humidity and high humidity. Some models use a slider to adjust the level.

These settings create two storage environments. The low-humidity setting lets more air move through the drawer. The high-humidity setting is closer to sealed, helping reduce water loss from produce.

That difference matters because fresh food can have different storage problems.

Some fruit releases ethylene gas as it matures. If nearby produce is exposed to that gas for too long, it may ripen too quickly and spoil. Other produce loses water quickly and begins to wilt.

So the important question is not simply, “Is this a fruit or a vegetable?”

The better question is, “What problem am I trying to prevent?”

For food that belongs in the refrigerator, the answer can help you choose the right setting. The drawer becomes more than a place to put produce. It becomes a way to separate foods with different needs.

### Sort by the problem, not the label

For refrigerated food, ask what is likely to happen first. Apples and pears release a lot of ethylene, so they fit the low-humidity, vented side. Leafy greens such as kale and vegetables such as broccoli lose water quickly, so the high-humidity, more closed side is a better fit. This test is more useful than simply saying “fruit” or “vegetable.”

### The refrigerator is not always the destination

The drawer choice comes second. First, ask whether the food belongs in the refrigerator at all. Tomatoes and bananas are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration and are recommended for a cool, dry place. So low humidity is not a universal fruit setting.

## In one line

A crisper drawer works best as a two-step map: first choose the right destination, then match refrigerated produce with the problem you want to prevent.
