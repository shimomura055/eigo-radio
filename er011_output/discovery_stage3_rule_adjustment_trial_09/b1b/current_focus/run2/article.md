# The Small Refrigerator Drawer With Two Very Different Jobs

A crisper drawer may look like a simple box, but its small slider can create two storage conditions: low humidity and high humidity. That choice can affect how long fresh produce stays in good condition.

High humidity is for foods that lose water quickly and can wilt, such as leafy greens and broccoli. Low humidity can suit foods such as apples and pears, which release ethylene as they mature.

The important point is that the choice is not simply “fruit or vegetable.” Food behavior matters. Some foods lose water quickly, while others release a gas as they mature.

So the crisper is not just a place to put one item. It is part of the refrigerator’s storage system. But before choosing a setting, ask another question: does the food belong in the refrigerator at all?

### The drawer has a neighborhood effect

Think of the drawer as a small neighborhood, not a private room. An apple or pear may release ethylene as it matures. That gas can affect nearby produce, making it ripen and deteriorate sooner. A low-humidity setting lets the gas move away more easily. So placement can protect the surrounding food, not just the item itself.

### Check the fridge before the slider

Before choosing a setting, ask a more basic question: should this food be refrigerated? Bananas and tomatoes are recommended for counter storage. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions, and garlic do not need refrigeration and favor a cool, dry place. The slider is useful, but it is not a solution for every food.

## In one line…

A crisper drawer is less about sorting fruits from vegetables than matching storage to how food behaves—and checking first whether it should be chilled at all. The right choice can help one item and the produce around it stay in better condition.
