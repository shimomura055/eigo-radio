# Saturn’s South Pole Reveals a Giant Ten-Sided Wave

Saturn has offered astronomers a strange new shape: a giant ten-sided wave wrapped around its south pole.

At first glance, it looks like a huge mark across the planet. But it is not a mark on the surface or simply a cloud. It is a large wave in Saturn’s atmosphere, inside a fast-moving band of air.

Researchers say this is the first confirmed large, regular polygon-shaped jet wave found in Saturn’s southern hemisphere. The feature lies between about 58 and 63 degrees south latitude. So it circles the south pole, but is not exactly at the pole.

The main Hubble image was taken in August 2025. The finding was announced in September 2026, when a paper called “A decagon wave around Saturn’s south pole” was published online in Science Advances.

The image is striking. But the real story is that Saturn’s atmosphere is producing a changing geometric pattern on a scale not previously confirmed there.

### A shape that moves differently

The outline is not traveling with the nearby wind. The wave moves east at about 2.5 meters per second, while the jet near 60.5 degrees south reaches about 116 meters per second. So this is not a solid shape or a cloud carried by the wind. Different wavelengths place it slightly differently, pointing to layers of air at different heights. The picture becomes a clue to Saturn’s hidden structure.

### A discovery built over time

This discovery also has a detective-story shape. In 2024, researchers noticed the waving band in ground-based images. It looked clearer in 2025. They then revisited older Hubble data and found signs of it as far back as 2023. The result came from a timeline assembled from different observers and datasets, not from one night’s picture.

## In one line…

Saturn’s new decagon is not a solid shape on the planet. It is a changing atmospheric wave, giving scientists a rare look at how the planet’s southern air is organized.
