# Saturn’s South Pole Reveals a Giant Ten-Sided Atmospheric Wave

At first glance, Saturn’s south pole seems to show a giant ten-sided shape. It may look like a solid object or a strange cloud. But it is neither. The shape is a huge atmospheric wave, embedded in a jet stream near the planet’s south pole.

NASA and ESA say the wave lies mainly between 58 and 63 degrees south latitude. So it is not sitting on the exact pole. Instead, it surrounds the pole in a high-latitude band of Saturn’s atmosphere. Scientists say this is the first large, regular polygon-shaped jet wave confirmed in Saturn’s southern hemisphere.

The main image was taken by the Hubble Space Telescope on August 29, 2025, with its Wide Field Camera 3. But the discovery did not come from one picture alone.

In 2024, Agustín Sánchez-Lavega and amateur astronomers Trevor Barry and Jean-Paul Oger noticed a strange, waving band in ground-based images. The pattern became clearer in 2025. The team then checked older Hubble data and found signs that the wave had already existed as far back as 2023.

On September 2, 2026, NASA and ESA officially announced the finding. On the same day, a paper about the wave was published online in Science Advances.

The decagon is an evolving pattern in Saturn’s atmosphere. Its shape is real, but it is not a permanent mark painted onto the planet.

### Not a flat cloud-top picture

Hubble saw the decagon in images made with different kinds of light, and the place where it seemed to sit changed slightly. That is a clue about height. The structure appears at several levels in Saturn’s atmosphere, so it is not just a flat pattern on one cloud layer. The image gives scientists a view into the atmosphere’s depth.

### A slow pattern in very fast air

The wave moves east at about 2.5 meters per second, or roughly 9 to 10 kilometers per hour. Near 60.5 degrees south, the nearby jet flows at about 116 meters per second, or roughly 400 to 418 kilometers per hour. So the decagon is a slowly moving wave inside much faster air, not a shape traveling at the jet’s speed.

## In one line…

Saturn’s south pole is not displaying a giant ten-sided object. It is revealing a layered atmospheric wave moving slowly through a much faster jet.
