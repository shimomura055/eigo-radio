# Saturn’s South Pole Reveals a Giant Ten-Sided Atmospheric Wave

At first glance, Saturn’s south pole seems to show a giant ten-sided shape. It may look like a solid object or a strange cloud. But it is neither. The shape is a huge atmospheric wave, embedded in a jet stream near the planet’s south pole.

Scientists say the wave lies mainly between 58 and 63 degrees south latitude. So it is not sitting on the exact pole. Instead, it surrounds the pole in a high-latitude band of Saturn’s atmosphere. They say this is the first large, regular polygon-shaped jet wave confirmed in Saturn’s southern hemisphere.

The main image was taken by the Hubble Space Telescope in August 2025. But the discovery did not come from one picture alone.

In 2024, a researcher and amateur astronomers noticed a strange, waving band in ground-based images. The pattern became clearer in 2025. The team then checked older Hubble data and found signs that the wave had already existed as far back as 2023.

In September 2026, the finding was officially announced. A paper about the wave was also published online in *Science Advances*.

The decagon is an evolving pattern in Saturn’s atmosphere. Its shape is real, but it is not a permanent mark painted onto the planet.

### Not a flat cloud-top picture

Hubble saw the decagon in images made with different kinds of light, and the place where it seemed to sit changed slightly. That is a clue about height. The structure appears at several levels in Saturn’s atmosphere, so it is not just a flat pattern on one cloud layer. The image gives scientists a view into the atmosphere’s depth.

### A slow pattern in very fast air

The wave moves east at about 2.5 meters per second. Near 60.5 degrees south, the nearby jet flows at about 116 meters per second. So the decagon is a slowly moving wave inside much faster air, not a shape traveling at the jet’s speed.

## In one line…

Saturn’s south pole is not displaying a giant ten-sided object. It is revealing a layered atmospheric wave moving slowly through a much faster jet.
