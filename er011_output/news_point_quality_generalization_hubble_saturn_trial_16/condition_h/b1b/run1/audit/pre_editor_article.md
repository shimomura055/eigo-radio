# Saturn’s South Pole Has a Wave with Ten Sides

Saturn’s south pole has revealed a surprising shape: a giant ten-sided wave moving through its atmosphere.

NASA and ESA found the feature in observations from the Hubble Space Telescope. It surrounds the south polar region at about 58 to 63 degrees south latitude. That means it is near the pole, but not directly on the pole itself.

This is the first confirmed large, regular polygon-shaped jet wave seen in Saturn’s southern hemisphere. It is not a solid object in space. It is a changing pattern inside a powerful band of air moving around the planet.

The main image was taken on August 29, 2025, using Hubble’s Wide Field Camera 3. NASA and ESA officially announced the discovery on September 2, 2026. On the same day, a paper about the wave was published online in Science Advances.

At first glance, the image looks like a strange ten-sided mark painted across Saturn’s clouds. But the full set of observations tells a more interesting story. The shape is part of Saturn’s atmosphere, and it has been developing over time.

### A discovery built from many images

This was not a single dramatic Hubble discovery. In 2024, Agustín Sánchez-Lavega and amateur astronomers Trevor Barry and Jean-Paul Oger noticed waviness in ground-based images. The pattern became clearer in 2025. Researchers then checked older Hubble images and found signs of it as far back as 2023. Time helped reveal the change.

### A slow wave inside a fast wind

The shape also needs to be read carefully. The wave moves east at about 2.5 meters per second, while the nearby jet stream moves at about 116 meters per second. Its apparent position also changes in images taken at different wavelengths. This shows that the pattern reaches through several atmospheric layers. The decagon is a slower wave inside a much faster flow.

## In one line…

Saturn’s south pole is not simply showing a fixed ten-sided cloud. It is revealing a changing atmospheric wave that can be followed across time and through different layers of the planet’s atmosphere.
