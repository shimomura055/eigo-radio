# Saturn’s Ten-Sided Secret: A Wave Around the South Pole

Saturn has revealed a shape that looks almost too neat to be natural: a huge, ten-sided pattern around its south pole.

But this is not a solid object or a giant piece of ice. Researchers say it is a large atmospheric wave inside a fast jet of air. A decagon, or ten-sided shape, has now been confirmed in Saturn’s southern hemisphere. It is the first large, regular ten-sided wave in a jet seen there.

The wave sits between 58 and 63 degrees south latitude. In other words, it surrounds the south pole, but it is not directly on the pole. It belongs to a high-latitude band in Saturn’s atmosphere.

The main image was taken by Hubble in 2025. The finding was announced in 2026, when a paper about it appeared online.

The important point is that this is a weather pattern, not a mark painted on the planet. Its shape can appear and change inside Saturn’s atmosphere.

### A discovery built from time

What makes this story unusual is the order of the clues. In 2024, amateur observers noticed a strange band in ground images. It looked clearer in 2025. Researchers then checked older Hubble data and traced signs of it back to 2023. Ground images raised the question, while Hubble’s archive helped answer it.

### A shape made by moving air

The decagon is not a rigid shape traveling like a wheel. It is a wave inside a much faster jet. The wave moves east at about 2.5 meters per second. The jet moves at about 116 meters per second. Images made with different kinds of light also show it at more than one height. Together, these clues show that the pattern reaches through different layers of Saturn’s air.

## In one line…

Saturn’s south pole is not wearing a giant ten-sided badge. It is revealing a changing wave in a fast, layered atmosphere—and old images may still hold part of the story.
