# Saturn’s South Pole Reveals a Slow-Moving Ten-Sided Wave

Saturn has shown astronomers a shape that looks almost drawn with a ruler: a huge ten-sided pattern around its south pole.

But this is not a solid object. It is a wave in Saturn’s atmosphere, built into a fast-moving band of air. It forms a decagon, a shape with ten sides.

The pattern lies around 58 to 63 degrees south latitude. That means it circles the southern polar region, but it is not sitting exactly on the pole. It is part of the high-latitude air around it.

This is the first time a large, regular many-sided jet wave has been seen in Saturn’s southern half. The finding came from Hubble observations made over several years. The main image was taken in 2025.

The discovery was announced in 2026, and a paper about the decagon appeared online the same day.

The picture is striking. But the real story is that Saturn’s atmosphere can hold a shape this organized while the air around it keeps moving. To understand the image, we need to look beyond its ten clear sides.

### A slow ripple inside a fast flow

Compare the speeds. The wave moves east at about 2.5 meters per second. The nearby jet moves at about 116 meters per second.

The pattern is therefore a slow ripple inside a much faster flow, not a mark moving at the jet’s full speed. Hubble pictures made with different kinds of light place the shape at slightly different heights. It reaches through several layers, not just one cloud top.

### A discovery that unfolded over time

The first clue came from Earth. In 2024, amateur astronomers noticed the waviness in ground images.

It looked clearer in 2025. Then researchers checked older Hubble records and found signs of it in 2023, when the corners and straight sides were weaker. A faint feature became a discovery through teamwork.

## In one line…

Saturn’s decagon is not a solid shape, but a changing pattern in the planet’s air, revealed by observations and teamwork over time.
