# Saturn’s South Pole Reveals a Giant Ten-Sided Wave

Saturn has shown scientists a strange new shape in its southern sky.

NASA and ESA have confirmed a huge ten-sided wave in Saturn’s atmosphere. It surrounds the planet’s south polar region, near 58 to 63 degrees south. This is the first confirmed large, regular many-sided jet pattern in Saturn’s southern hemisphere.

The shape is not on the ground. It is part of the moving air high above Saturn. Scientists call it a decagon, which simply means a shape with ten sides.

The discovery is based on Hubble observations from 2023 to 2025. The clearest image in the announcement was taken on August 29, 2025. It showed the ten-sided pattern around the high southern latitudes.

NASA and ESA announced the finding on September 2, 2026. On the same day, a paper about the discovery was published online in Science Advances.

The picture is striking because Saturn’s atmosphere has created a pattern that looks almost carefully designed. But the real story is not a solid shape. It is a large and changing wave in the planet’s air.

### A pattern inside a fast current

Here is the surprising part: the wave is not rushing around with the wind. It moves east at about 2.5 meters per second, or about 9 to 10 kilometers per hour. The nearby jet, a fast air current, moves at about 116 meters per second, or roughly 400 kilometers per hour. The shape is a slow pattern inside much faster-moving air.

### A discovery built from time and depth

The discovery was also a detective story. In 2024, ground images showed a strange waviness. In 2025, the ten-sided pattern became clearer. Researchers then checked older Hubble pictures and found signs of it as far back as 2023. Images made with different kinds of light placed the pattern at different heights. This shows that it is spread through several layers of air, not just painted on the top of a cloud.

## In one line…

Around Saturn’s south pole, a changing ten-sided wave has appeared—not as a solid object, but as an organized pattern moving through the planet’s atmosphere.
