# Saturn’s South Pole Reveals a Giant Ten-Sided Wave

Saturn seems to be showing scientists a new drawing—but it is not on the planet’s surface.

Hubble images show a huge ten-sided wave in Saturn’s atmosphere, circling the south pole. It lies around 60 degrees south latitude, so it surrounds the pole rather than sitting exactly on it.

The shape is part of a fast-moving belt of air. It is not a solid object or simply a ten-sided cloud.

This is the first large, regular many-sided jet-wave pattern found in Saturn’s southern half. The discovery was announced on September 2, 2026. A research paper appeared online the same day.

The main Hubble image was taken on August 29, 2025. Scientists can now see the shape clearly, but they still do not know exactly why it formed.

### A discovery built from many pictures

The story did not begin with a perfect space image. In 2024, a scientist and two amateur astronomers noticed waviness in ground-based pictures. The straight edges and corners looked clearer in 2025. A new look at old Hubble images found weaker signs back to 2023. Together, these pictures show change over time.

### Saturn’s poles do not tell the same story

Saturn’s two poles are not simple copies. The famous north-pole hexagon has lasted for more than 40 years, while earlier Hubble and Cassini views found no long-lived many-sided wave in the south. That difference gives scientists a new test: could curved air currents, repeated disturbances, or a nearby dark spinning air system help make different patterns? No single answer is confirmed yet.

## In one line…

Saturn’s south pole is not showing a finished symbol, but a changing wave. Its ten sides turn a striking image into a new question about why the planet’s two poles behave differently.
