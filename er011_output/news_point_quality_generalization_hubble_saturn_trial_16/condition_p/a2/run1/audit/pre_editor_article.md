# A Ten-Sided Wave Is Revealed Around Saturn’s South Pole

Saturn’s south pole has a new mystery: a huge wave with ten clear sides.

NASA and ESA say the Hubble Space Telescope found a giant decagon, or ten-sided pattern, around the south pole. It is not a solid object. It is a wave moving inside a fast air stream in Saturn’s atmosphere.

The wave is not exactly at the pole. It stretches around the planet between about 58 and 63 degrees south latitude.

The key Hubble image was taken on August 29, 2025. But the discovery story began earlier.

In 2024, researchers and amateur astronomers noticed unusual, bending bands in ground-based images. In 2025, the ten-sided pattern became easier to see. Scientists then checked older Hubble images. They found signs of the wave as far back as 2023, although the shape was much harder to see then.

On September 2, 2026, NASA and ESA officially announced the discovery. On the same day, a paper about the wave was published online in Science Advances.

This is the first confirmed large, regular polygonal wave found in Saturn’s southern hemisphere. The shape looks simple from far away. But it may tell scientists something important about the changing air around Saturn’s poles.

### Saturn’s two poles tell different stories

Saturn’s north-polar hexagon has lasted for more than 40 years and is almost steady. Older Hubble and Cassini observations found no matching long-lived polygon in the south. So this decagon is not simply a southern copy. It suggests that Saturn’s two polar air systems can behave in very different ways.

### A shape that acts like a clue

The ten sides are more than a striking picture. A computer model suggests they may be the visible form of a large, winding wave held inside a curved fast air stream. Scientists are testing two ideas: a repeated disturbance near the top of the stream, or an effect from a dark storm-like swirl nearby. Neither idea is confirmed. Watching the shape change may help explain how Saturn’s air moves.

## In one line…

Saturn’s new decagon is not a giant object in space. It is a changing atmospheric wave, giving scientists a new clue about how large patterns form around a giant planet’s pole.
