# Saturn’s South Pole Has a New Shape: A Giant Atmospheric Decagon

Saturn has drawn a new shape in its southern sky.

It is a huge ten-sided pattern, called a decagon, circling the planet’s south pole.

But this is not a solid object. It is not simply a cloud floating above the pole. It is a large wave in a fast-moving band of air. In simple terms, it is a changing pattern inside Saturn’s atmosphere.

The wave lies around 58 to 63 degrees south of Saturn’s equator. So, it circles the south pole, but it is not exactly on the pole itself.

NASA and ESA announced the finding on September 2, 2026. A study about it appeared online in Science Advances on the same day.

A key Hubble image was taken on August 29, 2025. It came from a Hubble camera that records ultraviolet and visible light.

This is the first confirmed large, regular polygon-shaped wave found in Saturn’s southern half.

The shape is now clear. Its cause is not. Scientists are still trying to understand how Saturn’s moving air can create and support such a large pattern.

### A clearer picture is not a birth date

The discovery did not begin with one perfect Hubble picture. In 2024, Sánchez-Lavega and amateur observers Trevor Barry and Jean-Paul Oger noticed changing bands in ground images. The pattern looked stronger in 2025. Older Hubble records then showed weaker signs from 2023. So, a clearer picture is not proof that the wave was born in 2025.

### Saturn’s poles tell different stories

Saturn’s poles are not mirror images. The north has kept a six-sided wave for over 40 years. Earlier Hubble and Cassini observations found no lasting southern match. Possible explanations include a wave held by a curved air current, a disturbance near its top, or pressure from a nearby dark, spinning air system. No single explanation is confirmed. That makes the decagon a clue to Saturn’s air, not just a shape.

## In one line…

Saturn’s south pole has revealed a ten-sided wave, but the bigger mystery is how its air could make it.
