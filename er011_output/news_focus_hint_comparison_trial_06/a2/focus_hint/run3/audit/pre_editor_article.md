# A Fast Start, Then a Firm Finish for Hanshin

## Main Story

Some wins are decided by one big swing. Hanshin’s 8–1 win over Hiroshima on August 16 was different. One swing opened the game, but the rest of the night made the lead stronger.

At Mazda Stadium, the Hanshin Tigers attacked first. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly moved ahead, 2–0.

Starting pitcher Rihito Ihara then gave the team a steady start. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, cutting the gap to one run.

But that was all Hiroshima could get.

Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew again, and the game moved away from the home team.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

This was not a game Hanshin barely escaped. It took the lead early, allowed one reply, and then created more distance late.

### One home run, but no lasting attack

One detail explains why Hiroshima’s home run did not start a comeback. It was a solo shot, and Hiroshima did not score again. Ihara had also limited the team to two hits and one run in five innings. Hiroshima found one scoring play, but not the continued pressure needed to change the game.

### The lead was rebuilt by several players

Then came the part that made the score decisive. Hanshin’s late runs had several contributors. Seishiro Sakamoto brought in a run in the seventh and added another with a double in the eighth. Pinch-hitter Tora Fushimi also brought one home. The early lead was not simply protected. It was rebuilt by different players after Hiroshima’s only score.

## In one line…

Hanshin’s win was not just an early home run. It was an early lead, one isolated Hiroshima answer, and enough late support to turn control into an 8–1 finish.
