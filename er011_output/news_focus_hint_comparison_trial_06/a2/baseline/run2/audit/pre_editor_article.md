# A Fast Start, Then a Firm Finish: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin did not wait long to make its mark.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin led 2-0 before the game had settled. That gave the visiting team an early advantage.

But an early lead is not enough. Hanshin still had to stop Hiroshima from making a comeback.

Starting pitcher Ryohto Ihara gave the team a strong base. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

That one run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run for Hiroshima. It made the score 2-1, but it was the only run Hiroshima scored all night.

Hanshin kept its lead. Then the offense returned late. The Tigers added runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The two-run lead grew into a seven-run advantage.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

This was a clear win built from an early home run, steady pitching, and more scoring later. Hanshin took control early, stayed in front after Hiroshima’s only scoring play, and made the gap larger before the game ended.

### The lead was built in layers

The final score was not just the result of Sato’s opening swing. Hanshin added to it through several run-scoring hits. Seishiro Sakamoto and Torai Fushimi, who came in to hit, added timely hits in the seventh. Sakamoto added another run-scoring double in the eighth. The margin was actively enlarged late.

### Hiroshima’s one answer stayed small

Montero’s home run mattered, but its shape mattered too. It was a solo shot, so Hiroshima scored only one run from it. Ihara had allowed just two hits and one run in five innings. Hiroshima never produced a multi-run rally. It found one scoring play, but not the larger response needed to close the gap.

## In one line…

Hanshin did not win with its first punch alone: it kept adding to its lead, while Hiroshima could answer only once.
