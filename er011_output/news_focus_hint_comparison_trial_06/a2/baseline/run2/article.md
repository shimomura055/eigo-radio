# A Fast Start, Then a Firm Finish: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin made its mark quickly.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin led 2-0 before the game had settled, giving the visiting team an early advantage.

But an early lead is not enough. Hanshin still had to stop Hiroshima from coming back.

Starting pitcher Ryohto Ihara gave the team a strong base. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

That run came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run for Hiroshima. The score became 2-1, but it was the only run Hiroshima scored all night.

Hanshin kept its lead, then added more runs late. The Tigers scored in the seventh and eighth innings, growing their two-run lead into a seven-run advantage.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

This was a clear win built on an early home run, steady pitching, and more scoring later. Hanshin took control early, stayed ahead after Hiroshima’s only scoring play, and widened the gap before the game ended.

### The lead was built in layers

The final score was not just the result of Sato’s opening swing. Hanshin added several more runs through scoring hits. Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Torai Fushimi added timely hits in the seventh. Sakamoto added another run-scoring double in the eighth. The margin grew late.

### Hiroshima’s one answer stayed small

Montero’s home run mattered, but it was a solo shot, so Hiroshima scored only one run from it. Ihara allowed just two hits and one run in five innings. Hiroshima never produced a multi-run rally. It found one scoring play, but not the larger response needed to close the gap.

## In one line…

Hanshin did not win with its first punch alone: it kept adding to its lead, while Hiroshima could answer only once.
