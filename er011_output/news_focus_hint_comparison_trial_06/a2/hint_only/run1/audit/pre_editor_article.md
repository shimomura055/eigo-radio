# One Big Swing, Then a Full-Team Win for Hanshin

## Main Story

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, the Hanshin Tigers beat the Hiroshima Carp 8–1.

Hanshin took control right away. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. It gave the Tigers a 2–0 lead before the game had time to settle.

Starting pitcher Rito Ihara then gave Hanshin a strong base. He allowed only two hits and one run in five innings.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. But Hiroshima could not add more runs after that.

Hanshin kept moving forward late in the game. The Tigers scored again in the seventh and eighth innings. Their early lead became larger, and Hiroshima could not close the distance.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

It was a complete win for the Tigers.

### One answer was not a comeback

Hiroshima’s fifth-inning home run gave the home team one run, but there was no second scoring play after it. That matters because a comeback needs pressure that keeps building. Here, the response stopped at one moment. Hanshin was not simply protecting a small lead; it was controlling the shape of the game.

### More than one way to add distance

The early lead came from Sato’s power. Later, the attack used a different mix. Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Taira Fushimi produced timely hits in the seventh, and Sakamoto added an RBI double in the eighth. That variety made the win less dependent on one big swing.

## In one line…

On this night, Hanshin turned an early advantage into a whole-game advantage.
