# A Fast Start, Then a Firm Finish

On August 16, the Hanshin Tigers turned an early lead into an 8-1 win over the Hiroshima Toyo Carp at Mazda Stadium.

The first big moment came in the first inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field, and Hanshin quickly moved ahead, 2-0.

Starting pitcher Rihito Ihara then gave the lead a strong base. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s answer came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, cutting the gap to one run. But that was the Carp’s only score of the game.

Hanshin did not let the game stay close. The Tigers added runs in the seventh and eighth innings, creating more space on the scoreboard.

By the end, the early lead had become a clear victory:

Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

It was a win that began with one powerful swing, stayed steady through the middle, and grew stronger late.

### A close middle before the final gap

The score tells an interesting story beyond the final 8-1 result. Sato’s home run made it 2-0, and Montero’s solo shot brought Hiroshima within 2-1. Hanshin then scored in the seventh and eighth innings. The victory came in two stages: early control, followed by a late push. That gives “complete” a more precise meaning here.

### More than one bat finished the job

The late scoring came from several contributors. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs in both the seventh and eighth innings. In the seventh, pinch-hitter Torai Fushimi also delivered a hit that brought in a run. Sato created the early advantage, but the final margin was built by a wider group, not by one home run alone.

## In one line…

One big swing opened the game, but a steady start and contributions from several players carried Hanshin to a complete win.
