# One Early Swing, Then a Complete Hanshin Win

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin gave Hiroshima little time to settle.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. The Tigers quickly took a 2–0 lead.

But an early lead is only a start. Hiroshima answered in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. It was the Carp’s only run.

Hanshin starter Rihito Ihara gave the team a steady start. He pitched five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

After that, Hanshin did not let the game stay close. The Tigers added runs in the seventh and eighth innings. Their lead grew, leaving Hiroshima less room to respond.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

This was a complete win: an early blow, a steady start from the pitcher, and more scoring later.

### Two clear ways to score

The 8–1 margin was not created by one swing. Sato provided the early power, while Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Tairi Fushimi produced timely hits in the seventh and eighth innings. Hiroshima had only Montero’s solo home run. The difference was the lack of a multi-run answer.

### Control without perfection

Ihara did not need to keep Hiroshima scoreless to be effective. He gave up one run but allowed only two hits in five innings. That kept Hiroshima from building repeated pressure. The pitching story was not perfection. It was control, and in this game, that was enough to protect Hanshin’s early advantage.

## In one line...

One early home run opened the game, but steady scoring and controlled pitching turned it into an 8–1 Hanshin victory.
