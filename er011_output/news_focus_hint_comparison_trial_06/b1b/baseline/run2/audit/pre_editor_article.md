# One Early Swing, Then a Complete Hanshin Win

## Main Story

The first blow was loud. The rest of the game was about control.

At Mazda Stadium on August 16, the Hanshin Tigers took the lead in the first inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field, giving Hanshin a 2-0 advantage.

But an early lead is only the beginning. Hanshin needed to keep Hiroshima from building a response.

Starter Rito Ihara did exactly that. He worked five innings. He allowed only two hits and one run. Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

That was the full extent of Hiroshima’s offense.

Hanshin, meanwhile, kept adding to its lead. In the seventh inning, Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Torai Fushimi delivered run-scoring hits. In the eighth, Sakamoto added a timely double that brought in another run.

So the game moved in one direction. Sato opened the scoring, Ihara limited the damage, and the later hits made the lead even bigger.

The final result was a clear 8-1 victory for Hanshin over Hiroshima.

### A single answer, not a turning point

Hiroshima’s one run mattered on the scoreboard, but it did not become pressure across several innings. Because it came from one solo home run and was the team’s only score, the game never became a back-and-forth fight. The early lead was challenged, but it was never close to being overturned.

### A win passed from one role to another

This was not a night that depended on Sato alone. Ihara first limited the damage over five innings. Later, Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Fushimi supplied important scoring hits. The key was the handoff: one player created the opening, the pitcher protected it, and other hitters widened it.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned one early home run into a full-game performance, holding Hiroshima to one run and finishing with an 8-1 win.
