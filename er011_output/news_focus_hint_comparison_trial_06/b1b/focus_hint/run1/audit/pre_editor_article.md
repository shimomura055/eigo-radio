# Hanshin’s Early Strike Became a Complete 8–1 Win

## Main Story

Some wins begin with one loud moment. This one began with a big swing, then became a controlled night for Hanshin.

On August 16, 2026, at Mazda Stadium, the Hanshin Tigers faced the Hiroshima Toyo Carp.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly moved ahead, 2–0.

The next question was simple: could that early lead last?

Hanshin starter Rihito Ihara gave his team a steady start. He pitched five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only run came in the bottom of the fifth. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, cutting the lead to 2–1.

But Hiroshima could not build on that moment. Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings, creating a much wider gap.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

Hanshin did not win only because of its fast start. The team protected that advantage, limited Hiroshima’s response, and added more offense later in the game.

### Hiroshima’s answer never became a rally

The important detail in the fifth inning was not only that Hiroshima scored. It was how that run came. Montero’s home run produced one run, but Hiroshima never turned its response into a larger attack. With Ihara giving up only two hits in five innings, the Carp had no sustained scoring surge.

### The finish came from different roles

Hanshin’s later offense was shared across the lineup. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in a run in the seventh and added a run-scoring double in the eighth. Pinch-hitter Taira Fushimi also delivered a timely hit in the seventh. The win was reinforced by several contributors, not just Sato’s opening home run.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first, kept Hiroshima’s response small, and then built the win step by step.

At Mazda Stadium, one early swing opened the game, but steady pitching and contributions from different players finished it.
