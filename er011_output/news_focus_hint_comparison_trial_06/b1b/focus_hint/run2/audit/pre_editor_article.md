# Hanshin Turns an Early Spark into an 8–1 Win

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin took control before Hiroshima had time to settle in.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. It was a powerful start, but Hanshin did not depend on that one swing alone.

Starting pitcher Rihito Ihara then gave the team a steady foundation. He allowed only two hits and one run over five innings. Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

After that, Hanshin kept adding to its lead. The Tigers scored again in the seventh and eighth innings, and the early advantage became a comfortable one.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

It was a complete win built in stages: an early strike, stable pitching, and more runs later in the game.

### The lead came from more than one swing

Hanshin’s later scoring showed the work of different players in the lineup. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in a run with a hit, and pinch-hitter Tora Fushimi also brought a runner home. Sakamoto added another run-scoring double in the eighth.

That made the win more than a game decided by Sato’s early home run. Hanshin created another scoring phase late in the game, giving the team a stronger and more lasting advantage.

### Hiroshima had an answer, but not sustained pressure

Montero’s home run gave Hiroshima one scoring play, but it did not lead to a larger attack. The Carp finished with only one run, and that run came from a solo home run rather than a multi-run inning.

That helps explain the shape of the game. Hiroshima did answer once, but the scoring record does not show continuing pressure. It also does not establish whether pitching, defense, or Hiroshima’s hitting was the specific reason.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned one early power swing into a complete team victory. Hiroshima answered once, but never built enough pressure to change the game.
