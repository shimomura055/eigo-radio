# Hanshin Turns an Early Spark into an 8–1 Win

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin took control before Hiroshima could settle in.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. It was a powerful start, but Hanshin did not rely on that swing alone.

Starting pitcher Rihito Ihara gave the team a steady foundation. He allowed only two hits and one run over five innings. Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

Hanshin then kept adding to its lead. The Tigers scored in the seventh and eighth innings, turning the early advantage into a comfortable one.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

It was a complete win built in stages: an early strike, steady pitching, and more runs later.

### The lead came from more than one swing

Hanshin’s later scoring showed contributions from different players. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in a run with a hit, and pinch-hitter Tora Fushimi brought a runner home. Sakamoto added another run-scoring double in the eighth.

The win was therefore more than a game decided by Sato’s early home run. Hanshin created another scoring phase late in the game, building a stronger, lasting advantage.

### Hiroshima had an answer, but not sustained pressure

Montero’s home run gave Hiroshima one scoring play, but it did not lead to a larger attack. The Carp finished with only one run, and it came from a solo home run rather than a multi-run inning.

That explains the shape of the game. Hiroshima answered once, but the scoring record does not show continuing pressure. It also does not establish whether pitching, defense, or Hiroshima’s hitting was the specific reason.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned one early power swing into a complete team victory. Hiroshima answered once, but never built enough pressure to change the game.
