# A Lead Built in Layers: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

An 8-1 score can make a game look easy from the start. But Hanshin’s win over Hiroshima on August 16 at Mazda Stadium was built in stages: an early lead, steady pitching, and more runs late in the game.

Teruaki Sato changed the game first. In the first inning, he hit a two-run home run to center field and put Hanshin ahead.

That was a fast start, but the game still had to be controlled. Starting pitcher Rihito Ibara gave Hanshin a strong base. He pitched five innings, allowing two hits and one run.

Hiroshima answered in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, giving the home team one run. But Hanshin did not lose its direction.

The Tigers added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew again, and Hiroshima could not answer a second time.

By the final out, Hanshin had turned an early advantage into an 8-1 victory. It was not just a game decided by one big swing. It was a game in which Hanshin started well, stayed steady, and finished the job.

### One answer, not sustained pressure

The quieter part of this win was Hiroshima’s limited attack. Its only run came from Montero’s solo home run in the fifth inning. Ibara allowed just two hits and one run over five innings. Hanshin was not coming back from an early deficit; its lead faced one setback, but no larger attack followed.

### The lead had several hands

The late cushion came through timely hits rather than another headline home run. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in a run in the seventh and added an RBI double in the eighth. Pinch-hitter Torai Fushimi also delivered an RBI hit in the seventh. Several parts of the lineup helped turn Sato’s early blast into a complete win.

## In one line…

Hanshin’s 8-1 victory was built step by step: a quick start, a steady middle, and timely contributions that kept Hiroshima from changing the game.
