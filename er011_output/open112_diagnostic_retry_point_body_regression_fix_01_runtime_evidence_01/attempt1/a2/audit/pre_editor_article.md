# Hanshin Builds an 8–1 Win in Two Different Ways

Some games are won by one big moment. This one began that way, but it ended through steady work.

On Sunday, August 16, Hanshin played Hiroshima at Mazda Stadium. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly took a 2–0 lead.

Hiroshima answered in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, cutting the lead to 2–1. The game was close again.

Hanshin’s starting pitcher, Rihito Ihara, kept the game under control. He pitched five innings and allowed two hits and one run.

Hanshin then added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The small lead grew, and Hiroshima could not close the gap again.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8.

### Power first, then timely hits

Hanshin scored in two different ways. Sato’s early home run gave the team its first cushion. After Hiroshima came within one run, Hanshin used hits at important moments. Seishiro Sakamoto and substitute batter Torai Fushimi scored runs in the seventh, and Sakamoto added another in the eighth. Hanshin did not need another home run to finish the game.

### One run, but no long attack

The key defensive point was what happened after Hiroshima scored. Montero’s home run was a solo shot, and Ihara allowed only two hits and one run in his five innings. So the 2–1 score did not become a long Hiroshima attack. Hanshin limited the pressure and kept the game from changing direction.

## In one line…

Hanshin took the lead with power, then finished the job with timely hitting and steady pitching. It was an 8–1 win built step by step.
