# A Fast Start, Then Full Control: Hanshin Beat Hiroshima 8-1

On August 16, 2026, Mazda Stadium saw a game that started with a clear message and ended in a complete Hanshin victory.

The Hanshin Tigers took control in the first inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field, giving Hanshin a 2-0 lead.

But that early lead needed support. Starter Rihito Ihara provided it. He pitched five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima's only score came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. It put Hiroshima on the board, but did not start a bigger attack.

Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The game moved from a close contest toward a one-sided finish.

The final score was:

Hiroshima Carp 1-8 Hanshin Tigers

Hanshin started fast, kept its pitching steady, and added runs until the victory was secure.

### The lead became a team effort

Sato created the first big moment, but he did not win the game alone. In the seventh and eighth innings, Seishiro Sakamoto brought in runs, and pinch-hitter Taii Fushimi delivered a hit that brought in a run. Those extra runs turned an early lead into a safer advantage. More than one Hanshin player helped carry the game forward.

### One big hit is not a rally

Look at Hiroshima's scoring, not just the final number. Montero's solo home run was the team's only score. Hiroshima had one clear scoring moment, but it did not produce a series of runs or continued pressure. Ihara's five innings, with only two hits allowed, helped keep that moment alone. The problem was not the lack of one big hit. It was the lack of a rally after it.

## In one line…

Hanshin did not win on one home run alone. It built an early lead, added late runs, and kept Hiroshima from turning one solo shot into a comeback.
