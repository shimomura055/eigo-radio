# One Big Start, Then Steady Pressure: Hanshin Beats Hiroshima 8-1

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin took control early against Hiroshima.

In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin quickly moved in front, 2-0.

Starting pitcher Rito Ihara then gave the team a steady foundation. He worked five innings, allowed two hits, and gave up one run. Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth inning, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

That cut Hanshin’s lead, but it did not turn into a larger attack. Hanshin added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead grew again, and Hiroshima could not close the gap.

The final score was:

Hiroshima 1–8 Hanshin

It was a complete win built from an early advantage, steady pitching, and more runs later in the game.

### More than one big swing

Sato’s home run gave Hanshin its first push, but the offense did not depend on that one moment. In the seventh inning, Seishiro Sakamoto and pinch-hitter Tora Fushimi each delivered an RBI hit. Sakamoto added another RBI with a double in the eighth.

That variety mattered. Hanshin widened the gap through several contributors and different kinds of hits, rather than waiting for one more huge home run.

### Hiroshima’s answer stayed small

Ihara’s contribution was not a perfect, scoreless outing. It was damage control. Montero’s home run gave Hiroshima one run, but it was a solo shot, and the team did not build a rally from it.

Ihara allowed only two hits over five innings, while Hiroshima finished with just that one scoring play. Its only counterattack was real, but it remained isolated. That helped Hanshin keep its early lead under control before adding more runs later.

## In one line…

Hanshin turned Sato’s early two-run spark into a complete 8-1 victory, while Hiroshima’s only reply stayed a single solo home run.
