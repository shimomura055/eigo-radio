# One Early Swing, Then a Complete Hanshin Win

Some games are decided by one big swing. This one began that way, but did not end there.

On August 16, at Mazda Stadium, Hanshin took the lead in the top of the first inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. Hanshin was ahead before the game found its rhythm.

The next task was to protect that early advantage. Hanshin’s starter, Rinto Ihara, did exactly that. He pitched five innings, allowing just two hits and one run.

Hiroshima scored in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run. But Hanshin added runs in the seventh and eighth innings, giving itself room to finish without a late scare.

The final score was Hiroshima 1, Hanshin 8. Hanshin started quickly, got a steady performance from its starting pitcher, and finished with a clear victory.

### More than one powerful swing

This was not only Teruaki Sato’s night. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in runs in the seventh and eighth innings. Tora Fushimi, a pinch-hitter sent in during the game, also delivered an RBI. Those contributions show how Hanshin built its winning margin through several bats, not one early swing.

### One answer, not a lasting comeback

Hiroshima’s fifth-inning home run was a response, but not the start of a comeback. Elehuris Montero’s shot was the Carp’s only run of the game. The important difference was between one scoring moment and a sustained rally: Hiroshima answered once, but could not create a longer sequence to change the game’s direction.

## In one line…

Hanshin opened with power, kept the game under control with Ihara’s pitching, and turned that fast start into an 8-1 win through contributions from across the lineup.
