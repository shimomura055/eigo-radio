# One Big Swing, Then a Full-Team Win

On August 16, at MAZDA Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima, the Hanshin Tigers turned a fast start into a comfortable win over the Hiroshima Toyo Carp.

The first big moment came in the first inning. Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run, giving Hanshin the lead before the game had time to settle.

But this was not a win built on one swing alone. Starting pitcher Takato Ihara worked five innings, allowing two hits and one run. Hiroshima’s only run came in the fifth, when Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run.

Hanshin then added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The lead kept growing, and Hiroshima never found a way back into the game.

The final score was Hanshin 8, Hiroshima 1.

There was no late drama this time. Hanshin took control early, protected that advantage, and added to it before the night was over.

### More than one source of offense

After the opening burst, Hanshin’s late scoring came from several contributors. Seishiro Sakamoto drove in a run in the seventh and added an RBI double in the eighth. Pinch-hitter Torai Fushimi also delivered an RBI hit in the seventh. The pressure kept coming from different parts of the lineup.

That made the victory feel like a team effort rather than a game decided by one star.

### The swing behind the score

Teruaki Sato’s 28th home run had a very clear shape. With a 1-1 count, Shohei Mori threw the third pitch, a two-seam fastball. Sato sent it to the back screen.

The home run also moved Sato to within one of teammate Shota Morishita, who had 29. So the opening blow carried a personal milestone as well as its value for the team.

## In one line…

Hanshin won with an early jolt, a strong start from Takato Ihara, and late help from across the lineup. The 8-1 result was a complete team performance, not just a one-swing victory.
