# One Early Blow, One Steady Night: Hanshin Beat Hiroshima 8–1

One swing gave the Hanshin Tigers the opening they needed.

On August 16, at MAZDA Zoom-Zoom Stadium Hiroshima, Hanshin faced the Hiroshima Toyo Carp. In the first inning, Teruaki Sato hit a two-run home run to center field. The Tigers quickly moved in front, 2–0.

From there, starting pitcher Takato Ihara gave Hanshin a strong base to build on. He worked through five innings, allowed only two hits, and gave up one run.

Hiroshima’s only score came in the fifth inning. Elehuris Montero hit a solo home run, but the Carp could not turn that moment into a larger attack.

Hanshin kept its lead, then added more runs in the seventh and eighth innings. The game moved away from Hiroshima, and the Tigers finished with a clear 8–1 victory.

It was a game built in two steps: an early hit that changed the shape of the night, followed by steady pitching and more runs late.

### The opening swing had a story of its own

Sato’s home run was more than a quick two-run start. It came on the third pitch, a two-seamer, after the count reached one ball and one strike. He sent it into the back screen for his 28th home run of the season. That put him just one behind teammate Shota Morishita, who had 29.

### A win with an immediate effect

The result also changed the short-term picture for both teams. Hanshin stopped a two-game losing streak and stayed in first place, keeping a two-game lead over the Giants. Hiroshima’s two-game winning streak ended before the Carp could make it three straight since the opening series. One game did not decide the season, but it gave Hanshin stability and stopped Hiroshima’s momentum.

## In one line…

Hanshin struck first, Ihara kept the game under control, and the Tigers turned one early home run into an 8–1 win with clear meaning beyond the final score.
