# Japan’s Young Travelers Want a Slower Pace — But Their Trips Are Still Short

By September 2026, a clear wish is showing up among young travelers in Japan, especially young men.

They want more room in their trips. They may travel alone, spend time on a hobby, or leave part of a tour open.

A survey found that men aged 29 and under often preferred solo travel and trips focused on a hobby. Each choice was selected by about one in four people.

A separate government survey found that about 90 percent of Japanese Gen Z respondents wanted free time during an overseas tour. About 80 percent wanted half a day or more.

This points to a new kind of travel wish. Many young people do not want every hour planned for them. They want to enjoy a place in their own way.

But here is the surprising part: this wish has not yet become a clear move to longer holidays.

When people imagined having one month off, the most common answer was a trip of about one week. It was chosen by about 24 percent.

But in an autumn survey of people planning a trip, the expected stay was still short. The average was about two nights, and the middle answer was also two nights.

A tourism white paper also says that longer stays are still an important task. So the country has not already moved fully from sightseeing to long, slow holidays.

The picture is also different for women. Among women aged 29 and under, interest in visiting famous tourist sites remained high, at about 45 percent.

This is not a complete change from one travel style to another. It is a gap between what young people want from a trip and how long they can actually stay.

### Slow does not only mean more nights

Slow travel has two sides. One is length. The other is how much control a traveler has over the day. A trip of about two nights may still feel slow in this second sense if it leaves room for one interest. For travel planners, open time may matter as much as adding nights.

### One young audience is not enough

Age and gender change the picture. For men aged 29 and under, solo travel and hobby travel were each chosen by about one in four. For women, food travel was chosen by about half, and famous sights still drew about 45 percent. One product for all young travelers may miss these different reasons for traveling.

## In one line…

Young travelers in Japan are clearly asking for more freedom and personal time, but the evidence does not yet show a full move to longer stays. The wish for slow travel is growing faster than the stay itself.
