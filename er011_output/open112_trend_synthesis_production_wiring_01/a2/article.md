# Young Travelers Want More Freedom, but Their Trips Are Still Short

As of September 2026, a quiet change is visible in how many young people in Japan think about travel.

The dream is not always to see more places. For many young men, it is choosing what to do and moving at their own speed.

In a 2025 survey, about 25% of men aged 29 and under chose solo travel as a preferred style. Travel that helped them go deeper into a hobby was close behind.

The same wish appears in overseas travel. In a survey of Japanese Gen Z people aged 19 to 25, about 90% wanted free time during a tour. Among them, about four in five wanted at least half a day.

But here is the surprising part.

The wish for a slower trip has not yet become long stays.

When people imagined having one month off, the most common answer was a trip of about one week. It was chosen by about 24% of people.

Yet, among people planning an autumn trip, the average stay was only about 1.8 nights. The middle answer was two nights.

A national tourism report also says that making stays longer is still a policy goal.

So Japan has not already moved completely from sightseeing to long, slow travel. The stronger story is the gap: young travelers want more freedom and depth, while measured trips are still short.

### Slow can happen inside a short trip

In this story, “slow” does not always mean adding many nights. It can mean leaving much of the day open, traveling alone, or making room for one strong interest. This suggests that travel providers may need flexible plans and real free time, not only packages with more nights.

### Young travelers are not one market

The male and female results point to two different audiences. Young men show stronger interest in solo and hobby-focused trips. Young women still show strong interest in food trips and famous sights: about 52% chose food travel, and about 45% chose famous tourist places. A separate survey also put solo travel first among men aged 18 to 29.

## In one line…

Japan’s young travelers are showing a clearer wish for self-directed trips, especially young men, but the evidence still shows an emerging direction—not a completed move to long stays.
