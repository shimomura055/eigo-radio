# A Cold Morning at the School Pond

The school pond froze for the first time this winter. On Monday morning, the science teacher, Mr. Reed, led his class outside to measure the ice. The class carried a ruler, a small saw, and a notebook.

First, a student named Mia broke the ice on the edge of the pond with a stick. She wanted to see how thick it was. The ice was only two centimeters thick, so it was not safe to walk on. Mr. Reed told everyone to stay on the grass.

Then a boy named Leo dropped a can of beans from his lunch bag. He opened it by mistake, and the beans spilled on the path. Everyone laughed, and Leo cleaned up quickly. A few birds came down to eat them.

While Mia was writing numbers, she bit her tongue because the wind was so cold. She said it hurt for a moment. Mr. Reed gave her some hot tea from a bottle.

Later, a girl named Sara lost her keys near the fence. The class looked for them for ten minutes. At last Leo opened his eyes wide and pointed. "They are under the bench!" he said. Sara thanked him and put them in her pocket.

The class wrote down three things: the thickness of the ice, the air temperature, and the time. Mr. Reed said they would measure the pond again next week to see whether the ice changed.

When the bell rang, everyone ran back to the building. Their hands were red, but they were excited to share the numbers with the other classes.

## In one line
A class measured the ice on the school pond and wrote down the results.
