# Rents Stop Climbing, but Is the Market Really Calm?

New data show that apartment rents in the city stayed almost flat last quarter. After three years of steady increases, the average monthly rent rose only 0.4%. Some renters felt the news was a gift. But analysts warn that one quiet quarter does not make a trend.

The city's rental market has been holding its breath. Landlords raised prices quickly after the pandemic, and many tenants simply said no. Empty apartments stayed on websites for longer, and the numbers began to whisper a different story from the headlines.

A housing economist, Dr. Alvarez, said the market "shrugged off" the latest interest rate news. Normally, higher borrowing costs push rents up, because owners pass costs on to tenants. This time, rents barely moved. "The old rules are asleep," she said, "and we do not yet know what will wake them."

There are signs of pressure under the surface. Building permits fell by 12% compared with the same period last year. That means fewer new apartments will arrive in two or three years. A tight supply can send rents upward again. In this sense, today's calm is a loan taken out against the future.

Renters should not relax too soon. Wages also grew more slowly than rents over the full three years, so many families still spend over a third of their income on housing.

The data do not show why rents paused. Higher borrowing costs, fewer new jobs, or a small drop in population could each explain the pause. Until more quarters of data arrive, the safest summary is that the market is resting, not retreating.

## In one line
City rents paused last quarter, but low building and slow wage growth may bring pressure back.
