# Solar Panels Gain Efficiency, but Costs Still Matter

A report from an energy research group says that the average efficiency of commercial solar panels reached 22.4% last year. Five years ago, the figure was 19.1%. Efficiency is the share of sunlight that a panel turns into electricity.

The report covered 1,350 panel models sold in 31 countries. It found that the best models reached 24.8%, while the lowest-priced models stayed near 18%. The difference mostly came from the materials used in the cells and from the coating on the glass surface.

Installation costs also changed. The average price for a household system fell by 14% over the five years, from $3.10 to $2.67 per watt. However, the report noted that labor and permits now make up 41% of the total cost, compared with 33% five years ago. These costs did not fall as quickly as the price of the panels themselves.

The report also looked at output over time. A typical panel loses about 0.5% of its output each year. After 25 years, most panels still produce more than 85% of their original power. The authors said the figure depends on temperature, humidity, and the quality of the installation.

Researchers cautioned that laboratory results do not always match results on a roof. A cell tested at 27% efficiency in a laboratory may reach only 23% after it is placed in a full-size panel. Shade and dust can lower the number further.

The group expects efficiency to continue to rise by about 0.4 percentage points each year. It said the next major gain will likely come from stacking two layers of cells in a single panel.

## In one line
Solar panel efficiency has risen to 22.4%, but installation costs are falling more slowly.
