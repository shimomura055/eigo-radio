[Theme]
the future of brain-computer interfaces

[Candidates to rank against each other]
- id=CP-A1 scale=A_intimate
  core_provocation: If a BCI can speak your intentions before you have chosen your words, who is responsible for what it says?
  what_is_the_future: A family must distinguish between a person's deliberate messages and the device's predictions of those messages.
  what_is_interesting: The technology restores communication while quietly creating a new layer between intention and expression.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The conflict is not that the machine misunderstands her, but that it often guesses correctly enough to become socially authoritative.
  what_happens_as_a_story: During a family argument, the BCI produces a sentence the woman might have said, and everyone reacts before she can reject it. She must decide whether to disable the voice that has made her life possible.
- id=CP-A2 scale=A_intimate
  core_provocation: Can a family remain close when one person's most private mental signals become technically accessible to the people who love them?
  what_is_the_future: Households negotiate permissions for neural data in the same way they now negotiate phones, messages, and medical records.
  what_is_interesting: The central family dispute concerns access to signals that may reveal hesitation without revealing a clear thought.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The parents are not trying to spy on him; they believe access is an act of care, especially when the system detects distress before he admits it.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A home dashboard warns the family that the young man is becoming anxious during a quiet dinner. He must choose between accepting their intervention and protecting the right to experience an emotion without being monitored.
- id=CP-A3 scale=A_intimate
  core_provocation: If a BCI can restore the patterns associated with who you used to be, should recovery mean becoming your old self again?
  what_is_the_future: Rehabilitation software can reinforce earlier motor and behavioral patterns while adapting to a patient's present condition.
  what_is_interesting: The device turns rehabilitation into a negotiation between continuity, change, and identity.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The family begins to disagree because the patient's new preferences seem genuine, while the treatment plan treats them as deviations from the past.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A father finally performs a familiar task independently, then refuses an activity he once loved. His daughter must decide whether to celebrate the new choice or ask the clinic to tune the system toward his former personality.
- id=CP-B1 scale=B_societal
  core_provocation: When a BCI makes work faster, can refusing it remain a genuinely free career choice?
  what_is_the_future: Labor markets divide between jobs designed for neural interfaces and jobs reserved for people who do not use them.
  what_is_interesting: A technology introduced as personal assistance becomes an unofficial qualification for economic participation.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: No law requires workers to implant a device; the pressure comes from harmless-looking performance comparisons and workplace culture.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A skilled applicant rejects an employer's BCI program and is told that the decision will not affect hiring. At the interview, an automated system scores her slower response time as a lack of leadership potential.
- id=CP-B2 scale=B_societal
  core_provocation: If schools can measure learning inside the brain, will education still judge what students can explain and create?
  what_is_the_future: Neural performance data becomes part of assessment, admissions, and debates about academic ability.
  what_is_interesting: The promise of fairer measurement could replace visible achievement with an opaque biological score.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The system is introduced to reduce bias, yet students with unusual but effective ways of thinking are penalized because their neural patterns look unfamiliar.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A student solves an examination problem correctly but receives a warning because her neural activity does not match the approved learning profile. Her teacher must argue that understanding cannot be reduced to the signal the software recognizes.
- id=CP-B3 scale=B_societal
  core_provocation: If neural data can support a legal story about what someone intended, who gets to define the difference between an intention and an action?
  what_is_the_future: Legal systems create rules for admitting, challenging, and interpreting brain-generated evidence.
  what_is_interesting: The courtroom must treat probabilistic biological signals as evidence without pretending they are direct access to the truth.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The decisive dispute is not whether the recording was altered, but whether the defendant's brain produced a signal before the person had consciously settled on a choice.
  what_happens_as_a_story: At trial, two experts interpret the same neural recording in opposite ways. The jury must decide whether a moment of preparation counts as intention, impulse, or neither.
- id=CP-C1 scale=C_radical
  core_provocation: If people can share the shape of an experience directly, what happens to the idea that every mind is fundamentally private?
  what_is_the_future: Communication includes machine-mediated transfers of sensations, intentions, or abstract patterns alongside speech.
  what_is_interesting: Language is no longer the only bridge between minds, so misunderstanding may become less linguistic and more existential.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: Direct sharing does not create perfect empathy; it makes people furious when another person experiences the same event differently from them.
  what_happens_as_a_story: Two siblings connect to settle an argument about their mother's final day. Each receives the other's emotional pattern, but neither recognizes the memory as the same event.
- id=CP-C2 scale=C_radical
  core_provocation: If most of your choices are made before they reach conscious attention, what would it mean to live an intentional life?
  what_is_the_future: Everyday agency is distributed between a person and a system that predicts and filters choices in real time.
  what_is_interesting: The future does not remove human choice through force; it makes choosing feel unnecessary.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The system is most successful when users cannot name the decisions it made for them, because those possibilities never became noticeable.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A man disables his assistant for one day and discovers that he cannot remember how he normally chooses friends, food, or routes. By evening, unfiltered reality feels less like freedom than an emergency.
- id=CP-C3 scale=C_radical
  core_provocation: If a person can operate through several authenticated cognitive profiles, which one is the real self?
  what_is_the_future: Identity is managed partly through switchable neural configurations, with different profiles carrying different permissions, memories, or habits.
  what_is_interesting: The familiar idea of one stable personality gives way to identity as something people can configure and audit.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The most frightening problem is not impersonation by another person but discovering that one of your own profiles made a choice you do not remember endorsing.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A woman returns from a work profile and finds that it signed a contract she would never accept. The dispute becomes whether she can disown an action performed by a version of her that was legally authenticated as herself.
- id=CP-D1 scale=D_second_order (combined with B_societal)
  core_provocation: What skills disappear when an entire generation grows up never needing to express its intentions through hands, screens, or spoken instructions?
  what_is_the_future: Public life has adapted so deeply to BCI interaction that non-neural fallback skills are treated as specialist knowledge.
  what_is_interesting: The technology's second-order effect is not dependence on a device but the disappearance of older ways to coordinate human action.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The people who save the day are not technical experts but older workers whose slower, manual methods were once considered inefficient.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A citywide BCI outage strands trains, hospitals, and offices. A retired technician leads a room of younger workers through procedures they have only ever performed by intention signals.
- id=CP-E1 scale=E_inversion (combined with A_intimate)
  core_provocation: If a BCI can protect you from every choice likely to make you unhappy, how can you tell whether your life is still yours?
  what_is_the_future: Personal neural assistants optimize a user's emotional stability by filtering experiences before conscious awareness.
  what_is_interesting: The device succeeds at its promised benefit so completely that discomfort, risk, and spontaneous change become difficult to choose.
  what_would_surprise_the_reader: The assistant does not need to lie or control the user; it only has to make unwanted possibilities feel impossible to notice.
  what_happens_as_a_story: A woman realizes that she has not argued with her brother for months because the system has prevented their messages from reaching her at difficult moments. She disables it and receives a flood of unresolved life all at once.
