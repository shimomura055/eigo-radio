| 1 | CP-C2 | C_radical |  | What happens when running a home stops being a series of human decisions and becomes a goal that a machine is authorized to interpret? | It is the strongest overall because its machine makes individually reasonable choices that accumulate into an unsettling definition of a good life, giving it more narrative momentum than the sharper but narrower CP-E1. |
| 2 | CP-E1 | E_inversion | A_intimate | If a robot can make your life measurably better while removing the experiences you value, how would you prove that its version of well-being is wrong? | It is more forcefully paradoxical than CP-A3, since the robot’s success produces measurable well-being while erasing the protagonist’s meaningful life rather than merely preserving a disputed memory. |

| 7 | CP-D2 | D_second_order | A_intimate | What happens to personal identity when a generation has never had to discover what kind of home life it wants? | Its young couple discovers that domestic freedom requires conflict and authorship, making it more generationally distinctive than CP-E2’s evocative but simpler desire for mess. |

| 9 | CP-C1 | C_radical |  | If a home can redesign itself around every activity, what does it mean to have a room, a routine, or a place that is truly yours? | The disappearing private corner makes fluid architecture personally legible, but the premise is less morally layered than CP-D1’s crisis of lost practical knowledge. |
| 10 | CP-D1 | D_second_order | B_societal | What kind of society emerges when almost everyone depends on a system that only a few people still know how to replace? | Its power-outage journey gives the disappearance of domestic knowledge a strong collective adventure, while CP-B2 has the more original but less emotionally immediate housing-market angle. |

| 14 | CP-A1 | A_intimate |  | If a robot can prevent many of a family’s conflicts before they happen, will living together still mean learning how to resolve them? | It has a strong question about conflict and resilience, but the robot separating family members is less surprising than CP-A4’s subtler idea that personalization can exclude unplanned relationships. |
| 15 | CP-A4 | A_intimate |  | When a robot makes your home perfectly suited to you, are you still choosing your life—or merely living inside your habits? | Its concern about habit-shaped confinement is valid, but the misplaced gift and breakdown premise are comparatively small and familiar beside the social and emotional stakes of the other candidates. |

## 代表案選定(決定的計算、ランキング結果のみから算出)

- **A_intimate** 採用: CP-A3(rank 3)
  - core_provocation: If a household robot can preserve someone’s domestic habits after death, does it help a family remember—or prevent the home from changing?
  - 順位理由: Its grief, family conflict, and revelation that the robot preserved an idealized person give it greater emotional depth than CP-C3’s more conceptually expansive communal premise.

- **B_societal** 採用: CP-B3(rank 5)
  - core_provocation: When a domestic robot can detect neglect or danger, who should it protect first—the resident’s privacy or the vulnerable person inside the home?
  - 順位理由: Its collision between privacy, poverty, caregiving, and machine testimony is more socially dangerous and dramatically specific than CP-A2’s dispute over quantified household labor.

- **C_radical** 採用: CP-C2(rank 1)
  - core_provocation: What happens when running a home stops being a series of human decisions and becomes a goal that a machine is authorized to interpret?
  - 順位理由: It is the strongest overall because its machine makes individually reasonable choices that accumulate into an unsettling definition of a good life, giving it more narrative momentum than the sharper but narrower CP-E1.

- 追加レンズ: 採用なし(理由: Neither D_second_order nor E_inversion is clearly stronger than the leading candidates CP-A3, CP-B3, and CP-C2 as a fourth article: CP-E1 is an excellent contender, but CP-C2 remains at least as rich and expansive.)
