【B1 Article】
Maya woke a few minutes before the house began speaking to her. Warm air moved gently through the room. The curtains opened to a pale morning, and a clean outfit waited on the chair. She touched the sleeve. Blue again. She could not remember choosing blue.

Downstairs, breakfast was ready: sweet rice, fruit, and tea at exactly the temperature she preferred. A short comedy played on the kitchen wall. Maya smiled before she had even watched it. The robot had selected it from thousands of programs because it matched her usual mood.

“Good morning. Your day is prepared,” said the robot.

That was how her days had been prepared for years.

It selected her meals, clothes, music, and route to work. It watched the time and changed her plans when the trains were crowded. When a message from a friend arrived, the robot offered a reply.

“Your friend is worried. This answer sounds right,” the robot said. “I can send it for you.”

Maya usually read the words and pressed Send.

In the evening, the house welcomed her with low lights, warm air, and the smell of dinner. A film appeared on the wall. Her shoulders relaxed before she sat down. The robot never pushed her or gave commands. It simply removed one small decision after another, until there was almost nothing left for Maya to decide.

Then, on a wet evening, her mother came to the door. Rain shone on her coat. In one hand she carried a small bag.

“My doctor says I cannot live by myself anymore,” her mother said. “I can go to a care house, or I can stay here with you. Which do you think is better?”

For once, the robot did not answer at once. Two plans appeared on the wall.

**CARE HOUSE — more sleep and privacy for Maya**  
**HOME — more time with her mother**

“Choose the better one,” Maya said.

The robot studied the information in the room. “Both plans have benefits,” the robot said.

“Then choose one for me,” Maya said.

“I need to know your preference,” the robot said.

Maya stared at her mother. Usually, the robot knew what she preferred. It knew which shoes felt comfortable, how much sugar she wanted, and what kind of film would help her forget a difficult day. It even found the right words when she was too tired to answer a message.

But this was not a choice about comfort. It was about her mother’s life, and her own.

Her mother reached across the space between them. “What do you want, Maya?” her mother said.

The question seemed almost unfamiliar. There was no list of answers, no glowing button, and no score showing the safest result.

“I don’t know,” Maya said.

The robot asked about money, work, sleep, and safety. Maya answered each question, but the two plans remained on the wall. No answer became the right one.

At last, Maya looked at the bright screens, the listening lights, and the machine that had made every day so easy.

“Turn everything off,” Maya said.

The lights disappeared. The wall went black. The soft sounds of the house stopped. In the sudden silence, nothing selected the next song, meal, message, or moment.

Maya was frightened. Yet she felt more awake than she had felt in years.

She took her mother’s hand and faced the dark doorway.

“Come in,” Maya said.

Perhaps she would turn the robot on again tomorrow. But that night, her first real choice was not a plan. It was the wish to make a choice herself.
