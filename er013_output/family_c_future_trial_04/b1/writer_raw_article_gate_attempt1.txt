## When the Floor Begins to Move

[[IMAGINED: around 2035]]
Picture a weekday morning around 2035, as home robots spread beyond the first places to receive them. In a small kitchen, a mobile robot leaves its charging space and begins the laundry. Its hands lift a shirt, its sensors pause at a chair, and an app directs it to the clean-clothes shelf. Later, it smooths the bed and moves a selected toy aside. A remote helper guides it around a tangled cord.

The resident has reserved a bare patch of floor for charging and keeps pet messes out of its path. She watches the machine return to work, then adds another task in the app.

“Try the bed next,” she says.

She uses the freed time to sit with her child before leaving home. The robot does not understand everything, but its small successes make her plan the morning with energy.
[[/IMAGINED]]

## When Help Becomes Ordinary

[[IMAGINED: around 2040]]
Picture these machines after wider adoption, around 2040. In one household, the floor robot runs more often, so routine vacuuming by hand becomes rare. The resident still checks corners, moves a cord, and cleans a mark the robot cannot lift.

In another home, stairs remain dusty, the ceiling needs a person, and a deep stain in a difficult room resists the machine. The robot pauses beside a pile of clothes, waiting for a decision. Its owner clears a path, then stops to decide whether to supervise or do the task herself.

At dinner she says, “It helps, but it does not let me forget the house.”

She keeps the robot for its regular work, yet writes “check, repeat, finish” on her household list. The cleaner floor saves effort; the unfinished edges keep her alert. The machine has reduced one kind of labor without removing responsibility.
[[/IMAGINED]]

## What Housework Becomes

If this change continued, housework could move rather than disappear. People might spend less time on routine floor cleaning, yet more attention on setup, checking, redoing, and difficult places. One family could welcome more frequent cleaning, while another could protect time for manual work. The attraction would be a home that keeps moving while people regain small pieces of their day; the unease would be that responsibility still returns whenever a robot meets a cord, a stain, a stair, or a room it cannot handle. Sleep and paid work could change for many reasons, so a robot alone would not decide how a household lives.

The future of housework is not a spotless home without effort, but a home where effort waits at the edges.
