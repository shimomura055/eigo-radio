## The First Morning

[[IMAGINED: around 2035]]
Picture a Tuesday morning in 2035, in a town where home robots have just reached local homes. Mina places a charging station beside the wall and opens an app. The robot rolls into the kitchen, avoids a chair, and moves a selected bag from the floor. In the bedroom, it pulls a sheet across the bed and carries clothes toward the laundry area.

When it stops at a loose cord, Mina clears the cord. A remote helper guides the robot around a tight corner. Mina smiles and says, “Try the hall next.” She plans breakfast with her child while the early cleaning continues. Then she leaves the robot working and walks outside without checking every room.
[[/IMAGINED]]

## When Help Becomes Routine

[[IMAGINED: later in the 2030s]]
Picture home robots after they have spread beyond the first regions. In one apartment, the robot cleans the floor each morning. Hand vacuuming is rare, and the resident lets the machine handle the same route again and again.

In another home, the robot stops at the stairs. It cannot reach the ceiling. Deep dirt remains near the doorway, and a cord makes it pause. The resident moves the cord, watches the robot finish, and then cleans the difficult places by hand. At the end of the day, she checks its charging space and asks, “What did you miss this time?”

The robot keeps the floor fresh, but it does not remove the need to supervise. The resident saves some effort, yet she also adds checking, guiding, and redoing to her routine. She keeps using it because the repeated work is useful. She does not trust it with every corner.
[[/IMAGINED]]

## The Work That Remains

If this change spreads, housework could shift rather than vanish. People might spend less time pushing a vacuum and more time checking paths, moving cords, redoing corners, and handling stairs or deep dirt. Cleaning could happen more often because the robot starts the routine, while human attention moves toward exceptions. For some people, that could feel like freedom. For others, it could feel like a new duty that never fully leaves the home.

## The Question in the Quiet

The floors may grow cleaner, but the quietest room could still leave one question awake: who is really in charge?
