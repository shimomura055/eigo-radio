## The First Morning

[[IMAGINED: around 2035]]
Picture a weekday morning around 2035, as home robots begin reaching Mina’s region. The robot rolls from its charging space. Mina has cleared a dedicated strip of floor for it. It recognizes a shoe, a cable, and a bag. In the app, she marks the bag for the hall. Its small arm moves the bag aside. The robot pauses at a twisted cord. A remote helper receives an alert. Mina clears the cord, and cleaning resumes. Later, the robot carries a light laundry load to the next room and straightens a bed. Mina watches from the doorway. “It is not only a vacuum,” she says. She sets a charging time and leaves smiling, ready for tomorrow’s task.
[[/IMAGINED]]

## The Home That Still Needs Hands

[[IMAGINED: around 2035]]
Picture a later evening, after home robots have spread and households have chosen different rules. In one apartment, a robot cleans the floor while its owner cooks. In another, the machine stops before the stairs. It leaves the upper steps untouched. The ceiling remains outside its reach. It avoids a dried spill and waits beside a chair tangled with cords. The owner brings out a manual vacuum and a cloth. She checks the robot’s path, then watches it return to its charging space. “I still have to guard the difficult parts,” she says. She keeps the robot because daily floor work is lighter. She does not trust it to handle every room alone.
[[/IMAGINED]]

## What Housework Becomes

If home robots become common, housework could move rather than vanish. People might spend less time pushing a vacuum across open floors, while spending more time checking paths, clearing cords, cleaning stairs, and handling deep dirt. Some homes could clean more often because routine work feels easy. Other homes could keep manual tools ready for ceilings, stairs, difficult surfaces, or rooms with too many obstacles. A robot could change when people choose to do chores, yet sleep and work decisions would not belong to the machine alone. Families would also decide how much space to give the robot, when to charge it, and when a person must step in. The promise is a lighter daily burden and a home that keeps itself in motion; the worry is that care, correction, and responsibility remain in human hands.

The future of housework is not a silent home, but a home where every pause asks: will you trust the machine, or step in?
