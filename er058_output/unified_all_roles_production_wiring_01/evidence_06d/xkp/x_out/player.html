Full Story Part 1
voice=Aoede		“Beans cost less now!” When you hear that, your wallet pumps its fist. You already imagine your usual cup costing less, too. But a shop’s price may not change right away. When a price tag makes you ask, “Haven’t you heard the news?”, it feels like there is a delay. The International Coffee Organization shared a measure of coffee prices. It fell 8.7% from the month before. The group said more beans picked in Brazil had reached the market. It also expected a good supply to continue. The US Department of Agriculture said several things had raised prices at once. There was bad weather in Brazil and Vietnam. Fewer beans were for sale. Demand was also strong.
Comment 2
voice=Aoede		コーヒー豆の市場価格は下がりましたが、お店の値札はすぐには変わらないようです。では、豆の価格が変わってから、お店の値段に反映されるまで、どれくらいかかるのでしょうか。
Full Story Part 2
voice=Aoede		At the same time, US data on what people pay showed coffee cost 6.1% more than in the same month a year earlier. You might wonder, “Did prices fall or rise?” But this data was for a different month than the bean price measure. It also does not show the price of coffee at a cafe. Don’t put the two numbers in the ring just yet. So, how long does it take for bean prices to reach a price tag? People in the market and experts spoke with Reuters. They estimate raw-bean prices take at least about nine months to appear in US shop prices. The road to a price tag is quite a long journey. The article says US companies that roast coffee keep raw beans in stock. Roasting and packing the beans also take time. These companies often talk with stores about prices on a regular basis. A shop’s price tag does not change the moment the market moves.
Comment 3
voice=Aoede		ここまで、コーヒー豆の価格が下がっても、店の価格がすぐに変わるとは限らないことを見てきました。これからは、豆の価格の変化が店の価格にどう関わるのかに注目してください。
Full Story Part 3
voice=Aoede		However, “about nine months” is an estimate based on interviews. It is not a rule for every company. And it is not a promise that “prices will fall in nine months.” It is too soon to mark the date on your calendar. A cafe cup also costs money for more than just beans. A person who runs a cafe in Chicago said three costs had pushed prices up. These were cups, workers’ pay, and buying beans. Even if bean prices fall, other costs do not always fall with them. If you are waiting for your usual cup to get cheaper, ask more than, “How much do beans cost now?” Also ask, “When does the bean price reach here?” A coffee price tag may have its own hidden clock, separate from the latest market news.
