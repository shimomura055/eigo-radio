# We Thought It Was AI—But There Was a Person Inside Meta’s Muse

Ring, ring. A call seemed to come from an AI agent. But as the conversation went on, the voice was not AI at all. It was a person.

Meta had run a test that caused exactly this surprise.

The test used the phone feature of its AI agent, Muse. In some calls through Muse, trained contract workers made the calls, not AI. They carried each conversation through to the end.

It was like someone took off an AI costume. Then a person stepped out.

Having a person take over is not always a bad thing. A human can handle situations that AI alone finds difficult. As a system, this may even be useful.

But this was where the problem began. A human staff member might handle the call. Then the user’s sensitive information could be shared with a contract worker at a call center.

It is one thing to speak because you think a machine is listening. It is another when you know a person is listening. When people share their names, plans, or personal situations, it matters who listens.

### The hidden person behind the AI sign

Here was the reveal. The test began without clearly telling users that contract workers would make the calls. A user might think the exchange was with AI, even though a person was involved.

AI had not learned to speak like a human. A human stood behind the sign that said AI.

It seemed like a story from the future. But the main question was very basic: Had users been clearly told who was making the call?

As AI becomes able to make calls or reservations for us, this question will become more familiar. The more useful the feature, the more people will want to know who is on the other side. They will want to know if it is AI or human.

### Meta admits a mistake

A Meta executive admitted the mistake. The test had begun without clearly telling users.

For now, Meta has pulled back the human concierge feature. In the AI age, technology that makes a voice sound human is not enough. We must also know, at the start, who is on the other end.

## In one line

Before AI speaks for us, we need to know whether the voice belongs to AI or a person.
